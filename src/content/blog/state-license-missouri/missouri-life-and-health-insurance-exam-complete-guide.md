Missouri's Life and Health insurance exams are Pearson VUE-administered tests available as separate exams (Life: 100 questions, $32, 2 hours; A&H: ~100 questions, $32, 2 hours) or as the combined Life & Accident and Health exam (170 questions, $40, 3 hours). Missouri reports the exam as a single scaled score on a 0–100 scale — 70 passes, and your score report reads only Pass or Fail. The questions are drawn from a general (national) portion and a Missouri state-specific portion, so state-law mastery is essential even though there is no separate per-section pass threshold and no section-only retake. That Missouri state-specific portion covers DCI Commissioner authority, RSMo Chapter 375/379 producer licensing provisions, Healthcare.gov (federal exchange — Missouri has NO state-based marketplace), MO HealthNet (Missouri Medicaid; voter-approved expansion in August 2020; finally implemented summer 2021), and Missouri's pure comparative negligence auto standard. Industry sources put first-attempt pass rates for all candidates at 40-60% — making specific Missouri state-law preparation the most important factor in exam success. ## Exam Format and Specs

Single scaled score, two content areas — the point candidates most often get wrong: Missouri reports one scaled score from 0 to 100 for the whole exam. A 70 passes, and the score report shows only Pass or Fail — it is not the number or percentage of questions you answered correctly, and it is not two separately-reported section percentages. The questions come from a general (national) portion and a Missouri state-specific portion, but there is no separate per-section pass mark and no section-only retake: if you fail, you re-sit the entire exam after a one-day wait. That single-score structure still rewards Missouri-specific preparation, because the state-specific portion is a meaningful share of the questions and is where under-prepared candidates lose the points that drop them below 70.

Combined exam advantage: Combined Life & A&H (170 questions, 3 hours, $40) covers both lines for $40 vs. two separate exams at $64 total ($32 each). For candidates pursuing both lines simultaneously, the combined exam is the efficient choice.

Retakes: Unlimited; one-day (24-hour) wait before re-sitting; you retake the entire exam, not a section. All in-person only as of May 7, 2025.

## Accident & Health Exam Content (~100 Questions)

National Content (~75-80 questions): Health plan types (HMO, PPO, POS); disability income; Medicare Parts A-D; Medicare Supplement; LTC; ACA; COBRA; HIPAA; group health

Missouri State Section (~20-25 questions):

Critical MO A&H state law facts:

Healthcare.gov: Missouri uses Healthcare.gov — the federal ACA marketplace. Missouri does NOT have a state-based exchange. This is the same as Indiana, Ohio, Tennessee, and Virginia. Different from Maryland (Maryland Health Connection), NJ (Get Covered NJ), MN (MNsure), CO (Connect for Health CO).

MO HealthNet — Missouri's Medicaid: Missouri's Medicaid program is called MO HealthNet. Missouri expanded Medicaid through:

August 2020: Missouri voters approved Amendment 2 (ballot initiative) expanding Medicaid

Legislature initially refused to fund it → courts ordered implementation

Summer 2021: Medicaid expansion finally implemented

Adults up to 138% FPL qualify for MO HealthNet

The controversy and ballot initiative history is specifically testable as a distinctively Missouri process

No Missouri individual mandate — no state tax penalty for being uninsured.

Missouri health insurance mandates: Missouri requires certain health insurance policy provisions — verify current mandates at insurance.mo.gov.
