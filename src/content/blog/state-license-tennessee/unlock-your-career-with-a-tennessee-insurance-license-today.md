## Understand the opportunity

A [Tennessee insurance license](/tennessee) opens the door to a flexible and rewarding career path. You not only help people protect their families, homes, and businesses, but you also position yourself for long-term growth in a robust industry. By earning your license, you become eligible to work with top insurance carriers, offer specialized coverage, and build lasting client relationships.

When you think about it, this field can suit many lifestyles. Whether you are a career changer seeking something more stable or a newcomer eager to learn a new trade, insurance can offer a fresh start. You get the chance to explore different lines of authority, such as life and health or property and casualty, depending on where your interests lie.

## Meet Tennessee's requirements

Before you can register for any exam or coursework, there are basic qualifications you must meet. In Tennessee, you need to be at least 18 years old. You also need to pass a background check, which typically involves fingerprinting and verification that you have no felonies or major financial misconduct.

These requirements help ensure that licensed agents are trustworthy and meet certain professional standards. It is worth double-checking current Tennessee regulations so you can stay on track. You do not want to get caught off guard by a missing document or an overlooked detail.

## Decide whether you want pre-licensing education

Here is where Tennessee differs from most states: pre-licensing education is not required. Effective March 21, 2023, the legislature struck the mandatory course requirement from T.C.A. § 56-6-106 (2023 Tenn. Acts ch. 57), so once you meet the age and background requirements you can go straight to the exam. There is no course certificate to upload and no minimum hour count to satisfy.

That does not make studying optional in any practical sense. The exam is written to a state content outline covering policy provisions, Tennessee insurance law, and ethics, and most first-time candidates use exam-prep coursework to get ready. ## Register for the state exam

Because Tennessee does not gate the exam behind a course, you can schedule with Pearson VUE as soon as you feel ready. Testing centers across the state offer in-person appointments, and remote options may be available, giving you some flexibility to pick the best setup for your schedule.

Study thoroughly as each exam covers specific regulations, ethical practices, and industry standards. You might see questions that test your understanding of policy types and how they apply to real-life scenarios. If you find yourself short on time, focus on your trouble spots rather than re-reading the entire textbook. Practice exams are invaluable here. They show you how the test questions are framed so you can pace yourself accurately.

## Submit your licensing application

After you pass your exam, you will complete a licensing application through the [Tennessee Department of Commerce and Insurance](/tennessee/requirements). This step usually involves paying a processing fee and confirming you have passed both your background check and the required test. Make sure to include all necessary identification and documentation, because any missing items can delay your approval.

It usually takes a short while for the state to issue your license, although processing times vary. During this waiting period, you might prepare your résumé or start networking with local agencies so you can be ready to jump into work as soon as your certification is official.

## Maintain your license over time

Earning your license is only the beginning. To keep it valid, you need to fulfill [Tennessee continuing education](/tennessee/continuing-education) (CE) hours as required by state law. Renewal cycles prompt you to update your knowledge on new products, changing regulations, and evolving industry trends.

At first, it may seem like another round of homework. In reality, CE courses give you the chance to expand your skill set and stay competitive in a fast-paced market. Whether you choose in-person workshops or online classes, these courses keep you current so your clients can trust your guidance.

## Common questions about Tennessee insurance license

### How long does it take to get a Tennessee insurance license?

It generally depends on your schedule and how soon you are ready to test. Tennessee requires no pre-licensing course, so nothing forces a minimum timeline — some candidates study on their own, pass, and hold a license within a month. If you use an optional prep course, expect roughly 20 to 40 hours of material depending on the line of authority. Others spread out their study time over a few months to accommodate work or family commitments.

### What type of pre-licensing course should I choose?

Since Tennessee does not mandate one, this is a study decision rather than a compliance requirement — pick the material that matches the lines of authority you plan to sell. Life and health courses generally focus on policies like whole, term, disability, long-term care, and medical coverage. Property and casualty training targets areas like homeowners, auto, and business insurance. Make sure whatever you choose matches your career goals.

### Can I take the state exam online?

Yes. Many testing centers offer remote testing options that allow you to take the exam from home. Be sure to confirm your computer meets the necessary technical requirements and follow all guidelines for proctoring. If you prefer the traditional approach, you can also take the exam in person at an approved facility.

### What does the entire process cost?

Your unavoidable expenses are the state exam fee and the license application fee, plus fingerprinting and background check costs. Study materials are on top of that and are entirely your call in Tennessee, since no course is required. Many providers bundle courses with study guides and practice tests, so it is a good idea to compare packages before you spend.

### Do I need separate licenses for different insurance lines?

No. Tennessee issues a single producer license that carries every line of authority you qualify for — you do not hold a separate license for each line. Each line does have its own exam, though, and you submit a separate application (with its own fee) to add each one. Tennessee does let you book Life and Accident & Health as one combined OnVUE reservation for $80 instead of $55 apiece, but that is still two separately scored exams in a single appointment rather than one merged test. You can build toward multiple lines by passing the exam for each one. Some agents choose to start with one line and expand later, while others prepare for multiple lines at once.
