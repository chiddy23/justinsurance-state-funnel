Arizona law still ties your license to a physical compliance footprint before you worry about carrier appointments. **A.R.S. § 20-290** requires every individual producer to keep "the usual and customary records" for licensed transactions at the producer's **principal place of business**, retain them for **three years** after each transaction completes, and make them available to the director during business hours per the Arizona Legislature's [statute page](https://www.azleg.gov/ars/20/00290.htm). The same section requires business entities to keep at least one appropriately licensed individual producer in each Arizona office where the entity transacts insurance. If you cannot describe that office on day one, you are not ready to file.

This guide walks through the actual sequence Arizona resident producers follow in 2026 — including the September 2025 PSI vendor transition, the records and CE workload most candidates underestimate, and the long-term care training rule that quietly disqualifies producers who skip it.

## Step 1 — Pick your line of authority and confirm prerequisites

Arizona issues separate authority for Life, Accident & Health (A&H), Property, Casualty, and Personal Lines. Most new producers start with one of two paths:

- **Life + A&H combined** — covers life insurance, annuities, health, disability, and accident products. Most common path for financial services and Medicare-adjacent agents.
- **Property + Casualty (P&C) combined** — covers homeowners, auto, commercial property, and liability lines.

You can hold both, but you sit for separate PSI exams and pay separate fees. The [DIFI individual producer application page](https://difi.az.gov/producers/producer-agentbroker/producer-agentbroker-license-application-individual) lists the application steps, residency confirmation, and the linked NIPR portal you will use to file.

If you plan to sell long-term care insurance, you must hold A&H authority **first** — there is no shortcut. **A.R.S. § 20-1691.12** (published on the [Arizona Legislature site](https://www.azleg.gov/ars/20/01691-12.htm)) blocks any producer from selling, soliciting, or negotiating LTC insurance until the producer holds accident and health authority **and** finishes **8 hours of initial Arizona-approved LTC training**, followed by **4 hours in each subsequent two-year period** after the initial training period.

## Step 2 — Schedule and pass the PSI exam

PSI replaced Pearson VUE as Arizona's licensing exam vendor in September 2025. NIPR's [Arizona licensing overview](https://nipr.com/licensing-center/state-information/arizona) lists PSI as the current vendor and confirms exam scores remain valid for **12 months** from the test date. Schedule directly through the [PSI Arizona portal](https://test-takers.psiexams.com/anzins) — accounts created on the old Pearson VUE Arizona portal do not transfer.

What to expect on test day:

- Government-issued photo ID matching your registration name exactly
- Arrival 30 minutes before your appointment
- Fingerprinting and palm-vein scanning at check-in
- No personal items in the testing room (lockers provided)
- Score report printed at the test center immediately after submission

If you fail, PSI publishes the section-level score breakdown so you know which content area to re-study before retaking. Arizona does not impose a retake waiting period beyond PSI's own 24-hour cooldown.

## Step 3 — Submit fingerprints and background disclosures

Arizona requires electronic fingerprinting through DIFI's approved vendor before license issuance. The [DIFI licensing hub](https://difi.az.gov/licensing) links the current fingerprinting instructions; do not submit through a generic IdentoGO page without a service code from DIFI, or your prints will not be transmitted to the right queue.

Background disclosures appear on the NIPR application. Disclose every misdemeanor, every alias, and every prior license action — even ones from 20 years ago in another state. DIFI cross-checks against NAIC's Regulatory Information Retrieval System, and undisclosed actions trigger investigative holds far longer than the actions themselves would have caused if disclosed cleanly.

## Step 4 — File on NIPR and wait for DIFI issuance

Once PSI scores are in and fingerprints clear, file your application through [NIPR LicenseHub](https://pdb.nipr.com/my-nipr/frontend/identify-licensee). DIFI's typical issuance window is 7–14 business days for clean applications; background hits or incomplete disclosures can stretch that to 30–60 days while DIFI's investigations team reviews.

You'll receive your National Producer Number (NPN) and license number once approved. Don't market or solicit business until both numbers are issued — Arizona enforcement orders cite producers who relied on "imminent issuance" promises from recruiters and started writing before authority existed.

## Continuing education: the 48/6 rhythm DIFI sets

DIFI's [continuing education page](https://difi.az.gov/insurance-ce) documents the **48 hours of CE per 4-year license term** for most major-line resident producers, with a **6-hour ethics subset** that must appear in each license term. The 4-year cycle is unusual — most states run 2-year cycles. Plan your CE pacing accordingly so you don't stack 48 hours into the final 6 months before renewal.

Nonresidents generally rely on home-state CE reciprocity when their resident state recognizes Arizona credits. Verify both states' rules before you assume reciprocity clears Arizona audits — DIFI has cited producers who relied on home-state CE that did not actually satisfy the Arizona content categories.

If you sell National Flood Insurance Program (NFIP) policies, DIFI's CE materials incorporate federal flood training expectations beyond the 48-hour state requirement. Treat that as post-license homework separate from the PSI exam content.

## Step 5 — Records, principal place, and the audit trail

Back to **§ 20-290**: the three-year retention clock starts at transaction completion, not at policy issuance. For an annuity sold in 2026, your file must remain retrievable through 2029 even if the policy lapses in 2027. For a P&C policy renewed annually, each year creates a new transaction and a new clock.

DIFI examiners look for:

- Application copies (signed, dated, with replacement disclosures if applicable)
- Premium payment records and trust account reconciliations
- Communication logs (email, text, recorded calls where required)
- Carrier appointment paperwork and termination notices
- E&O policy declarations covering the relevant period

Set up your CRM and document storage plan **while** you study for PSI — not after your first E&O scare or DIFI audit notice.

## Common pitfalls Arizona resident producers hit

- **Confusing PSI with the old Pearson VUE portal.** Pearson VUE accounts no longer exist for Arizona insurance — start fresh on the PSI portal.
- **Selling LTC without § 20-1691.12 training.** This is a strict-liability statute. No grandfather clause, no "I'll get the CE next month" defense.
- **Skipping fingerprints from the DIFI-coded vendor.** Generic IdentoGO submissions don't reach DIFI.
- **Misreading the 4-year CE clock as 2 years.** The 48-hour requirement is per 4-year term — but it's still 12 hours per year on average if you pace it properly.
- **Ignoring the principal-place requirement for business entities.** If your agency operates from a Phoenix address, that address must be on file with DIFI as the principal place of business for the entity license.

## Comparing Arizona's post-license load with Georgia and Pennsylvania

Other states emphasize different parts of the licensing pipeline:

- **Georgia** (per [OCI Rule 120-2-3-.08](https://oci.georgia.gov/agents-agency-licensing/education-providers-courses-and-instructors)) reduced prelicensing to **8.0 hours per major line** in a recent rule change, but maintains 24 hours of CE every 2 years.
- **Pennsylvania** charges a **$55** resident application fee, routes exams through PSI with a separate IdentoGO fingerprinting code, and runs CE on a 2-year cycle of 24 hours per the [PA initial licensing page](https://www.pa.gov/agencies/insurance/licensing/licensees/initial-insurance-producer-licensing-process.html).
- **Arizona** emphasizes records and principal place on the front end, then 48/6 CE plus 8/4 LTC training over a longer 4-year cycle.

Multistate producers should not assume Georgia's lighter prelicensing course satisfies Arizona's CE or LTC statutes. Each state's rules apply independently to your resident license.

## Application mechanics and licensing timeline

Realistic timeline for a clean Arizona application:

| Step | Typical duration |
|---|---|
| Prelicensing study (recommended) | 1–3 weeks |
| PSI exam scheduling | 3–7 days |
| PSI exam + score report | Same day |
| Fingerprinting + clearance | 5–10 business days |
| NIPR application + DIFI review | 7–14 business days |
| **Total (clean application)** | **3–6 weeks** |

Background hits or disclosure issues can extend this to 60–90 days. A.R.S. § 20-283 lists the narrow [exceptions to producer licensing](https://www.azleg.gov/ars/20/00283.htm) — clerical employees, certain group enrollment clerks, and a handful of other tightly defined roles. If you fall outside those carveouts, plan on PSI regardless of what a recruiter promised about "captive desk licenses."

## Why Arizona pairs CE rigor with strict recordkeeping

Because Arizona pairs **48/6 CE** with strict **§ 20-290** recordkeeping, audits hit both the classes you finished last quarter and the files you kept three years ago. The state's enforcement focus is on suitability, especially in annuity and LTC sales — and the audit chain almost always pulls CE certificates and transaction records together. Build your CE tracking system at the same time as your CRM, not after the first compliance letter arrives.

## Bottom line

Secure a real principal office that satisfies **§ 20-290**, pass PSI within the 12-month NIPR score window, file through NIPR/DIFI as instructed, then line up **48/6 CE plus 8/4 LTC training** before you market regulated products.
