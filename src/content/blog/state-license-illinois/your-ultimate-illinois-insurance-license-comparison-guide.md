## Understand your license options

Knowing the differences between license types is the key to gaining the right certification. All Illinois licenses grant you permission to sell and advise on specific insurance lines. However, you will find unique requirements depending on whether you focus on Life, Health, Property, or Casualty coverage.

Before picking your track, ask yourself what kind of policies resonate with you. Do you like discussing long-term financial security? Life Insurance might be a natural fit. If you are passionate about helping people with medical concerns, Health Insurance could be your go-to. And if you see a future in working with homeowners and drivers, then Property and Casualty could jumpstart your career in those areas.

## Review the [pre-licensing requirements](/illinois/prelicensing)

Once you have pinned down the license or combination you want, the official next step is to complete your mandatory pre-licensing coursework. In Illinois, this typically involves [20 hours of training per insurance line, with at least 7.5 of those hours delivered through a live or interactive format](https://idoi.illinois.gov/producers/licensescertificationsfaqs/become-resident-producer.html). That means you can study online through self-study modules, but you also need something more hands-on, like webinars or in-person classes. JustInsurance offers the Life and Health prelicensing courses; property and casualty prelicensing is completed through a state-approved P&C provider.

It is critical to look for reputable programs that are authorized by the state. Valid pre-licensing courses provide a certificate of completion, which you will need to show when you register for the final licensing exam. On top of meeting the formal hour requirement, aim to pick a course that fits your learning style. Some providers offer extra practice quizzes, flashcards, and instructor support, which can make a big difference in your overall comfort on test day.

The key is to not rush your coursework. Budget enough time to digest the material, especially if you are tackling multiple lines together. By pacing yourself, you set the groundwork for understanding core concepts and how to apply them in real-world scenarios.

## Explore your study methods

How you approach studying will directly impact your success. Everyone learns differently, so do not settle for a method that leaves you bored or overwhelmed. If you thrive in group discussions, look for live webinars or in-person classes. If you prefer setting your own pace, an online self-study course might be the right pick.

## Register for the exam

After you finish your mandated coursework, the next item on your to-do list is to schedule your licensing exam with a state-approved testing vendor. Typically, you can register online or by phone, and you will need to pay an exam fee at the time of booking. In most cases, you can choose a date and testing site that suits your location and schedule.

The exam itself is multiple-choice and covers both general insurance knowledge and Illinois-specific regulations. Each test section varies slightly, but you can expect questions on topics like policy provisions, licensing requirements, ethics, and more. Pay attention to the recommended passing scores since each license line has its own benchmark. If you have studied consistently and understand the concepts, you will be well-positioned to pass on your first attempt.

Many test-takers benefit from arriving early on exam day and setting aside a little time to review flashcards or notes before you go in. Make sure you bring the necessary documents or IDs required by the state. Once you finish, you will receive a preliminary pass or fail result. If you pass, the official next steps become much simpler.

## Plan for costs and fees

Whether you are going for a single line or combining two insurance licenses, it is essential to be aware of the financial details. Beyond the cost of your pre-licensing course, you will pay a [$92 Pearson VUE fee for each exam portion](https://home.pearsonvue.com/il/insurance) — [Illinois splits every major-line exam into a General portion and a State portion](https://idoi.illinois.gov/producers/licensescertificationsfaqs/faqs-producer-licensing-exam-changes-pearsonvuetesting.html), so budget $184 per line unless you book both portions together on the same order for a discount. [Illinois does not require fingerprinting for most resident applicants](https://idoi.illinois.gov/producers/licensescertificationsfaqs/become-resident-producer.html) — the state relies on application disclosures instead, so you can budget $0 for that step in most cases.

Additionally, you will need to pay the state license application fee, which can vary based on your combined or single line of authority. It is safest to set aside extra funds for any unforeseen costs, including study materials or retake fees, just in case. Being prepared for these expenses prevents last-minute stress and keeps you focused on your studies rather than scrambling for cash.
