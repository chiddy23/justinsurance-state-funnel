If you’re ready to launch a new career in the insurance industry, earning your [Texas life and health insurance license](/texas) is your crucial first step. This license allows you to help fellow Texans safeguard their financial futures, and it can open up new opportunities for professional growth. Because the process involves optional [pre-licensing](/texas/prelicensing) education, a state exam, and a final application, it helps to have a clear plan from the start.

Below, you’ll learn each stage of the journey, from confirming your eligibility through maintaining your license. You’ll also discover practical tips on meeting requirements, preparing for exams, and keeping track of important documentation. By the end, you should have a better sense of how to navigate the entire licensing process with confidence.

## Confirm your eligibility

Before you invest time and resources, take a moment to ensure you meet the state’s basic prerequisites. In Texas, you typically need to be at least 18 years old and a resident of the state, with no disqualifying criminal history. Most individuals fall under these requirements with no issue, but it’s best to check the [Texas Department of Insurance](/texas/requirements) (TDI) website for the latest details and any special circumstances.

You’ll also need a Social Security number and a valid government-issued photo ID. Keep these documents handy, because you’ll likely need quick access when you fill out applications and verify your identity later on. If you find that you have a unique situation—such as a past criminal record—consult the TDI’s guidelines to see if you qualify or require any additional steps.

## Consider a pre-licensing course (optional)

Unlike most states, Texas does not require you to complete [Texas pre-licensing education](/texas/prelicensing) before you can sit for the life and health insurance exam. Even so, many candidates opt for a structured course — usually around 40 hours of coursework covering topics like policy provisions, ethical conduct, state regulations, and insurance products — to give themselves a solid foundation before test day.

Pre-licensing courses are offered online or in-person, providing flexibility to fit your schedule. If you’re someone who wants a more hands-on approach, a classroom setting might be a good fit. On the other hand, online courses can be ideal if you’re balancing a full-time job or other commitments. In either case, make sure the course you select is approved by the TDI so you receive the official course completion certificate.

## Schedule your Texas exam

Once you feel prepared — whether or not you took a pre-licensing course — the next step is to register for the state licensing exam. Scheduling is done through authorized testing vendors who coordinate with the TDI. You’ll set an exam date that fits your calendar and pay an exam fee—fees can vary, so always check the official testing site for the current amount.

When you register, you’ll also have the option to choose your exam location. Test centers are located throughout the state, ensuring you can likely find a convenient spot. Keep in mind that popular times fill up fast. If weekend or evening slots are in high demand, consider booking a few weeks in advance to secure your preferred time.

## Study effectively for the exam

Even if you took a pre-licensing course that covers the primary information, it’s a good idea to brush up on crucial details and practice with sample questions. Examination topics often include underwriting procedures, state laws, policy types, and ethical guidelines. Building a well-rounded study plan will boost your confidence when exam day arrives.

Here are a few strategies you may find helpful:

-   Review your course materials daily, focusing on areas you found most challenging.
-   Complete full-length practice exams to develop test-taking stamina.
-   Form or join a study group for encouragement and idea-sharing.
-   Break complex topics into smaller parts—for instance, practice one policy type in the morning, then move on to another concept in the afternoon.

Consider treating your study schedule like a job. Set specific goals each day, and keep track of your progress. That way, you’re less likely to feel overwhelmed and more likely to retain what you learn.

## Take and pass the exam

On exam day, arrive at the test center early so you can settle in and reduce any last-minute nerves. Make sure you have the required identification, as you may be turned away without it. You’ll sign in, secure personal items, and follow the proctor’s instructions on how to begin.

The exam itself typically includes multiple-choice questions. Some will be straightforward definitions, while others test your ability to apply knowledge to real-life scenarios. It’s important to pace yourself, read each question carefully, and use any time-management strategies you practiced with your sample tests. If you’ve studied diligently, you’ll recognize most terms and policies without much trouble.

Once you’ve completed the exam, you’ll usually receive immediate feedback on whether you passed. If you’re successful, save or print a copy of your exam results. This document confirms you’ve met the testing requirement and can proceed with the final application steps. If you didn’t pass on your first try, don’t be discouraged—many people retake the exam after reviewing their weaker areas.

## Apply and maintain your license

After you pass, submit your official application to the TDI. The application typically asks for personal information, exam documentation, and a background check. You’ll also pay a licensing fee, so have a credit or debit card ready for online submissions.

This final stage can take a few weeks for processing and background checks. Once approved, you’ll receive your Texas life and health insurance license, allowing you to start working with clients and connecting them to important coverage options. From there, it’s all about maintaining your license by completing [continuing education](/texas/continuing-education) (CE) credits. CE ensures you’re always up to date with the latest industry regulations and best practices.

## Keep key documents in order

Throughout this process, you’ll collect course certificates, exam passes, and application forms. Organizing your records in one place makes it easier to provide any required follow-ups. While digital storage works well for many people, you might also keep hard copies in a dedicated folder at home. Here are some of the most important documents to file:

-   Pre-licensing certificate of completion
-   Exam entry confirmation and score report
-   Application submission confirmation
-   Payment receipts for exam and application fees
-   Proof of [continuing education](/texas/continuing-education) (required periodically after licensing)

Staying organized helps you respond faster if the TDI requests additional information. It also ensures you won’t have to scramble to find documents when it’s time to renew.
