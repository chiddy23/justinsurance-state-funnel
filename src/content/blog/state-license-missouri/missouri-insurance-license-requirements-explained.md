Missouri's licensing requirements are among the most accessible of all comparison states — no PLE, no fingerprinting, exam fees of $32-$40 that are among the lowest in the country, and only 16 CE hours required for renewal. The most distinctive features are: the recent May 2025 elimination of remote online exams (now in-person only); the flat $100 application fee covering all lines; and a temporary license that exists only for business continuity, never as a bridge for first-time applicants. Missouri's pure comparative negligence auto system (plaintiff can recover even at 99% fault), the 5-employee workers' comp threshold (with 1-employee construction exception), and MO HealthNet (Missouri's Medicaid expansion program) are the key state law areas on the exam. Here's the complete breakdown.

## Eligibility Requirements

To apply for a Missouri resident insurance producer license:

Be at least 18 years old

Disclose all prior administrative actions, criminal history, license denials/revocations

Federal restriction: 18 U.S.C. § 1033 applies (certain felony convictions)

Criminal background checked as part of application

1033 Waiver available for those with certain felony convictions — must be obtained before submitting application

## PLE Status — Not Required

Missouri does not require [prelicensing](/missouri/prelicensing) education. No mandated course hours, no PLE certification exam. Candidates proceed directly to scheduling the Pearson VUE exam.

Study preparation is strongly recommended — industry sources cite first-attempt pass rates for all candidates of 40-60%, and Missouri's exam includes a state-specific portion that requires targeted Missouri state law preparation that generic national materials don't provide. ## Fingerprinting — Not Required

Missouri does not require a separate fingerprinting vendor appointment for the standard insurance producer license application. A background check occurs through the application review process.

## Application Requirements

Through NIPR or paper:

Application fee: $100 (flat; covers all lines on same application)

NIPR transaction fee: $5.60

Wait 24-48 hours after passing before applying

Apply within 1 year of passing exam

Processing: 5-10 business days

Fee waiver: Available for qualifying low-income individuals and veterans — apply to DCI.

1033 Waiver: Candidates with certain felony convictions must request consent from DCI before submitting license application.

Temporary license — business continuity only: Missouri does issue temporary producer licenses, but not to first-time applicants. Under RSMo § 375.025 the DCI director may grant a temporary license for up to 90 days, without an exam, to keep an existing book of business serviced — for example to the surviving spouse or court-appointed personal representative of a producer who dies or becomes disabled, to a member or employee of a licensed business entity after the individual designated on its license dies or becomes disabled, or to the designee of a producer entering active service in the Armed Forces. The director may also issue one in any other circumstance where the public interest is best served. A new candidate has no temporary option and cannot sell until the regular license is issued.

## 5 Frequently Asked Questions

- What changed about Missouri exams in May 2025? Effective May 7, 2025, Pearson VUE discontinued remote online exam delivery (OnVUE) for Missouri. All Missouri insurance licensing exams must now be taken in-person at a Pearson VUE test center. Candidates who were planning remote testing must schedule at a physical test center instead.
- How is Missouri's insurance exam structured? Missouri's Pearson VUE exam covers both national/general insurance content and Missouri-specific state laws, rules, and regulations. It is reported as a single combined scaled score from 0 to 100, and you need an overall 70 to pass — the national and state portions are not scored or passed separately. If you fail, you wait 24 hours and retake the full exam at the standard fee.
- How does Missouri's $100 flat application fee compare to other states? Missouri's $100 flat fee covers all lines of authority on the same application — the most you'll pay regardless of how many lines you apply for. Indiana charges $40 per line (two-line application = $80); MN charges $50/line + $20 tech (two-line = $120); MD charges $54. For single-line applications, Missouri's $100 is the highest among comparison states. For multi-line applications, Missouri's flat fee becomes relatively more favorable.
- What is Missouri's pure comparative negligence standard? Missouri uses pure comparative negligence (Mo. Rev. Stat. § 537.765) — a plaintiff can recover damages even when partially responsible for an accident. Even a plaintiff who is 99% at fault can recover 1% of damages. This is the most plaintiff-friendly comparative fault standard — different from Indiana (51% bar), MN/CO (50% bar), and completely different from MD/VA (contributory negligence; any fault = zero recovery).
- What is MO HealthNet and how did Medicaid expand in Missouri? MO HealthNet is Missouri's Medicaid program name. Missouri voters approved Medicaid expansion (Amendment 2) in August 2020 by ballot initiative. The Missouri legislature initially refused to fund the expansion, but courts ordered implementation — expansion was finally implemented in summer 2021. Adults up to 138% FPL now qualify for MO HealthNet.
