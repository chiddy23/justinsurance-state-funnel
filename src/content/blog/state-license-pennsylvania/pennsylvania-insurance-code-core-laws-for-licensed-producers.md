Passing the state exam gets you licensed. Staying compliant as a working Pennsylvania producer is a separate challenge — and the Pennsylvania Insurance Code (Title 40 of the Pennsylvania Statutes) is the master rulebook you need to navigate. Pennsylvania has a sophisticated regulatory environment that has evolved significantly in recent years, including the elimination of [prelicense](/pennsylvania/prelicensing) education requirements (Act 142 of 2024) and new mandatory CE topic requirements (effective April 29, 2025).

Here are the most important areas of Pennsylvania insurance law every producer needs to understand.

## What the Pennsylvania Insurance Code Is

The Pennsylvania Insurance Code is the statutory framework that regulates insurance throughout the Commonwealth. It's found in:

Pennsylvania Statutes Title 40 (40 P.S.) — comprehensive insurance code

Title 75 (Vehicles) — Motor Vehicle Financial Responsibility Law

Pennsylvania Insurance Department Regulations (31 Pa. Code) — administrative rules implementing the Code

Department Bulletins, Circular Letters, and Notices — guidance from the Insurance Department

Key sections include:

40 P.S. §233 et seq. — producer licensing provisions

40 Pa. Stat. §310.8 — [continuing education](/pennsylvania/continuing-education) requirements

75 Pa. C.S. §1705 — auto insurance Tort Choice (Limited vs. Full Tort)

Section 403.1-B of Act 48 — Annuity Best Interest training requirements

Various other sections affecting specific topics

The Code is administered by the Pennsylvania Insurance Department (PID), led by the Pennsylvania Insurance Commissioner.

The Code covers everything from licensing and producer duties to policy forms, claims handling, consumer protections, and enforcement. Every licensed producer, broker, adjuster, and insurance company operating in Pennsylvania is bound by it.

## Key Sections Producers Should Know

Producer Licensing Provisions. Foundation provisions covering producer qualifications, licensing requirements, examinations, and standards of conduct.

40 Pa. Stat. §310.8 — Continuing Education. Establishes the 24-hour CE requirement (with 3 hours Ethics for all and 2 hours Flood for P&C/Personal Lines effective April 29, 2025).

75 Pa. C.S. §1705 — Auto Tort Choice. Pennsylvania's distinctive Limited Tort vs. Full Tort election system for auto insurance.

Unfair Methods of Competition and Unfair Practices. Pennsylvania prohibits specific conduct considered unfair or deceptive — misrepresentation, twisting, churning, rebating, defamation, and more.

Replacement of Life Insurance and Annuities. Specific rules govern the replacement of existing life insurance and annuity contracts.

Annuity Best Interest Standards (Section 403.1-B of Act 48). Pennsylvania adopted enhanced Annuity Best Interest standards aligned with NAIC 2020 Annuity Model Law. Producers licensed on or after June 20, 2022 must complete a 4-hour Annuity Best Interest course.

Unfair Claims Settlement Practices. Rules governing how claims must be handled by insurers and adjusters.

Free-Look Periods. Pennsylvania requires free-look periods on life insurance and annuity contracts.

Long-Term Care Insurance Standards. Specific rules for LTC sales, including the 8-hour initial and 4-hour ongoing training requirements with LTC Partnership-specific provisions.

Privacy and Information Protection. How you collect, store, and share client information is regulated under Pennsylvania law and federal frameworks like HIPAA and GLBA.

Pennie. Pennsylvania's state-based health insurance exchange (established 2020).

## Recent Major Changes

Act 142 of 2024 (Effective April 29, 2025). Eliminated the prelicense education (PLE) requirement for Pennsylvania insurance producer licensure. Pennsylvania now joins a small group of states without mandatory PLE.

New CE Topic Requirements (Effective April 29, 2025). Pennsylvania introduced new mandatory CE topic requirements:

3 hours Ethics for all producers

2 hours Flood Insurance for P&C/Personal Lines producers (even if not actively selling flood)

New compliance deadlines for existing and new licensees

Enhanced Premium Tax Credit Expiration (December 31, 2025). While this was a federal change, it had significant Pennsylvania impact through Pennie marketplace coverage. Average premiums for Pennie enrollees increased 102% in 2026 due to expiration.

Annuity Best Interest Standards. Pennsylvania adopted enhanced standards aligned with NAIC 2020 Annuity Model Law, with reciprocity for training completed in other adopting states.

These changes reflect Pennsylvania's ongoing modernization and responsiveness to evolving insurance markets and federal policy developments.

Pennsylvania's insurance regulator is the Pennsylvania Insurance Commissioner, who leads the PID. The current Commissioner (as of 2026) is Michael Humphreys. The Commissioner:

Licenses insurance producers and companies

Regulates insurance companies operating in Pennsylvania

Enforces the Insurance Code

## Pennsylvania's Regulatory Environment

Compared to many states, Pennsylvania's regulatory environment is generally viewed as:

Mature given Pennsylvania's long insurance industry history

Detail-oriented with specific requirements in replacement, suitability, and consumer protections

Modernizing with recent regulatory updates (PLE elimination, new CE topics)

Consumer-protective through robust consumer protection enforcement

Distinctive with Tort Choice auto insurance, Pennie state-based exchange, and Pennsylvania-specific producer requirements

Accessible following recent licensing modernization

Working in Pennsylvania means real attention to compliance — but with sophisticated rules reflecting the state's regulatory expertise.

## Why Knowing the Code Matters

New producers sometimes treat the Insurance Code as academic — material to study for the exam and forget afterward. That's a serious mistake.

Every disciplinary action the PID takes against a Pennsylvania producer is rooted in a specific section of the Code. Understanding the rules isn't just about passing the exam — it's about recognizing what you can and can't do, what you must disclose, and what conduct puts your license at risk.

The PID also publishes Circular Letters, regulations, and guidance through insurance.pa.gov that clarify how specific sections are applied.

## Required Notification of Changes

Once you have your Pennsylvania license, you must notify the PID of any change in:

Business address

Residence address

Email address

Employer

Name (e.g., due to marriage, divorce)

Updates can typically be made through Sircon, NIPR, or directly with the PID.

## How to Stay Compliant

Know the rules for your products. If you sell life insurance, know replacement rules. If you sell annuities, know Best Interest. If you sell LTC, know the training requirements.

Document everything. Notes on client conversations, reasons for recommendations, forms signed and retained. Documentation is your primary defense if questions arise.

Follow PID Circular Letters. Subscribe to PID communications and review them periodically through insurance.pa.gov.

Complete your CE seriously. Continuing education is where compliance knowledge stays current. Don't treat it as busywork.

Update your information promptly. Pennsylvania requires timely notification of changes.

When uncertain, ask. The PID and reputable industry attorneys can provide guidance on specific situations.

## Pennsylvania Insurance Fraud Considerations

Pennsylvania prohibits and prosecutes insurance fraud:

Fraud investigations through the PID and law enforcement

Producer fraud violations result in license loss

Both criminal and civil consequences possible

Reporting suspected fraud is a producer obligation

Quality CE includes anti-fraud content and reporting procedures.

## 5 Frequently Asked Questions

- Do I need to read the entire Pennsylvania Insurance Code? No. Focus on the sections that apply to your practice — producer licensing provisions, replacement rules, suitability standards, free-look periods, and the specific product areas you sell.
- Where can I access Pennsylvania insurance laws? Pennsylvania Statutes Title 40 is available through the Pennsylvania General Assembly website. Pennsylvania Insurance Code regulations (31 Pa. Code) are available through the Pennsylvania Bulletin. PID guidance is available at insurance.pa.gov.
- What's the most significant recent change in Pennsylvania insurance regulation? Act 142 of 2024 eliminated the prelicense education requirement (effective April 29, 2025). Combined with new CE topic requirements (3-hour Ethics and 2-hour Flood Insurance), Pennsylvania has substantially modernized its producer licensing framework.
- What happens if I unintentionally violate a Code provision? Intent matters in the PID's evaluation, but it's not a complete defense. Penalties range from warnings to fines, suspension, or revocation depending on severity, history, and circumstances.
- Who is Pennsylvania's Insurance Commissioner? As of 2026, Michael Humphreys serves as Pennsylvania Insurance Commissioner, leading the Pennsylvania Insurance Department.
