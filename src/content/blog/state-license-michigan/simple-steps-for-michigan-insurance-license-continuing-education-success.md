If you’re eager to build a successful insurance career in Michigan, getting your license and staying current with continuing education requirements is essential. The path can feel overwhelming at first, but breaking it into manageable steps makes all the difference. ## Understand the basics

Earning your Michigan insurance license involves several important milestones. You must be at least 18 years old, complete mandatory pre-licensing education, and pass the state licensing exam. After that, you’ll need to fulfill regular continuing education requirements to keep your license active.

Give yourself enough time to absorb and practice each step. By knowing what to expect upfront, you can plan your schedule and budget more efficiently. Trust yourself to learn the material, and remember that plenty of resources are available to guide you.

## Complete your pre-licensing

Before you can take the state exam, Michigan requires you to finish a set number of pre-licensing hours for each insurance line you want to practice. For example, you might need 20 hours per line of authority, focusing on topics like policy types, ethics, and state regulations.

Taking this education seriously helps you establish a strong footing:

1.  Pick a reputable pre-licensing provider. Look for approved courses that match your learning style—online, in-person, or a blend of both.
2.  Create a study routine. Plan to review material daily or weekly. Consistent study sessions often yield better results than last-minute cramming.
3.  Practice with sample quizzes. Quizzes mirror the exam’s multiple-choice format and can pinpoint areas where you may need extra review.

These study habits ensure you develop a strong knowledge base, laying the groundwork for passing your exam on the first try.

## Take the licensing exam

Once you’ve finished your pre-licensing hours, you’re ready for the official test. Michigan typically partners with exam administrators like PSI, where you can schedule a convenient time and location. The exam fee usually ranges from about $40 to $60, though costs can vary.

This test is multiple choice, and you need a passing score—often around 70 percent, depending on the line of authority. On exam day:

• Arrive early to park, check in, and get settled without rushing.  
• Bring valid identification. You won’t be allowed to test without it.  
• Stay calm and paced. Skim the entire question, then carefully review each answer option.

If you pass, you’ll receive instructions for applying for your license. There’s typically a small application fee, so plan that into your overall budget. Once approved, you’ll officially hold a Michigan insurance license.

## Pursue continuing education

Obtaining your license marks the beginning of your professional journey. As soon as you’re licensed, you need to keep it in good standing by meeting continuing education (CE) requirements. This “michigan insurance license continuing education” is more than a box to check—it's your chance to stay informed on industry updates and strengthen your expertise.

Most Michigan insurance professionals must complete 24 hours of CE every renewal period, including at least three hours of ethics. Renewal cycles often happen every two years, but it’s always wise to confirm the latest requirements with the state to avoid missing deadlines.

Here’s how to stay on top of your CE:

-   Plan your renewal timeline. Mark important dates, so you won’t scramble at the last minute.
-   Choose courses that interest you. Many CE providers offer topics beyond the basics, letting you refine specialized skills.
-   Keep documentation. Save completion certificates in a secure spot in case you need to verify them later.
-   Spread your coursework throughout the cycle. Finishing early keeps you from panicking close to the deadline.

This approach ensures you remain a legit, dependable choice for clients looking for someone knowledgeable in insurance products and industry changes.
