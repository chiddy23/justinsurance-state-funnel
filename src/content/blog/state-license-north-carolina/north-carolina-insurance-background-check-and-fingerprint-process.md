North Carolina requires a thorough background check for new insurance producer license applicants — and the process is distinctive among states. North Carolina uses local law enforcement agencies for fingerprinting (rather than centralized providers like IdentoGO used in many states), processes prints through the State Bureau of Investigation (SBI) for state and federal criminal history checks, and sequences fingerprinting after the NIPR application rather than before it. It is a one-time requirement tied to initial resident licensure — N.C.G.S. § 58-33-48(c)(1) expressly exempts renewal and continuation applicants. Understanding the process, what NCDOI considers in evaluating background results, and how to handle disclosure issues helps you navigate licensing successfully.

Here's a complete guide to North Carolina insurance background check and fingerprinting requirements.

## Why North Carolina Background Checks Matter

The North Carolina background check serves multiple purposes:

Consumer protection. Identifying applicants who pose risks to insurance consumers based on prior conduct.

Industry integrity. Maintaining standards for licensed professionals.

Regulatory accountability. Following federal and state requirements for criminal history verification.

Risk assessment. Evaluating applicant fitness for licensed insurance practice.

Insurance producers handle sensitive financial and personal information, advise on major life decisions, and process transactions involving substantial money. The background check process ensures producers meet baseline character standards.

## North Carolina's Distinctive Process

North Carolina's background check process is distinctive in several ways:

Local law enforcement fingerprinting. Unlike states using IdentoGO or similar centralized providers, North Carolina uses local law enforcement agencies for Livescan fingerprinting.

Application-first sequence. Fingerprints required AFTER NIPR application submission, not before.

30-day fingerprint window. Must be submitted within 30 days of application.

SBI processing. State Bureau of Investigation processes results for state and federal criminal checks.

One-time requirement. Fingerprints are required with the initial resident license application only. N.C.G.S. § 58-33-48(c)(1) exempts persons applying for renewal or continuation of a home state or nonresident producer license, so there is no periodic re-fingerprinting cycle.

Residents only. Nonresident producer license applicants are not fingerprinted in North Carolina.

These distinctive features require specific attention.

## The Fingerprinting Process

Step 1: Submit NIPR application. Application generates fingerprinting requirement and provides forms link.

Step 2: Print Fingerprint Submission Packet-Releases Forms. Available through email link from NIPR after application submission. Includes:

Cover page

Page 1 (you complete)

Page 2 (law enforcement signs)

Page 3 (you complete)

Step 3: Find Livescan-capable local law enforcement.

Call local police department, sheriff's office, or other law enforcement agency

Confirm they perform Livescan fingerprints for non-criminal reasons

Schedule appointment

Some agencies may charge a fee for the service

Note: Fingerprint cards are NOT accepted — must be Livescan electronic

Step 4: Attend fingerprinting appointment.

Bring valid photo ID

Bring completed packet (with pages 1 and 3 filled out)

Have law enforcement person sign page 2

Pay any local fingerprinting fee

Receive completed packet

Step 5: Upload the completed SBI release forms to the NIPR Attachment Warehouse.

Upload pages 1 through 3 of the completed packet at nipr.com/licensing-center/attachments

The signed SBI Release Form is mandatory — Pearson VUE's NC Licensing Office cannot issue the license without it

The local law enforcement agency transmits the fingerprint impressions electronically to the SBI; you do not mail prints anywhere

Keep copies of all documentation

Step 6: SBI processes background check.

SBI conducts state criminal history check

SBI submits prints to FBI for federal criminal history check

Results sent to NC Licensing Office at Pearson VUE

Step 7: NCDOI reviews results.

Department reviews any concerning findings

May request additional documentation if issues identified

Final licensing decision made based on combined application, exam, and background check

Fingerprinting fee: a $38 criminal record history check fee is paid through NIPR during the initial resident license application. It is separate from the NCDOI license registration fee, the NIPR transaction fee, the Pearson VUE application processing fee, and any fee the local law enforcement agency charges to take the prints. Confirm current amounts on the North Carolina Insurance Resident Licensing Fees chart linked from Pearson VUE's NC insurance page before you apply.

## State Bureau of Investigation (SBI) Role

The North Carolina State Bureau of Investigation plays a central role in the background check:

State criminal history check. Searches North Carolina criminal records.

Federal coordination. Submits prints to FBI for federal criminal history check.

Result transmission. Sends results to NC Licensing Office at Pearson VUE.

Hard copy provision. Sends physical copy to Pearson VUE for review.

The SBI processes are typically efficient, with results returned to Pearson VUE within 1-3 weeks of fingerprint submission.

## Required Application Disclosures

Beyond the fingerprint background check, NCDOI requires honest application disclosures regarding:

Criminal history. All arrests, charges, and convictions (including DUI, juvenile records depending on jurisdiction, and military convictions).

Prior license actions. Denials, suspensions, revocations, fines, or other regulatory actions in any state.

Civil judgments. Significant civil judgments and lawsuits.

Bankruptcy. Personal bankruptcies (general timeframe applies).

Insurance company terminations for cause. Carriers terminating you for cause.

Pending matters. Cases currently active.

Honesty in disclosure is critical. NCDOI cross-references disclosures against background check results. Inconsistencies create serious problems — sometimes more serious than the underlying issue.

## Submitting Disclosure Documentation

For "yes" answers to disclosure questions, submit supporting documentation through NIPR's Attachments Warehouse:

Required documentation:

Written statement explaining circumstances

Certified copy of official document showing resolution (court records, official orders, etc.)

Final judgment or disposition documentation

Quality matters. Clear, comprehensive documentation expedites review and demonstrates good faith.

Submission methods:

Through NIPR Attachments Warehouse (most common)

Direct to NC Licensing Office of Pearson VUE (alternative)

## What NCDOI Considers in Evaluation

NCDOI evaluates background check results and disclosure issues considering:

Nature of the conduct. Was it dishonesty-related (fraud, theft) vs. unrelated (DUI, simple assault)?

Severity. Minor offense vs. serious felony.

Time elapsed. Recent issue vs. distant past.

Pattern of conduct. Single incident vs. pattern of behavior.

Evidence of rehabilitation. Steps taken since incident.

Connection to insurance industry. Direct relevance to fitness for insurance practice.

Other relevant factors. Specific circumstances of each case.

## Issues That Are Particularly Concerning

Some background check findings are particularly concerning for NCDOI:

Felony fraud convictions. Particularly fraud against financial institutions or insurance companies.

Theft and embezzlement. Direct relevance to financial services positions.

Insurance industry violations. Prior license actions in any state.

Recent serious felonies. Time elapsed since conviction matters.

Multiple convictions. Pattern of misconduct.

Pending serious cases. Cases not yet resolved.

These issues don't automatically disqualify but require thorough review.

## Issues That Are Often Resolvable

Other findings can typically be resolved with proper documentation:

Minor offenses. Misdemeanors with substantial time elapsed.

Single DUI long ago. Particularly with rehabilitation evidence.

Civil matters resolved. With documentation showing resolution.

Bankruptcies. Particularly Chapter 7 with substantial time elapsed.

Juvenile records. Often handled with appropriate documentation.

These issues require attention but typically don't prevent licensure.

## If Background Check Reveals Issues

If your background check reveals concerns:

NCDOI requests documentation. You'll receive written notice of concerns and document requests.

You provide explanation and documentation. Submit requested materials including court records, explanation letters, and other relevant documents.

NCDOI evaluates. Each case is evaluated individually based on factors above.

Decision rendered. Approval, conditional approval, or denial.

Appeal rights. If denied, you typically have rights to contest through hearing process.

Timeline impact. Background check issues typically add 4-12 weeks to processing time.

## Fingerprinting Is One-Time, Not Recurring

This is worth stating plainly because it is a common misconception: North Carolina does not re-fingerprint producers on a schedule.

Statutory basis. N.C.G.S. § 58-33-48(c)(1) provides that the criminal history record check section "does not apply to ... a person applying for renewal or continuation of a home state insurance producer license or a nonresident insurance producer license." Renewal applicants are exempt by statute.

What that means in practice. You are fingerprinted once, in connection with your initial resident producer license application. There is no every-other-cycle re-print, no 4-year re-print, and no recurring $38 fingerprint fee. Because resident producer licenses are perpetual, there is no periodic renewal filing at all — ongoing accountability comes through the producer's continuing duty to report criminal and administrative actions to NCDOI, not through a repeat print.

When you may be printed again. A new fingerprint submission can be triggered by a new application event rather than by the calendar — for example, applying for a resident license after a lapse in license authority, or applying for a license type such as resident public adjuster that carries its own fingerprint requirement.

## Fingerprinting Exemption (Legacy)

Producers licensed in North Carolina before October 1, 2010 predate the fingerprint requirement. Per NCDOI, insurance producers licensed prior to 10/1/2010 do not need to submit fingerprints when adding a line of authority or license, unless there have been certain lapses of license authority.

If your license authority has lapsed, confirm your status with NCDOI Agent Services before assuming the legacy exemption still applies to a new application.

## How to Prepare for Background Check

Review your own background. Order your own criminal background check to know what NCDOI will see.

Address open issues. Resolve pending cases before applying if possible.

Gather documentation. Court records, regulatory action records, civil judgment records — gather these in advance.

Be honest in disclosures. Honest disclosure with documentation is far better than concealment that gets discovered.

Get legal advice if needed. For applicants with substantial concerns, consultation with attorney experienced in insurance licensing helps navigate the process.

## What If You Have Disclosure Concerns

For applicants worried about background check or disclosure issues:

Be proactive. Address concerns rather than ignoring them.

Provide context. Explanations matter as much as facts.

Show rehabilitation. Evidence of changed circumstances and good conduct.

Seek professional help. Insurance licensing attorneys can guide complex cases.

Be patient. Background-issue applications take longer.

Don't give up. Many applicants with background concerns successfully license after proper documentation.

## Background Check vs. Other Licensing Steps

Understanding how background check fits into the overall process:

Application: Submitted through NIPR before exam.

Authorization to test: Generated by application.

Exam: Pass each line's exam (scaled score of 70 on Pearson VUE's 0–100 scale, not 70% of questions correct).

Fingerprinting: Within 30 days of application.

Background check: Processes during 1-3 weeks after fingerprinting.

License decision: After all elements complete.

Background check is typically the longest single processing step (other than handling substantive disclosure issues).

## 5 Frequently Asked Questions

- How does North Carolina's fingerprinting process differ from other states? North Carolina uses local law enforcement agencies for Livescan fingerprinting (rather than centralized providers like IdentoGO used in many states). Prints are processed through the NC State Bureau of Investigation for state and federal criminal history checks.
- When do I submit fingerprints in North Carolina? After submitting your NIPR application. You must submit fingerprints within 30 days of application submission. Fingerprints submitted before application aren't accepted.
- Does North Carolina require periodic re-fingerprinting? No. Fingerprints are required only with your initial resident license application. N.C.G.S. § 58-33-48(c)(1) expressly exempts persons applying for renewal or continuation of a home state or nonresident producer license, and resident producer licenses are perpetual anyway, so there is no periodic re-print cycle and no recurring fingerprint fee. A new fingerprint submission is triggered by a new application event — such as applying after a lapse in license authority — not by a fixed schedule.
- What if my background check reveals concerning findings? NCDOI evaluates findings considering nature, severity, time elapsed, rehabilitation, and connection to insurance industry. Many findings are resolvable with proper documentation. Typically adds 4-12 weeks to processing time.
- Can I get fingerprinted before applying? No. Fingerprints submitted before NIPR application aren't accepted. Apply first, then schedule fingerprinting appointment within the 30-day window.
