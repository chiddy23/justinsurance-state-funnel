The Connecticut Insurance Department (CID) handles licensing for all insurance professionals operating in Connecticut — and understanding how the application process works helps you submit a clean application and avoid delays. Connecticut's process is efficient and well-documented, but has specific requirements you need to navigate correctly.

Here's a step-by-step walkthrough of the Connecticut Insurance Department licensing application process.

## Who the Connecticut Insurance Department Is

The Connecticut Insurance Department regulates the insurance industry in Connecticut. The CID:

Licenses insurance producers, adjusters, consultants, and related professionals

Regulates insurance companies operating in Connecticut

Enforces insurance laws and regulations under Connecticut General Statutes Title 38a

Investigates consumer complaints

Handles disciplinary matters

Plays a significant role given Connecticut's status as a major insurance industry center

Connecticut Insurance Department Contact Information:

Address: 153 Market Street, 7th Floor, Hartford, CT 06103

Phone: 860-297-3845

Email: cid.licensing@ct.gov

Website: portal.ct.gov/cid

## Before You Apply

Before submitting your application, you should have:

Completed Connecticut-approved [prelicense](/connecticut/prelicensing) education (or qualified for a designation-based exemption)

Passed the Connecticut state exam through Pearson VUE

Gathered documentation for any disclosure answers

Prepared to pay the application fee ($140 plus any vendor transaction fees)

Your application won't be processed unless these foundational steps are complete.

## Step 1: Access NIPR for Application

Connecticut license applications are submitted through the National Insurance Producer Registry (NIPR) at nipr.com.

NIPR serves as the standard platform for Connecticut insurance licensing applications.

## Step 2: Create or Log Into Your NIPR Account

If you're new to NIPR, create an account with:

Your legal name (matching your government-issued ID exactly)

Date of birth

Social Security number (or FEIN if applicable)

Contact information

## Step 3: Select Connecticut and Your License Type

Choose:

Connecticut as your target state

Resident or non-resident producer (as applicable)

The license type matching your prelicense education and exam

## Step 4: Complete the Uniform Application Form

Connecticut uses the Uniform Application for individual insurance producers. The application walks you through several sections:

Personal Information.

Legal name (must match ID exactly — discrepancies cause delays)

Date of birth

Social Security number or FEIN

Connecticut home address (for resident applicants)

Contact details

Business Information. If affiliated with a specific agency, provide that information.

License Type and Lines of Authority. Specify exactly which license types you're applying for.

Disclosure Questions. Critical section. You'll answer questions about:

Criminal history (arrests, charges, convictions)

Prior license denials, suspensions, or revocations in any state

Regulatory actions against you

Civil judgments and significant lawsuits

Bankruptcy history

Insurance company terminations for cause

Answer honestly. Connecticut cross-references your answers against background check results.

## Step 5: Upload Supporting Documentation

For any "yes" answers to disclosure questions, upload supporting documentation:

Court records for criminal matters

Documentation of prior regulatory actions

Explanation letters providing context

Evidence of rehabilitation

Other relevant documents

Incomplete documentation is a common cause of application delays.

## Step 6: Pay the Application Fee

Connecticut producer license application fees:

State application fee: $140

Vendor transaction fees: Apply through NIPR

Payment methods accepted by NIPR: Visa, MasterCard, Discover, American Express, PayPal, electronic checks. Cash and paper checks are NOT accepted.

## Step 7: Submit and Track Your Application

Review all information carefully before submitting. Once submitted:

Your application is transmitted to the Connecticut Insurance Department

You receive confirmation via email

You can track status through NIPR

## What the CID Reviews

After submission, the CID reviews:

Your exam completion. Pearson VUE reports your exam results electronically.

Your background check. State and federal criminal history check results flow to the CID.

Your disclosure answers. Cross-referenced against available records.

Your application completeness. Missing information triggers requests for more.

Your documentation. For disclosure items, supporting materials are evaluated.

## Processing Times

Clean applications typically process within 7-10 business days.

Applications with disclosures typically take longer depending on complexity.

Complex cases can take significantly longer as the CID evaluates whether to approve, deny, or approve with conditions.

## Common Application Mistakes

Name inconsistencies. Your name on the application must match your ID exactly. Any variation (middle initial vs. middle name, maiden name issues, suffixes, etc.) cause delays.

Incomplete disclosure answers. Answering "no" when "yes" is the honest answer creates serious problems.

Missing documentation. "Yes" disclosure answers typically require supporting documents.

Wrong license type. Applying for licenses you didn't complete prelicense education or exam for means rejection.

Submitting application past prelicense Certificate validity. Your Certificate of Completion is valid for 1 year. After that, you must retake prelicense.

Inadequate proof of residency. Connecticut requires proof of residency in the state for resident applicants.

## If Your Application Is Denied

If the CID denies your application, you receive written notice explaining the reason. You have rights including:

Requesting a hearing to contest the denial

Appealing through Connecticut's administrative process

Consulting with an attorney familiar with Connecticut insurance licensing

Denial isn't always permanent. Some applicants successfully appeal or reapply after addressing concerns.

## If Your Application Is Approved

Approved applications result in an active Connecticut producer license. You'll receive:

Notification through NIPR

Your license number

Your license effective date and renewal date

You can now pursue carrier appointments and begin conducting insurance business in Connecticut.

## Required Notification of Changes

Once you have your Connecticut license, you must notify the CID in writing within 30 days of any change in:

Business address

Residence address

Email address

Employer

Name (e.g., due to marriage, divorce)

Designated responsible licensee for compliance

Contact Change Requests are supported via NIPR Gateway.

## Adding Lines of Authority to an Existing License

Once your Connecticut license is active, you can add additional lines of authority later:

Submit application through NIPR

Pay applicable fees

Complete prelicense education for the new line (if not exempt by designation)

Pass the corresponding state exam

No additional background check is typically required

## Special Cases

Reciprocity from another state. Producers moving to Connecticut from another state can apply within 90 days of relocation without retaking prelicense.

Designation-based exemptions. Specific industry designations exempt holders from prelicense (and sometimes exam) requirements.

Limited lines. Some limited lines (Travel Only, Credit Only) have streamlined requirements and no CE requirements.

## Initial License Cycle Note

For new licensees, the first license cycle may be shorter than two years. Example: License issued May 10, 2024, with last birthday December 4, 2023; the expiration would be December 31, 2025. Subsequent renewals follow the standard two-year cycle.

This means your first renewal date may not be exactly two years from initial licensure.

## 5 Frequently Asked Questions

- How long does Connecticut take to approve a license application? Typically 7-10 business days for clean applications, longer for applications with disclosures.
- How much is the Connecticut license application fee? $140 state application fee plus any vendor transaction fees through NIPR.
- What payment methods does NIPR accept for Connecticut applications? Visa, MasterCard, Discover, American Express, PayPal, and electronic checks. Cash and paper checks are not accepted.
- Do I need to update my address with the Connecticut Insurance Department? Yes, within 30 days of any change. Contact Change Requests are supported via NIPR Gateway.
- How long is my prelicense Certificate of Completion valid? One year from the date of completion. You must complete the state exam and apply for licensure within this window.
