## Understand Texas license basics

Before diving into any coursework, it helps to know exactly what a Texas insurance license entails. The [Texas Department of Insurance](/texas/requirements) (TDI) oversees the licensing process for producers across all insurance lines, including life and health, property and casualty, and more specialized areas.

Each line of insurance is unique, so you will want to confirm which type of coverage you plan to sell. Most new agents start with either life insurance or property and casualty, but it depends on your career goals. You can always add lines later, giving you the flexibility to grow your expertise over time.

Additionally, anyone seeking to become a licensed insurance agent in Texas must be at least 18 years old and a resident of the state (or a non-resident who meets Texas guidelines). You will also need to pass a background check that verifies your trustworthiness with clients’ financial and personal information.

## Meet [pre-licensing requirements](/texas/prelicensing)

Texas does not mandate any coursework before you can sit for the licensing exam — [pre-licensing is optional here](https://www.tdi.texas.gov/tips/be-an-agent.html), and there is no minimum hour count to satisfy. Even so, plenty of life, health, and property and casualty applicants take a structured exam-prep course anyway, simply because the state exam is content-heavy. Worth knowing before you shop: because Texas doesn't require the education, TDI doesn't approve agent pre-licensing courses either, so you're choosing a study program on its merits rather than checking off a state credential. These classes break down insurance terminology, state regulations, policy structures, and the ethical standards you'll be held to.

Schools frequently offer flexible options. You might choose an online program that lets you study at your own pace, or you can attend in-person classes if you enjoy a classroom setting. Either way, your pre-licensing coursework is designed to help you fully understand Texas insurance laws and policy details, so you feel prepared when exam day arrives.

Cost can range from around one hundred to several hundred dollars, depending on the school and program you pick. You might also consider any extra resources, such as textbooks or practice exams, especially if you learn best through a variety of study methods. Ultimately, you should see this investment as foundational for your future as a licensed professional.

## Choose your insurance school

With so many Texas insurance license schools available, it can feel overwhelming to pick the right program. Start by being clear about what you're actually buying. [TDI approves continuing education courses and providers](https://www.tdi.texas.gov/agent/agcehome.html) for agents who are already licensed, and it separately certifies adjuster pre-licensing courses — but it runs no approval program for agent pre-licensing, because Texas doesn't require agent pre-licensing in the first place. A school advertising a "TDI-approved pre-licensing course" for a life, health, or property and casualty agent license is describing a credential the state doesn't issue, and that's a useful early filter. Judge programs on what they teach and how well they support you instead. Some schools excel at engaging in-person lectures where you can ask immediate questions, whereas others shine at delivering interactive digital lessons and practice quizzes.

You may want to compare features such as:

-   Curriculum depth: Ensure the program fully covers Texas regulations and exam-relevant topics.
-   Instructor support: Check if the school offers email, phone, or live-chat assistance for questions.
-   Retake policy: Check whether the provider charges extra for retakes or includes a retake in the course price.
-   Student resources: See if they include flashcards, exam simulators, or additional reading materials.

When making your decision, remember that convenience, price, and teaching style all matter. Choosing a reputable institution can make an enormous difference in your confidence and readiness to succeed. If you are still unsure, you could read online reviews from former students or talk to an insurance professional who has been through the process.

## Ace the licensing exam

Once you feel ready — whether or not you took a pre-licensing course — the next big milestone is passing the official state exam. TDI contracts with [Pearson VUE](https://www.pearsonvue.com/content/dam/VUE/vue/en/documents/publications/124400.pdf) to administer Texas insurance licensing exams, so that's where you schedule and sit. Every question is four-option multiple choice, and the exam evaluates your comprehension of general insurance concepts alongside a substantial block of Texas-specific law, policy provisions, and ethical standards.

Preparation is key at this stage. Paying close attention to the exam outline provided by your school or the testing authority will help you prioritize what to review. Practice quizzes or exam simulation software can be especially helpful since they mirror the question format you will see on test day. Many people also create their own study schedule, dedicating a set amount of time each day to reviewing tricky concepts like policy exclusions or endorsements.

On exam day, you will take a computer-based test at a Pearson VUE test center and leave with an official score report in hand. If you pass, you can proceed with applying for your license through the TDI. If you do not pass on the first attempt, do not worry — Texas imposes no mandatory waiting period. [Pearson VUE's Texas handbook](https://www.pearsonvue.com/content/dam/VUE/vue/en/documents/publications/124400.pdf) states that a candidate who failed can schedule a new exam within one day and that there is no limit to the number of attempts on the same exam, though you pay the exam fee again each time. It is not uncommon for students to benefit from repeat practice and an extra round of focused study.

## Plan your next steps

After passing your exam, you will submit an application and any required documentation to the TDI for final approval. Fingerprinting and a criminal background check are part of that process, handled through [IdentoGO](https://www.tdi.texas.gov/agent/fingerprint-instructions.html) — schedule those early, because appointment availability varies by metro. Once those steps are done, you will have your official license and can begin selling insurance in the state of Texas.

The journey does not stop there. Building your career means finding a supportive agency or broker to partner with, continuing your education as regulations and products evolve, and nurturing relationships with clients. Maintaining your license will involve completing [continuing education](/texas/continuing-education) (CE) courses that keep you up to date on the latest industry trends.
