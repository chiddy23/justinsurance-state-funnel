Pennsylvania still wants you to pass PSI exams before you wander into fingerprinting, yet every month someone pays IdentoGO twice because they printed before passing. The Commonwealth’s [initial producer licensing page](https://www.pa.gov/agencies/insurance/licensing/licensees/initial-insurance-producer-licensing-process.html) spells out the sequence: verify eligibility, schedule PSI, pass, apply immediately on Sircon or NIPR, then fingerprint using service code **1KGBGJ** with **844-321-2101** as the IdentoGO scheduling line. Resident individual producers also owe a **$55** application fee at filing, while nonresidents pay **$110** on the same page. Treat those numbers as budget line items, not footnotes.

## Schedule PSI with the PID-specific phone tree

Pennsylvania’s licensing page routes you to the [PSI Pennsylvania test-taker portal](https://test-takers.psiexams.com/pa/insurance) and lists **888-818-5822** as the scheduling phone. Keep a credit card handy; the page recommends Visa, MasterCard, or American Express because you can apply for the license at the testing center through Sircon or NIPR right after you pass. That is a convenience feature—still read the ID rules so remote proctors do not bounce you for a mismatched middle initial.

## Fingerprint only after you pass and file—otherwise you repay

The PID page is explicit: do not register for IdentoGO until after examinations and license application. If fingerprints arrive without a matching application, the Commonwealth makes you redo prints and repay. Use service code **1KGBGJ**, carry the registration number IdentoGO emails, and remember the center only takes card, certified check, or money order—no cash, no personal checks.

## Know where Pennsylvania codifies training—even if your line is not LTC

Long-term care authority carries hard-coded hour minimums in **31 Pa. Code § 39a.9** on the Pennsylvania Code & Bulletin site. The rule requires **8** hours of initial long-term care training for producers licensed after **April 1, 2011**, plus **4** hours each subsequent **24**-month licensing cycle when you continue to sell or solicit LTC. It also spells partnership-policy Medicaid content for part of that initial **8** hours. Even if you are not selling LTC on day one, read § **39a.9** so you know what “training” means in Pennsylvania statute versus what your CE vendor markets as a generic ethics webinar.

## Exam scores stay valid for 12 months on NIPR’s clock

NIPR’s [Pennsylvania overview](https://nipr.com/licensing-center/state-information/pennsylvania) lists **12** months of exam score validity. That is double Ohio’s **6**-month window on NIPR’s [Ohio page](https://nipr.com/licensing-center/state-information/ohio), so multistate producers cannot assume every neighbor state gives the same cushion. If you pass in March but delay fingerprinting until the next spring, Pennsylvania might still be fine while Ohio would have forced a retest.

## Business entities need Pennsylvania Department of State coordination

The Commonwealth’s initial licensing page reminds resident business producers to obtain PID name approval and register with the Pennsylvania Department of State before assuming the entity license is automatic. Nonresident entities may receive Department of State instructions only after issuance. If you are opening an agency, parallel-track corporate filings while humans finish PSI—corporate delays are not PSI’s problem.

## Producer help desk numbers matter when Sircon glitches

PID lists **717-787-3840** and **RA-InProducerMail@PA.gov** on its licensing FAQ PDF link from NIPR. When Sircon attachments fail or PSI score transmission lags, those contacts beat posting in random forums. Keep a paper trail: dates, ticket numbers, and exam confirmation codes.

## Continuing education is a separate chapter from pre-examination courses

PID maintains a standalone [Maintain Your License](https://www.insurance.pa.gov/Licensees/MaintainYourLicense/Pages/default.aspx) hub for CE. Pre-examination coursework and CE share providers sometimes, but not credit buckets. Point new hires at our [Pennsylvania continuing education](https://justinsuranceco.com/pennsylvania/continuing-education) page once they have a license number, and use the national [CE hub](https://justinsuranceco.com/continuing-education) for multistate stacking rules.

## Federal flood training shows up in the same Chapter 39A article

Section **39a.9(d)** of the Pennsylvania Code ties resident producers who sell federal flood policies to the minimum training requirements of **42 U.S.C.A. § 4011** (the Bunning-Bereuter-Blumenauer Flood Insurance Reform Act) plus FEMA’s published basic flood education outlines. That is another reason to read § **39a.9** in full—it is not only long-term care.

## Bottom line

Pass PSI, pay the **$55** resident application fee through Sircon or NIPR immediately afterward, then fingerprint through IdentoGO with code **1KGBGJ** only when PID says to. Read **31 Pa. Code § 39a.9** before you market LTC, track the **12**-month NIPR score clock, and keep PID’s phone numbers handy when uploads fail. Drill exams with [Pennsylvania practice tests](https://justinsuranceco.com/pennsylvania/practice-exam), pair statutes with the [study guide](https://justinsuranceco.com/study-guide), and start coursework through [Pennsylvania prelicensing](https://justinsuranceco.com/pennsylvania/prelicensing) once you know which lines need separate PSI registrations.
