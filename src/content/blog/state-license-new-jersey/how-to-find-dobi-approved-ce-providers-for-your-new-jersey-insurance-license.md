Every [continuing education](/new-jersey/continuing-education) course a New Jersey insurance producer completes for license renewal credit must be approved by the New Jersey Department of Banking and Insurance. That is not a suggestion or a best practice — it is the law. Hours completed through unapproved providers, unapproved courses, or courses that are no longer active in the DOBI system will not appear on your CE transcript and will not count toward your renewal. Yet a meaningful number of producers each renewal cycle discover this problem at the worst possible moment: when they submit their renewal application and find that hours they believed were valid are not in the system. The solution is knowing how to verify provider and course approval before you register, not after.

## The Official Starting Point: Two Different Lookups

The first thing to know is that New Jersey splits this across two places, and producers who only check one of them are the ones who get surprised at renewal.

DOBI publishes the approved provider list. On the Insurance Licensing and Education page at nj.gov/dobi/inslic.htm, DOBI posts a downloadable spreadsheet, "List of New Jersey Approved Insurance Education Providers." That file tells you whether a company is an approved NJ insurance education provider. It is a provider roster, not a course catalog, and it is not a live search tool.

PSI hosts the course-level lookup and your CE transcript. DOBI contracted PSI Services LLC as its producer testing and continuing education credit banking vendor, and PSI's CE site at ce.psiexams.com is where you look up approved courses and view your own CE transcript. DOBI's Insurance Licensing and Education page points licensees straight there — "Current licensees may access their continuing education transcript at https://ce.psiexams.com" — and PSI's New Jersey CE FAQ describes the mechanics: open the Continuing Education Transcript link and enter your last name and New Jersey license number, dropping any leading zeros. The transcript shows your CE review period begin and end dates and every course credited inside that period. (Non-resident NJ licensees have no CE requirement and cannot pull a transcript.) For courses, the Approved Courses Inquiry link lets you search by provider, course number, or subject.

Between the two, you can confirm:

Provider name and approval status

Approved courses offered by that provider

Course credit hours

Credit type (ethics, general, specialty training)

Course approval status and term

Approval terms matter. DOBI approves courses for a set term, and providers must submit each course for approval at least 30 days before it begins (N.J.A.C. 11:17-3.6(f)). A course that was valid when you looked it up months ago may not be current when you complete it — and the credit may not be accepted. Always verify approval status at the time of registration, not months before.

## What to Verify Before Registering for Any CE Course

Before registering for a CE course through any provider, confirm four things — in DOBI's provider list, on PSI's CE site, or with the provider directly:

1. The provider is currently approved by DOBI. Active approval status is required. A provider whose approval has lapsed — even temporarily — cannot report CE credits that will be accepted by DOBI.

2. The specific course you are taking is currently approved. Provider approval does not automatically extend to every course the provider offers. Each course must be individually approved. Verify the course-level approval, not just the provider's standing.

3. The course carries the correct credit type for your needs. If you need ethics credit, the course must be approved specifically for ethics credit — not just general CE credit from a provider who also happens to offer ethics courses. Same for the specialty training categories below.

4. The credit hours match your expectation. Some courses are listed at 3 hours but may have a different DOBI-approved credit count. Confirm the exact DOBI-approved hours before relying on that course to fill a specific portion of your requirement.

## How DOBI-Approved Providers Deliver CE in New Jersey

First, clear up a myth that circulates widely: New Jersey does not impose a classroom minimum. N.J.A.C. 11:17-3.6(a) states the requirement as 24 credit hours of approved continuing education during the previous licensing term, including three credit hours related to professional ethics — with no minimum number of live, in-person, or "classroom equivalent" hours and no cap on self-study. DOBI's own licensing page states it the same way: "24 continuing education credits with at least three credit hours in an approved ethics course." If you want to complete all 24 hours as approved online self-study, the regulation permits it. (There is also no defined regulatory category called "classroom equivalent" — that is industry shorthand, not a DOBI credit type.)

What the regulation does define, in N.J.A.C. 11:17-1.2, is two course types:

Contact course (or class): "a classroom presentation, seminar, lecture or teleconference with monitored attendance and an instructor present." Attendance-based, with a live instructor — whether that's a physical room or a real-time teleconference.

Self-study or self-directed course: "a continuing education course with study material in text, video, audio, or computerized format including interactive online courses, that is completed by successfully passing a test of the subject matter."

On the self-study exam requirement, the rule has an exception that matters. N.J.A.C. 11:17-3.6(c) provides that self-study courses are considered for approval if the instruction "culminates in a monitored examination of the subject presented" — but it goes on to say that "a monitored examination is not required for an interactive, online program that the Department determines has sufficient internal testing to validate the student's full comprehension." So a proctored final is the default for self-study, not a universal rule; a properly approved interactive online course can satisfy DOBI without one. If a course does require a monitored exam, ask the provider how that step is fulfilled before you register.

## Questions to Ask Any CE Provider Before Registering

Even when using a well-known national CE platform, verify these specifics for New Jersey compliance:

"Is this course currently approved by the New Jersey DOBI, and can you provide the DOBI course ID?"

"What credit type does DOBI recognize for this course — ethics, general, or specialty training?"

"Is this a contact course with a live instructor, or is it classified as self-study?"

"How soon after I complete the course will you report the credit to DOBI's CE vendor?"

"Does this course require a monitored final exam, or is it an approved interactive online program that doesn't? If it does, how is the monitoring fulfilled?"

A reputable DOBI-approved provider will answer these questions directly and specifically. If a provider cannot tell you the DOBI course ID or credit type for a specific course, that is a warning sign.

## Specialty Training: Verify Separately

The specialty training requirements — LTC initial and ongoing training, Annuity Best Interest training, and NFIP flood training — each have their own DOBI approval requirements. A course approved for general CE does not automatically satisfy a specialty training requirement, and a specialty training course must be specifically designated for that purpose.

When searching for LTC training, look for courses explicitly approved as initial 8-hour LTC training or ongoing 4-hour LTC training. When searching for Annuity Best Interest training, look for courses specifically approved for the 4-hour Best Interest certification (or the 1-hour update, though the October 2025 deadline for that option has passed). For flood training, look for courses explicitly approved as NFIP certification training — not merely courses that include flood insurance content.

## Reporting Timelines

Once you complete a DOBI-approved CE course through an approved provider, the provider is responsible for reporting your completion to the state. Most approved NJ providers report completions within one business day. N.J.A.C. 11:17-3.6(g) requires the provider's education director to report completions within 15 days of course completion or certification of credits, whichever is later. This is important to understand because completions do not appear on your transcript until they are reported — and if a provider is slow to report or fails to report, the credit will not appear even though you legitimately completed the course.

Know where the recordkeeping duty actually sits. The five-year retention requirement people cite is a provider obligation, not a producer one: N.J.A.C. 11:17-3.2 requires an approved education provider to "maintain for five years the records of each student including the name(s) of the course or courses taken," along with proof the final exam was passed and copies of the certificates issued. What the regulation puts on you is different — N.J.A.C. 11:17-3.6(i) makes producers "responsible for verifying their compliance with continuing education credits by viewing the online producer transcript," and subsections (j) and (k) require you to ask your provider to file corrected or supplemental reports when credits are wrong or missing.

Keep your certificates anyway. There's no DOBI rule requiring you to hold them for five years, but they are your evidence in a reporting dispute, and the burden of proving a credit is yours to carry.

## Frequently Asked Questions

Where do I look up DOBI-approved CE providers and courses?

Two places, because New Jersey splits it. For providers, go to nj.gov/dobi/inslic.htm and download DOBI's spreadsheet, "List of New Jersey Approved Insurance Education Providers." For courses and for your own CE transcript, use PSI's CE site at ce.psiexams.com — DOBI contracted PSI Services LLC as its continuing education credit banking vendor, and the course-level lookup lives there rather than on dobi.nj.gov. You can search by provider, course number, or subject, and see credit hours, credit type, and approval status. If a provider or course does not appear with active approval status, it is not currently approved for NJ CE credit. Run this check before registering, particularly with a provider you have not used before or are returning to after several years. If a course's approval status is unclear, DOBI's Education unit can answer directly: insed@dobi.nj.gov, 609-292-7272 or 609-940-7480.

Is it safe to rely on a CE provider's website to confirm DOBI approval, or should I check the official sources directly?

Check the official sources directly. CE providers have a financial incentive to market their courses as approved, and their websites may not reflect changes to approval status in real time — particularly if a course's approval has expired or a provider is in the process of renewing. DOBI's approved-provider list and PSI's course lookup are the authoritative sources. Use them to verify the specific course and approval status before registering, even for providers you have used successfully in previous renewal periods. Course catalogs change, approvals expire, and new courses replace old ones — what was valid last renewal cycle may not be this cycle.

What should I do if I complete a CE course and the credit never appears on my CE transcript?

First, check the provider's stated reporting timeline — most NJ providers report within one business day, and the regulation gives them 15 days. If the completion has not appeared on your transcript within five business days, contact the provider directly with your certificate of completion and request confirmation that the completion was submitted. This is also what N.J.A.C. 11:17-3.6(j) and (k) require of you: when credits are inaccurate or missing, you ask the provider to file a corrected or supplemental report. If the provider confirms submission but the credit still does not appear, contact DOBI's Education unit at insed@dobi.nj.gov with your name, NPN, the provider's name, the course ID, your completion date, and a copy of your certificate. Do not wait until your renewal deadline to address missing credits — the earlier you escalate, the more time you have to resolve it.

Can I use any online insurance training platform for NJ CE, or only approved ones?

Only providers with current DOBI approval can provide CE credit for NJ license renewal. The national insurance CE market includes dozens of online platforms, and many are approved in multiple states — but DOBI approval is state-specific. A platform approved for CE in Florida, Texas, or California may or may not be approved in New Jersey, and even a platform approved in NJ may not have approval for every specific course in its catalog. Check DOBI's approved-provider list and the course lookup on PSI's CE site before registering with any platform, regardless of how well-known it is elsewhere.

Do I have to take some of my 24 hours in a live classroom?

No. This is one of the most persistent myths about NJ CE. N.J.A.C. 11:17-3.6(a) requires 24 credit hours of approved CE per licensing term including three ethics credits, and it sets no minimum number of live or in-person hours and no cap on self-study. DOBI's licensing page states the same requirement with no format condition attached. Approved self-study and interactive online courses can satisfy the full 24 hours. What still matters is that each course is individually approved for New Jersey and carries the credit type you need — particularly the three ethics credits and any specialty training.

Finding DOBI-approved CE providers in New Jersey is not complicated — but verifying approval before you register, rather than assuming it, is the difference between hours that count and hours that do not. DOBI's approved-provider list, the course lookup and transcript on PSI's CE site, and your own certificates of completion are the three tools that keep your CE compliance airtight every renewal period.
