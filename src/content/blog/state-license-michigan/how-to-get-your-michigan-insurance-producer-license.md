Getting your Michigan insurance producer license opens doors to one of the most unique insurance markets in the country. Detroit's substantial automotive and manufacturing industries, Grand Rapids' growing professional services, Michigan's distinctive no-fault auto insurance system, and substantial healthcare markets across the state create real opportunity for licensed agents. Michigan's licensing process has several distinctive features compared to other states — including no fingerprinting requirement (unusual nationally), variable passing scores by exam type, and a mandatory [prelicensing](/michigan/prelicensing) education requirement that must be completed before attempting the state exam.

Here's exactly how to get your Michigan insurance producer license.

## Step 1: Meet the Basic Eligibility Requirements

Before starting, confirm you meet Michigan's baseline requirements:

You must be at least 18 years old

You must have a Social Security Number (SSN) or Federal Employer Identification Number (FEIN)

You must be a Michigan resident (for resident license) — non-residents apply through reciprocity

You must be able to pass a background check conducted by DIFS

You must truthfully complete the license application and disclose any relevant history

These requirements are governed under Michigan's Insurance Code (Michigan Compiled Laws Chapter 500, MCL 500) and administered by the Michigan Department of Insurance and Financial Services (DIFS).

## Step 2: Apply for Your License Through NIPR First

Michigan uses an application-first approach for some steps — apply through NIPR before taking your state exam, as you'll need your application in the system. However, completing prelicensing education is your practical first step.

Practical sequence for Michigan:

Complete prelicensing education

Apply through NIPR

Schedule and take state exam

Receive license

Apply through: National Insurance Producer Registry (NIPR) at nipr.com

Application fees:

$10 application fee + $5.60 transaction fee = approximately $15.60 total

Among the lowest application fees in the country

Application validity: 180 days from entry into the DIFS database. You must complete all requirements (including passing the exam) within this window. If you fail an exam within your 180-day window, you must submit a new application and fee before rescheduling the exam.

## Step 3: Complete Prelicensing Education — REQUIRED

Unlike some states that have recently eliminated PLE, Michigan requires prelicensing education for all major lines of authority. This is a mandatory step that must be completed before taking the state exam.

Important: If you pass the state exam before completing your prelicensing education, you will be required to retest with PSI. Complete PLE first.

PLE requirements by line:

PLE structure: Each 20-hour course includes 14 hours of subject-specific content and 6 hours of ethics and Michigan insurance law.

PLE final exam: After completing the course, you must pass a Certification Exam with 70% or greater. Under Mich. Admin. Code R 500.5, self-study and online PLE courses must conclude this exam under a signed proctor affidavit from a disinterested third-party proctor — you arrange the proctor, and your provider supplies the exam and affidavit.

Certificate of Completion: Valid for 12 months from the date of completion. You must take and pass the state exam within this window.

Submission: Your course provider reports completions to DIFS within 2 business days. Keep your Certificate of Completion — you'll submit it to DIFS to verify course completion with your license application.

## PLE Exemptions (Designation Waivers)

Michigan allows PLE waivers for candidates holding specific professional designations or college credits. Qualifying designations include:

CLU, CPCU, RHU, ARM, CFP, AAI, ChFC, CIC, FLMI, REBC, LUTCF, HIA, CEBS, or a particular concentration in college credits in insurance from an accredited college.

Waiver process:

Submit a written waiver request to DIFS

Provide confirmation of your designation or college credits

Written waiver must be received by DIFS before taking the exam

Present the waiver to the exam provider at the exam site

Designation holders who qualify still must take and pass the state exam — the waiver only eliminates the PLE requirement.

## Step 4: Schedule and Take the State Exam Through PSI

After completing PLE, schedule your state exam through PSI Services:

PSI contact:

Website: test-takers.psiexams.com/midifs

Pay the $41 exam fee at registration

Important: In-person only. Effective June 10, 2025, PSI discontinued remote proctoring for Michigan insurance exams. All exams must be taken at a physical PSI testing center. Plan accordingly for travel to the nearest center.

Cancel or change: Contact PSI at least 48 hours in advance or forfeit the $41 exam fee.

Identification: Bring a valid government-issued photo ID (with signature) on exam day.

## Michigan Exam Structure

Michigan's exams are distinctive for their variable passing scores — unlike most states that use a flat 70% passing threshold.

Major exam specifications:

DIFS publishes a cut score for every exam it offers. Among the core producer lines, Accident and Health Producer carries the highest cut score at 76%, followed by Property Producer and Personal Lines Producer at 75%, then Casualty Producer and the combined Property and Casualty Producer exam at 74%. Life Producer sits lowest at 72%, and the combined Life, Accident and Health Producer exam is 75%.

It is worth knowing that 76% is not the highest bar in the state overall — Surplus Lines Producer is 77%, and the counselor exams run higher still (Life Counselor 80%, Property and Casualty Counselor 80%, Accident & Health Counselor 82%). The variable cut scores come from a PSI job analysis and Michigan subject-matter-expert review rather than a single flat standard, so check the current DIFS Insurance Examination Cut Scores sheet for the exam you are actually sitting.

No limit on retakes. Michigan allows unlimited retake attempts, but requires a 24-hour wait between attempts and charges $41 for each.

Results: Provided immediately after exam completion. If you fail, you receive a diagnostic report showing strengths and weaknesses by content area.

## Step 5: DIFS Reviews and Issues Your License

After passing your exam, DIFS reviews your application, exam results, and background information. Processing typically takes 14-21 business days from the date you submit all requirements.

Michigan mails physical licenses to the mailing address you provided on your application — distinctive from some states that only provide electronic licenses.

You can check application status through NIPR or by contacting DIFS directly.

## Step 6: Get Appointed by Insurance Carriers

Michigan requires producers to be appointed by carriers to sell their products. The carrier files the appointment with DIFS after you contract with them.

Michigan bills carrier appointments annually in January, with payment through the DIFS ePay portal — important to understand for agency management purposes.

## Total Timeline

From starting PLE to holding your active Michigan license, most candidates finish in 4-8 weeks:

Prelicensing education: 2-4 weeks (depending on study pace)

NIPR application: Same day

Exam scheduling and completion: 1-2 weeks

DIFS review and license issuance: 14-21 business days after exam completion

## No Fingerprinting Required

One of Michigan's most distinctive features: Michigan does not require fingerprinting for insurance producer license applicants. This is unusual nationally — most states require fingerprinting as part of the background check process.

Michigan's background check is conducted through DIFS's review of your application disclosures and records rather than fingerprint-based federal criminal history checks.

## Michigan License Lines of Authority

Common Michigan insurance producer license types include:

Life — life insurance products

Accident & Health — health, disability, long-term care

Property — property insurance coverage

Casualty — liability and related coverage

Personal Lines — limited P&C for personal (non-commercial) coverage

Variable Life and Variable Annuity — variable products requiring securities credentials

Title Insurance — title insurance

Various Limited Lines — specific limited products (Travel, Credit, etc.)

## Michigan License Renewal

Michigan producer licenses are perpetual — they remain active as long as CE requirements are met, without requiring a separate formal renewal application in the traditional sense.

CE requirements:

24 hours every 2 years

Compliance date: first day of birth month (even/odd year system)

Various exemptions for limited line licenses

CE failure consequences:

License suspended for 90 days (or until CE completed)

After 90 days: license qualifications terminated

Can reinstate up to 1 year after termination

After 1 year: must reapply as new applicant (including new PLE and exam)

## Non-Resident Licensing

If you're already licensed as an insurance producer in another state and want a Michigan non-resident license:

No PLE required

No state exam required (for resident producers in good standing in home state)

Apply through NIPR

DIFS verifies license status through the State Producer Licensing Database

Standard reciprocity provisions apply

Note: Michigan does not allow the term "Brokerage" — this terminology is not used in Michigan insurance licensing.

## 5 Frequently Asked Questions

- Does Michigan require prelicensing education? Yes. Michigan requires 20 hours of prelicensing education per line of authority before taking the state exam. Unlike some states that have recently eliminated PLE requirements, Michigan maintains this requirement. You must complete PLE before taking the state exam — passing the exam before completing PLE invalidates your results.
- Does Michigan require fingerprinting for insurance producers? No. Michigan does not require fingerprinting for insurance producer license applicants — an unusual feature nationally. DIFS conducts its background review through application disclosures and records.
- How much does the Michigan insurance exam cost? $41 per exam, paid to PSI at registration.
- What's the passing score for Michigan insurance exams? Michigan sets a separate cut score for each exam rather than a flat threshold: 72% for Life Producer, 76% for Accident and Health Producer, 75% for Property Producer and for Personal Lines Producer, and 74% for Casualty Producer and for the combined Property and Casualty Producer exam. Most of these sit above the 70% standard used by many states. Accident and Health is the highest of the core producer lines, though not the highest in the state — Surplus Lines Producer is 77% and the counselor exams reach 80-82%. DIFS publishes the full list in its Insurance Examination Cut Scores sheet.
- How long does Michigan license processing take? 14-21 business days from the date you submit all requirements (exam results, completed application). Michigan mails a physical license to your address on file.
