Michigan is one of a number of states that still require [prelicensing](/michigan/prelicensing) education (PLE) for insurance producer licensure — holding firm on this requirement even as neighboring states have moved away from mandatory PLE. Understanding Michigan's PLE framework — what's required, how to approach it strategically, the designation waivers available, and what happens when candidates skip or rush this step — prepares you for efficient, successful licensing.

Here's everything you need to know about Michigan insurance prelicensing education requirements.

## Michigan Requires PLE — No Recent Changes

While states like Pennsylvania (April 2025), North Carolina (October 2025), Massachusetts, South Carolina, Alabama, and Nevada have recently eliminated or previously eliminated PLE requirements, Michigan maintains its PLE requirement for all major lines of authority. There have been no recent legislative changes eliminating Michigan PLE.

Michigan's position reflects a view that:

Structured prelicensing education improves consumer protection by ensuring new producers have adequate product and regulatory knowledge

Michigan's unique insurance regulatory environment (particularly its no-fault auto system — substantially reformed in 2020) warrants thorough preparation

Quality prelicense education produces better-prepared producers

For candidates: Don't assume Michigan follows the national trend toward PLE elimination. Michigan PLE is required, and attempting to take the state exam before completing PLE will result in having to retest.

## PLE Content Structure

Each 20-hour Michigan PLE course includes:

14 hours: Subject-specific content covering the substantive insurance knowledge for the line (life insurance products, health insurance plans, property coverage forms, casualty coverage, etc.)

6 hours: Ethics and Michigan insurance law — required for every line's PLE course, covering:

Professional ethics standards

Michigan Insurance Code (MCL 500) basics

Michigan-specific regulations

DIFS licensing rules

Producer conduct standards

This 6-hour ethics/Michigan law component means every Michigan producer — regardless of line — receives foundational instruction in Michigan-specific insurance regulation before sitting for the state exam.

## The PLE Certification Exam

After completing your PLE course, you must pass a Certification Exam:

Passing score: 70% or greater

Proctor requirement: None — the certification exam is not monitored

Purpose: Certifies completion of the PLE requirement

Certificate validity: 12 months from issuance

Your provider issues a Certificate of Completion after you pass the certification exam. This certificate must be submitted to DIFS to verify PLE completion.

Provider reporting: Your course provider reports completions to DIFS within 2 business days of certificate issuance.

## Certificate Validity: 12-Month Window

Your PLE Certificate of Completion is valid for 12 months. You must take and pass the state exam within this 12-month window.

What this means practically:

Don't complete PLE too far in advance of your intended exam date

If life circumstances delay your exam beyond 12 months, you'll need to retake PLE

Coordinate your PLE completion with your study plan so you can exam within a few months

Recommended approach: Complete PLE, allow 2-4 weeks of additional exam-focused preparation, then take the state exam. This keeps you well within the 12-month window while maximizing preparation.

## The Critical Rule: Complete PLE Before Taking State Exam

Michigan strictly enforces the PLE-before-exam sequence:

If you pass the state exam before completing your PLE course, you will be required to retest with PSI.

Your exam results will be invalidated and you'll pay another $41 to retake. The PLE completion must precede your state exam attempt — not just be in progress when you take it.

Checklist before scheduling your state exam: ✅ PLE course completed (all lessons) ✅ PLE certification exam passed (70%+) ✅ Certificate of Completion received and downloaded ✅ Certificate reported to DIFS by provider (within 2 business days) ✅ NIPR application submitted

Only after all of the above should you schedule your PSI state exam.

## PLE Format Options

Michigan-approved PLE is available in multiple formats:

Online self-paced. Most popular format. Work through material at your own pace. Typically includes video content, readings, chapter quizzes, and a final certification exam.

Live classroom/webinar. Instructor-led instruction. Requires scheduling and attendance. Some students find the structure helpful.

Accelerated courses. Some providers offer accelerated formats requiring significant pre-study before attending a compressed live session. national providers and national providers offer accelerated options.

Self-study. Book-based self-study with online certification exam.

Progressive completion requirement: Michigan requires candidates to complete all lessons in the PLE course before accessing the PLE final exam — you can't skip ahead. This is a DIFS requirement building on the progressive course structure.

## PLE Designation Waivers

Michigan allows PLE waivers for candidates holding specific professional designations or qualifying college credits:

Qualifying designations: CLU, CPCU, RHU, ARM, CFP, AAI, ChFC, CIC, FLMI, REBC, LUTCF, HIA, CEBS

Also qualifying: Specific concentration in college credits in insurance from an accredited college

Waiver process:

Compile confirmation of your designation or college credits

Submit a written waiver request to DIFS with supporting documentation

DIFS must receive the written waiver before you take the exam

Present the waiver at the exam site on exam day

Note: PLE waiver does not waive the state exam requirement. Designation holders still must take and pass the Michigan state exam.

Common mistake: Assuming the designation waiver is automatic. You must proactively request the waiver from DIFS before scheduling your exam.

## Michigan's PLE vs. States Without PLE

Given that many states have eliminated PLE, prospective Michigan candidates sometimes wonder why Michigan still requires it. From a practical standpoint:

Michigan's no-fault auto system. Michigan's distinctive no-fault auto insurance law — substantially reformed in 2020 with ongoing complexity — is uniquely tested on Michigan exams. PLE ensures candidates understand this complex Michigan-specific system before licensing.

Consumer protection priority. Michigan DIFS has consistently maintained that PLE produces better-prepared producers with lower complaint rates and better compliance outcomes.

Moderate time investment. 20 hours per line (or 40 for combined) is a meaningful but manageable investment, particularly when high-quality online self-paced courses are available.

Quality PLE improves exam [pass rates](/pass-rates). Michigan's variable passing scores (72-76%) are higher than most states' standard 70%. Well-structured PLE substantially improves first-attempt pass rates.

Bottom line: Michigan PLE is required and serves a real preparation function. Budget 2-4 weeks of part-time study to complete PLE, then follow up with focused exam preparation before your state exam.

## 5 Frequently Asked Questions

- How many hours of prelicensing education does Michigan require? 20 hours per major line of authority. For both Life and A&H (combined pursuit), that's 40 hours total with separate certificates. Same for combined Property and Casualty.
- What happens if I take the state exam before completing my PLE course? Your exam results will be invalidated and you'll be required to retest with PSI — costing another $41. Complete your PLE course, pass the certification exam (70%+), receive your certificate, then schedule your state exam.
- Can my professional designation waive Michigan's PLE requirement? Yes, if you hold CLU, CPCU, RHU, ARM, CFP, AAI, ChFC, CIC, FLMI, REBC, LUTCF, HIA, CEBS, or qualifying college credits. Submit a written waiver request to DIFS with supporting documentation before taking your exam. The waiver does not eliminate the state exam requirement.
- How long is my Michigan PLE certificate valid? 12 months from issuance. You must take and pass the state exam within this window. If more than 12 months pass before your exam, you'll need to retake PLE.
- Is Michigan likely to eliminate its PLE requirement like some other states? No change is currently announced. Michigan has maintained PLE through recent national trends toward elimination. Michigan's distinctive regulatory environment — particularly the 2020 no-fault auto reform — supports the continued requirement.
