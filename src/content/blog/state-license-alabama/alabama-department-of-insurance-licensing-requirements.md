The Alabama Department of Insurance (ALDOI) regulates every aspect of insurance in the state — and understanding exactly what the ALDOI requires from license applicants helps you submit a clean application the first time. Here's a complete breakdown of Alabama Department of Insurance licensing requirements.

## Who the ALDOI Is

The Alabama Department of Insurance is the state agency that regulates insurance in Alabama. The ALDOI:

Licenses insurance producers, adjusters, and service representatives

Regulates insurance companies operating in Alabama

Enforces insurance laws and regulations under Title 27 of the Alabama Code

Investigates consumer complaints

Handles disciplinary matters

The Commissioner of Insurance leads the ALDOI. Unlike some states with elected commissioners, Alabama's commissioner is appointed.

ALDOI Contact Information:

Address: 201 Monroe Street, Suite 502, Montgomery, AL 36104

Mailing: P.O. Box 303351, Montgomery, AL 36130-3351

Phone: 334-241-4126

Producer Licensing Email: ProducerLicensing@insurance.alabama.gov

Website: aldoi.gov

## Basic Eligibility Requirements

To be licensed as an Alabama insurance producer, you must:

Be at least 18 years old. Alabama doesn't license minors as insurance producers.

Be a U.S. citizen or legal resident. Lawful presence in the U.S. is required.

Establish Alabama residency (for resident license) or hold a valid resident license in another state (for non-resident license).

Pass the appropriate state exam. For each line of authority you want, you must pass the corresponding Alabama exam.

Complete fingerprint-based background check. All resident applicants must be fingerprinted.

Demonstrate character and fitness. Serious criminal history, financial problems, or prior regulatory issues can disqualify applicants.

Pay required fees. Application fees vary by license type.

## What's NOT Required as of 2024

One important change: [Prelicense](/alabama/prelicensing) education is no longer required under Alabama Act No. 2023-104, effective January 1, 2024. The ALDOI amended Insurance Regulation No. 147 to reflect this change.

This makes Alabama one of the states where prelicense education is optional rather than mandatory. However, most candidates still complete prelicense courses to prepare for the state exam effectively.

## Fingerprinting Requirements

All resident applicants for producer, adjuster, or title agent licenses must complete fingerprinting:

Timing: Get fingerprinted AFTER passing the exam and ONE DAY before applying

Validity: Fingerprints remain in the ALDOI system only 30 days after being taken

Approved Vendor: Alabama uses Fieldprint Alabama for fingerprinting

Results: Submitted electronically to the ALDOI for background review

Must be insurance-specific: Fingerprints done for other purposes don't count

Time your fingerprinting carefully — too early, and you'll have to redo it.

## The License Application

Applications are typically submitted through NIPR (nipr.com) or SBS. The application requires:

Personal Information:

Legal name (matching ID exactly)

Date of birth

Social Security number

Home address

Contact information

License Selection:

License type and lines of authority

Resident or non-resident status

Disclosure Questions:

Criminal history (arrests, charges, convictions)

Prior license denials, suspensions, or revocations

Regulatory actions

Civil judgments and lawsuits

Bankruptcy history

Insurance company terminations for cause

Supporting Documentation: For "yes" answers to disclosure questions, upload supporting documents (court records, explanation letters, rehabilitation evidence).

## Fees

Alabama license fees vary by license type. Current fee information is available through NIPR or directly from the ALDOI. Examples:

License renewal fee: $70 (per renewal cycle)

NIPR transaction fee: $5.00 per initial or renewal transaction (charged on top of the required state fee, for all license types)

CE reporting fee: $1.00 per credit hour

Late renewal fee: $50 (within 30-day grace period)

Confirm current fees at the time of your application.

## What the ALDOI Reviews

After submitting your application, the ALDOI reviews:

Exam completion. Reported electronically from the University of Alabama within 3-5 days of passing.

Background check. Fingerprint results submitted through Fieldprint Alabama.

Disclosure answers. Cross-referenced against available records.

Application completeness. Missing information triggers requests for more.

Documentation. For disclosure items, supporting materials are evaluated.

## Processing Times

Clean applications typically process within 5 business days after submission.

Applications with disclosures typically take 2-4 weeks depending on complexity.

Complex cases can take longer as the ALDOI evaluates context.

You can check your license status by visiting aldoi.gov and using License Search under Producers/Agents.

## Grounds for License Denial

The ALDOI can deny applications for:

Fraud or misrepresentation in the application

Criminal convictions (particularly involving dishonesty or fraud)

Prior license revocations in any state

Inadequate financial responsibility

Prior violations of insurance laws

Failure to meet character and fitness standards

Denials aren't always permanent — appeals and future reapplication after addressing concerns are possible.

## Common Application Mistakes

Name inconsistencies. Your name must match your ID exactly. Middle name variations cause delays.

Incomplete disclosure. Answering "no" when "yes" is the honest answer creates serious problems.

Timing fingerprinting wrong. Getting fingerprinted too early (30+ days before applying) means you'll need to redo it.

Missing documentation. "Yes" disclosure answers typically require supporting documents.

Wrong license type. Applying for license types you didn't pass exams for means rejection.

## 5 Frequently Asked Questions

- Is prelicense education required in Alabama? No. As of January 1, 2024, Alabama no longer requires prelicense education under Alabama Act No. 2023-104, though it's still highly recommended for exam preparation.
- Where does Alabama administer insurance exams? Through The University of Alabama. Register at training.ua.edu/insurance-testing or call (205) 348-3000.
- How long do fingerprints stay valid before license application? Only 30 days. Time your fingerprinting carefully — get fingerprinted after passing the exam and one day before applying.
- How long does Alabama take to approve a license application? Typically 5 business days for clean applications, 2-4 weeks for applications with disclosures.
- Who regulates insurance in Alabama? The Alabama Department of Insurance (ALDOI), led by an appointed Commissioner of Insurance. Contact: 334-241-4126, aldoi.gov.
