Arizona law still ties your license to a physical compliance footprint before you worry about carrier appointments. **A.R.S. § 20-290** requires every individual producer to keep “the usual and customary records” for licensed transactions at the producer’s **principal place of business**, retain them for **three** years after each transaction completes, and make them available to the director during business hours on the Arizona Legislature’s [statute page](https://www.azleg.gov/ars/20/00290.htm). The same section requires business entities to keep at least one appropriately licensed individual producer in each Arizona office where the entity transacts insurance. If you cannot describe that office on day one, you are not ready to file.

## Schedule PSI and track the 12-month score validity NIPR publishes

NIPR’s [Arizona licensing overview](https://nipr.com/licensing-center/state-information/arizona) lists PSI as the exam vendor (**800-733-9267**) and states exam scores remain valid for **12** months. That overview also links DIFI’s continuing education hub at [`https://difi.az.gov/insurance-ce`](https://difi.az.gov/insurance-ce) for renewal-hour rules—bookmark it next to PSI because Arizona’s post-license workload is heavier than the exam itself.

## Long-term care sales require statutory training, not a vendor upsell

**A.R.S. § 20-1691.12** (published on the Arizona Legislature site) blocks any producer from selling, soliciting, or negotiating long-term care insurance until the producer holds accident and health authority **and** finishes **8** hours of initial Arizona-approved long-term care training, followed by **4** hours in each subsequent **two**-year period after the period that contained the initial **8** hours. Nonresidents can rely on substantially similar training from another state, but you still need certificates DIFI staff can audit.

## Exceptions are narrow—read **A.R.S. § 20-283** before you skip PSI

Title **20** also lists who may operate without a producer license. The legislature’s [exceptions statute](https://www.azleg.gov/ars/20/00283.htm) covers insurer employees doing clerical work, certain group-enrollment clerks, and other tightly defined roles. If you are outside those carveouts, plan on PSI regardless of what a recruiter told you about “captive desk licenses.”

## Continuing education: DIFI’s program (linked on NIPR) sets the 48-hour rhythm

NIPR’s Arizona page instructs resident producers to read DIFI’s continuing education materials for renewal compliance. DIFI’s CE site (linked above) documents the **48**-hour requirement for most major-line resident producers and the **6**-hour ethics subset that must appear each license term unless you qualify for the grandfathering test spelled out there. Nonresidents generally rely on home-state CE reciprocity when their resident state recognizes Arizona credits—verify both states before you assume reciprocity clears Arizona audits.

## Federal flood training stacks on CE if you touch NFIP policies

DIFI’s CE materials (same `insurance-ce` hub linked from NIPR) incorporate federal flood training expectations for producers who sell National Flood Insurance Program policies. Treat that as post-license homework separate from PSI item banks.

## Compare Arizona’s post-license load with Georgia and Pennsylvania

Georgia’s OCI education page documents **8.0**-hour prelicensing per major line under Rule **120-2-3-.08** ([OCI education providers](https://oci.georgia.gov/agents-agency-licensing/education-providers-courses-and-instructors)). Pennsylvania’s [initial licensing page](https://www.pa.gov/agencies/insurance/licensing/licensees/initial-insurance-producer-licensing-process.html) lists **$55** resident application fees and PSI scheduling with IdentoGO code **1KGBGJ**. Arizona’s pattern differs: statutes emphasize records, principal place, PSI testing, and **48**/**6** CE rather than a single tall prelicensing hour stack. Multistate producers should not assume Georgia’s new **8.0**-hour course satisfies Arizona CE or LTC statutes.

## Application mechanics still flow through DIFI and NIPR portals

NIPR’s overview tells resident and nonresident producers to use [LicenseHub](https://pdb.nipr.com/my-nipr/frontend/identify-licensee) when filing, but Arizona-specific PDFs and background questions update on DIFI’s site first. Read DIFI’s producer compliance pages anytime you add lines midyear—Arizona publishes bulletins faster than aggregators summarize them.

## Fingerprinting and background questions still gate slow approvals

NIPR’s Arizona page links DIFI’s licensing sections for residents who must submit electronic fingerprints. Even when PSI results are clean, background hits can delay issuance if you omit aliases or misdemeanor dispositions on the DIFI questionnaire. Answer those forms slowly; Arizona’s enforcement orders cited in CE grandfathering clauses are not theoretical.

## Why Arizona emphasizes CE in the same breath as initial testing

Because Arizona pairs **48**/**6** CE with strict **§ 20-290** recordkeeping, audits hit both the classes you finished last month and the files you kept three years ago. Build your CRM and document storage plan while you study for PSI—not after your first E&O scare.

## Bottom line

Secure a real principal office that satisfies **§ 20-290**, pass PSI within the **12**-month NIPR score window, file through NIPR/DIFI as instructed, then line up **48**/**6** CE plus **8**/**4** LTC training before you market regulated products. Drill item-level work on [Arizona practice exams](https://justinsuranceco.com/arizona/practice-exam), read the [insurance exam guide](https://justinsuranceco.com/insurance-exam-guide) for vendor-specific traps, and book [Arizona prelicensing](https://justinsuranceco.com/arizona/prelicensing) if you still want structured reading even when statutes emphasize records and CE over clock-hour theatrics.
