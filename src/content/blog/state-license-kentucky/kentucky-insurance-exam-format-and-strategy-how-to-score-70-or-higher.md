Knowing what to expect from the Kentucky insurance exam format is half the battle. The other half is knowing how to approach it on test day. This guide dives deep into exam structure, the types of questions you will encounter, and the test-taking strategies that separate candidates who pass on the first attempt from those who need a second or third try.

## Understanding the Exam Structure

Kentucky's insurance licensing exam for each major line covers two broad knowledge areas: general insurance concepts (the national content common to insurance exams across the country) and Kentucky-specific insurance law, regulations, and ethics. These two areas are integrated into a single scored exam rather than being administered as separate sections with separate passing thresholds. This is an important structural distinction compared to Missouri, which requires 70% on both the national and Missouri state sections independently and only makes you retake the failed section. In Kentucky, your single integrated score must meet 70% overall.

The exam is computerized, administered at KDOI testing facilities across the state, and graded immediately upon submission. You will know your result before leaving the testing room.

## National Content: What It Covers

The general insurance content covers the foundational principles that apply regardless of which state you are in. For Life exams, this includes types of life insurance (term, whole life, universal, variable), policy provisions, beneficiary designations, the basics of annuities, taxation of life insurance proceeds, and group versus individual coverage. For Property & Casualty, it includes homeowners policies, commercial property basics, liability concepts, auto insurance coverages, and policy analysis. For Accident & Health, expect coverage of group health plans, individual health insurance structures, disability income concepts, Medicare supplement policies, and long-term care insurance basics.

## Kentucky-Specific Content: What It Covers

State-specific questions are where unprepared candidates lose points. The Kentucky content covers the producer licensing framework under KRS Chapter 304, the KDOI's authority and regulatory structure, unfair trade practices, claims handling requirements, the Motor Vehicle Reparations Act (KRS 304.39) including the choice no-fault system and 25/50/25 minimums, Kentucky workers' compensation basics under KRS Chapter 342, the Annuity Best Interest standard (806 KAR 12:120, effective January 1, 2022), and the structure of Kentucky's health insurance marketplace through kynect.

State law content also tests your knowledge of the producer licensing requirements themselves — PLE hours, exam rules, renewal periods, CE obligations, and grounds for license denial, suspension, or revocation. Many candidates who fail do so primarily because they underestimated how thoroughly the state law content is tested.

## High-Frequency Topic Areas by Line

For the Life exam, prioritize: whole life versus term versus universal life policy structures, beneficiary rules, policy loan provisions, annuity types and their Kentucky Best Interest requirements, and KRS Chapter 304 producer obligations.

For the Accident & Health exam, prioritize: coordination of benefits, COBRA continuation, Kentucky's kynect marketplace and Medicaid expansion to 138% FPL, disability income definitions, and LTC training requirements.

For the Property & Casualty exam, prioritize: Kentucky's choice no-fault auto insurance system (KRS 304.39), the 25/50/25 + $10,000 PIP minimum, pure comparative negligence, UM/UIM offered and can be rejected in writing, Kentucky workers' comp (KRS Chapter 342, 1+ employee threshold, NCCI, KEMI), and homeowners coverage basics.

## Test-Taking Strategy

Read every question completely before selecting an answer. Kentucky exam questions often include distractors that contain partially correct information. The correct answer is the most complete and accurate response to the question as written. When two answers seem similar, identify which one is technically more precise under Kentucky law.

For calculation questions, write out the numbers before attempting to solve. Attempting arithmetic in your head under exam pressure leads to errors. When you genuinely do not know the answer, eliminate the clearly wrong choices first, then apply logic to the remaining options. Guess rather than leave a blank — there is no penalty for incorrect answers on the Kentucky exam. Pace yourself and flag uncertain questions to return to after completing the rest of the exam.

## The Day Before and Day-Of

The night before your exam, review your weaker topic areas rather than trying to re-read everything. Focus especially on Kentucky-specific statutory content — that is where last-minute review provides the most marginal benefit. Get adequate sleep. On exam day, arrive at least 10 minutes early and bring a government-issued photo ID. Trust your preparation.

## Frequently Asked Questions

- Are the national and Kentucky state content sections scored separately? No. Unlike Missouri, Kentucky administers an integrated exam for each line of authority with a single overall score. You need a 70% on the combined exam to pass.
- How many questions are on the Kentucky insurance exam? The exact number of questions varies by line of authority. Review the Candidate Handbook available through your eServices account for the specific question count and time allocation for your line. Consultant exams are 150 questions with 190 minutes.
- Is there a penalty for guessing on the Kentucky exam? No. There is no penalty for incorrect answers. You should answer every question, using the process of elimination to make educated guesses when you are uncertain.
- Can I use a calculator or notes during the exam? No. The Kentucky insurance licensing exam does not permit notes, study materials, or outside calculators.
- What is the most common reason candidates fail the Kentucky exam?
