On July 1, 2023, Virginia changed how underinsured motorist (UIM) coverage works — a change so significant that it altered the calculus for every auto insurance policy in the Commonwealth. The new rule, codified in Va. Code § 38.2-2206, allows UIM coverage to "stack" on top of the at-fault driver's liability payout rather than being reduced by it. The practical effect is substantial: in many accident scenarios, the total compensation available to an injured Virginia driver doubled or more compared to what was available under the prior rule. This post explains exactly how the rule works, what it means for producers advising clients on UM/UIM coverage limits, and the one important nuance around written rejection.

## How UIM Coverage Worked Before July 1, 2023

Under the prior rule, Virginia UIM coverage operated on an offset (subtraction) basis. Your UIM benefit was reduced by — offset against — the at-fault driver's liability payout.

Example under the old rule:

At-fault driver: $50,000 liability coverage

Injured victim: $100,000 UIM coverage

At-fault driver pays $50,000 (their policy limit)

Victim's UIM pays: $100,000 minus $50,000 = $50,000

Total available to victim: $100,000

In effect, the victim's UIM coverage limit was the ceiling — the at-fault driver's liability payment was subtracted from it, and the UIM only topped up to the UIM limit.

## How UIM Coverage Works After July 1, 2023

Under the new stacking rule, UIM coverage adds on top of the at-fault driver's liability payment. There is no offset.

Example under the new rule:

At-fault driver: $50,000 liability coverage

Injured victim: $100,000 UIM coverage

At-fault driver pays $50,000 (their policy limit)

Victim's UIM pays: $100,000 (full UIM limit, no reduction)

Total available to victim: $150,000

This change effectively increases the total compensation pool available to seriously injured Virginia drivers by the full amount of the at-fault driver's liability coverage. In the example above, the victim went from $100,000 available to $150,000 — a 50% increase in accessible compensation — with no change to their own policy.

## The Default and the Written Rejection

The stacking rule applies automatically to all policies issued or renewed after July 1, 2023. Policyholders do not need to take any action to receive the benefit — stacking is the default.

However, Virginia law allows policyholders to reject UIM stacking in writing. When the law changed, many insurers contacted policyholders offering premium savings in exchange for signing a written rejection of stacking. The Bureau of Insurance warned producers and consumers that this waiver reduces coverage.

The producer's obligation: A producer whose client is presented with a UIM stacking rejection form must ensure the client understands exactly what they are giving up — not just that their premium will be slightly lower. A client who signs away stacking without understanding that they are reverting to the old offset rule is not being served properly.

## Practical Advisory Implications

The UIM stacking change reshapes the value proposition of higher UIM limits in Virginia:

UIM limits now compound with liability coverage. Under the old rule, a client carrying $250,000 in UIM coverage effectively had a ceiling of $250,000 in total compensation (since the at-fault driver's liability payout was subtracted). Under the new rule, $250,000 in UIM coverage means $250,000 on top of whatever the at-fault driver carries — potentially $300,000, $350,000, or more total.

Higher UIM limits are more valuable than they were before. If your UIM coverage now adds on top of liability rather than being offset, the full dollar amount of your UIM limit is actually available. This makes the cost-benefit analysis of higher UIM limits significantly more favorable.

The combination with new minimums. Since January 1, 2025, Virginia's minimum UM/UIM coverage is $50,000 per person / $100,000 per accident — up from $30,000/$60,000. At minimum limits with stacking, a victim can access $50,000 (at-fault driver's minimum liability) plus $50,000 (victim's minimum UIM) = $100,000 in a worst-case scenario. Previously, the minimum scenario yielded $30,000 in liability plus $0 additional from UIM (since the old minimums matched). The combination of higher minimums and stacking represents a fundamentally stronger baseline for Virginia's most underinsured accident scenarios.

## Interaction with Contributory Negligence

One critical nuance: the UIM stacking benefit applies to plaintiffs who are not contributorily negligent. Virginia's pure contributory negligence doctrine bars any recovery — including UIM claims — if the injured party shares any fault for the accident. A victim who is 1% at fault cannot collect UIM benefits from the at-fault driver's insurer (that claim is barred), and their own UIM claim against their own insurer may also be contested depending on the specific facts.

The stacking rule is most valuable to Virginia drivers who are clearly not at fault and whose damages exceed the at-fault driver's policy limits. For this population — which represents many serious accident victims — the change significantly expanded available compensation.

## Frequently Asked Questions

Did insurance premiums increase for Virginia policyholders after UIM stacking became the default?

Premiums did increase for many Virginia policyholders following the stacking change, because the risk of UIM claims increased substantially. When UIM coverage adds on top of liability rather than being offset, the insurer's potential UIM payment is higher in scenarios where the at-fault driver's coverage is inadequate. This increased exposure is reflected in premiums. The premium impact varies by insurer and coverage level — clients carrying higher UIM limits saw larger premium increases. Producers should explain this context to clients who ask why their auto insurance premium increased: the increase reflects genuinely expanded coverage protection, not arbitrary pricing.

If my client signed a written rejection of UIM stacking, can they change their mind?

Yes. A policyholder who previously rejected UIM stacking in writing can request reinstatement of stacking at renewal. The rejection is not permanent — it is a policy election that can be changed. Producers who have clients who signed stacking rejection waivers (often encouraged by insurers immediately after the 2023 law change) should discuss the value of reinstating stacking, particularly for clients with significant assets or high potential income loss from an accident injury. Reinstating stacking will likely increase the premium, but the coverage benefit — access to the full UIM limit on top of the at-fault driver's coverage — may well justify the cost.

Does UIM stacking in Virginia apply to multi-vehicle households with separate policies?

The stacking rule under Va. Code § 38.2-2206 applies to the relationship between the at-fault driver's liability coverage and the injured party's UIM coverage on the same policy. It does not automatically create "inter-policy stacking" — the ability to stack UIM coverage across multiple policies held by the same household. Virginia's anti-stacking provisions for multiple vehicles or policies in the same household are separate from the liability-UIM stacking change. For households with multiple vehicles or policies, the specific policy language and Virginia's stacking limitations on multiple coverages should be reviewed carefully. Contact the Bureau of Insurance or consult with a licensed Virginia attorney for guidance on multi-policy stacking scenarios.

How does the UIM stacking change affect commercial auto policies in Virginia?

Va. Code § 38.2-2206 applies to Virginia motor vehicle liability insurance policies broadly — including commercial auto policies. Commercial vehicles insured under Virginia policies with UIM coverage are subject to the same stacking rule. The practical implication for commercial fleet operators is that their UM/UIM coverage now adds on top of at-fault drivers' liability rather than being offset — the same benefit that applies to personal auto policies. Producers advising commercial accounts on fleet coverage should review UM/UIM limits in light of the stacking change and ensure clients understand both the expanded protection and the potential premium impact.

Was there any pushback from insurers against the UIM stacking change?

Yes. The insurance industry opposed the UIM stacking legislation during the General Assembly deliberations, citing concerns about premium increases and the expanded claims exposure. After the law passed, some insurers' claims practices — including actively soliciting policyholders to sign written rejections of stacking — drew attention from consumer advocates who argued that carriers were trying to claw back the consumer benefit the legislature had just enacted. The Bureau of Insurance did not prohibit the rejection practice (which is authorized by the statute), but the public scrutiny it generated is why the rejection issue is now a well-known advisory point for Virginia producers.

Virginia's UIM stacking change is one of the most pro-consumer auto insurance reforms in the Commonwealth's recent history. Producers who understand it, explain it accurately, and advise clients against uninformed waivers of the benefit are providing genuine value that generic policy placement does not.
