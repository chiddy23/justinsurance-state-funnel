If you hold both an agent license and an adjuster license in Texas — or you're thinking about adding one to the other — you have probably been warned that the two carry different CE rules. In Texas, they don't. TDI runs one continuing education system for agents and adjusters alike, and knowing that saves dual-licensed producers from buying hours they never owed.

Here's a clear breakdown of what's actually different, what isn't, and what counts.

## Texas Agent CE Requirements

Licensed agents in Texas must complete 24 hours of [continuing education](/texas/continuing-education) every 2 years, with:

3 hours in certified ethics or consumer protection courses

At least 12 of the 24 hours in certified classroom or classroom-equivalent courses

This applies to Life, Health, Property, Casualty, and any other line of authority an agent might hold. The hours are combined across all lines — you don't multiply by the number of licenses.

## Texas Adjuster CE Requirements

Licensed adjusters in Texas [face the identical requirement](https://www.tdi.texas.gov/agent/agcehome.html). TDI's own CE table lists "Adjusters and Public Adjusters: 24 hours, including 3 hours of ethics" — the same line it prints for General Lines agents, Life agents, Personal Lines P&C agents, and managing general agents. The 50% classroom or classroom-equivalent minimum applies to adjusters too, because 28 TAC §19.1003 writes the rule once, for "each licensee," rather than license type by license type.

There is no separate pool of adjuster-only subject matter to satisfy. Claims handling, investigation, and damage assessment courses are excellent professional development for an adjuster — but they carry credit because TDI certified them as General courses, not because they are labeled for adjusters.

## The Difference Most People Expect — And What Texas Actually Does

This is the part that trips people up. Texas does not divide CE courses by license type at all. [TDI states it directly on its CE page for agents and adjusters](https://www.tdi.texas.gov/agent/agcehome.html): "There are only two topic groups, 'General' and 'Ethics.' Any licensee may take courses from these two groups. Courses are not divided by license type."

So a TDI-certified General course credits toward an agent license and an adjuster license alike. There is no "agent-only" or "adjuster-only" certification to check for, and a product- or sales-focused certified course is not disqualified for an adjuster. What matters is that the course is TDI-certified and in the right topic group — General or Ethics — not who it was written for.

## What If You Hold Both Licenses?

This is where the dual-licensed producer comes out ahead. 28 TAC §19.1003 provides that licensees "holding more than one license issued under the Insurance Code are not required to complete more than the number of continuing education hours required under their greatest single license requirement."

In plain terms: 24 hours, including 3 ethics and at least 12 classroom or classroom-equivalent, satisfies every license you hold. Not 24 per credential. Not 48. There is no condition attached to that — you do not have to hunt for "dual-approved" courses, because no such category exists in Texas. Any certified course counts for everything you hold.

## What Counts Across Both Credentials

Regardless of whether you're an agent or adjuster, these are the general rules for what counts:

The course must be certified by TDI and taken through a TDI-approved provider

At least half your hours — 12 of the 24 — [must be certified classroom or classroom-equivalent courses](https://www.tdi.texas.gov/tips/agents-ce.html). A classroom-equivalent course can be delivered online, but under 28 TAC §19.1011 it is not purely self-paced: the provider must build in periodic interactive inquiries of at least five questions each, and you need at least 70% on every inquiry section. Only up to half your requirement can be ordinary self-study.

You must complete and pass any required course assessment

The provider reports your completion directly to TDI

Hours must be completed within your 2-year renewal cycle, which ends on the last day of your birth month

## 5 Frequently Asked Questions

- Can agent CE courses count toward my adjuster CE? Yes. TDI certifies courses in only two topic groups, General and Ethics, and states that "courses are not divided by license type." Any licensee may take courses from either group, so a certified course credits toward both credentials.
- Do I need separate ethics hours for my agent and adjuster licenses? No. One certified 3-hour ethics course satisfies the ethics requirement for every license you hold.
- Are non-resident adjusters in Texas subject to the same CE rules? No. Under 28 TAC §19.1004(d), a nonresident licensee — including an adjuster whose designated home state license was issued by another state — who is in compliance with their resident or designated home state's CE requirements is not required to complete Texas CE. You will need to provide proof of licensure in good standing in that state.
- Does claims handling experience substitute for CE hours? No. Real-world experience doesn't reduce your CE requirement. You must complete the hours through approved courses regardless of how active you are in the field.
- What happens if I don't finish my hours by my expiration date? TDI gives you [90 days to complete the deficient hours](https://www.tdi.texas.gov/agent/agcehome.html) and pay a fine of [$50 per deficient hour](https://www.tdi.texas.gov/tips/agents-ce.html). Miss that window and the license is inactivated — you have to stop transacting and apply for a new license. Because 24 hours covers every credential you hold, a shortfall puts all of them at risk at once, not just one.
