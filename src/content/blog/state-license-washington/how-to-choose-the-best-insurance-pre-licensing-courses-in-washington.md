## Understand Washington’s [pre-licensing requirements](/washington/prelicensing)

Before you invest time and money in a pre-licensing program, it pays to get familiar with statewide standards. Washington does not require a set number of study hours for any line of authority — Life, Health, Property, or Casualty — since the pre-licensing education requirement was eliminated in 2023. Even so, optional coursework can help you understand important insurance concepts and state regulations that the official exam will test you on.

Since course offerings vary by line, confirm what topics are covered for the line(s) you plan to pursue. If you intend to earn multiple licenses, you may want additional coursework for each. When you select a provider, look for programs that cover the needed topics in a way that aligns with the [Washington Office of the Insurance Commissioner](/washington/requirements)’s guidelines.

Most pre-licensing courses include lessons on insurance ethics, policy types, and relevant state laws. However, some find it helpful when a provider goes further by offering practice exams or interactive quizzes. Getting comfortable with multiple-choice formats can sharpen your test-taking skills and help reinforce key concepts.

## Identify your learning preferences

Pre-licensing courses come in various formats, from online self-study to live webinars and in-person classes. Think about the style that best fits your schedule and how you learn:

• If you thrive with structure and real-time feedback, a classroom or live webinar may be ideal. You can ask questions on the spot and interact with classmates.  
• If you need the freedom to study whenever you have time, choose an on-demand or self-paced online course. This route helps you balance work and family commitments without missing essential lessons.

Before you commit, preview a course demo or request sample materials. That way, you can be sure the tone, complexity, and teaching methods match your needs. Some providers feature video modules for visual learners, while others offer more reading-based lessons, so it is smart to see a snippet of the content in advance.

## Use these study strategies

Simply enrolling in a pre-licensing course is not enough to guarantee success. You will need an organized study plan and consistent follow-through. To maximize your results, consider:

1.  Scheduling regular study blocks. Pick days and times each week dedicated to coursework or practice exams. Keeping a set routine builds momentum and reduces the chance of falling behind.
2.  Mixing up learning methods. Combine reading, watching lectures, doing quizzes, and discussing topics with peers. Variety deepens your understanding and helps boost memory retention.
3.  Setting small goals per session. Rather than cramming all week, aim to learn or review a specific topic each time you study. Achieving little milestones creates a sense of progress.
4.  Reviewing your knowledge daily. Even five minutes spent reciting definitions or insurance rules can help lock in details, particularly if you struggle with tricky terminology.

If you find a concept confusing, remember most course providers, including JustInsurance, have support resources. Taking a few minutes to chat with a mentor or instructor can clear up confusing points before they snowball into bigger issues.

## Frequently asked questions

1\. Do I need to take separate courses for different types of insurance?  
No course is required at all — Washington does not mandate pre-licensing hours for any line of authority. If you choose optional coursework and plan on licensing in multiple areas, expect some overlap between tracks, but also unique content for each specialty.

2\. How long do I have to complete my pre-licensing course?  
Your course provider will outline how much time you have once you enroll. Many programs give you several months of access to materials, although exact durations vary. When choosing a course, check that the access window aligns with your schedule.

3\. Will I need additional resources beyond the primary course?  
Most individuals do well with the standard materials, but you may benefit from optional study guides, flashcards, or supplemental quizzes. If your provider includes memory aids or test simulations, take advantage of them for the best results.

4\. Is there a limit to how many times I can take the exam?  
Washington officials generally allow you to retake the exam if you do not pass on your first try. However, you will have to pay the exam fee each time. A high-quality pre-licensing course helps reduce the likelihood of failing and having to schedule multiple attempts.

5\. Why choose a state-specific program?  
State regulations can differ significantly, and Washington has its own set of rules and statutes. A Washington-focused course ensures that you learn not just general insurance concepts, but also local laws that may appear on your exam.
