Minnesota, Wisconsin, and Iowa share borders and producers — agents who live near the state line routinely serve clients in two or three states simultaneously, and producers who relocate between these states face licensing transitions that are easier to navigate when they understand exactly how each state's requirements differ from the others. This post compares the licensing process across all three states across every dimension that matters to a working producer: [prelicensing](/minnesota/prelicensing) education, exam administration, application and fingerprinting, CE requirements, renewal rules, reinstatement, and what reciprocity looks like when crossing between these three markets.

## The Fundamental Difference: Prelicensing Education

The single largest structural difference between these three states is their approach to prelicensing education — and the contrast is dramatic.

Minnesota: 20 hours of Department-approved prelicensing education is required per line of authority, under Minn. Stat. §60K.36 Subd. 4. A combined P&C license requires 40 hours; combined Life and A&H requires 40 hours. The internal certification exam must be passed at 70% or higher with a proctor present. The certificate of completion never expires.

Wisconsin: 20 hours of Office of the Commissioner of Insurance-approved prelicensing education is required before sitting for the exam. Of those 20 hours, 8 must cover Principles of Insurance, General Wisconsin Insurance Laws, and Ethics — a foundational module shared across lines. Once the 8-hour module is completed, it does not need to be repeated for additional lines within a 12-month period. The remaining 12 hours are line-specific. The certificate of completion is valid for one year from completion — unlike Minnesota's never-expiring certificate, Wisconsin's certificate has an expiration date that can catch unprepared candidates.

Iowa: Iowa requires no prelicensing education whatsoever. It is one of a small number of states that allows candidates to sit for the state licensing exam without completing any mandatory prelicensing course. Optional exam prep courses are available and strongly recommended — the exam covers the same material that Minnesota and Wisconsin candidates study in their 20-hour courses — but Iowa imposes no statutory education requirement before exam registration.

This difference has significant practical implications. A candidate who wants to enter insurance in Iowa can schedule their Pearson VUE exam without any preliminary coursework — they may walk in unprepared, which contributes to lower first-attempt [pass rates](/pass-rates) in states without prelicensing requirements. A Minnesota or Wisconsin candidate must invest 20 hours in coursework before the exam clock even starts. For a producer relocating between these states, the prelicensing requirement (or absence of one) is one of the first factors to assess.

## Exam Administration: Different Vendors, Different Logistics

All three states use recognized national testing vendors — but not the same one.

Minnesota: PSI Services LLC administers all Minnesota insurance licensing exams. In-person test centers are located in Rochester, St. Paul, Willmar, Woodbury, and Brainerd among others. Remote testing is available via PSI Bridge using Google Chrome. Exam fee: $45 for major lines. Retakes require a 24-hour wait; no limit on attempts. Exam results are valid for three years — the longest validity window of the three states.

Wisconsin: PSI also administers Wisconsin's insurance licensing exams. PSI call center: (888) 818-5805. Remote via PSI Bridge. Exam fee varies by line — confirm with PSI at scheduling. A critical Wisconsin-specific detail: first-time applicants must select both the general and state-specific series labeled exams. Exams labeled as "state specific series" only are for previously licensed applicants relicensing in the same line. Selecting the wrong exam is a common Wisconsin mistake. Exam results are valid for only 180 days — significantly shorter than Minnesota's three-year window, creating time pressure that Minnesota candidates do not face.

Iowa: Pearson VUE (now Pearson Professional Assessments) administers Iowa's insurance licensing exams — a different vendor from both Minnesota and Wisconsin. Schedule at pearsonvue.com/ia/insurance or call (877) 540-5825. Exam fee: $44 per exam. Remote testing via OnVUE — candidates get only one remote attempt; all subsequent attempts must be in person at a Pearson VUE test center, which is a notable restriction not found in Minnesota or Wisconsin. Exam results are valid for only 90 days — the shortest window of the three states. A candidate who passes the Iowa exam must submit their license application within 90 days or the results expire and they must retake the exam.

## Fingerprinting and Background Checks

All three states now require fingerprinting — but the timeline and vendor differ.

Minnesota: Electronic fingerprinting at a PSI test center ($65) on the day of the exam is the recommended approach. Alternatively, fingerprints can be taken at a local police station and mailed with a $33.25 payment to the Department of Commerce. Prints go to the Minnesota Bureau of Criminal Apprehension and the FBI. Required for all resident applicants; not required for non-resident applicants.

Wisconsin: Fingerprinting for unlicensed applicants through Fieldprint Wisconsin. The Wisconsin Office of the Commissioner of Insurance (OCI) directs applicants to Fieldprint locations. Wisconsin does not use PSI for fingerprinting — a key difference from Minnesota.

Iowa: Effective June 2, 2025, Iowa began requiring fingerprinting and criminal history background checks for resident producer applicants. Iowa uses Fieldprint with Iowa Fieldprint code FPIADOI. This is a recent requirement — producers who obtained their Iowa license before June 2, 2025 were not fingerprinted at initial licensing, though new applicants and renewals post-June 2025 are subject to the new requirement. Iowa uses Fieldprint, not PSI, for fingerprinting.

## Application: Fees and Timing

All three states accept applications through NIPR, but fee structures differ.

Minnesota: $50 per line of authority + technology fee ($10–$20) + $5.60 NIPR transaction fee. Combined P&C: $100 + fees ≈ $125.60. Combined Life & A&H: $100 + fees ≈ $125.60. No waiting period between passing the exam and submitting the application. Application valid for 36 months after passing the exam.

Wisconsin: License application must be submitted through NIPR. Wisconsin requires applicants to wait 48–72 hours after passing the exam before submitting the electronic application — a mandatory delay that Minnesota and Iowa do not impose. Wisconsin's specific application fee should be confirmed through NIPR at the time of application, as fee schedules update periodically.

Iowa: Application must be submitted within 90 days of passing the exam — no exceptions and no extensions. This 90-day window is the tightest of the three states and is driven by Iowa's short exam result validity period. Iowa's application fee structure should be confirmed through NIPR; total government fees for both P&C lines are approximately $222.35 based on verified 2026 data.

## Continuing Education: Three Different Frameworks

The CE structures of Minnesota, Wisconsin, and Iowa differ in total hours, renewal period length, and format rules — creating meaningfully different ongoing obligations.

Minnesota: 24 hours every 2 years, including 3 hours of ethics. At least 12 hours must not be sponsored by or affiliated with an insurance company — this requirement can be satisfied entirely through self-paced online courses from an independent provider; Minnesota does not require any live or in-person instruction. Renewal deadline: last day of birth month, biennial. Renewal fee: $50 + $30 technology surcharge = $80.

Wisconsin: 24 hours every 2 years, including 3 hours of ethics. Wisconsin does not carry over excess CE credits to the next period — any hours completed beyond 24 in a reporting period are forfeited. Wisconsin requires a proctor for self-study CE exam completion — a requirement that applies to CE as well as prelicensing, unlike Minnesota where the proctor requirement is only at prelicensing. Renewal deadline: last day of birth month, biennial.

Iowa: 36 hours every 3 years, including 3 hours of ethics. Iowa's 3-year renewal cycle is longer than both Minnesota and Wisconsin's 2-year cycles, which means producers manage fewer renewals over a career but must plan for a larger single-period CE obligation. Renewal deadline: last day of birth month, triennial.

## Reinstatement After Lapse

All three states provide a reinstatement window for lapsed licenses, with differences in requirements.

Minnesota: 12-month reinstatement window. Penalty: double the unpaid renewal fee (~$160). No new prelicensing, exam, or fingerprinting required within the 12-month window. After 12 months: full relicensing required.

Wisconsin: Reinstatement available within 1 year of lapse. Wisconsin's reinstating producers do not need to retake the prelicensing exam or prelicensing education — but all overdue CE must be completed before reinstatement is granted. After 1 year: full relicensing required. Wisconsin explicitly does not offer a grace period for late renewals — the license lapses on the deadline day, not with any grace extension.

Iowa: Reinstatement available up to 1 year after the expiration date. After 1 year: the producer must reapply as a new applicant and retake the licensing exam. Iowa also does not offer a grace period for late renewals.

All three states have a hard cutoff at approximately 12 months — the most consequential date for any producer who has missed a renewal.

## Relocation Between States: What Changes

### From Minnesota to Wisconsin

A producer relocating from Minnesota to Wisconsin can transfer their resident license without completing Wisconsin prelicensing or taking the Wisconsin state exam, provided they apply for the Wisconsin resident license within one year of surrendering or allowing their Minnesota resident license to become inactive. Documentation of the prior Minnesota license is required. This generous transfer window is longer than Iowa's 90-day window and reflects Wisconsin's recognition of Minnesota's comparable licensing standards.

### From Minnesota to Iowa

Iowa allows producers relocating from other states to transfer their resident license within 90 days of surrendering the prior resident license without retaking Iowa's licensing exams. Given that Iowa has no prelicensing requirement of its own, this primarily means exam exemption — the relocating Minnesota producer applies through NIPR, pays Iowa's fees, completes fingerprinting through Fieldprint, and receives the Iowa resident license without sitting for the Iowa Pearson VUE exam.

### From Wisconsin to Minnesota

Wisconsin producers relocating to Minnesota can obtain a Minnesota resident license without completing Minnesota's 20-hour prelicensing or taking the Minnesota PSI exam, provided they apply for the Minnesota resident license within 90 days of the Wisconsin resident license going inactive. Fingerprinting through PSI and the Minnesota application fee are still required.

### From Iowa to Minnesota

Iowa producers relocating to Minnesota have the same 90-day window — apply for a Minnesota resident license within 90 days of the Iowa license going inactive and no Minnesota prelicensing or exam is required. The absence of Iowa prelicensing does not affect this — the exemption is based on the active Iowa license, not on what that license required to obtain.

## Non-Resident Licensing Across the Three States

Producers who want to serve clients across state lines without establishing new residency apply for non-resident licenses. All three states participate in NIPR's electronic licensing system and have reciprocity with each other, meaning:

A Minnesota producer serving Wisconsin clients needs a Wisconsin non-resident license — no Wisconsin prelicensing or exam required, just a Wisconsin non-resident application through NIPR and payment of Wisconsin's non-resident application fee. CE obligations remain with Minnesota (the home state).

A Wisconsin producer serving Minnesota clients needs a Minnesota non-resident license — same streamlined process, no Minnesota prelicensing or exam, Minnesota non-resident application through NIPR ($50 per line + fees), no fingerprinting required. CE remains with Wisconsin.

An Iowa producer serving Minnesota and Wisconsin clients follows the same process — non-resident applications through NIPR for each state, no prelicensing or exam in the non-home states, CE remains with Iowa under Iowa's 36-hour triennial framework.

## The Bottom Line: Choosing Your Entry State

The structural differences between these three states create practical implications for producers who have a choice about where to establish their primary license:

Minnesota offers the longest exam result validity (3 years), a never-expiring prelicensing certificate, clear fingerprinting integration with the PSI exam day, and a well-organized 2-year CE cycle. The 20-hour prelicensing requirement is a real investment — but it prepares candidates thoroughly for the exam and for the market.

Wisconsin has a similar structure to Minnesota with the important difference that the certificate of completion expires in one year (versus never in Minnesota) and exam results are valid for only 180 days (versus 3 years in Minnesota). The shared 8-hour foundation module reduces the marginal education burden when adding a second line within 12 months. The CE proctor requirement for self-study is a modest additional obligation compared to Minnesota.

Iowa offers the fastest path to a license from a regulatory standpoint — no required prelicensing education means a motivated candidate can study independently and sit for the Pearson VUE exam on their own timeline. However, the 90-day exam result validity window creates pressure to apply quickly, the single remote exam attempt rule restricts flexibility, and the 36-hour triennial CE requirement with 18 mandatory in-person hours is the most demanding ongoing obligation of the three states.

## Frequently Asked Questions

I hold a Minnesota license and want to add Wisconsin and Iowa non-resident licenses. Do I need to take any additional exams?

No. As a Minnesota resident producer with an active Minnesota license, you can obtain non-resident licenses in both Wisconsin and Iowa through NIPR without completing prelicensing education or sitting for their state exams. Reciprocity between all three states means your Minnesota license serves as the basis for your non-resident authority in the other two. You pay each state's non-resident application fee, answer the disclosure questions, and meet any other eligibility requirements (Iowa requires fingerprinting from residents of certain states — confirm whether this applies to Minnesota residents obtaining Iowa non-resident licenses). Your CE obligations remain with Minnesota — Wisconsin and Iowa do not impose separate CE requirements on non-resident producers from reciprocal states.

I'm relocating from Iowa to Minnesota. Iowa didn't require prelicensing. Do I still get the exam exemption when applying for a Minnesota resident license?

Yes. Minnesota's exemption for relocating producers is based on your active Iowa resident license, not on what Iowa required to obtain that license. The fact that Iowa has no prelicensing requirement does not affect your eligibility for the Minnesota exemption. You must apply for your Minnesota resident license within 90 days of your Iowa resident license going inactive. Fingerprinting through PSI and the Minnesota application fee are required — the exemption only waives prelicensing education and the Minnesota state exam, not the administrative steps.

Wisconsin's CE requires a proctor for self-study exams. Does this apply to online CE courses taken from my home?

Yes. Wisconsin requires a proctor for the final examination in self-study and online CE courses — just as it requires a proctor for the prelicensing certification exam. The proctor can be a friend, neighbor, or coworker who is not a family member and has no financial relationship with the licensee. Unlike prelicensing proctors, Wisconsin CE proctors do not need to be a licensed insurance agent. This requirement is stricter than Minnesota's ongoing CE obligation, which does not impose a proctor requirement on CE courses (only on the prelicensing certification exam). Producers maintaining Wisconsin non-resident licenses while based in Minnesota should verify Wisconsin's specific CE proctor requirements with their Wisconsin-approved CE provider.
