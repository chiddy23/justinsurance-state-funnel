Minnesota's [prelicensing](/minnesota/prelicensing) education requirement is the foundation of the licensing process — and the step where the most confusion arises. Applicants who misunderstand what the 20-hour requirement actually demands, how the certification exam works, or what the certificate of completion authorizes often delay their licensing timeline unnecessarily or, worse, sit for the state exam before satisfying all prelicensing requirements and forfeit their exam fee. This post covers exactly how Minnesota's prelicensing requirement works: what the law requires, how the hours are counted, what the internal certification exam demands, how the proctor requirement functions, what the certificate of completion is and is not, and how the requirement differs across lines of authority.

## The Statutory Basis: Minn. Stat. §60K.36 Subd. 4

Minnesota's prelicensing education requirement is established by Minn. Stat. §60K.36 Subd. 4, which requires applicants for producer licenses to complete a Minnesota Department of Commerce-approved prelicensing course of study before sitting for the state licensing exam. The statute specifies that the course must consist of 20 hours per line of authority covering the general principles of insurance for that line.

The Department of Commerce administers the prelicensing education system — approving course providers, setting content standards, and enforcing the sequence requirement that prelicensing must precede the state exam. Course providers are listed on Sircon, the Department's licensing portal. Only courses from Department-approved providers satisfy the prelicensing requirement. Completing a general insurance education course, a college course in insurance, or a study guide from a non-approved source does not satisfy the statute.

## What 20 Hours Actually Means

The 20-hour requirement is not a guideline or an estimate of how long the course takes to complete. It is a minimum time floor that course providers must verify independently of whether the applicant has completed the course content.

Time verification: Minnesota-approved providers track the time each student spends actively engaged with course content. A student who completes all readings and videos in 12 hours has not satisfied the 20-hour requirement even if they understand the material perfectly. The provider confirms time compliance before issuing the certificate of completion. Students who rush through material below the 20-hour floor will not receive their certificate until the time requirement is satisfied.

What counts toward the 20 hours: Active engagement with approved course content — reading material, watching videos, completing module exercises, and participating in course activities. Time spent on practice exams may or may not count depending on the provider's course design. Students should verify with their provider exactly how [practice exam](/minnesota/practice-exam) time is tracked and credited.

What does not count: Time spent on general study outside the approved course, reviewing personal notes, reading supplementary textbooks, or taking non-course practice exams does not count toward the 20-hour minimum. The hours are specific to time spent within the approved course itself.

The two-part completion standard: A student satisfies the prelicensing requirement only when both conditions are met: (1) the 20-hour time floor has been reached and verified by the provider, and (2) the internal certification exam has been passed with 70% or higher. Completing the time requirement without passing the certification exam does not yield a certificate. Passing the certification exam before the time requirement is verified does not yield a certificate. Both must be satisfied.

## Lines of Authority and Their Hour Requirements

The 20-hour requirement applies per line of authority for the following lines:

Combined line requirements: A producer seeking both Property and Casualty must complete 40 total hours of prelicensing — 20 hours specifically covering Property content and 20 hours specifically covering Casualty content. A producer seeking both Life and A&H must similarly complete 40 hours. Most approved providers offer integrated combined-line courses — a single 40-hour package covering both lines simultaneously — which is the most efficient path for producers seeking combined authority.

Variable Life and Variable Annuities: This line has a separate education structure. The Variable line requires separate FINRA securities registration in addition to the insurance license. The prelicensing education requirement for this line differs from the major lines — consult the Department of Commerce directly for the current specific requirement.

## Course Content: What the 20 Hours Covers

Each line of authority's prelicensing course covers the general principles of insurance for that specific line. The content outline is aligned with the PSI state exam content outline, ensuring that completing the course prepares the student for the exam they will take.

Property course content: Homeowners policies (HO-1 through HO-8 forms), dwelling policies, commercial property, inland marine, the National Flood Insurance Program, insurance terms and concepts (indemnity, insurable interest, ACV vs. replacement cost, subrogation), policy provisions and contract law, and Minnesota-specific property insurance statutes and regulations.

Casualty course content: Commercial general liability, personal auto and business auto, workers' compensation and employers liability, crime coverage, bonds, professional liability, umbrella and excess liability, insurance terms and concepts (negligence, liability, occurrence, certificates of insurance), policy provisions, and Minnesota-specific casualty insurance laws.

Personal Lines course content: An integrated overview of both property and casualty principles as they apply to personal, family, and household risks — homeowners, personal auto, personal umbrella — with Minnesota-specific personal lines regulations.

Life course content: Types of life insurance (term, whole life, universal life, variable life), policy provisions, annuities (fixed, variable, indexed), life insurance taxation, beneficiary designations, policy replacement, and Minnesota-specific life insurance statutes including replacement regulations and suitability requirements.

A&H course content: Types of health insurance (individual, group, HMO, PPO, HDHP), disability income policies, long-term care insurance, Medicare supplement (Medigap) policies, government programs (Medicare, Medicaid, Medical Assistance), COBRA continuation, ACA provisions, and Minnesota-specific A&H laws including MNsure, Medical Assistance, and MinnesotaCare.

## The Internal Certification Exam

After completing the 20 hours of coursework, the applicant must pass an internal certification exam administered by the course provider. This exam is separate from and preliminary to the PSI state licensing exam. It serves as the course's final assessment of whether the applicant has absorbed the material.

Passing standard: 70% or higher on the certification exam. If the applicant fails, they may retake the certification exam — policies on retake waiting periods vary by provider. Most providers allow students to review course material between attempts.

The proctor requirement: Minnesota law requires that the certification exam be monitored by a disinterested third party — a proctor. The Department of Commerce defines a proctor as someone who has no conflict of interest with the applicant, verifies the applicant's identity, and processes an affidavit confirming that the student received no outside assistance during the exam. Both the applicant and the proctor must sign this affidavit at the conclusion of the exam.

Who can serve as proctor: Any person who satisfies the disinterested standard — no family relationship to the applicant, no financial interest in the applicant's success, and not above or below the applicant in a supervisory work relationship. Acceptable proctors include professional colleagues from outside the supervisory chain, librarians, teachers, notaries, or professional proctoring services. Online proctoring services that verify identity through webcam are also commonly used for courses offered remotely.

Who cannot serve as proctor: Parents, spouses, siblings, children, other family members. Anyone who has a financial stake in the applicant's outcome — such as a recruiter or agency owner who benefits from the applicant becoming licensed. A co-worker who is either a supervisor of the applicant or supervised by the applicant.

Online proctoring mechanics: For self-paced online courses, the certification exam is typically unlocked only after the proctor verifies the applicant's identity electronically — usually through a webcam-based identity confirmation. Once the exam begins, the applicant cannot return to course content. The exam must be completed continuously; the proctor monitors the session throughout. At conclusion, both parties complete the digital affidavit.

## The Certificate of Completion

Upon passing the certification exam with 70% or higher and satisfying the 20-hour time requirement, the course provider issues a Certificate of Completion. This document serves as official proof that the applicant has satisfied Minnesota's prelicensing education requirement and is authorized to schedule and sit for the PSI state licensing exam.

What the certificate authorizes: The certificate authorizes the applicant to schedule and take the PSI state licensing exam for the specific line of authority the course covered. It does not authorize the applicant to transact insurance, hold themselves out as an insurance producer, or take any other regulatory step — it simply clears the prelicensing hurdle for the exam step.

The certificate never expires. A student who completes their 20-hour Property course and passes the certification exam in January may take the PSI Property exam in January, June, or the following year — the certificate remains valid indefinitely. This is a meaningful advantage for applicants whose schedules or personal circumstances prevent them from immediately scheduling the state exam. There is no urgency imposed by a certificate expiration date.

Presentation at the exam center: The certificate must be physically or digitally presented at the PSI test center at the time of the state exam. Applicants who arrive without their certificate will not be admitted and will forfeit their exam fee. Most providers deliver the certificate as a downloadable PDF — save it and bring it to your exam session, whether in-person or for remote check-in.

Provider reporting to the Department: Course providers are required to report course completions to the Minnesota Department of Commerce within five business days of completion. This reporting allows PSI and the Department to verify that an applicant's prelicensing is on file before releasing state exam results. The Department verifies prelicensing status electronically — even if the provider has reported, applicants should have their certificate in hand as backup documentation.

## Exemptions: When Prelicensing Education Is Not Required

Minnesota's prelicensing requirement does not apply to all applicants. Specific professional designations and academic credentials qualify applicants for an exemption from the education requirement — but not from the state exam, fingerprinting, or application. All exempt applicants must still pass the PSI state licensing exam, complete the background check, and submit the license application.

Life line exemptions: CEBS, ChFC, CIC, CLU, FLMI, or LUTCF designation holders are exempt from Life prelicensing.

A&H line exemptions: CEBS, HIA, REBC, or RHU designation holders are exempt from A&H prelicensing.

Property, Casualty, or Personal Lines exemptions: AAI, ARM, CIC, or CPCU designation holders are exempt from Property, Casualty, or Personal Lines prelicensing.

Academic exemption: A four-year college degree in business with an insurance emphasis qualifies for exemption. The applicant must present documentation of the degree and its insurance-specific curriculum.

Applicants relocating from another state: Producers who held an active resident license in another state and apply for a Minnesota resident license within 90 days of their prior license becoming inactive are exempt from both the Minnesota prelicensing requirement and the Minnesota state exam. Fingerprinting and the application fee are still required.

Farm Property and Farm Liability: No prelicensing required for this limited line.

Documentation at the exam center: Applicants claiming an exemption must bring documentation of the qualifying designation or credential to the PSI exam center. Arriving at the exam center without documentation of your exemption may prevent you from being admitted.

## Choosing a Prelicensing Provider

Minnesota-approved providers are listed on Sircon. The choice of provider affects the learning experience, the quality of exam preparation, and the efficiency of the certification process — but all approved providers satisfy the same statutory requirement.

Self-paced online vs. live classroom: Self-paced online courses allow applicants to study on their own schedule, completing the 20 hours over days or weeks as their schedule permits. Live classroom courses — offered by some providers on a scheduled basis — provide structured instruction and real-time interaction but require attending sessions at scheduled times. Both formats satisfy the Minnesota prelicensing requirement.

Quality indicators for provider selection: A high-quality prelicensing provider offers content that is closely aligned with the PSI state exam content outline, includes practice exams that reflect the format and difficulty of the state exam, provides clear explanations of Minnesota-specific legal requirements (not just general insurance principles), has a transparent proctoring process for the certification exam, and reports completions to the Department promptly. ## Common Prelicensing Mistakes and How to Avoid Them

Mistake 1 — Scheduling the state exam before completing prelicensing. This is the most costly mistake. Applicants who sit for the PSI exam without a valid certificate of completion forfeit their $45 exam fee and must retake the exam after properly completing prelicensing. Do not schedule your PSI exam until your certificate is in hand.

Mistake 2 — Counting non-course study time toward the 20-hour minimum. Time spent reading supplementary materials, reviewing notes, or taking outside practice exams does not count. Only time within the approved course counts. If you complete the course content in less than 20 hours, you must continue engaging with approved course materials until the time floor is met.

Mistake 3 — Using an ineligible proctor for the certification exam. An exam proctored by a family member or by a supervisor is not valid. If the proctor affidavit is invalid, the certification exam result may be voided. Choose a proctor who clearly satisfies the disinterested standard before scheduling your certification exam session.

Mistake 4 — Completing prelicensing for one line when you need two. A producer who completes the 20-hour Property course but needs both Property and Casualty authority must also complete the 20-hour Casualty course. Completing only one line's prelicensing and passing the state exam for that line produces a license with only that line's authority — not the combined authority the producer intended. Plan your line selections before enrolling.

Mistake 5 — Waiting too long after the certification exam to take the state exam. While the certificate of completion itself never expires, delaying the state exam by months means the course content becomes less fresh. Most candidates perform best on the PSI exam within two to four weeks of completing their prelicensing course, when the material is most current in their memory.

## Frequently Asked Questions

I completed a prelicensing course years ago but never took the state exam. Do I need to redo the course?

Minnesota's certificate of completion never expires — your course completion remains valid regardless of how much time has passed. However, your practical readiness for the exam may have diminished if significant time has passed since you completed the course. If you completed the course more than a year ago, reviewing the course material — or enrolling in a refresher through your provider, if available — before scheduling the PSI exam is worth considering. The state law section of the exam is particularly time-sensitive because Minnesota statutes and regulations do change. Confirm with your provider that their course content reflects current Minnesota law before relying on a significantly dated completion.

Can I take the prelicensing certification exam without a proctor if I'm taking the course online?

No. The proctor requirement applies to all Minnesota prelicensing certification exams regardless of whether the course is taken online or in a classroom. Online course providers use webcam-based proctoring systems to satisfy this requirement remotely. The proctor verifies your identity electronically before the exam begins, monitors the session via webcam, and completes the digital affidavit with you at conclusion. If you attempt to take the certification exam without a proctor confirming your identity and monitoring the session, the exam result is invalid and the provider cannot issue your certificate of completion.

My employer wants me licensed as quickly as possible. What is the fastest realistic timeline?

The fastest realistic timeline from enrollment to a submitted license application is approximately two weeks for a self-motivated candidate. The breakdown: completing the 20-hour prelicensing course takes five to ten days of focused study. Scheduling the PSI exam requires at least two days' advance notice for remote testing. Getting fingerprinted at the PSI test center on exam day adds no additional time. Submitting the application through NIPR takes minutes, and the Department processes applications in approximately 10 business days. For a candidate who studies full-time and tests within days of completing prelicensing, the entire process from enrollment to approved license can be completed in two to three weeks. For a candidate studying part-time, three to four weeks is typical. There is no way to shorten the 20-hour prelicensing requirement — it is a minimum time floor, not an approximation.

Does completing prelicensing qualify me to sell insurance before I take the state exam?

No. Completing prelicensing education — even earning your certificate of completion — does not authorize you to sell, solicit, or negotiate insurance. Prelicensing education qualifies you to sit for the state exam. The state exam, if passed, qualifies you to apply for a license. The license, once issued, authorizes you to hold yourself out as a licensed producer. The appointment from a carrier, once submitted, authorizes you to transact business for that carrier. Each step in the sequence has a specific function, and none of the earlier steps substitute for the later ones. A producer who attempts to sell insurance after completing prelicensing but before receiving a license is engaging in unlicensed activity — a regulatory violation under Minnesota law.

Candidates who follow this sequence without shortcuts consistently complete the process on time and with confidence on exam day.
