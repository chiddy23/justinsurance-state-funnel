If you already hold a Virginia insurance producer license and want to expand what you can sell — adding Property & Casualty to an existing Life & Health license, or adding Health to a Life & Annuities license — you are adding a line of authority. Virginia's process for adding a line follows the same general framework as initial licensing, with a few important distinctions around fingerprinting, application fees, and the CE implications of expanding your license portfolio. Here is exactly how the process works and what changes when you already hold a license.

## What Is a Line of Authority?

Virginia issues producer licenses by line of authority — the specific qualification you are authorized to sell. The qualifications that require a Prometric exam are:

Life and Annuities

Health

Property and Casualty (a single combined qualification — the Series 11-03 exam covers both, and it already includes the Personal Lines classes of insurance)

Personal Lines (the narrower, personal-only alternative to full Property and Casualty)

Title (requires 16-hour [prelicensing](/virginia/prelicensing) course)

Each qualification is added separately to your license. You can hold one, several, or all of them. Adding a line means obtaining authorization to transact an additional insurance type you do not currently hold. Note that Property and Casualty is one qualification, not two — Virginia does not issue a standalone "Property" or "Casualty" line.

## Step 1: Pass the Prometric Exam for the New Line

You must pass the Prometric licensing exam for the new line of authority before applying to add it. There is no shortcut or waiver for this requirement (unless an exemption applies — see below).

Register with Prometric at prometric.com/virginia/insurance or call (866) 891-6396. The exam fee is $35 per exam. Your passing score is valid for 183 days — and Virginia requires the new line of authority to be *issued* within that window, not merely applied for (Va. Code § 38.2-1817). If the license is not issued within 183 calendar days of the exam, the grade is void and the exam and application fees are forfeited. Submit at least a month early to leave room for the Bureau's roughly 15-business-day processing.

Exemption — Variable Contracts: The Variable Contracts qualification does not require a Prometric exam. Per the Bureau of Insurance, you must instead already hold an active Life & Annuities line of authority and have passed the FINRA Series 6 or Series 7 exam; the application requires your FINRA CRD number so the Bureau can verify that exam. Those are the Bureau's only two conditions for this qualification. Separately, actually selling variable products requires registration as a securities agent of a broker-dealer with the SCC's Division of Securities and Retail Franchising — Virginia's securities regulator. That securities registration is a distinct process from the insurance qualification, and it is where any Series 63 or Series 66 obligation would come from, not from the Bureau of Insurance.

## Step 2: Determine Whether New Fingerprints Are Required

This is the step that most existing licensees get wrong — almost always because "license type" means something narrower here than it does for CE.

You do NOT need new fingerprints if:

You have submitted fingerprints to Fieldprint in the last 12 months, AND

You are currently licensed in Virginia, AND

You are applying for the same license type you already hold

You DO need new fingerprints if:

Your prior fingerprints are more than 12 months old, OR

You are applying for a different license type than you currently hold

What "license type" means here: the Bureau of Insurance defines license type by the Fieldprint code you schedule under, and there are only five —

FPVABOIProducer — Producer licenses (including limited lines, motor vehicle rental contract, and temporary licenses)

FPVABOIConsultant — Consultant licenses

FPVABOISurplus — Surplus Lines Broker licenses

FPVABOIPubAdj — Public Adjuster licenses

FPVABOIViatical — Viatical Settlement licenses

Every line of authority discussed in this article — Life and Annuities, Health, Property and Casualty, Personal Lines, Title — is a qualification on the Producer license. So adding Property & Casualty to an existing Life & Health producer license is still the Producer license type, and the 12-month exemption applies exactly the same way it does when you add Health to a Life & Annuities license. "A different license type" means moving between those five categories — Producer to Consultant, Surplus Lines Broker, Public Adjuster, or Viatical Settlement. That is when new prints are required, and the Bureau states there are no exceptions.

Do not confuse this with the CE meaning of "license type," which is a separate concept covered below. For CE purposes, Life/Annuities/Health count as one type and Property & Casualty as another — so adding P&C does raise your CE hours even though it does not, by itself, trigger a new fingerprint check.

The practical question for most add-line applicants is simply the calendar: were your prints submitted within the last 12 months? If you are not sure of your submission date, contact the Bureau of Insurance at AgentLicensing@scc.virginia.gov before scheduling a Fieldprint appointment.

Fieldprint Code (if required): FPVABOIProducer at fieldprintvirginia.com. Cost: $34.95.

## Step 3: Apply Through NIPR or Sircon

Submit your add-line application electronically through NIPR (nipr.com) or Sircon (sircon.com/virginia). The fee is $15 per new line of authority being added, plus the $5.60 NIPR/Sircon processing fee per session.

Your criminal background is verified through fingerprinting, not a record report you obtain and upload. When Virginia moved to fingerprint-based checks (effective January 1, 2020) it retired the old requirement to mail in a Criminal History Record Report from the State Police: your prints are captured by Fieldprint and transmitted to the Virginia State Police and the FBI, and the Bureau pulls the results directly. So if your prints are already on file from within the last 12 months and you are staying on the Producer license (see Step 2), you attach nothing here. If any background information has changed since your last application — a new charge or conviction — attach the disclosure documents Virginia requires for it: a written explanation of the circumstances plus the relevant court records.

Applications are processed in date order. Allow approximately 15 business days.

## CE Impact of Adding a Line

Adding a line of authority in Virginia has a direct impact on your [continuing education](/virginia/continuing-education) requirements going forward. This is not just an administrative note — it changes your CE obligation for every renewal cycle after the new line is added.

Single license type: 16 CE hours biennial (3 ethics hours required) Two or more license types: 24 CE hours biennial (3 ethics hours required, minimum 8 hours per line type held)

Life, Annuities, and Health are treated as a single license type for CE purposes. If you hold Life & Annuities and add Health (or vice versa), you remain at 16 hours biennial.

If you hold a Life & Health license and add Property & Casualty (or any P&C line), you now hold two license types — your CE obligation increases from 16 to 24 hours biennial, with a minimum of 8 hours in each license type.

Plan for this change before adding a line. Adding P&C to an existing L&H license is valuable for cross-selling but adds 8 CE hours per cycle and requires CE in a second subject area.

## Frequently Asked Questions

Can I add a line of authority at any time, or only at renewal?

You can apply to add a line of authority at any time — you do not need to wait for your renewal period. Pass the Prometric exam for the new line, complete any required fingerprinting, and submit your add-line application through NIPR or Sircon. The $15 per-line fee and the 183-day exam score validity window apply the same way as an initial application. There is no procedural advantage to waiting for your renewal date, and there is a meaningful disadvantage: if your exam score expires (183 days), you must retake the exam. Add your new line as soon as you are ready.

If I already hold a Life & Annuities license and want to add Health — or Property & Casualty — do I need a new fingerprint check?

Generally only if your last fingerprint submission is more than 12 months old. The Bureau's exemption has three conditions: prints submitted within the last 12 months, currently licensed, and applying for the same license type. Health, Property and Casualty, and Personal Lines are all lines of authority on the Producer license, so an existing producer adding any of them is applying for the same license type — which means the question turns on the 12-month window, not on which line you are adding. New prints are required when you apply for a genuinely different license type, such as Consultant, Surplus Lines Broker, Public Adjuster, or Viatical Settlement. If your prints are older than 12 months, budget the $34.95 Fieldprint fee and schedule early, because results must reach the Bureau before your application can be approved. If you cannot pin down your own submission date, confirm with the Bureau at AgentLicensing@scc.virginia.gov before you book the appointment.

What is the Personal Lines line of authority, and who should add it?

The Personal Lines line of authority in Virginia allows you to sell personal (non-commercial) Property and Casualty insurance products — primarily personal auto, homeowners, renters, and similar individual risk coverage. It is a narrower authorization than the full Property and Casualty qualification. Producers who want to sell only personal auto and homeowners — without commercial lines authority — can hold Personal Lines rather than the full Property and Casualty qualification. However, most producers who intend to build a comprehensive P&C practice take the full Property and Casualty exam rather than the Personal Lines exam, because the commercial lines authority opens access to a much larger market. Personal Lines is appropriate for producers focused exclusively on personal insurance who want a more targeted (and slightly narrower) exam scope.

Does adding a line of authority restart my license's renewal cycle?

No. Adding a line of authority does not reset your existing license's renewal date. All lines on your Virginia producer license renew together on your individual birth-month deadline — the last day of your birth month, in the odd or even year matching your birth year. Your CE compliance must cover all lines you hold by that renewal date. The new line you add will be included in your next renewal cycle on that same birth-month date. You may need to complete CE for the new line in a partial biennium if you add it close to the renewal date, so add lines early in the renewal cycle if possible.

Can I hold a Virginia insurance license without any active carrier appointments?

Yes. Your Virginia producer license is independent of your carrier appointments. You can hold an active license — including all the lines you are authorized for — without having any insurer appointments on file. An appointment is required before you can transact insurance on behalf of a specific carrier, but the license itself does not require any appointments to remain active. Producers who are between agencies, building an independent practice, or studying for additional lines routinely maintain active licenses without appointments. The Bureau of Insurance does not require appointment activity as a condition of license renewal.

Adding a line of authority in Virginia is a straightforward extension of the initial licensing process — same exam, same Fieldprint requirements (when applicable), same application portal. The one additional consideration is your CE obligation, which increases when you cross from one license type to two. Plan for that before you apply, and the rest is a matter of scheduling and paperwork.
