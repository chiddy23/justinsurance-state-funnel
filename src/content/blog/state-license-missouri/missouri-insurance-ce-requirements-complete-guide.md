Missouri's CE framework is distinctive for one overriding fact: 16 hours every 2 years — the lowest CE requirement of all comparison states. Most comparison states require 24 hours; Virginia requires 16-24 hours (L&H gets 16; P&C gets 24); Maryland requires 24. Missouri's 16-hour requirement, combined with no PLE requirement and a $32 Pearson VUE exam fee ($40 for a combined exam), makes it one of the lighter compliance loads among the states covered here. Missouri does not have annuity best-interest, long-term care, or NFIP flood specialty training requirements on record with the DCI — if you sell these product lines, confirm current specialty CE obligations directly with the [Missouri Department of Commerce and Insurance](https://insurance.mo.gov/continuing-education), since rules can change. Here's the complete guide.

## The Basic Framework

Under RSMo Chapter 375:

16 hours of CE every 2 years — the lowest of all comparison states

3 hours Ethics mandatory

Remaining 13 hours: approved CE content

CE before renewal submission

Renewal: every 2 years on your birth date (not month-end)

## Specialty Training Requirements

Missouri's DCI does not have annuity best-interest, long-term care, or NFIP flood specialty training requirements on record for CE credit at this time. If you sell annuities, long-term care, or flood insurance products, contact the Missouri DCI directly (or your CE provider) to confirm whether any specialty training currently applies to your license type — specialty CE rules can be added or changed, and producers who sell these product lines should verify before their renewal deadline.

Ethics: 3 hours ✓

Total: 16 hours ✓

## CE Tracking and Renewal Process

Complete 16 CE hours (3 Ethics + general/elective CE, plus any specialty training that applies to your license type) before renewal

CE tracked through DCI-approved providers — verify in DCI's CE database

Submit renewal through NIPR or DCI website

Pay the $100 biennial renewal fee (confirm the current amount at insurance.mo.gov)

License renewed for next 2-year cycle

## Non-Resident CE

Non-residents who are licensed in good standing in their home state may maintain their Missouri non-resident license by complying with their home state's CE requirements. Missouri non-resident CE reciprocity is broad.

## 5 Frequently Asked Questions

- Why does Missouri only require 16 CE hours when most states require 24? Missouri's 16-hour CE requirement reflects DCI's policy choice to maintain an accessible [continuing education](/missouri/continuing-education) standard. Combined with no PLE requirement and a $32 single-line Pearson VUE exam fee ($40 combined), Missouri keeps the total cost of entry and upkeep low. Virginia also uses 16 hours for some lines, but most Midwest comparison states (Indiana, Ohio, Minnesota) require 24.
- Does Missouri require specialty CE for annuities, LTC, or flood insurance? Missouri does not have these specialty training requirements on record with the DCI. If you sell these product lines, confirm current requirements directly with the Missouri DCI or your CE provider before relying on any specific hour count or effective date.
- Can Missouri resident producers complete CE in other states? For standard CE: Missouri-approved CE courses may be completed through any DCI-approved provider regardless of location.
- What happens if I miss the renewal deadline in Missouri? The Missouri producer license renewal fee is $100 biennially (per the DCI fee chart reproduced in the Pearson VUE Missouri candidate handbook), and DCI's published late-renewal policy adds $25 per month from the day after expiration — there is no grace period — capped at a 12-month reinstatement window. Past 12 months you have to apply for a new license from scratch. Indiana handles a missed renewal differently: a flat reinstatement penalty of $120 on top of its $40 renewal fee, or $160 all-in to bring the license current within 12 months. Confirm the current Missouri amounts at insurance.mo.gov before you rely on them.
- Is there a daily CE limit in Missouri like Minnesota's 8-hour cap? Minnesota prohibits completing more than 8 CE hours in a single day. Verify current Missouri DCI policy at insurance.mo.gov — Missouri may not have an explicit daily limit, but CE providers are DCI-approved and follow DCI reporting requirements. Complete CE well before the renewal deadline regardless of any daily limits.
