The Michigan Department of Insurance and Financial Services manages insurance producer licensing in Michigan, working with PSI for exam administration and NIPR for application processing. Understanding how these three systems interact — and what choices you face in applying — helps you submit a clean application, avoid costly mistakes, and get licensed efficiently. Michigan has several distinctive application features including one of the lowest fee structures in the country, no fingerprinting requirement, and a 180-day application validity window that requires active management.

Here's a complete walkthrough of the Michigan DIFS insurance license application process.

## Who DIFS Is

The Michigan Department of Insurance and Financial Services (DIFS) regulates insurance throughout Michigan. The Department:

Licenses insurance producers, adjusters, and other insurance professionals

Regulates insurance companies operating in Michigan

Enforces insurance laws under Michigan Compiled Laws Chapter 500 (MCL 500)

Investigates consumer complaints

Maintains the Michigan licensee database

Conducts rate review for insurance products

Has jurisdiction over banking, securities, and mortgage in addition to insurance

DIFS Contact Information:

Address: 530 W. Allegan St., 7th Floor, Lansing, MI 48933-1521

Mailing: PO Box 30220, Lansing, MI 48909-7720

Phone: (877) 999-6442

Email: difs-licensing@michigan.gov

Website: michigan.gov/difs

DIFS is distinctive among state insurance departments because it also regulates banking, credit unions, securities, and mortgage lending — making it one of the more integrated financial regulatory agencies.

## The Michigan Application Sequence

Michigan's sequence differs from some states:

Correct Michigan sequence:

Complete [prelicensing](/michigan/prelicensing) education (PLE) for your chosen line(s)

Pass PLE certification exam (70%+) and receive Certificate of Completion

Apply through NIPR (pay $10 + $5.60 fee)

Schedule state exam through PSI (pay $41 per exam)

Pass state exam

DIFS reviews application (up to 14 business days)

Receive physical license by mail

Common mistake: Taking the state exam before completing PLE. If you pass the state exam before completing your PLE course, you will be required to retest with PSI. Complete the PLE certification exam first, then schedule your state exam.

## Decision 1: Which Lines to Apply For

Michigan's application structure allows flexibility in choosing lines:

Single-line applications:

Life only

Accident & Health only

Property only

Casualty only

Personal Lines only (for agents serving exclusively personal non-commercial P&C)

Combined applications:

Life and Accident & Health (most common L&H combination)

Property and Casualty (most common P&C combination)

All major lines (broadest market access)

Cost implications:

Michigan's application fee doesn't increase per line in the way some states charge $50+ per line of authority. The ~$15.60 total covers the application regardless of how many lines you're applying for — making Michigan's fee structure especially affordable for multi-line applications.

## Decision 2: Application Timing Relative to Exam

Michigan's 180-day application validity window requires strategic timing:

Option A: Apply, then complete PLE, then take exam

Apply early to establish your NIPR record

Complete PLE within the 180-day window

Take exam and submit results within 180 days

Risk: If PLE takes longer than expected, clock is running

Option B: Complete PLE, then apply, then take exam (most common)

Ensure PLE is completed and certificate in hand

Apply through NIPR

Schedule exam promptly

Risk: Lower — certificate is already in hand when applying

Option C: Complete PLE and apply simultaneously

Apply while PLE is in progress

Coordinate certificate submission with application

Works well if PLE course is nearly complete

Recommended approach: Complete or nearly complete PLE before applying, then apply and schedule exam promptly. This minimizes the risk of the 180-day window expiring before passing the exam.

Critical rule: If you fail the exam within your 180-day application window, you must submit a new NIPR application and fee (~$15.60) before rescheduling. This makes first-attempt pass even more financially valuable.

## Application Steps Through NIPR

Step 1: Go to nipr.com.

Step 2: Create or log into your NIPR account.

Step 3: Select "Apply for a License" and choose Michigan.

Step 4: Select "Resident" (Michigan resident) or "Non-Resident" (already licensed elsewhere).

Step 5: Select "Individual."

Step 6: Complete the application:

Legal name (must match government-issued ID exactly)

Date of birth

Social Security Number

Michigan home address

Contact information

Lines of authority

Employment history (last 5 years; no gaps — list unemployment periods with dates)

Step 7: Answer background disclosure questions:

Criminal history

Prior license actions

Civil judgments

Bankruptcy

Regulatory actions

Insurance company terminations

Step 8: Upload or plan to submit Certificate of Completion from PLE provider.

Step 9: Pay $10 application fee + $5.60 transaction fee.

Step 10: Print receipt at time of submission.

Important: DIFS strongly recommends against applying if you were convicted of a felony within the last 10 years, or if your felony involves violence, threats of violence, or financial crimes — though DIFS reviews each application individually.

## No Fingerprinting — Michigan's Distinctive Feature

Michigan does not require fingerprinting. This is genuinely distinctive nationally — most states require fingerprinting as part of the background check for insurance producer licensing.

Michigan's background review occurs through:

DIFS review of application disclosure answers

Cross-referencing available records

Review of supporting documentation for "yes" disclosure answers

DIFS staff investigation as needed

Practical implications:

Faster processing (no fingerprint scheduling)

Lower cost (no fingerprint fee)

No IdentoGO, no local law enforcement visits

Background check is still substantive — honest disclosure is critical

## Disclosure Questions in Detail

Michigan's application asks about prior conduct. Answer honestly:

Criminal history disclosures:

Felony convictions (including those more than 10 years ago — disclose, though current guidance strongly recommends against applying for recent violent or financial felonies)

Misdemeanor convictions involving dishonesty or financial misconduct

Pending charges

Insurance industry history:

Prior license denial, suspension, or revocation in any state

Prior regulatory actions

Termination for cause from insurance company

Financial history:

Bankruptcy within specified timeframe

Significant civil judgments

Other professional history:

Other professional license issues

For "yes" answers, NIPR's Attachment Warehouse allows supporting document submission. Provide:

Written explanation of circumstances

Certified copies of relevant court or regulatory documents

Evidence of resolution

Evidence of rehabilitation where applicable

## What DIFS Reviews

After submission, DIFS reviews:

PLE completion. Your Certificate of Completion should be reported to DIFS by your provider within 2 business days of completing the course.

Exam results. PSI electronically submits passing scores to DIFS.

Application disclosures. Cross-referenced against available records.

Supporting documentation. For disclosure items.

Application completeness. Any missing information triggers follow-up requests.

## Processing Times

Standard clean applications: Up to 14 business days from submission of all requirements.

Applications with disclosures: May take longer depending on complexity.

Physical license delivery: Michigan mails physical licenses, adding mail time beyond the processing window.

Total realistic timeline: 3-5 weeks from completing PLE to receiving physical license for clean applications.

## After License Issuance

Physical license mailed. Michigan sends a physical license by mail to your address on file.

License is perpetual. Michigan producer licenses don't have traditional expiration dates — they remain active as long as you meet CE requirements.

CE compliance date. The last day of your birth month in your even or odd birth year determines your CE compliance cycle.

Carrier appointments. Contact carriers to complete appointment paperwork. Michigan carriers receive annual appointment invoices from DIFS in January.

"Brokerage" terminology. Michigan does not allow the term "brokerage" in the insurance context — use "agency" or other appropriate terminology.

## Non-Resident Application Path

For producers already licensed in another state:

No PLE required — DIFS waives PLE for non-residents in good standing in home state.

No state exam required — DIFS waives exam for non-residents in good standing.

Apply through NIPR — Same portal, select non-resident application.

Verification — DIFS verifies your resident license through the State Producer Licensing Database.

Fee — Same $10 + $5.60 transaction fee.

Timeline — Typically faster than resident licensing due to no exam requirement.

## 5 Frequently Asked Questions

- What is DIFS and what does it regulate? The Michigan Department of Insurance and Financial Services regulates insurance, banking, securities, and mortgage in Michigan. For insurance producers, DIFS manages licensing, renewals, CE compliance, and disciplinary actions under MCL Chapter 500.
- How long is my Michigan NIPR application valid? 180 days from entry into the DIFS database. All requirements (PLE completion, exam passage) must be satisfied within this window. If you fail your exam within the 180-day period, you must submit a new application and fee before rescheduling.
- Does Michigan charge per-line application fees? No. Michigan's approximately $15.60 total application fee (through NIPR) covers the application regardless of how many lines of authority you're applying for — making Michigan one of the most affordable states for multi-line applications.
- How long after submitting all requirements does Michigan take to issue a license? Up to 14 business days. Michigan mails a physical license, so allow additional mail time. Total realistic timeline from submitting all requirements to receiving the physical license is 3-5 weeks for clean applications.
- Can a Michigan non-resident apply without taking the state exam? Yes. Non-resident producers who are currently licensed as a resident and in good standing in their home state can obtain a Michigan non-resident license without completing Michigan PLE or taking the Michigan state exam. Apply through NIPR and DIFS verifies home state license through the State Producer Licensing Database.
