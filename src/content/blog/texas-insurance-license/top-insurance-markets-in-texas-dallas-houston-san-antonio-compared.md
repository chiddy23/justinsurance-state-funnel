Texas is one of the largest insurance markets in the country, and its three biggest metro areas — Dallas, Houston, and San Antonio — each offer unique opportunities for insurance agents. Understanding the differences between these markets helps you position yourself where you're most likely to thrive.

Here's how the three cities compare for building an insurance career.

## Dallas-Fort Worth: Corporate Depth and Wealth Management

The Dallas-Fort Worth metroplex is one of the largest economies in the United States, with a heavy concentration of corporate headquarters, financial services, and high-income professionals.

What works well here:

High-net-worth life insurance and estate planning

Group health and employee benefits (large employer base)

Annuities and retirement planning

Professional markets — doctors, attorneys, executives

Challenges:

Higher competition, especially from established wealth advisors

Client expectations are higher — sophisticated buyers want sophisticated advisors

Higher cost of doing business if you operate from Dallas proper

Dallas suits agents who want to serve professional and high-income clients and are willing to build a business rooted in expertise and consultative selling.

## Houston: Diverse Economy and Strong Blue-Collar Market

Houston has one of the most diverse metro economies in the country, spanning energy, healthcare, manufacturing, shipping, aerospace, and a growing tech sector. The population is culturally diverse, which creates opportunity for agents who can serve specific communities.

What works well here:

Life insurance across income levels

Medicare and final expense (large aging population)

Group benefits for mid-sized businesses

Community-specific markets — Hispanic, Asian, African, and other cultural communities

Challenges:

Weather-related property risks affect how some coverage is positioned

Traffic and geographic sprawl make in-person prospecting more time-consuming

Competitive in dense areas, less so in outlying suburbs and surrounding counties

Houston suits agents who want breadth — the ability to serve multiple demographics and product lines rather than specializing narrowly from day one.

## San Antonio: Military Presence and Working-Class Strength

San Antonio has a large military and veteran population thanks to Joint Base San Antonio and nearby installations. It also has a strong working-class and middle-income population, with a slower pace than Dallas or Houston.

What works well here:

Final expense and senior markets

Life insurance for working-class families

Veteran-focused insurance products

Annuities and retirement planning for middle-income clients

Challenges:

Lower average policy sizes than Dallas or Houston

USAA has a dominant presence for military clients, which creates competition for that niche

The overall market is smaller than Dallas or Houston

San Antonio suits agents who want a relational, community-based practice and are comfortable building a career on volume and loyalty rather than large individual premiums.

## Don't Forget the Other Texas Markets

Austin, El Paso, McAllen, Lubbock, Amarillo, Corpus Christi — Texas has many secondary markets that are often easier to break into than the big three. Less competition, tighter communities, and specific economic drivers (tech in Austin, border commerce in McAllen, agriculture in West Texas) create real opportunities for agents willing to serve those markets.

## What Matters Most Regardless of Market

Your success depends more on how you work than where you work. Consistent prospecting, genuine relationship building, and deep product knowledge matter in every Texas market. Location influences the mix of products and clients — but not whether you succeed.

## 5 Frequently Asked Questions

- Which Texas city has the highest-earning insurance agents? Dallas-Fort Worth tends to produce the highest individual agent incomes due to its wealth and corporate density, but top agents exist in every Texas market.
- Is it harder to start in a competitive market like Dallas? Competition is higher, yes — but so is the size of the opportunity. Starting in a competitive market forces better positioning and skill development, which pays off long-term.
- Should new agents start in smaller Texas markets? Smaller markets can be easier to penetrate and build quickly. Some agents use that as a launching point before expanding into larger markets later.
- Does Texas license work in all three of these cities? Yes. A Texas insurance license allows you to sell anywhere in the state. You can serve clients across Dallas, Houston, San Antonio, and everywhere in between.
- Which market is best for Medicare and final expense? San Antonio and Houston have large senior populations that make these markets strong. Dallas has opportunity too, though it's more competitive.
