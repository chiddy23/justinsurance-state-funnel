[Maryland insurance license](/maryland) eligibility is more than just a box to check. It is your first step toward a rewarding career in the insurance industry. Whether you plan to specialize in life and health or property and casualty, understanding the requirements set by the [Maryland Insurance Administration](/maryland/requirements) helps you prepare confidently. This guide walks you through each step so you can meet the standards and get closer to helping individuals and businesses protect what matters most.

## Understand your basic requirements

Before you even consider signing up for courses or testing, you need to confirm you meet the very first criteria: age, residency, and legal status. Maryland generally keeps these requirements straightforward so you do not get bogged down in red tape.

Most importantly, you must be at least 18 years old. At that age, you can legally sign contracts, which is crucial for working in insurance. Meanwhile, you also need to be a Maryland resident or plan to declare residency here. You will find some exceptions if you already hold a license in another state, but for brand-new applicants, in-state residency is typically mandatory.

It also helps to have a clear sense of purpose. If you know you want to help others choose the right coverage or guide them through complex claims, meeting any additional requirements may feel much more motivating. Maryland wants to ensure that individuals earning an insurance license are responsible, dedicated, and focused on following ethical practices.

## Check age and residency rules

You already know you must be 18, but take a moment to confirm your residency details. If you recently moved to Maryland, check that your ID and other documentation reflect your new address. Be prepared to show proof—like a driver’s license, voter registration, or utility bills—if requested.

Residency is not just for record-keeping. By showing you are truly based in Maryland, the state can expect you to understand the unique insurance laws and regulations that impact local consumers. This knowledge makes a real difference when helping Marylanders protect their homes, cars, and health.

## Complete your pre-licensing education

To qualify for the state exam, you need formal pre-licensing education tied to each line of authority you wish to pursue. This requirement ensures you have a deep understanding of insurance fundamentals, policy specifics, and state regulations. While course details vary by provider, you can typically expect these topics:

-   Maryland insurance laws and ethics
-   Policy types, terms, and definitions
-   Claims procedures and underwriting tools
-   Best practices for client consultations
-   Specific guidelines for your chosen line of authority (life and health or property and casualty)

You can often choose between classroom-based sessions or online courses that fit your schedule. Make sure to pick an approved provider so your education hours count toward the official requirement.

## Pass the mandatory insurance exams

After completing the required education hours, you are ready to tackle the Maryland insurance exams. Each line of authority has its own exam, so if you plan to offer multiple lines, you may need to pass more than one test. The exams cover core insurance concepts and Maryland-specific regulations, so thorough study is key.

The exam typically uses a multiple-choice format. You will face a strict time limit, which means it is important to manage your pacing. Scoring at or above the required passing mark qualifies you to move on to the next step: submitting your license application.

### Tips to boost your exam success

1.  Review all pre-licensing notes, especially legal requirements specific to Maryland.
2.  Take practice tests to identify areas you need to strengthen.
3.  Arrive early on exam day so you can focus without feeling rushed.
4.  Keep calm if you encounter tricky questions; mark them, then come back later.

Your diligence in understanding the material will pay off the moment you click the “submit” button and see a passing score.

## Prepare for a background check

When you apply for your Maryland insurance license, expect a criminal history review. The state requires this to ensure you can be trusted with sensitive financial and personal information. Many applicants worry that small violations or past errors in judgment will prevent them from obtaining a license. However, each situation is evaluated on its own facts, and not all incidents automatically disqualify you.

If you do have a record, be upfront and provide any documentation or explanations if requested. Honesty counts heavily. Maryland may ask for additional paperwork, and you might experience a longer processing time, but transparency often leads to a fair outcome.

## Common FAQ about your license

You may still have questions about Maryland insurance license eligibility. Here are five of the most common ones.

### 1\. Do I need to be a US citizen to get a Maryland insurance license?

You do not have to be a US citizen, but you generally need the legal right to work in the US. Providing valid documentation, such as a work permit, is usually the key step here.

### 2\. Can I hold more than one line of authority at once?

Yes. Many people hold multiple lines—like life and health, plus property and casualty—to serve a broader spectrum of clients. You will just need education and exams for each line.

### 3\. What if I fail the licensing exam on my first try?

Failures happen. If you do not pass, review where you struggled, schedule a retake, and study more intensely in those areas. Most aspiring agents pass within a few attempts, so stay motivated.

### 4\. How long does it take to get my license after passing the exam?

Processing times vary, but you can typically expect a few weeks after submitting all your paperwork and passing the background check. Keeping your documents organized helps speed up this process.

### 5\. Will a misdemeanor or DUI bar me from getting a license?

Not automatically. Maryland considers the details of any convictions. Disclosing your record and offering clear explanations or proof of rehabilitation can help demonstrate that you are eligible.
