North Carolina's insurance exam format is one of the most distinctive in the country — separate exams for each line of authority, shorter question counts than most states, and a two-ID requirement on test day. Understanding these distinctive features and building a strategic approach to multi-line licensing helps you pass efficiently and control costs. Whether you're pursuing Life only, Property and Casualty together, or all four major lines, your approach to the North Carolina exam format determines how fast and cost-effectively you get licensed.

Here's a detailed look at North Carolina insurance exam format and per-line strategy.

## The Fundamental Distinction: Per-Line Exams

Most states offer combined exam options:

Combined Life and Health (one exam covering both)

Combined Property and Casualty (one exam covering both)

North Carolina does neither. Every line requires its own separate exam:

Pursuing all four major lines in North Carolina = 4 separate exams = $180 in exam fees plus separate scheduling, preparation, and test days.

This per-line structure has significant implications for exam strategy.

## Exam Format Basics

All North Carolina major-line insurance exams share common format characteristics:

Computer-based. Taken at Pearson VUE testing centers, including select test centers on military installations.

Multiple choice. All questions are four-option multiple choice.

Two-section structure. General Knowledge and North Carolina Statutes are combined in one exam. The two parts are scored together, not individually — you need an overall passing score, not a passing score on each part.

Scaled score of 70 to pass. This is the detail candidates most often misunderstand. North Carolina reports a scaled score on a 0–100 scale. Per [Pearson VUE's NC candidate handbook](https://www.pearsonvue.com/content/dam/VUE/vue/en/documents/publications/123400.pdf), "the scaled score that is reported to you is neither the number of questions you answered correctly nor the percentage of questions you answered correctly." Scaling exists so that a slightly harder exam form and a slightly easier one represent the same standard, which means the raw number of correct answers needed to pass varies by form and is not published. Plan to know the material, not to budget a fixed number of misses.

[55 scored questions on the Life, Accident and Health or Sickness, Property, and Casualty exams — plus up to 5 unscored pretest questions](https://www.pearsonvue.com/content/dam/VUE/vue/en/documents/publications/123415.pdf) mixed in and not identified. Shorter than most states.

1 hour 15 minutes for each of those four exams.

Personal Lines is the exception. The Personal Lines Agent exam is also a major line of authority, but it runs [80 scored questions plus up to 16 pretest questions with 1 hour 45 minutes allotted](https://www.pearsonvue.com/content/dam/VUE/vue/en/documents/publications/123415.pdf). If you are testing Personal Lines, none of the 55-question pacing math below applies to you.

Multiple exam forms. Different question sets per candidate, same content outline.

Two forms of ID required. Primary must be government-issued with photo and signature; secondary must carry a valid signature. Distinctive to North Carolina among most states.

## Time per Question Analysis

Understanding pacing helps manage exam time. Pace against the total number of items in front of you, not the scored count — pretest questions are mixed in and not identified, so you will answer all of them.

Life, A&H, Property, Casualty — up to 60 items in 75 minutes:

Average time per item: ~75 seconds

Personal Lines Agent — up to 96 items in 105 minutes:

Average time per item: ~65 seconds

There is no "you can miss X" number to aim at. Because North Carolina reports a scaled score, the raw count needed to pass shifts with the exam form, so a margin-of-error strategy is not something you can plan around. Most prepared candidates finish with time remaining, and the shorter question count on the four single-line exams rewards candidates who trust their preparation and don't overthink.

Time management approach:

First pass: Answer all confident questions

Flag uncertain questions for review

Second pass: Address flagged questions with remaining time

Never leave questions unanswered — guess if needed

## Question Type Distribution

North Carolina exams use the same question types as other state exams:

Recall questions (~20-25%). Test factual knowledge directly.

"What is the grace period in a standard life insurance policy?"

Application questions (~40-50%). Test ability to apply knowledge to scenarios.

"A client's life insurance policy has lapsed. Which provision would allow her to reinstate it?"

Analysis questions (~20-25%). Test deeper conceptual understanding.

"Which of the following best describes the purpose of the incontestability clause?"

North Carolina-specific questions (roughly a quarter of the scored questions — about 24-27% on the four single-line exams, 30% on Personal Lines). Test knowledge of NC General Statutes Chapter 58 and NCDOI rules.

"Under North Carolina law, which of the following must the Commissioner of Insurance approve?"

Given only 55 scored questions on the single-line exams (80 on Personal Lines), each content area carries significant weight. Don't neglect any of them.

## What General vs. NC-Specific Means

General Knowledge (about 70-76%, depending on the exam): Insurance principles applicable in any state — products, provisions, concepts, and federal regulations.

NC Statutes and Regulations (about 24-30%, depending on the exam): North Carolina-specific insurance laws from G.S. 58, NCDOI regulations, and distinctive NC market rules. Per the [Pearson VUE North Carolina content outline (effective March 2, 2026)](https://www.pearsonvue.com/content/dam/VUE/vue/en/documents/publications/123415.pdf), the dedicated "North Carolina Statutes and Regulations" section is 14 of the 55 scored questions on Life and on Accident & Health (about 25%), 13 of 55 on Property (about 24%), 15 of 55 on Casualty (about 27%), and 24 of 80 on Personal Lines (30%).

Both sections appear in the same exam — you don't choose which to take. A single score across both sections determines pass or fail.

Practical implication: Even if you score 100% on general knowledge, poor performance on NC-specific questions alone can cause failure. NC-specific content requires dedicated study.

## The NC Rate Bureau Distinction

One of the most distinctive North Carolina-specific content areas: the North Carolina Rate Bureau.

Most states use ISO (Insurance Services Office) policy forms as the basis for property and casualty insurance products. North Carolina is one of a small number of states with its own Rate Bureau that files rates on behalf of member insurers.

What this means for Property and Casualty exams:

NC property exams reference NC Rate Bureau forms rather than standard ISO forms

Personal auto in NC uses the North Carolina Rate Bureau personal auto form rather than the ISO Personal Auto Policy

Homeowners policies reference NC-adapted forms ([HO-2011 program adapted by NC Rate Bureau](https://www.pearsonvue.com/content/dam/VUE/vue/en/documents/publications/123415.pdf))

Questions may specifically reference NC Rate Bureau forms and rules

If you've studied national materials only: You may have studied ISO forms and may not have covered NC Rate Bureau forms. This is a meaningful difference that can affect exam performance.

What to study: North Carolina Rate Bureau personal auto policy form provisions, NC-adapted homeowners forms, and how NC Rate Bureau functions as a collective ratemaking body.

## Multiple Exam Forms: What This Means

North Carolina uses multiple exam forms — different question versions across candidates. Even two candidates taking the same exam on the same day may see different questions.

Implications:

Your specific exam may be slightly easier or harder than a classmate's

Content outline coverage is consistent even if specific questions differ

Passing rate statistics reflect average across all forms

Practice exams approximate rather than exactly replicate your specific form

Preparation approach: Master the full content outline rather than memorizing specific questions. [Practice questions](/north-carolina/practice-exam) develop knowledge depth that transfers across forms.

## Recommended Exam Sequences

For Life and Health candidates:

Study Life content (2-3 weeks)

Take Life exam

Study A&H content (2-3 weeks, building on Life knowledge)

Take A&H exam

Alternative: Study both simultaneously (4-5 weeks), take both exams close together.

For Property and Casualty candidates:

Study Property content (2-3 weeks)

Take Property exam

Study Casualty content (2-3 weeks, building on Property knowledge)

Take Casualty exam

For all four lines:

Life → A&H → Property → Casualty (most common)

Property → Casualty → Life → A&H (alternative)

Spreading over 8-14 weeks with focused preparation per line is typical.

## Cancellation and Rescheduling Policy

[Cancel or change at least 48 hours in advance to avoid forfeiting the $45 exam fee](https://www.pearsonvue.com/content/dam/VUE/vue/en/documents/publications/123400.pdf).

Contact: Pearson VUE at (866) 265-6329 or (800) 274-0668.

No-show: Forfeit exam fee.

Failed attempt: Reschedule and pay another $45 per exam.

Planning implication: Don't schedule your exam until you're confident in preparation — each failed attempt costs $45.

## Where You Test: Pearson VUE Test Centers

North Carolina insurance exams are [delivered at Pearson VUE testing centers](https://www.pearsonvue.com/us/en/nc/insurance.html). The current [NC candidate handbook](https://www.pearsonvue.com/content/dam/VUE/vue/en/documents/publications/123400.pdf) does not document an at-home online-proctored option for these exams, so plan on an in-person appointment.

Physical locations throughout North Carolina

Also available at select Pearson VUE test centers on military installations, for service members, dependents, and contractors with authorized base access

Professional, controlled environment with a published test center policy — no personal items in the testing room, simple-function calculator permitted

Practical planning point: because there is no remote option to fall back on, build travel time into your appointment. Candidates who arrive late are treated as absent and forfeit the $45 fee. If Pearson VUE adds a remote-proctored option for North Carolina, it will appear on the NC insurance program page and in the candidate handbook — check there rather than assuming a delivery mode is available.

## The Two-ID Requirement in Practice

North Carolina's two-ID requirement on exam day:

Primary ID (required): Photo + signature on same document

Driver's license

Passport

Military ID

State-issued ID card

Secondary ID (required): Valid signature on the document

A debit (ATM) card or credit card

Or any second document from the primary ID list

Neither ID may be expired — [Pearson VUE does not recognize state renewal grace periods](https://www.pearsonvue.com/content/dam/VUE/vue/en/documents/publications/123400.pdf)

Do not arrive with only one form of ID. You'll be denied admission and forfeit your exam fee.

Names must match: Both IDs and your Pearson VUE registration should reflect your legal name exactly.

## 5 Frequently Asked Questions

- Why does North Carolina require separate exams for each line rather than combined exams? North Carolina's licensing structure requires separate lines of authority, and historically administered separate exams per line. The per-line exam structure allows candidates to license for only the lines they need rather than testing on all combined content.
- How do I decide whether to pursue Personal Lines or full Property and Casualty? Personal Lines is appropriate if you'll exclusively serve personal (non-commercial) clients with home and auto coverage. Full Property and Casualty is appropriate if you want to serve both personal and commercial clients, or if you anticipate expanding into commercial markets.
- What's the NC Rate Bureau and why does it matter for the exam? North Carolina's Rate Bureau files rates on behalf of member insurers and creates NC-specific policy forms for auto and homeowners insurance. NC exams reference NC Rate Bureau forms rather than standard ISO forms, making NC-specific study important for Property and Casualty candidates.
- How does failing one exam affect my other exam appointments? Each exam is independent. Failing the Life exam doesn't affect your A&H exam appointment. You can retake failed exams while continuing to take other scheduled exams.
- Are there question banks that match North Carolina's exam exactly? No. Pearson VUE uses multiple exam forms with different question sets. Quality [prelicense](/north-carolina/prelicensing) courses provide practice questions that cover the same content outline, training your knowledge to perform across any form you encounter.
