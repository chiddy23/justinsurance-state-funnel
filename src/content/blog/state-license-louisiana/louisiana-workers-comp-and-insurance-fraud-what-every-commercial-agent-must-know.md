If you're a commercial lines agent in Louisiana — or you're preparing for your LDI Property & Casualty exam — workers' compensation and insurance fraud are two areas where incomplete knowledge creates real professional and legal risk. Louisiana's workers' comp framework is built around a 1-employee coverage threshold, a unique state-chartered carrier in LWCC, and an administration structure that routes through the Louisiana Workforce Commission rather than the LDI. Insurance fraud enforcement is active and the consequences are serious. Here is what you need to know before you write your first commercial policy.

## Louisiana Workers' Compensation: The Framework

Louisiana workers' compensation law is governed by the Louisiana Workers' Compensation Act (RS 23:1021 et seq.) and administered by the Louisiana Workforce Commission (LWC) through its Office of Workers' Compensation Administration (OWCA). A critical distinction for commercial lines producers: workers' comp regulatory inquiries and claims administration go to the LWC/OWCA, not the LDI. The LDI regulates the insurance aspects of workers' comp — policy forms, rates, and carrier licensing — while the OWCA handles claims administration and employer compliance. Understanding which agency handles what prevents confusion for both you and your commercial clients.

NCCI (National Council on Compensation Insurance) is the rating bureau for Louisiana workers' compensation. NCCI calculates advisory loss costs that form the basis for insurer rate filings and administers the experience modification factor system. Louisiana workers' comp rates have generally trended downward in recent years — the LDI approved a 9% overall rate reduction for 2024, and rates have fallen cumulatively by approximately 49% over the prior 10 years.

## Coverage Threshold: 1 or More Employees

Louisiana requires employers with one or more employees to carry workers' compensation insurance. This is the same 1-employee threshold as Kentucky and Indiana — a strict standard that covers essentially all employment relationships except for specifically enumerated exemptions.

Exemptions include certain agricultural laborers, domestic servants in private homes, employees of employers who elect to be excluded under specific conditions, and others enumerated in the statute. For commercial clients in standard industries — construction, manufacturing, retail, healthcare, hospitality, logistics — the 1-employee rule applies without exception. A sole proprietor who hires a single part-time employee is legally required to have coverage in place before that employee's first workday.

## Louisiana Workers' Compensation Corporation (LWCC)

The Louisiana Workers' Compensation Corporation (LWCC) is a state-chartered private nonprofit mutual insurance company and the largest workers' compensation carrier in Louisiana. LWCC was created by the Louisiana legislature in 1992 to stabilize the workers' compensation marketplace. It is not a state fund in the traditional sense — it is a private carrier with a public mission that competes in the open market alongside private insurers.

Travelers serves as the residual market/assigned risk carrier for Louisiana workers' compensation — the market of last resort for employers who cannot obtain coverage in the standard private market.

For commercial producers, familiarity with both LWCC and the private carrier market is important. LWCC is often competitive for small to mid-sized Louisiana businesses, but comparing LWCC's offerings against private market alternatives is good practice for every commercial client.

## Benefits and Exclusive Remedy

Louisiana workers' compensation provides: medical benefits (all reasonably necessary treatment at no cost to the employee), temporary total disability (TTD) at 66 2/3% of average weekly wage with a maximum of $845 per week (September 1, 2024 through August 31, 2025), permanent disability benefits, and death benefits. The exclusive remedy doctrine under the Louisiana Workers' Compensation Act bars employees covered by workers' comp from suing their employer in civil court for work-related injuries covered by the system, with narrow exceptions for intentional employer conduct.

Louisiana's energy sector creates a distinctive workers' comp consideration: offshore workers may fall under federal maritime laws — the Jones Act and the Longshore and Harbor Workers' Compensation Act — rather than state workers' comp. Commercial producers serving oil and gas, maritime, or offshore industry clients need to understand this distinction and ensure clients have the appropriate coverage.

## Insurance Fraud: Louisiana Law and Enforcement

Insurance fraud in Louisiana is a criminal offense under Title 22. Fraud includes presenting false information in an insurance application, filing a false claim, misrepresenting material facts to an insurer, and staging accidents or injuries. Workers' compensation fraud is a specific enforcement priority — both employer fraud (misclassifying workers or underreporting payroll to reduce premiums) and employee fraud (exaggerating or fabricating injuries) are actively investigated.

Producers who knowingly participate in fraudulent transactions — submitting false applications, misrepresenting coverage terms, or facilitating premium fraud schemes — face license revocation on top of criminal liability. Producers also have an affirmative professional duty to report known or suspected fraud. Report to the LDI Fraud Division through ldi.la.gov or the LDI fraud hotline. Also follow your appointing insurer's internal special investigative unit (SIU) reporting procedures.

Louisiana's large storm and hurricane exposure creates a specific fraud pattern worth noting: post-storm fraud schemes — staged accident claims, inflated damage claims, and contractor fraud following hurricane events — are a recurring enforcement priority for both the LDI and state law enforcement. Producers who are aware of suspicious claim patterns following storm events have both a professional and legal obligation to report them.

## Frequently Asked Questions

- Which agency administers workers' compensation in Louisiana — the LDI or the LWC? The Louisiana Workforce Commission (LWC), through its Office of Workers' Compensation Administration (OWCA), administers workers' compensation claims and employer compliance. The LDI regulates the insurance aspects — policy forms, rates, and carrier licensing. Workers' comp regulatory and claims questions for commercial clients should be directed to the LWC/OWCA, not the LDI.
- What is LWCC and how does it differ from a standard WC insurer? LWCC (Louisiana Workers' Compensation Corporation) is a state-chartered private nonprofit mutual insurance company — the largest WC carrier in Louisiana. Created by the legislature in 1992 to stabilize the WC marketplace, it competes in the open market alongside private insurers. It is not a traditional state fund but has a public mission of ensuring coverage availability for Louisiana employers. Travelers serves as the residual/assigned risk carrier.
- How does Louisiana's offshore and maritime industry affect workers' compensation? Offshore workers in Louisiana's oil and gas, maritime, and shipping sectors may be covered by federal maritime law — the Jones Act or the Longshore and Harbor Workers' Compensation Act — rather than state workers' comp. Commercial producers serving energy, offshore, or maritime industry clients must understand which coverage framework applies and ensure clients have the appropriate federal or state coverage.
- What are the penalties for an employer operating without workers' compensation coverage in Louisiana? Penalties for non-compliance include stop-work orders, significant civil fines, and personal liability for all workers' compensation benefits owed to injured employees during the period of non-coverage. Contact the LWC OWCA for current penalty schedules. Louisiana's penalties, while serious, are less severe than Kentucky's Class D felony standard for WC non-compliance.
- How do I report suspected insurance fraud in Louisiana? Report to the LDI Fraud Division through ldi.la.gov or by calling the LDI. Also follow your appointing insurer's SIU reporting procedures. Reports can often be made anonymously. Producers who suspect fraud related to post-storm claims should be particularly attentive — Louisiana's hurricane and storm exposure makes this a recurring fraud enforcement area.
