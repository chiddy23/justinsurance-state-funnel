If you’re working toward your Minnesota insurance license continuing education, it’s essential to stay organized and informed. The state’s requirements can feel overwhelming at first, especially if you’re juggling multiple responsibilities. But by breaking everything down into manageable steps, you can complete your continuing education more efficiently and advance your career with confidence.

## Understand Minnesota continuing education requirements

Every two years, Minnesota expects you to renew your insurance license and show proof of continuing education (CE) credits. Typically, you need 24 total hours of approved coursework for each two-year term. Of those 24 hours, at least 3 must focus on ethics, ensuring you stay up-to-date with professional standards and best practices.

The remaining hours depend on your lines of authority. Whether you’re selling life and health insurance, property and casualty coverage, or both, your CE should cover relevant topics to help you serve clients’ needs more effectively. Although the required amount may sound high, the variety of course options available—classroom, self-study, and online modules—lets you work at your own pace.

## Align your CE with your license type

When choosing your courses, pay close attention to your specific license type. If you hold a life and health license, prioritize topics such as annuities, health policy updates, or regulatory changes impacting claims. If you’re in property and casualty, you might explore subjects like homeowners policy nuances or updates in commercial liability coverage.

You can further refine your CE plan by factoring in areas you’d like to strengthen. Maybe you’ve always wanted to understand long-term care insurance more thoroughly, or you’ve had questions about recent legislation affecting auto coverage. Incorporating relevant courses keeps you in touch with the latest industry developments and makes your license more valuable to clients and employers alike.

## Strategies to stay on track

Completing your continuing education isn’t just about checking boxes. It’s also about maintaining a steady workflow and ensuring you never miss renewal deadlines. Try these strategies to keep everything running smoothly:

1.  Schedule in advance. Mark your license renewal date on your calendar, then work backward to decide when to start your CE courses.
2.  Mix up your learning formats. Alternate between online, classroom, and self-study offerings if available. Experimenting with different course types can make studying more engaging and less stressful.
3.  Organize your documentation. Keep proof of completion for each course in an easy-to-find folder, whether physical or virtual. You’ll need this information when you report your credits.
4.  Use provider support. Many providers, like JustInsurance, offer reminders and progress tracking so you know exactly how many credits you have left to earn.

Staying ahead of schedule also gives you time to handle any unexpected obstacles. If you wait until the last minute, you risk technical glitches, course availability issues, or just not having enough hours in the day. Spreading out your CE over a few months makes the entire process far less stressful.

## Frequently asked questions

### 1\. How often do I need to renew my Minnesota insurance license?

You must renew your license every two years. This involves completing 24 hours of CE, including 3 hours in ethics, and submitting your application and fees by the renewal deadline.

### 2\. What happens if I miss my CE deadline?

Missing the deadline could result in late fees or even the temporary loss of your license. If you let too much time pass, you might need to start the licensing process over. Always keep a close eye on your renewal dates and plan accordingly.

### 3\. Do I have to take specific ethics courses?

Yes. Minnesota requires 3 hours of ethics-focused coursework for each renewal period. Be sure to choose an approved provider offering courses that fulfill this requirement.

### 4\. Can I earn CE credits from online courses?

Absolutely. Online learning is a popular option, especially if you are balancing a full-time job. Online courses let you progress at your own pace, often with flexible modules and quizzes to track your understanding.
