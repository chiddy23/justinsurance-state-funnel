North Carolina's CE provider approval and administration system is more distinctive than most states. While many states use Sircon or a state agency to manage CE, North Carolina uses Prometric as its CE administrator — handling provider approval, course approval, roster processing, and compliance calculations. Understanding Prometric's role, how to find approved courses, and what separates quality providers from the rest helps you complete CE efficiently without compliance surprises.

Here's how to find NCDOI-approved CE providers in North Carolina.

## Why Provider Approval Matters

Only courses from NCDOI-approved providers count toward your North Carolina CE requirement. The approval process ensures:

Course content meets North Carolina standards

Providers comply with NCDOI reporting requirements through Prometric

Materials are current and accurate

Course delivery meets quality standards

A course from an unapproved provider — however excellent the content — won't count toward your CE requirement.

## Prometric's Role in North Carolina CE

[Prometric serves as NCDOI's CE administrator](https://www.ncdoi.gov/licensees/insurance-producer-and-adjuster-licensing/continuing-education-insurance-producers-and-adjusters). This is North Carolina's distinctive arrangement — Prometric (not Sircon, not NCDOI directly) handles all CE services:

What Prometric does:

Approves CE providers

Approves individual CE courses

Processes course completion rosters from providers

Calculates licensee CE compliance

Maintains CE transcripts

Reports compliance information to NCDOI

What Sircon does (separate from Prometric):

Processes license transactions and records — [producer licenses are perpetual and require no renewal application](https://www.ncdoi.gov/licensees/insurance-producer-and-adjuster-licensing); only the classes that actually renew (adjuster, public adjuster, surplus lines) file renewals through the state's licensing system

Maintains producer license records

How to check your CE record: Through Prometric's licensee portal or through a Sircon transcript inquiry.

Practical implication: When a CE provider says they "report to the state," they're reporting through Prometric's system. Courses not set up through Prometric's provider approval system won't appear in your compliance record.

## Where to Find Approved Providers

Primary sources:

NCDOI Website (ncdoi.gov): The NCDOI website provides information on CE requirements and links to approved provider resources. Always verify current provider approval through NCDOI.

Prometric's Website: As CE administrator, Prometric maintains records of approved providers and courses. Licensees can search for courses and view CE records through Prometric's portal.

Provider Direct Websites: Most established CE providers prominently display their NCDOI/Prometric approval and can provide course approval information directly.

CE Marketplace Platforms: Some platforms aggregate CE courses from multiple providers, allowing comparison shopping.

## Key Information to Verify Before Enrolling

NCDOI/Prometric approval. Course must be specifically North Carolina-approved.

Course type designation. Verify whether the course satisfies:

General CE (any approved topic)

Ethics (3-hour mandatory requirement)

Flood Insurance (3-hour requirement for P&C/Personal Lines selling NFIP flood; first period and every 4 years)

LTC Partnership initial training (8-hour one-time)

LTC Partnership ongoing training (4-hour every 2-year period)

Annuity Best Interest (4-hour one-time)

Hour count. Confirm credit hours match what you need.

Reporting reliability. Provider should report completions promptly through Prometric's system.

State reporting fee. $2.05 per credit hour — should be transparently disclosed at purchase.

Course format. Online self-study, webinar, classroom — confirm format fits your schedule.

Customer support. Access to help when issues arise.

## Course Format Options

North Carolina accepts CE in multiple formats:

Online self-study. Most popular. Self-paced. No proctor/monitor required for final exams — North Carolina eliminated the monitor requirement for online courses, making this the most convenient format.

Webinars. Live or recorded with attendance verification.

Classroom instruction. In-person at provider locations.

Correspondence courses. Book-based self-study — these DO require a disinterested third-party monitor for final exams. Much less common than online options.

No minimum classroom or webinar requirement. All 24 hours can be completed online.

## No Monitor Required: North Carolina's Convenient Approach

North Carolina's elimination of the proctor/monitor requirement for online CE final exams is a significant convenience:

Complete courses at home, office, or anywhere

No scheduling a proctor

No third-party monitoring fees

Same credit as in-person courses

This is not universal among states — many states require proctored final exams for online CE. North Carolina's approach makes compliance significantly more convenient.

## Pricing and Fee Structure

North Carolina CE pricing varies by provider and format:

Individual courses typically range from $15-$30 per CE hour.

24-hour CE packages covering full requirements often cost $100-$250 total (including reporting fees).

Specialty training pricing:

3-hour Ethics: typically $30-$80

3-hour Flood Insurance: typically $30-$80

8-hour LTC initial: typically $50-$150

4-hour LTC ongoing: typically $25-$80

4-hour Annuity Best Interest: typically $30-$100

State reporting fee: $2.05 per credit hour, added at purchase. Full 24-hour package adds ~$49.20 in reporting fees.

Bundle discounts are common for full CE packages. Comparing total price (including reporting fees) across providers is recommended.

## What Information to Provide Your CE Provider

When enrolling in North Carolina CE:

Your National Producer Number (NPN). This is how providers report your completion to Prometric. Incorrect NPN = completion reported to wrong record or not reported at all.

Your North Carolina license number. Found on your license.

Your legal name as it appears on your license. Spelling variations cause reporting failures.

Contact information. Email for course materials and certificate delivery.

Double-check your NPN before enrolling — it's the most critical field.

## Reporting Timeline

After completing a NC CE course:

Provider reporting: Prometric requires timely reporting. Quality providers report completions within 1-2 business days. Some providers report the next business day.

Prometric processing: Typically a few days after provider reports.

Total expectation: Allow 5-10 business days for credits to appear in your Prometric/Sircon record.

Verify 30+ days before compliance deadline. Don't complete CE the week before your birth month deadline.

## What If a Course Doesn't Report Correctly

If CE hours don't appear after 10 business days:

Step 1: Contact your CE provider. Most reporting issues resolve at provider level.

Step 2: Confirm your NPN was entered correctly. Wrong NPN is the #1 cause of reporting failures.

Step 3: Check certificate. Keep your course completion certificate as backup documentation.

Step 4: Contact Prometric.** As CE administrator, Prometric is the authority on CE records.

Step 5: Contact NCDOI Agent Services** if issues persist: (919) 807-6800.

## Checking Your CE Compliance Status

Verify CE compliance through:

Prometric's licensee portal. Primary CE record source.

Sircon transcript inquiry. Also provides access to CE records.

NCDOI's Check a License service. ncdoi.gov provides license status verification.

Check your compliance status periodically throughout your 2-year period — catching a reporting problem 6 months early is far better than catching it 2 weeks before your deadline.

## 5 Frequently Asked Questions

- Who administers North Carolina CE and where do I check my record? Prometric is NCDOI's CE administrator — they approve providers and courses, process completions, and maintain compliance records. You can check your CE record through Prometric's portal or through Sircon's transcript inquiry function.
- How quickly should CE hours appear in my record? Quality providers report within 1-2 business days. Allow 5-10 business days for credits to appear in your Prometric/Sircon record. Verify well before your compliance deadline.
- Do North Carolina CE final exams require a proctor or monitor? No — for online self-study courses. North Carolina eliminated the monitor requirement for online CE final exams. Correspondence courses still require a disinterested third-party monitor.
- What's the state reporting fee for North Carolina CE? $2.05 per credit hour, added at course purchase. For a full 24-hour requirement, this totals approximately $49.20 in reporting fees beyond the course price.
- Can I take all 24 CE hours online in North Carolina? Yes. North Carolina has no minimum classroom or webinar requirement — all 24 hours can be completed through online self-study courses. No proctor required for online final exams.
