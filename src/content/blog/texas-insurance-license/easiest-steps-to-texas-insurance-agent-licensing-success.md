If you’re planning to become an insurance agent in the Lone Star State, understanding the Texas insurance agent licensing process is your first big milestone. Earning your license can feel intimidating at first, but with the right approach, you’ll move through each requirement more smoothly than you might expect. In fact, you could be on your way to helping individuals, families, and businesses with their insurance needs in less time than you ever thought possible.

Following a structured plan is the best way to tackle each stage, from choosing what type of license you want to obtaining specialized study materials. Below, you’ll find an easy-to-follow breakdown that explains every major step and prepares you for a successful licensing journey.

## Know the Texas insurance requirements

You can only apply for a [Texas insurance license](/texas) if you meet the state prerequisites, so it’s wise to confirm your eligibility upfront. Generally, you must be at least 18 years old and meet the [Texas Department of Insurance](/texas/requirements)’s (TDI) standards, which include submitting to fingerprinting and a background check. These checks verify that you haven’t committed any disqualifying offenses that might keep you from working with sensitive client information.

You’ll also want to decide early on if you aim to focus on more than one line of authority (such as Life & Health or Property & Casualty). Having a clear sense of your goals will guide you toward specialized courses and help you plan for multiple exams, if needed. Some future agents choose to start with one license type and add new lines at a later date.

## Choose your license focus

To figure out which license suits you best, consider the types of clients you plan to serve. If you’re interested in healthcare coverage, a Life & Health license is likely the right path. On the other hand, if you want to offer homeowners and auto policies, go for a Property & Casualty license. Each license requires its own exam and corresponds to a unique set of regulations under the TDI.

Some new agents also explore personal lines insurance, which focuses on policies for individuals rather than businesses. Comparing the pros and cons of each type will help you connect with your ideal clients and ensure that your career interests and license align. By choosing the right path from the start, you’ll maximize your focus and be able to offer reliable guidance.

## Consider pre-licensing education (optional)

Texas does not require you to take a [Texas pre-licensing course](/texas/prelicensing) before you sit for the official exam — it's optional, though recommended. These courses are designed to familiarize you with critical insurance topics, including ethics, state regulations, contract structure, and more. If you choose one, the number of hours varies depending on the license you seek, so it’s worth confirming what each provider offers.

Even though it's not mandatory, solid coursework gives you a better foundation for advising future clients. Many individuals find that working with a company like JustInsurance can help streamline course selection, ensuring you’re fully prepared for the exam. The more thorough you are with your studies now, the more confident you’ll feel when it’s time to test.

## Prepare for the exam

Your next step is to schedule and prepare for the licensing exam. If you took a pre-licensing course, you should already be comfortable with the core insurance concepts it covered. However, most people still benefit from supplemental study techniques and practice tests to sharpen their knowledge. Reviewing sample questions helps you feel at ease with the exam’s format, which includes multiple-choice questions that span various insurance scenarios.

On exam day, being calm and organized can significantly improve your performance. To minimize stress, keep these tips in mind:

-   Bring two forms of valid ID to the test center.
-   Arrive at least 30 minutes early, giving yourself extra time in case of traffic or check-in slowdowns.
-   Wear comfortable clothing so you’re not distracted by discomfort.
-   Scan the test instructions thoroughly and budget your time so you can work through each section at a steady pace.

Once you finish the exam, you’ll typically receive your results immediately. If you pass, congratulations — you’re one step closer to helping Texas residents with their insurance needs.

## Apply for your license

After passing the exam, you’re ready to submit your license application and pay the required fees. At this point, you should already have completed fingerprinting and a background check, both of which are necessary for approval. Typically, you’ll submit your application and documentation through the designated online portal. Be sure that you fill out every field accurately, since any missing or incorrect information can delay processing.

Once your application is approved, you’ll receive confirmation from the TDI. You may see your official license status posted online, and a physical license might be mailed to you if you opted for that service. From there, you’re officially authorized to solicit and write insurance in the state of Texas. It’s an exciting moment and marks the beginning of your new professional journey.

## Maintain ongoing compliance

Getting your license is only the first part of being a conscientious insurance professional. To continue serving clients legally, you’ll need to stay on top of Texas continuation and renewal rules. Most lines of insurance require ongoing [continuing education](/texas/continuing-education) (CE) to ensure your knowledge remains current. CE courses focus on areas like policy changes, industry updates, and ethics compliance.

It’s easy to meet CE requirements by consulting approved providers and scheduling classes that align with your work calendar. Keeping up with these courses — and any new regulations the TDI sets forth — not only protects your license status but also boosts your reputation among clients. Remember that you want to be the go-to source for reliable insurance guidance, and continual learning is an excellent way to demonstrate your expertise.

Once you’ve completed your CE credits and submitted renewal fees on time, your license remains active without interruption. It’s a fairly simple process, as long as you’re mindful of the deadlines and thoroughly track your progress every renewal cycle.
