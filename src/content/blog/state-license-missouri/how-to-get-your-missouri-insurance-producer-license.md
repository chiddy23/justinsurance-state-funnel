Missouri's insurance licensing process has several features that make it one of the most accessible in the comparison states. The Missouri Department of Commerce and Insurance (DCI) — also called the Department of Insurance, Financial Institutions & Professional Registration (DIFP) — does not require [prelicensing](/missouri/prelicensing) education, making Missouri join Maryland (eliminated PLE in October 2024) and Virginia among states where the Pearson VUE exam is the first mandatory step. There is no fingerprinting requirement. The exam fee of $32 (single line) to $40 (combined) is the lowest of all comparison states. And the 16-hour CE requirement (vs. 24 hours in most comparison states) means ongoing license maintenance is less burdensome. Missouri's location at the geographic center of the United States — with Edward Jones (world's largest independent broker-dealer by advisor count) headquartered in St. Louis, Centene Corporation (Fortune 500 managed care), Boeing Defense, Cerner/Oracle Health in Kansas City, and Anheuser-Busch InBev in St. Louis — creates a distinctive professional insurance advisory market. Here's the complete guide.

## Who Regulates Missouri Insurance

Missouri Department of Commerce and Insurance (DCI):

Mailing address: PO Box 4001, Jefferson City, MO 65102

Website: insurance.mo.gov

Insurance Code: Missouri Revised Statutes (RSMo), Chapters 375/379 (insurance)

DCI is also known as the Department of Insurance, Financial Institutions & Professional Registration (DIFP)

DCI's distinctive multi-sector mandate: Like Minnesota's DOC, Missouri's DCI regulates not just insurance but also financial institutions, professional registration (physicians, architects, CPAs, bail bond agents, tattoo artists, and many others), and bank/credit union chartering. This multi-sector mandate makes Missouri's regulator broader than a standalone insurance department.

## Step 1: Prepare for the Pearson VUE Exam (No PLE Required)

Missouri does not require prelicensing education. Candidates can schedule the Pearson VUE exam immediately without completing any DCI-approved prelicensing course.

## Step 2: Schedule and Pass the Pearson VUE Exam

Exam administrator: Pearson VUE

Website: pearsonvue.com/us/en/mo/insurance.html

Phone: (866) 247-4740

Effective May 7, 2025: Remote online exams (OnVUE) are NO LONGER available for Missouri — all exams must be taken in-person at Pearson VUE test centers

Schedule at least 24 hours in advance (electronic check payments require 5 days)

Exam fee: $32 single-line / $40 combined (lowest of all comparison states) Passing score: a scaled score of 70 (the exam covers a national/general portion and a Missouri state-specific portion, reported as one combined score) Score validity: 1 year Results: Immediate pass/fail; score report available in Pearson VUE account

Retakes: Unlimited; 24-hour wait before rescheduling; you retake the full exam.

Cancel/reschedule: At least 48 hours in advance or forfeit fee.

Exam content — what's tested: Missouri's exam covers both national/general insurance content and Missouri-specific laws, rules, and regulations. Like most states (such as Maryland and Indiana), these are combined into a single scaled score — you need an overall 70 to pass, and the national and state portions are not scored or passed separately.

Missouri Exam Specifications:

Exam day: Arrive 30 minutes early; two government-issued IDs (one with photo); no personal items in testing room.

## Step 3: Submit License Application

No fingerprinting required in Missouri — one of the most applicant-friendly features.

After passing the Pearson VUE exam:

Wait 24-48 hours after passing (to allow results to load in DCI system), then apply through NIPR:

Application fee: $100

NIPR transaction fee: $5.60

Paper alternative: Download Uniform Application form → mail with $100 check to DCI PO Box 4001, Jefferson City, MO 65102.

Processing: 5-10 business days.

Fee waiver: Certain low-income individuals and veterans can apply for a License Fee Waiver with DCI.

Temporary license: Missouri does issue temporary producer licenses, but not as a bridge for new candidates. Under RSMo § 375.025 the DCI director may grant a temporary license for up to 90 days, without an exam, to keep an existing book of business serviced — for example to the surviving spouse or court-appointed personal representative of a producer who dies or becomes disabled, to a member or employee of a licensed business entity after the individual designated on its license dies or becomes disabled, or to the designee of a producer entering active service in the Armed Forces. A first-time applicant has no temporary option and cannot sell until the regular license is issued.

## Step 4: Maintain Your License

Renewal: every 2 years on your birth date (the birthday itself, not month-end). CE: 16 hours every 2 years (3 Ethics) — the lowest CE requirement of all comparison states (most require 24 hours).

## 5 Frequently Asked Questions

- How is Missouri's insurance exam structured? Missouri's Pearson VUE exam covers both national/general insurance content and Missouri-specific laws, rules, and regulations. It is reported as a single combined scaled score from 0 to 100, and you need an overall 70 to pass — the national and state portions are not scored or passed separately. If you fail, you wait 24 hours and retake the full exam. Either way, it rewards candidates who prepare specifically for Missouri state law, not just national insurance content.
- Why are Missouri's exam fees so much lower than other states? Missouri's Pearson VUE exam fees ($32-$40) are the lowest of all comparison states — MD charges $60; IN charges $69; MN charges $45. This reflects Missouri's deliberately accessible licensing structure. The $32 individual-line exam fee and $40 combined exam fee create minimal financial barrier to entry.
- What happened to remote exams in Missouri? Effective May 7, 2025, Pearson VUE discontinued remote online exam delivery for Missouri. All Missouri insurance licensing exams must now be taken in-person at a Pearson VUE test center. Candidates who were hoping to test from home must schedule an in-person appointment. This is a significant recent change from the prior OnVUE availability.
- Why does Missouri have a $100 application fee when Indiana charges $40? Missouri's $100 application fee — regardless of lines of authority — is one of the highest per-application fees among comparison states. The fee is the same whether applying for one line or multiple lines on the same application. Indiana charges $40 per line; Minnesota charges $50 per line + $20 technology; Maryland charges $54. Missouri's flat $100 means multiple-line candidates pay the same as single-line candidates — a relative advantage for full-market agents.
- Can I get a temporary Missouri license while I wait for my producer license? Not as a new applicant. Missouri does have a temporary producer license — RSMo § 375.025 lets the DCI director issue one for up to 90 days without an exam — but it exists to keep an existing book of business serviced, not to bridge first-time candidates. It goes to people such as the surviving spouse or court-appointed personal representative of a producer who dies or becomes disabled, a member or employee of a licensed business entity after the designated individual dies or becomes disabled, or the designee of a producer entering active military service. So a new candidate has no temporary option: at 5-10 business day processing, the fastest path from passing the exam to selling is approximately 2 weeks — fast, but without the new-applicant bridge that states like Indiana (180 days), Minnesota (180 days), and Maryland (15-month Life) provide.
