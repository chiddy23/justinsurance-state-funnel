If there's one section of the Pennsylvania insurance exam that catches unprepared candidates off guard, it's the Pennsylvania-specific law portion. General insurance concepts transfer across states — but Pennsylvania has unique laws, distinctive regulatory programs (notably Pennie, the state-based health insurance exchange established in 2020), and producer requirements that the exam tests in detail.

Here are the Pennsylvania-specific insurance laws you need to know for the state exam.

## Pennsylvania's Insurance Industry Heritage

Before diving into specific laws, understand the context: Pennsylvania has been a significant insurance market since the early days of American insurance. The state hosts substantial insurance industry presence in Philadelphia (with major healthcare insurance companies), Pittsburgh, and across the state. Pennsylvania's insurance regulation reflects mature, sophisticated approaches developed over many decades.

This heritage means Pennsylvania takes insurance regulation seriously. The exam reflects this — Pennsylvania content is detailed and substantive.

## The Pennsylvania Insurance Code

Pennsylvania insurance law is found primarily in:

Pennsylvania Statutes Title 40 (40 P.S.) — comprehensive insurance code

Pennsylvania Insurance Department Regulations — administrative rules implementing the Code

Department Bulletins and Notices — guidance from the Insurance Department

Key sections include:

40 P.S. §233 et seq. — producer licensing provisions

40 Pa. Stat. §310.8 — [continuing education](/pennsylvania/continuing-education) requirements

Various other sections affecting specific topics

The Code is administered by the Pennsylvania Insurance Department, led by the Insurance Commissioner.

## Recent Major Statutory Change: Act 142 of 2024

A central recent change for the exam:

Pennsylvania Act 142 of 2024, effective April 29, 2025, eliminated the [prelicense](/pennsylvania/prelicensing) education (PLE) requirement for Pennsylvania insurance producer licensure. This is a major recent change that may appear on exam questions. Key facts:

PLE no longer required for new producer applicants

Pennsylvania remains a state that requires the licensing exam

Recent regulatory modernization reflecting other states' approaches

Producers can now go directly from study to exam to application

## Key Sections Producers Should Know

Pennsylvania Insurance Code (Title 40). Foundation provisions covering producer qualifications, licensing requirements, examinations, and standards of conduct.

Producer Licensing Provisions. Specific licensing rules for agents, brokers, and consultants in Pennsylvania.

Continuing Education (40 Pa. Stat. §310.8). Pennsylvania requires 24 hours every 2 years (3 hours Ethics + 2 hours Flood for P&C/Personal Lines).

Unfair Methods of Competition and Unfair Practices. Pennsylvania prohibits specific conduct considered unfair or deceptive — misrepresentation, twisting, churning, rebating, defamation, and more.

Replacement of Life Insurance and Annuities. Specific rules govern the replacement of existing life insurance and annuity contracts.

Unfair Claims Settlement Practices. Rules governing how claims must be handled by insurers and adjusters.

Free-Look Periods. Pennsylvania requires free-look periods on life insurance and annuity contracts.

Long-Term Care Insurance Standards. Specific rules for LTC sales, including training requirements.

Annuity Suitability Standards. Pennsylvania requires Best Interest standards for annuity sales.

Privacy and Information Protection. How you collect, store, and share client information is regulated under Pennsylvania law and federal frameworks like HIPAA and GLBA.

Pennie. Pennsylvania's state-based health insurance exchange (established 2020).

## Pennsylvania Licensing Requirements

Exam questions test understanding of Pennsylvania licensing rules:

Age requirement: 18+

Residency: Pennsylvania resident required for resident license

Prelicense education: NOT required (eliminated by Act 142 of 2024 effective April 29, 2025)

Examination: Required for most license types

Background check: Required (with fingerprinting through IdentoGO using service code 1KGBGJ)

Application fee: $55 resident / $110 non-resident

Continuing education: 24 hours every 2 years

Renewal cycle: Biennial

Know the factual details — specific hour requirements, what's required, and what triggers exemptions.

## Pennsylvania Producer Conduct Standards

Pennsylvania law establishes specific standards for producer conduct:

Honest representation. Producers must accurately represent products, premiums, benefits, and other material aspects.

Suitability. Recommendations must fit client needs, particularly for annuities and long-term care.

Disclosure. Required disclosures must be made completely and accurately.

Documentation. Records of transactions must be maintained per Pennsylvania requirements.

Cooperation with the Department. Producers must cooperate with Department investigations.

Notification of changes. Pennsylvania requires notification of address changes, employer changes, name changes, and other material changes.

## Pennsylvania Unfair Trade Practices

Pennsylvania prohibits specific conduct as unfair or deceptive:

Misrepresentation. False statements about policies, benefits, premiums, dividends, or insurer financial condition.

Twisting. Using misrepresentation to induce a replacement.

Churning. Repeatedly replacing a client's own policies for commissions without benefit.

Rebating. Offering anything of material value outside policy terms as a purchase inducement (narrow exceptions apply).

Defamation. False, malicious statements about competitors.

Boycott, coercion, and intimidation. Anti-competitive conduct.

False financial statements. Misrepresenting an insurer's financial strength.

Unfair discrimination. Using prohibited factors in underwriting.

Know each practice by name. Exam questions often present scenarios and ask which unfair practice is described.

## Pennsylvania Replacement Rules

Pennsylvania has specific rules for replacing life insurance and annuity contracts:

Notice Regarding Replacement. Must be provided to applicants and signed.

Statement of existing coverage. Applicants must disclose existing policies.

Comparison information. Producers must provide comparison between existing and new policies.

Existing insurer notification. The new insurer must notify the existing insurer of the pending replacement.

Conservation period. The existing insurer has an opportunity to retain the business.

Sales material retention. All materials used in the sale must be retained.

Expect multiple exam questions on what triggers replacement rules and what the producer must do.

## Pennie — Pennsylvania's State-Based Health Insurance Exchange

In 2020, Pennsylvania established Pennie (Pennsylvania Insurance Exchange) as the state's own health insurance marketplace, transitioning from Healthcare.gov:

What Pennie is:

Pennsylvania's state-based health insurance marketplace

Established in 2020 (transitioned from federal exchange)

Coordinates ACA-related coverage for Pennsylvania residents

Operates Open Enrollment annually starting November 1

Coordinates with Pennsylvania Medical Assistance and Pennsylvania CHIP

Why it matters for the exam:

Producer questions may test understanding that Pennsylvania has its own state-based exchange

Marketplace certification requirements

Coordination with Pennsylvania Medicaid and CHIP

## Pennsylvania Medical Assistance (Medicaid)

Pennsylvania Medical Assistance is Pennsylvania's Medicaid program:

Provides comprehensive coverage for eligible Pennsylvania residents

Coordinates with Pennie for eligibility determination

Generally not producer-sold (similar to other state Medicaid programs)

Producers should understand Pennsylvania Medical Assistance for client coordination purposes.

## Pennsylvania CHIP

Pennsylvania CHIP is Pennsylvania's Children's Health Insurance Program:

Provides coverage for children regardless of income (different cost-sharing levels)

Coordinates with Pennie and Pennsylvania Medical Assistance

Available year-round

## Pennsylvania Auto Insurance Considerations

For the P&C exam, Pennsylvania-specific auto insurance topics include:

Pennsylvania Auto Insurance Choice. Pennsylvania allows drivers to choose between:

Limited Tort — Lower premiums, but waives the right to recover non-economic damages (pain and suffering) except in serious cases

Full Tort — Higher premiums, but preserves full right to sue for all damages including non-economic

Pennsylvania Mandatory Coverage Requirements:

Bodily Injury Liability

Property Damage Liability

First Party Benefits (medical benefits)

Uninsured/Underinsured Motorist

Specific minimum limits apply

The Tort Choice system is distinctive to Pennsylvania (one of only a few states with this system) and frequently appears on Property and Casualty exam questions.

## 5 Frequently Asked Questions

- How much of the Pennsylvania exam is state-specific law? Approximately 15-25% of the exam focuses on Pennsylvania-specific content.
- What's distinctive about Pennsylvania's health insurance exchange? Pennsylvania operates Pennie as a state-based marketplace (established 2020), unlike most states which use the federal Healthcare.gov.
- What's the Pennsylvania Tort Choice system? Pennsylvania allows drivers to choose between Limited Tort (lower premium, restricted lawsuit rights) and Full Tort (higher premium, full lawsuit rights) for auto insurance — distinctive to Pennsylvania and a few other states.
- What's Pennsylvania Act 142 of 2024? Effective April 29, 2025, this Act eliminated the prelicense education requirement for Pennsylvania insurance producer licensure.
- Can national study materials cover Pennsylvania law? Partially. National materials cover general insurance concepts well but rarely go deep on Pennsylvania-specific rules. Use Pennsylvania-specific prelicense courses for state content.
