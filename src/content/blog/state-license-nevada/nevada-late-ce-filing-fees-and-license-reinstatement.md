Despite best intentions, sometimes Nevada producers miss their CE deadlines or renewal dates. Whether due to oversight, life circumstances, or unexpected challenges, late filing situations happen — and how you respond determines whether you face a minor inconvenience or a major career disruption. Understanding Nevada's late filing rules and reinstatement procedures helps you respond correctly when problems arise.

Here's what Nevada producers should know about late CE filing and license reinstatement.

## Nevada's Renewal Timeline

Before discussing late filing, here's the standard Nevada renewal framework:

Initial license cycle. Major Lines licensees first renew on the last day of their birth month following their initial 2-year period.

Subsequent cycles. Every 3 years on the first day of the month following the issuance month.

CE requirement: 30 hours of CE (3 hours ethics) completed and reported before submitting renewal application.

Renewal process: Through Sircon (sircon.com/nevada).

When you miss this standard timeline, late filing rules apply.

## The 30-Day Late Renewal Period

Nevada provides a 30-day late renewal period after the expiration date.

During this 30-day grace period:

You can still renew your license

You must pay a late renewal fee in addition to the standard renewal fee

You must have completed all required CE

Your license technically isn't lapsed during this period

This is your friendly window. Most situations that cause renewal delay can be resolved within 30 days with relatively minor consequences.

## What Happens After 30 Days

If you don't renew within the 30-day late renewal period:

License expires. Your license becomes inactive/expired.

Cannot conduct insurance business. You cannot sell, solicit, or negotiate insurance products.

Reinstatement procedures apply. Different from late renewal — more complex and costly.

Possible reapplication. Extended periods of inactivity may require fresh application processes.

The longer your license remains expired, the more difficult reinstatement becomes.

## License Reinstatement After Expiration

Nevada provides reinstatement paths depending on how long your license has been expired:

Within 12 months of expiration:

Generally less complex reinstatement

Catch up on required CE

Pay reinstatement fees (typically more than late renewal fees)

May not require retaking exam in many cases

After 12 months of expiration:

More complex reinstatement procedures

May require retaking the licensing exam

Additional documentation requirements

Possibly fresh application as new applicant

Long-term lapsed (years):

May require complete reapplication including fresh exam

All initial application requirements apply

Background check requirements typically apply

## Best Practices to Avoid Late Filing

Far better to avoid late filing than navigate it:

Track your renewal date prominently. Calendar reminders multiple months before deadline.

Complete CE early in your cycle. Don't wait until the final months of the 3-year cycle.

Set milestone reminders:

18 months before renewal: confirm CE plan and progress

12 months before renewal: be on track to complete

6 months before renewal: 80%+ of CE complete

3 months before renewal: final CE underway

1 month before renewal: complete and verified

Verify CE reporting regularly. Through Sircon's Continuing Education Transcript Inquiry service.

Keep contact information current. Update your Sircon profile to ensure you receive renewal notifications.

Build buffer time. Plan to complete CE 30+ days before renewal deadline. This gives time for reporting and any corrections.

## What to Do If You Realize You'll Miss Your Deadline

If you realize you'll miss your renewal deadline:

Don't panic. Nevada's 30-day late renewal period gives you breathing room.

Complete CE immediately. Even with a tight timeline, completing CE is your priority.

Verify CE reporting. Make sure your CE shows on Sircon transcript.

Contact DOI proactively. If extenuating circumstances exist, contact DOI before your deadline.

Plan to renew within 30-day grace period. This avoids reinstatement complexities.

Pay late renewal fee. Accept this cost as the price of late filing.

## Late Renewal Process

If you're filing during the 30-day late renewal period:

Step 1: Complete CE. All required CE must be completed before renewal application.

Step 2: Verify CE reporting. Confirm hours show correctly on Sircon transcript.

Step 3: Log into Sircon. Access sircon.com/nevada.

Step 4: Submit renewal application. Use the Renew or Reinstate a License service.

Step 5: Pay fees. Standard renewal fee plus late renewal fee.

Step 6: Receive confirmation. When renewal is processed.

The late renewal process is similar to standard renewal — just with the additional late fee and tighter timing.

## Reinstatement Process After Expiration

If you missed the 30-day late renewal period:

Step 1: Determine current status. Through Sircon, check your specific license status.

Step 2: Complete CE. All required CE must be completed.

Step 3: Address any specialty training gaps. LTC, Annuity Best Interest, NFIP if applicable.

Step 4: Submit reinstatement application. Through Sircon.

Step 5: Pay reinstatement fees. Higher than late renewal fees.

Step 6: Provide additional documentation. As required for your specific situation.

Step 7: Wait for review. Reinstatement processing typically takes longer than renewal.

Step 8: Possible exam requirement. Depending on length of expiration.

## Extenuating Circumstances

Nevada recognizes that some situations genuinely prevent timely renewal:

Active military duty. Federal protections for active-duty military members may apply.

Serious medical conditions. Hospitalization, serious illness, or other medical situations preventing normal activities.

Caregiving responsibilities. Caring for seriously ill family members.

Major personal emergencies. Significant life disruptions.

Natural disasters. Wildfire, flood, or other disasters affecting business operations.

How to request consideration:

Contact the Nevada DOI directly:

Email: Renewal.Desk@doi.nv.gov

Phone: (775) 687-0700 (Carson City) or (702) 486-4009 (Las Vegas)

Provide:

Specific description of circumstances

Documentation supporting your situation

Specific accommodation request

Timeline of when you can complete renewal

Apply early. Apply before your deadline if possible. Late requests are harder to accommodate.

## Costs of Late Filing

The cost of late filing escalates the longer you delay:

On-time renewal: Standard renewal fee only.

30-day late renewal: Standard renewal fee plus late renewal fee.

Reinstatement (within 12 months of expiration): Higher reinstatement fees.

Long-term reinstatement: Full reapplication fees plus possibly exam fees.

Lost income. During any period your license is inactive, you cannot conduct insurance business — direct income loss.

Carrier consequences. Carriers may not maintain appointments during license expiration.

The financial difference between on-time renewal and reinstatement after expiration can be substantial.

## Common Late Filing Mistakes

Assuming you have more time than you do. The 30-day grace period seems generous but passes quickly.

Continuing to do business with expired license. Conducting insurance business with an expired license is a serious violation.

Ignoring renewal notifications. Sircon and DOI send notifications, but they're easy to overlook.

Letting CE lapse without addressing. Don't simply not renew — address the situation.

Not maintaining documentation. Keep records of any communications with DOI about late filing.

Waiting too long to seek reinstatement. The longer you wait after expiration, the harder reinstatement becomes.

## Specialty Training Considerations During Reinstatement

LTC training. May still need to be current to sell LTC products after reinstatement.

Annuity Best Interest training. Required to sell annuities. Effective November 15, 2024, this is a one-time 4-hour requirement.

NFIP Flood training. Required to sell flood. One-time 3-hour requirement.

These specialty trainings may need verification during reinstatement processes.

## Career Impact of License Lapses

Beyond direct fees and lost income, license lapses can affect:

Carrier appointments. Some carriers may terminate appointments during lapses.

Reputation. Disclosure of license history may affect employer or carrier decisions.

Future state applications. Other state insurance regulators may consider Nevada license history.

Errors and Omissions (E&O) coverage. E&O coverage may be affected.

These considerations make timely renewal worth the effort.

## How to Recover Effectively

If you've experienced license lapses:

Address the underlying cause. Why did the lapse happen? Don't just renew without addressing root cause.

Build better tracking systems. Implement personal tracking and reminders.

Consider professional help. Insurance attorneys can help with complex reinstatement situations.

Plan for the next cycle. Set up systems to prevent recurrence.

Maintain transparency. With carriers and other states about your license history.

Document recovery. Keep records of all communications with DOI about your situation.

## 5 Frequently Asked Questions

- How long does Nevada's late renewal grace period last? 30 days after your expiration date. During this period, you can renew with a late fee.
- What happens if I miss the 30-day grace period? Your license expires. You'll need to go through reinstatement procedures, which are more complex and costly than late renewal.
- Can I keep doing business if my license has expired? No. Conducting insurance business with an expired license is a violation that can result in serious consequences including penalties and additional disciplinary action.
- How long do I have to reinstate an expired Nevada license? Reinstatement difficulty increases over time. Within 12 months is more straightforward; longer expirations may require complete reapplication including exam.
- Can extenuating circumstances waive late fees? The DOI has discretion in extenuating circumstances. Contact Renewal.Desk@doi.nv.gov early in your situation to discuss accommodation possibilities.
