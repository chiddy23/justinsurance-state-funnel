The Virginia insurance licensing exam is administered by Prometric — a detail that matters from the moment you register, because Prometric's platform, scheduling system, and test center logistics are different from the PSI and Pearson VUE systems used by many other states. Virginia candidates schedule at prometric.com/virginia/insurance or by calling (866) 891-6396. The exam is available at physical Prometric test centers throughout Virginia — or via Prometric's remote ProProctor platform. Every candidate who walks into exam day understanding exactly what the test looks like and how it is scored performs better than candidates who read the summary and assume the rest. This post covers every structural detail of the Virginia insurance exam.

## One Exam, One Score: The Most Important Thing to Understand

Virginia does not run a two-part exam with separately-passed national and state sections. Some states do, and advice written for those states gets recycled onto Virginia pages constantly. It does not apply here.

Each Virginia exam is a single integrated blueprint. Prometric's official content outlines set it out plainly:

Series 11-01 (Life, Annuities & Health): 140 scored questions plus 10 pretest questions, 2.5-hour time limit, drawn from 16 weighted content sections — Insurance Regulation 10% (14 items), General Insurance 6% (8), Life Insurance Basics 6% (8), Life Insurance Policies 10% (14), Policy Provisions/Options/Riders 11% (16), Annuities 6% (8), and so on through Federal Tax Considerations for Health Insurance 2% (3).

Series 11-03 (Property & Casualty): 135 scored questions plus 10 pretest questions, 2.5-hour time limit, drawn from 10 weighted content sections — Insurance Regulation 15% (20 items), General Insurance 9% (12), P&C Basics 13% (18), Dwelling Policy 5% (7), Homeowners Policy 13% (18), Auto Insurance 16% (21), Commercial Package Policy 12% (16), Businessowners Policy 5% (7), Workers Compensation 7% (9), Other Coverages and Options 5% (7).

Critical scoring rule: there is one score, and you need 70% of the whole exam. The Virginia Insurance Licensing Information Bulletin states it directly — "Your total percentage score is computed by dividing the number of questions you answered correctly by the total number of questions in the examination. The total score is not computed by adding the section percentages and dividing by the total number of sections."

Your score report does break out the percentage correct in each content-outline section, and that is where the confusion starts. Those numbers are diagnostic. The Bulletin explains they are shown "to guide you, or your employer and/or trainer, about areas requiring additional preparation." No section can be failed on its own, and the section percentages will not average out to your total, because the sections carry different numbers of questions.

What is true is that Virginia law is heavily tested — it is simply not fenced off into its own section. On Series 11-03, Insurance Regulation at 15% (20 items) is one of the exam's largest sections — second only to Auto Insurance at 16% (21 items) — and it is almost entirely Virginia producer licensing and SCC authority with Va. Code citations attached, and Virginia statutes are cited again inside P&C Basics (§ 3.4), Auto Insurance (§ 6.1) and Workers Compensation. So the practical advice survives the correction: candidates who study only general insurance concepts and skip Virginia-specific material still put themselves at real risk of failing. They just fail on one total score rather than on a separate state section.

## Pretest Questions

The pretest questions are unscored experimental items distributed throughout the exam. They are not labeled as pretest — you cannot tell which questions count and which do not. Answer all questions as if they count. Do not skip any question on the assumption that it might be a pretest item.

## The Question Format

All questions are four-option multiple-choice. Prometric designs questions to test knowledge and comprehension, not pattern recognition or test-taking tricks. The Virginia Bureau of Insurance candidate bulletin identifies three item formats:

Direct question (Format 1): A complete question with one correct answer among the four options.

Incomplete sentence (Format 2): A stem written as a partial sentence that the correct option completes.

"All of the following EXCEPT" (Format 3): A negatively worded item in which three of the four options are true and your job is to find the one that is not. Read every stem carefully for the word EXCEPT — overlooking it is one of the most common avoidable mistakes on the exam.

Questions and topics are distributed throughout the exam — Virginia statutory material and general insurance content are mixed together, not grouped into blocks. You cannot tell from the sequence of questions which content-outline section you are in.

## Scheduling at Prometric

Online: prometric.com/virginia/insurance — fastest and most flexible; available 24/7 Phone: (866) 891-6396 Payment: MasterCard, Visa, money order, company check, or cashier's check — personal checks not accepted; pay at time of registration Fee: $35 per exam

You may take your exam at any Prometric test center in the United States — not just in Virginia. Prometric operates test centers across the Commonwealth, and locations are subject to change — verify your site when you schedule. The Prometric network has more than 300 locations nationwide.

Rescheduling/Cancellation: To change or cancel your appointment, you must do so at least 24 hours before your scheduled time, using the Reschedule/Cancel option on the Prometric website or the automated voice response system at (866) 891-6396. There is no charge for changing an appointment within the same testing window if you give at least 24 hours' notice. Change or cancel without proper notice — or miss your appointment, or arrive too late to be admitted — and you forfeit the entire examination fee. There is no partial forfeiture: you re-register and pay the $35 again.

## Test Day: In-Person Protocol

Arrive 30 minutes before your appointment for check-in. The process includes identity verification, biometric intake (digital fingerprint or palm scan at the center), and a review of testing center rules.

What to bring:

Two forms of valid government-issued ID — primary must have photo and signature; secondary must have signature

Your name must match your registration exactly

What is prohibited:

Cell phones, electronic devices, calculators, smartwatches

Food and drink

Bags, wallets, or personal items (stored outside the testing room)

Study materials of any kind

The proctor cannot answer any questions about exam content. If you encounter a question you don't understand, answer it to the best of your ability and continue. If you need to leave the test room, the bulletin's test center rules apply: you must sign out each time you leave, tell the Test Center Administrator before accessing anything in your locker (including medicine), and go back through the full security screening and ID check before you are re-admitted. The exam clock keeps running while you are out, so any break comes out of your own testing time.

## Results

Your score appears on screen immediately upon completing the exam, and a score report is emailed to you. Every candidate gets the same report, pass or fail: it shows the overall score and grade — the numerical percentage of questions you answered correctly and whether you passed — plus the percentage correct in each major section of the content outline. If you did not pass, that section breakdown is your retake study plan. If you did pass, the Bulletin still suggests using it: the weak areas it exposes are the ones to shore up before you start advising clients. Duplicate copies are available through Prometric's Score Report Portal.

Prometric reports your exam results to the Virginia Bureau of Insurance electronically, so you do not have to send anything to the Bureau yourself; your on-screen score and emailed report are your immediate confirmation.

## Frequently Asked Questions

Does Virginia have a separate state law section I have to pass on its own?

No. This is the most common misconception about the Virginia exam, and it comes from candidates reading guidance written for states that do split their exams. Virginia's Prometric exams are single integrated blueprints — Series 11-01 is 140 scored questions across 16 weighted sections, Series 11-03 is 135 scored questions across 10 — and your grade is one total percentage: correct answers divided by total questions, 70% to pass. Virginia law is embedded in the sections rather than separated out. On Series 11-03 that means Insurance Regulation is 15% of the exam (20 questions) and is overwhelmingly Virginia licensing, appointment and SCC-authority material, with more Virginia statutes cited inside P&C Basics, Auto Insurance and Workers Compensation. So skipping Virginia-specific study is still one of the most reliable ways to fail — you just fail on the total score, not on a separate section. Your score report shows section-level percentages either way, so a failed attempt tells you exactly where the deficit was.

Can I take the Virginia insurance exam at any Prometric location, or only in Virginia?

You can take the Virginia insurance licensing exam at any Prometric test center in the United States — not just Virginia locations. This is a significant benefit for candidates who live near a state border, are temporarily out of state, or find a more convenient scheduling option at a center in a neighboring state. Virginia is served by Prometric test centers within the Commonwealth, and Prometric operates more than 300 centers nationally. When you schedule at prometric.com/virginia/insurance, you can select any location. You can also take the exam remotely via Prometric's ProProctor platform from your home or office if your computer is compatible with the ProProctor software.

What happens if I don't understand a question on the exam?

Answer it to the best of your ability and continue. Prometric test center proctors are explicitly prohibited from answering any questions about exam content. If you encounter a question that is unclear, confusing, or covers material you don't recognize, make your best guess from the four options and move forward. Do not skip questions — unanswered questions count as wrong. One useful technique: eliminate the options you know are incorrect, then choose the best remaining answer. A well-prepared candidate will recognize most questions. For the few that are genuinely unfamiliar, the four-option format always gives you at least a 25% chance by guessing, and elimination improves those odds. Unscored pretest questions are mixed throughout — a question that seems completely foreign to you may be a pretest item and not affect your score at all.

How long does the entire Prometric exam session take, including check-in?

Both of the major exams run 2.5 hours. Series 11-01 (Life, Annuities & Health) is 150 minutes of testing time for 150 questions, and Series 11-03 (Property & Casualty) is also 150 minutes, for 145 questions. Add 30 minutes of check-in and setup and you are at the test center for approximately three hours either way. The shorter series are quicker — the Virginia Bureau's exam registration form lists 120 minutes for Life & Annuities (11-05), Health (11-06) and Personal Lines (11-07), 90 minutes for Title (11-04) and 60 minutes for the Public Adjuster exam (11-08). The check-in process includes identity verification and a brief tutorial on the computer testing interface — you can practice navigating the software before the exam begins, but the tutorial does not count toward your exam time. Most candidates report finishing the scored content with time remaining; do not rush, but do not linger over any single question long enough to jeopardize time for later ones.

What should I do the day before the Virginia insurance exam?

Do not cram the night before. If you are not prepared by the evening before your exam, a final night of memorization is unlikely to change the outcome and may increase anxiety. Instead: review your weakest topics lightly using flashcards or a condensed review sheet, confirm your Prometric appointment details and test center address (or verify ProProctor compatibility if testing remotely), prepare your two forms of ID, set an alarm that gives you time to arrive 30 minutes early, and get a full night of sleep. Your performance on an exam this length is significantly affected by fatigue — candidates who are well-rested consistently outperform those who are not, at every knowledge level. You are facing 145 to 150 questions in a single sitting; your brain's ability to access what you already know is optimized by rest, not by last-minute reading.

The Virginia insurance exam is not designed to surprise prepared candidates. It is designed to be challenging for unprepared ones. Understand that you are being graded on one total score, prepare the general insurance content and the Virginia statutory material woven through it, and walk into the Prometric test center knowing exactly what to expect from the first question to the score report.
