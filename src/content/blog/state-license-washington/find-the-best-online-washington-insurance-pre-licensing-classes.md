## Understand Washington pre-licensing requirements

Before you dive into your online [Washington insurance](/washington) [pre-licensing](/washington/prelicensing) classes, it’s essential to know exactly what the state requires of you. Washington does not require a specified number of pre-licensing hours for any line of authority — life & disability (health) and property & casualty included — since that requirement was eliminated in 2023. What Washington does require is passing a state licensing exam, which covers everything from basic insurance terminology to Washington-specific regulations, and optional coursework like this can help you prepare for it.

Each line of authority has its own prerequisites, and fulfilling them ensures you’re well-prepared to represent clients accurately and ethically. Think of it as building a foundation of knowledge that you’ll continue to refine throughout your career. And if you’re new to the industry, don’t worry: many programs are designed to guide you step by step, so you can feel confident in your progress.

## Consider course format and flexibility

Most busy adults juggle work, family, and other commitments, making flexible coursework a huge benefit. With online classes, you can usually take lessons at your own pace—whether that’s a few lessons each day or a weekend marathon. This convenience means you don’t have to put your life on hold to prepare for your state exam.

However, online learning isn’t one-size-fits-all. Some platforms offer live webinars that simulate a classroom environment, while others provide self-directed modules you can tackle solo. Determining which style works for you largely depends on the way you learn best. If you thrive on real-time interaction, choose a course with instructor-led sessions. If you prefer flexibility, a self-paced course might be the perfect match.

## Key features to look for

Not all pre-licensing programs deliver the same experience. To make an informed decision, consider focusing on a few crucial areas:

1.  Instructor support  
    You’ll want access to instructors who understand your challenges and can answer complex questions. Look for courses that provide email, phone, or even live-chat support.
2.  Practice exams  
    Many providers include full-length practice tests that mirror Washington’s exam format. These are essential for highlighting areas where you might need extra study.
3.  Compliance and updates  
    Insurance regulations change frequently. Make sure your provider offers updated content so you’re not learning outdated laws or practices.
4.  Mobile compatibility  
    If you plan on reviewing flashcards or short quizzes on the go, confirm that your course materials work seamlessly on smartphones and tablets.
5.  Budget considerations  
    While cheaper courses can be tempting, make sure you’re not missing out on features like tutor support or robust practice exams. The goal is to pass your exam on the first try, so consider value alongside price.

## Your top five FAQs

Below are some common questions people ask when deciding on online pre-licensing classes. If yours doesn’t appear here, it’s still worth checking the course provider’s FAQ section or reaching out directly for more details.

1.  How long do I have to complete the pre-licensing course?  
    Most programs give you between 30 to 90 days of access for each line of authority. It’s best to confirm the exact time frame with your chosen provider to ensure you meet the state’s requirements comfortably.
2.  Is one course enough to cover both life & health and property & casualty?  
    Yes, there are courses that bundle both lines of authority. However, they typically break down the material into separate modules since each line has unique laws, regulations, and best practices.
3.  Will an online course prepare me as thoroughly as an in-person class?  
    Absolutely—online courses can be just as comprehensive, especially if they offer instructor support, practice exams, and interactive materials. The key is to find a platform that aligns with your learning style.
4.  Are there tuition assistance plans available?  
    Many providers offer payment plans or discounts if you bundle multiple lines of authority. Check with your course provider to see if you qualify for any financial incentives.
5.  How do I schedule my licensing exam once I finish?  
    Whenever you feel ready, whether you completed an optional course or studied on your own, you’ll typically visit the [Washington Office of the Insurance Commissioner](/washington/requirements) website or an approved testing vendor. From there, you can pick a date and time that fits your schedule.
