Tennessee insurance producer licenses renew every two years on the last day of the producer's birth month. That single sentence contains the entire deadline structure — but understanding exactly what it means in practice, what must be completed before renewal can be submitted, what happens the moment the expiration date passes, and how renewal missed by days versus months versus more than a year plays out requires more than the headline rule. This post covers every dimension of Tennessee license renewal that working producers need to manage their compliance without surprises: the deadline calculation, the CE prerequisite, the renewal submission process, the fee structure, why Tennessee gives producers no grace period, the late renewal window, and the reinstatement path when a license has lapsed entirely.

## How the Renewal Deadline Works

### The Birth Month Structure

Tennessee licenses expire on the last day of the producer's birth month in the renewal year. The renewal deadline is tied to the producer's birthday — not to the date the license was originally issued, not to a fixed calendar date, and not to when the producer last renewed.

How to identify your renewal year: Tennessee licenses are issued on a biennial cycle — every two years. Whether a producer renews in an odd year or an even year depends on when their license was originally issued. A producer who received their initial license in 2022 renews in 2024 and 2026. A producer who received their license in 2023 renews in 2025 and 2027. If you are uncertain which year your license renews, verify through the TDCI's online license lookup at tn.gov/commerce/insurance or through your NIPR account at nipr.com.

Calculating your exact deadline: Combine your birth month with your renewal year. A producer born in March whose renewal year is 2026 must renew by March 31, 2026. A producer born in September whose renewal year is 2025 must renew by September 30, 2025. A producer born in February must renew by February 28 (or February 29 in a leap year).

Why birth month matters for planning: Because every producer's deadline is different, there are no universal industry-wide renewal deadlines in Tennessee. A producer cannot rely on colleagues' reminders or industry announcements — their personal deadline is their own responsibility.

### The Biennial Cycle After the First Renewal

After the first renewal, the biennial cycle continues from the renewed date. Renewal submissions do not reset the birth month deadline — the deadline remains the last day of the birth month in every subsequent renewal year regardless of when the renewal was submitted.

## What Must Be Completed Before Renewal

Renewal is not automatic. Three conditions must be satisfied before a Tennessee producer license renewal can be successfully submitted and processed.

### Condition 1: CE Completion

24 CE hours including 3 ethics hours must be completed and reported to the TDCI before submitting the renewal application. The CE must be from TDCI-approved providers and approved courses.

The reporting verification step: CE providers report completions to the TDCI — but there is typically a delay of one to several business days between course completion and hours appearing in the TDCI's records. Submitting the NIPR renewal application before CE completions appear in the TDCI's records produces a rejected application. Verify your CE standing in the TDCI's system before submitting — not in the CE provider's system alone.

How to verify: Contact the TDCI at (615) 741-2241 or ce.agent.licensing@tn.gov, or access the producer portal at tn.gov/commerce/insurance. Your confirmed CE total in the TDCI's records is the only count that matters for renewal processing.

Specialty training: LTC ongoing training (4 hours every 24 months) and other specialty training requirements are separate compliance obligations with separate deadlines. They count toward the 24-hour CE total but are tracked on independent schedules. Satisfying the 24-hour general CE requirement does not automatically satisfy outstanding specialty training — and conversely, completing specialty training counts toward the 24-hour total.

### Condition 2: Accurate Background Disclosure

The NIPR renewal application includes background disclosure questions covering criminal history, regulatory actions, financial matters, and other disclosable events since the last renewal. These questions must be answered truthfully. Any new matters — criminal convictions, regulatory actions, judgments — must be disclosed accurately.

Inaccurate or incomplete disclosure on a renewal application is itself a ground for license discipline under TCA §56-6-112 — potentially more serious than the underlying matter being disclosed. When in doubt about whether something requires disclosure, contact the TDCI before submitting the renewal rather than omitting it and hoping it is not discovered.

### Condition 3: Payment

The renewal fee must be paid at the time of NIPR application submission. There is no invoice-and-pay process — payment is collected at submission.

## The Renewal Fee Structure

The state renewal fee is $60 for a license carrying at least one major line or Title ($30 for limited-line-only licenses), plus the NIPR transaction fee. Under Tenn. Code Ann. § 56-6-107(d), a renewal fee received after the due date carries a penalty of double the unpaid renewal fee — $120 for a major-line or Title license, and $60 for a limited-line-only license.

Payment method: Credit card or debit card through NIPR. No checks or cash accepted through the online portal.

## The Renewal Submission Process

### Step 1: Verify CE Completion in TDCI Records

Confirm all 24 CE hours including 3 ethics hours appear in the TDCI's system — not just in your CE provider's records. Allow at least several business days after your final course completion before expecting TDCI records to reflect it. Complete CE at least 30 days before the renewal deadline to provide adequate buffer for reporting.

### Step 2: Log in to NIPR

Navigate to nipr.com and log in to your existing NIPR account. Use the same account used for your original license application — your National Producer Number (NPN) is the consistent identifier across all licensing transactions.

### Step 3: Navigate to Renewal

From your account dashboard, select the renewal option for your Tennessee resident license. NIPR's interface will display your current Tennessee license information and the renewal options available.

### Step 4: Complete Background Disclosure Questions

Answer all background questions accurately and completely. Disclose any new matters since your last renewal.

### Step 5: Pay and Submit

Pay the standard renewal fee (check your NIPR renewal screen or contact the TDCI for the current amount) by credit card or debit card. Submit. NIPR routes your renewal to the TDCI for processing.

### Step 6: Confirm Active Status

After submission, monitor your license status through the TDCI's online license lookup at tn.gov/commerce/insurance. Renewal processing is usually completed within a few business days for uncomplicated applications, though timing varies. Do not resume transacting business based on NIPR confirmation alone — confirm active status in the TDCI's system.

## There Is No Grace Period

Tennessee producer licenses have no post-expiration grace period. The renewal window runs from 90 days before the expiration date through the expiration date itself, and a renewal counts as on time only if it is submitted on or before that date. There is no no-fee cushion after the deadline: a renewal submitted even one day late is a late renewal and carries the late fee described below.

Your next deadline does not move: renewing — whether on time or late — does not shift your deadline off the last day of your birth month. The following renewal is still measured from that birth-month expiration date, not from the date you happened to submit.

Do not transact on an expired license: A Tennessee producer license that has passed its expiration date is expired. Producers who continue to sell, solicit, or negotiate insurance on an expired license are operating without a valid license — there is no grace window that authorizes continued business after the expiration date. Renew on or before the deadline.

Why this matters: Because Tennessee offers no no-fee grace period, the expiration date is a hard line. Producers who plan to renew "around" their deadline rather than before it are one administrative slip away from a $120 late fee — and, if CE is not yet reported, from a lapse. Treat the birth-month expiration date as the deadline it is.

## Late Renewal: After the Expiration Date

When a producer does not renew on or before the expiration date, the license lapses. Late renewal is available for up to one year from the expiration date — but it carries a late fee.

### The Late Renewal Fee

The late charge is a statutory penalty, not a discretionary or unpublished fee. Under Tenn. Code Ann. § 56-6-107(d), a renewal fee received after the due date carries a penalty of double the unpaid renewal fee — $120 for a license carrying at least one major line or Title, and $60 for a limited-line-only license. That penalty is what makes a late renewal cost materially more than an on-time one, so confirm the exact total due on your NIPR renewal screen before you submit.

### CE Requirements for Late Renewal

Before submitting a late renewal application, all CE requirements for the lapsed period must be satisfied. A producer who allowed their license to lapse in part because they had not completed CE must complete CE before the late renewal will be approved. The same 24-hour requirement including 3 ethics hours applies — and the same TDCI record verification step is required before submitting the late renewal.

### The Late Renewal Process

Late renewal is submitted through NIPR under the same process as standard renewal. Select the late renewal option when it appears in the NIPR interface. Pay the standard renewal fee plus the late fee (confirm the current total with the TDCI). The TDCI processes late renewals and restores the license to active status upon approval.

### The One-Year Hard Deadline

Late renewal is available only within one year from the expiration date. A license that expired March 31, 2025 can be late-renewed through March 31, 2026. On April 1, 2026, late renewal is unavailable regardless of how close to the deadline the producer attempts to submit.

This one-year threshold is not negotiable. There are no extensions, no exception applications, and no TDCI discretion to permit late renewal beyond one year. After one year, full relicensing is the only path.

## Full Relicensing After One Year

When a license has been expired for more than one year, the producer must complete the entire initial licensing process from the beginning. Every step of new licensure applies:

Pass the Pearson VUE exam. Prior passing scores are not preserved. The exam must be retaken for every line of authority the producer wants to hold — at $55 per line per attempt.

Complete IdentoGO fingerprinting. A new background check is required. The $37.15 fingerprinting fee applies.

Submit the Fingerprint Policy and Acknowledgement Form to the TDCI by email or fax.

Wait the 48-hour mandatory post-exam period before submitting the NIPR application.

Submit a new NIPR application — a single $50 application fee regardless of how many lines you include, plus the $5.60 NIPR transaction fee.

Reestablish carrier appointments. All prior appointments are terminated with a lapsed license beyond the one-year threshold. New appointments must be filed by each carrier before the producer can transact business.

The full relicensing cost for two lines (Property and Casualty) remote exam plus all fees is approximately $202.75 — not counting a prep course. For all four major lines remote, approximately $312.75. These costs are avoidable through timely renewal.

## Non-Resident Renewal

### The Same Deadline Structure

Tennessee non-resident producer licenses follow the same biennial birth month renewal structure as resident licenses. The deadline is the last day of the birth month in the renewal year — identical to resident renewal.

### CE Satisfaction Through Home State

Non-resident producers are exempt from Tennessee's CE requirements when their home state CE is current. A producer whose home state requires 24 biennial CE hours and who has completed those hours has simultaneously satisfied Tennessee's non-resident CE obligation. No additional Tennessee-specific CE is required.

LTC training follows the same reciprocity logic: Tenn. Comp. R. & Regs. 0780-01-61-.31 provides that non-resident producers who meet the long-term care education and training requirements of their home state are deemed to meet Tennessee's LTC education and training requirements. A non-resident who has completed their home state's LTC training does not repeat Tennessee's 8-hour initial or 4-hour ongoing LTC training.

The limit of that reciprocity: it is satisfaction of the home state's LTC training that transfers — not the mere fact of being a non-resident. A producer whose home state imposes no LTC training requirement has nothing to carry over, and must complete Tennessee's 8-hour initial LTC training before selling, soliciting, or negotiating LTC products in Tennessee, plus 4 hours of ongoing LTC training every 24 months. If your home state's LTC training status is unclear, confirm it with the TDCI before writing Tennessee LTC business rather than assuming the deemer applies.

### Non-Resident Renewal Fee

Identical to resident renewal. Non-resident late renewal carries the same late fee as resident late renewal.

### The Home State License Dependency

A Tennessee non-resident license is contingent on the home state resident license remaining active and in good standing. If the home state license lapses, is suspended, or is revoked, the Tennessee non-resident license loses its good standing basis and cannot be renewed. Non-residents must maintain their home state license as the foundation of all non-resident licenses.

## Building a Renewal Management System

### The Three-Alert Calendar System

Set three calendar reminders tied to your renewal deadline — the last day of your birth month in your renewal year:

90 days before the deadline: Begin or continue CE coursework. At 90 days out, you have time to complete courses at a comfortable pace, allow for reporting delays, and address any CE discrepancies before the deadline creates urgency.

60 days before the deadline: CE verification checkpoint. Confirm that all completed hours appear in the TDCI's records. Address any reporting gaps with CE providers. If CE is not yet complete, identify what remains and schedule the remaining courses immediately.

30 days before the deadline: Renewal submission target. Complete and submit the NIPR renewal application well before the deadline — not on the deadline. Submitting early provides buffer for any processing issues, payment failures, or application corrections without risking a lapse.

### Address Currency

The TDCI sends renewal reminders to the address on file. Producers who have moved without updating their address through NIPR do not receive these reminders — and are more likely to miss their renewal deadline as a result. Update your address within 30 days of any change through your NIPR account. This is a TCA §56-6-108(c) requirement for non-residents and a professional compliance obligation for all producers.

### Multi-Line License Renewal

Producers holding multiple lines of authority renew all lines simultaneously through a single NIPR renewal application. All CE hours satisfy all lines together — the 24-hour total is not multiplied by the number of lines held. The single standard renewal fee covers all lines. There is no per-line renewal fee structure.

### Multi-State Renewal Management

Producers holding licenses in multiple states must track each state's independent renewal schedule. Tennessee's birth month biennial deadline may not align with other states' renewal schedules. A producer with Tennessee resident and Georgia non-resident licenses may have different renewal deadlines in each state. Maintain a separate calendar entry for each state's license deadline and manage them independently.

## Frequently Asked Questions

My license expires at the end of this month and I have completed all 24 CE hours, but two of them have not yet appeared in the TDCI's records. Should I submit renewal now or wait?

Wait until all CE hours appear in the TDCI's records before submitting your renewal. A renewal application submitted with unverified CE will be rejected as non-compliant. Contact the CE provider immediately and request confirmation that the two outstanding completions were submitted to the TDCI — and ask for a resubmission if they were not. Simultaneously contact the TDCI to report the discrepancy. If the hours cannot be confirmed in the TDCI's records before your expiration date, the license will lapse and you will have to late-renew once the hours are confirmed — and Tenn. Code Ann. § 56-6-107(d) then imposes a penalty of double the unpaid renewal fee ($120 for a major-line or Title license), because Tennessee gives producers no no-fee grace period. This situation is the reason the standard recommendation is to complete CE at least 30 days before the renewal deadline: reporting delays of one to several business days are normal, and a 30-day buffer accommodates them without any risk of a lapse.

I renewed my Tennessee license six months ago but I am now moving to Georgia. Can I keep my Tennessee license as a non-resident license?

Yes. When you establish Georgia residency, you must convert your Tennessee resident license to a non-resident license rather than allowing it to lapse. Under TCA §56-6-108(c), you must file a change of address with the TDCI and provide certification from your new resident state (Georgia) within 30 days of the change of address. Your Tennessee license continues as a non-resident license — it does not terminate. Tennessee non-resident license renewal follows the same biennial birth month structure. Once you have established Georgia as your home state and Tennessee as a non-resident license, your Georgia CE satisfaction will cover your Tennessee non-resident CE obligation going forward. If you are within 90 days of establishing Georgia residency and have not yet surrendered your prior resident license, you may be able to transfer your existing Tennessee resident license to a Georgia resident license without retaking the Georgia exam — subject to Georgia's transfer rules.

Is there any way to find out my Tennessee license renewal date without contacting the TDCI?

Yes — through two sources. First, log in to your NIPR account at nipr.com. Your license record in NIPR shows the expiration date of your Tennessee license. Second, use the TDCI's online license lookup tool at tn.gov/commerce/insurance. Search for your license by NPN or by name and license number. Your license record shows the expiration date. Both sources display the same information — the last day of your birth month in your renewal year. If your license record shows an expiration date that does not match the last day of your birth month, contact the TDCI to verify and correct the record before your renewal deadline approaches.

Tennessee license renewal is a predictable, manageable compliance obligation for producers who know their deadline, complete CE well in advance, verify CE reporting before submitting, and submit renewal before the expiration date rather than after it — because Tennessee gives producers no no-fee grace period once that date passes. The producers who pay late fees, face reinstatement challenges, or require full relicensing are almost universally those who manage renewal reactively — responding to the deadline as it arrives rather than planning for it 90 days in advance. The three-alert calendar system, early CE completion, and address currency are the three practices that eliminate virtually every renewal compliance problem before it occurs.
