Most Oregon insurance producers complete their CE on time, every cycle. But sometimes life happens. Active military duty, serious illness, family emergencies, or other extenuating circumstances can affect your ability to complete CE before your renewal deadline. Oregon recognizes this and provides specific paths for license expiration extensions in qualifying situations.

Here's what Oregon producers should know about CE deadline extensions.

## The Standard Oregon CE Framework

Before discussing extensions, here's the baseline:

24 hours of CE every 2 years

3 hours Ethics

3 hours Oregon Statutes and Administrative Rules

Renewal deadline on the last day of your birth month

No carryover of excess hours

No course repetition within a 2-year period

Producers requesting extensions are asking for additional time beyond this standard framework due to specific circumstances.

## Oregon's Extension Authority

Oregon's authority to grant license expiration extensions is found primarily in OAR 836-071-0148 — Extended License Expiration Date, Active Military Duty and Other Extenuating Circumstances.

This rule recognizes that producers may face circumstances genuinely preventing CE completion or license renewal on the standard schedule. The DFR has discretion to grant extensions in qualifying situations.

## Active Military Duty Extensions

Oregon, consistent with federal law and other states, provides specific protections for licensees on active military duty.

Servicemembers Civil Relief Act (SCRA) protections. Active-duty military members may qualify for extensions on licensing requirements, including CE.

Deployment-related extensions. If military deployment affects your ability to complete CE on time, the DFR can grant license expiration extensions covering the deployment period.

Spousal considerations. Some protections may extend to military spouses whose lives are disrupted by their service member's deployment or relocation. Note that Oregon also offers temporary licenses for military spouses with valid out-of-state licenses through a separate process.

Documentation typically required:

Military orders confirming active duty status

Documentation of impact on ability to complete CE

Specific request for accommodation

For military-related questions, contact the Oregon DFR Producer Licensing at (503) 378-4140.

## Other Extenuating Circumstances

Beyond military duty, the DFR can grant license expiration extensions for "extenuating circumstances." Examples that may qualify include:

Serious medical conditions. Hospitalization, serious illness, or other medical situations preventing normal activities including CE completion.

Caregiving responsibilities. Caring for seriously ill family members where the demands prevent CE completion.

Major personal emergencies. Significant life disruptions affecting your ability to manage normal professional responsibilities.

Natural disasters. Wildfire, flood, or other natural disasters affecting your ability to access CE or normal business activities.

Documentation requirements typically include:

Specific description of the circumstance

Documentation of the situation (medical records, emergency documentation, etc.)

Demonstration of how the circumstance prevented CE completion

Specific request for extension

The DFR evaluates these requests case-by-case.

## What Extensions Don't Cover

Important to understand what doesn't typically qualify for extensions:

Forgetting your deadline. Simply missing your renewal date due to oversight doesn't qualify.

General busyness. Being too busy with normal work and life isn't grounds for extension.

Procrastination. Choosing not to complete CE on time isn't extenuating circumstance.

Financial difficulty. While CE costs money, financial difficulty alone typically isn't grounds for extension.

Lack of available courses. With Oregon's robust CE provider market, lack of availability rarely qualifies.

The DFR distinguishes between genuine inability to complete CE due to qualifying circumstances and producer choices to delay completion.

## How to Apply for an Extension

If you believe you qualify for a CE deadline extension:

Step 1 — Apply Early. Don't wait until after your deadline passes. Apply as soon as you recognize you may not be able to complete CE on time.

Step 2 — Contact the DFR. Reach out to Oregon Producer Licensing:

Phone: (503) 947-7981

Email: web.insagent@dcbs.oregon.gov

Address: 350 Winter St NE, Salem, OR 97301-3883

Step 3 — Submit Written Request. Provide a written request explaining:

Your specific circumstances

How they affected your ability to complete CE

The accommodation you're requesting

Supporting documentation

Step 4 — Provide Documentation. Bring documentation supporting your request:

Military orders for military-related requests

Medical records for medical hardship

Emergency documentation for personal emergencies

Disaster declarations or evidence for disaster-related requests

Step 5 — Follow Up. The DFR may need additional information to process your request. Respond promptly.

Step 6 — Document the Decision. Keep documentation of the DFR's decision for your records.

## Extensions vs. Reinstatement

Important distinction:

Extension — You receive additional time beyond your standard renewal deadline to complete CE and renew. Your license remains active during the extension period.

Reinstatement — Your license has expired (because you didn't renew on time and didn't have an extension). You're now reapplying for license restoration through Oregon's reinstatement procedures.

Extensions are far better than reinstatement. Apply for an extension before your deadline if you anticipate inability to complete CE.

## What Happens If Your Extension Is Granted

If the DFR approves your extension:

Specific extension period. You receive a defined extension period, typically tied to your circumstances.

License remains active. Your license stays active during the extension period.

CE completion required. You still need to complete the standard CE requirement before your extended deadline.

Documentation requirement. Keep documentation of your approved extension.

Follow-up. Some extensions require follow-up reporting on your circumstances.

## What Happens If Your Extension Is Denied

If the DFR denies your extension request, you have options:

Complete CE quickly. Even with a tight timeline, catching up may be faster than appeals.

Request reconsideration. With additional documentation or clarification, you may ask the DFR to reconsider.

Consider reinstatement. If your license has already expired, focus on reinstatement procedures.

Appeal. Oregon provides administrative appeals processes for licensing decisions.

## Standard Reinstatement Process

If your license expires without extension, Oregon's reinstatement procedures apply:

Within 12 months of expiration: Often less complex reinstatement available, including CE catch-up.

Beyond 12 months: May require complete reapplication including new exam.

The longer you let your license remain expired, the more difficult reinstatement becomes.

## Best Practices to Avoid Needing Extensions

Complete CE early. Don't wait until the final months of your cycle.

Track your deadline. Calendar your renewal date prominently.

Sign up for DFR notifications. Oregon provides renewal notices but you should track deadlines independently.

Spread CE across the cycle. Spreading hours reduces your vulnerability to last-minute issues.

Have backup plan. Know which providers can offer quick courses if you need to catch up.

Apply for extensions early. If circumstances arise that may affect CE completion, contact DFR early rather than waiting until your deadline.

## Common Extension Mistakes

Waiting until after deadline. The longer you wait, the more difficult resolution becomes.

Missing documentation. Extensions require specific documentation. Provide everything supporting your request.

Vague circumstances. Be specific about how circumstances affected your ability to complete CE.

Missing follow-up. If the DFR requests additional information, respond promptly.

Stopping CE while waiting for decision. Even while extension is being considered, completing CE you might need is the safer approach.

## Special Considerations for Oregon

Oregon's distinctive 3-hour law requirement. Even with extensions, you'll still need to complete the 3-hour Oregon law CE before final renewal.

Specialty training. Extensions typically don't excuse specialty training tied to products like LTC, Annuity Best Interest, or NFIP flood. Confirm current requirements with the Oregon DFR, since these remain required for product sales.

Multi-state licensing. If you hold licenses in multiple states, an Oregon extension doesn't automatically affect other state licenses. Verify with each state.

Reporting timing. Even with extensions, providers still need 1-15 business days to report CE. Don't wait until the last day of an extension to complete CE.

## 5 Frequently Asked Questions

- When can I qualify for an Oregon CE deadline extension? Active military duty and other extenuating circumstances (serious illness, caregiving responsibilities, natural disasters) may qualify. Routine reasons like forgetting or being busy don't qualify.
- How do I apply for an Oregon CE extension? Contact Oregon Producer Licensing at (503) 947-7981 or web.insagent@dcbs.oregon.gov with a written request, your specific circumstances, and supporting documentation.
- Should I apply before or after my deadline passes? Before. Apply as soon as you recognize you may not be able to complete CE on time. Waiting until after your deadline complicates the situation.
- What's the difference between an extension and reinstatement? Extensions give you more time before your license expires. Reinstatement is what you do after your license has already expired. Extensions are better when possible.
- Will the DFR grant me an extension if I just forgot my deadline? Generally no. Extensions are for genuine extenuating circumstances, not normal deadline misses. Forgetting your deadline typically requires reinstatement procedures, not extensions.
