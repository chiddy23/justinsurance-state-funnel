The Pennsylvania Insurance Department is the regulator behind every insurance producer license in the state — and understanding how the application process works helps you choose the right application path, submit a clean application, and avoid the pitfalls that delay other applicants. Pennsylvania's process involves choices: NIPR vs. Sircon vs. testing center kiosk submission, individual vs. business entity applications, single vs. multi-line applications. This guide walks through those decisions.

Here's a complete walkthrough of the Pennsylvania Insurance Department license application process.

## Who the Pennsylvania Insurance Department Is

The Pennsylvania Insurance Department regulates insurance throughout the Commonwealth of Pennsylvania. The Department:

Licenses insurance producers, adjusters, and other insurance professionals

Regulates insurance companies operating in Pennsylvania

Enforces insurance laws under the Pennsylvania Insurance Code (Title 40)

Investigates consumer complaints

Maintains comprehensive licensee database

Supports consumer education

## Decision 1: NIPR vs. Sircon vs. Testing Center Kiosk

You have three submission options for your application:

Option A: National Insurance Producer Registry (NIPR)

Apply through nipr.com. Advantages:

Standardized process across multiple states

Familiar interface for multi-state producers

Electronic submission with email confirmations

Streamlined for resident producer applications

Option B: Sircon

Apply through sircon.com/pennsylvania. Advantages:

Direct integration with Pennsylvania licensing systems

Often faster processing for Pennsylvania-specific applications

Pennsylvania Department of Insurance recommends Sircon for expedited processing

Option C: Testing Center Kiosk

Submit immediately after passing your exam at the PSI testing center. Advantages:

Most efficient — apply same day as passing

Bring credit card to exam to enable kiosk submission

Pay fingerprinting fee at the same time

Get fingerprinted at the same testing center if available

For most candidates, the testing center kiosk offers the fastest path. NIPR and Sircon both work well for those who don't apply at the testing center.

## Decision 2: Individual vs. Business Entity Application

Individual applicants apply for personal licenses. Business entities (agencies, corporations, LLCs) apply separately for entity licenses. If you operate through a business entity, both you AND the entity may need licenses.

For new producer applicants, start with the individual license. Business entity licensing comes later as your practice grows.

## Decision 3: Single vs. Multiple Lines of Authority

You must submit a separate application (and pay a separate filing fee) for each line of authority. Common decisions:

Apply for Life and Health together: Most common starting point. Either separate exams or combined exam, then single application for both lines.

Apply for Property & Casualty: Either separate Property and Casualty exams or combined P&C exam (150 questions, 2 hours 50 minutes), then application.

Apply for Personal Lines (instead of full P&C): If you'll only sell personal P&C (homeowners, auto), Personal Lines is a faster path than full P&C.

Apply for all four major lines: Most expensive but provides broadest market access.

Choose based on your career goals and target markets.

## Before You Apply

Before submitting your application, you should have:

Passed the Pennsylvania state exam(s) for each line you're applying for

Valid identification matching your application name exactly

Documentation for any disclosure answers

Prepared to pay application fees ($55 resident or $110 non-resident per line)

Decided on your application path

Your application won't be processed unless you've passed the relevant exams.

## Application Steps Through Sircon (Pennsylvania-Recommended Path)

Step 1: Visit sircon.com/pennsylvania.

Step 2: Create or log into your Sircon account.

Step 3: Select "Resident Producer Application" (or appropriate type).

Step 4: Complete the application form with:

Legal name (matching government-issued ID exactly)

Date of birth

SSN or FEIN

Pennsylvania home address

Contact details

Step 5: Specify your line(s) of authority based on exams completed.

Step 6: Answer disclosure questions honestly:

Criminal history (arrests, charges, convictions)

Prior license denials, suspensions, or revocations in any state

Regulatory actions

Civil judgments and significant lawsuits

Bankruptcy history

Insurance company terminations for cause

Step 7: Upload supporting documentation:

Court records for any "yes" disclosure answers

Documentation of prior regulatory actions

Other relevant supporting documents

Step 8: Pay the application fee ($55 for resident).

Step 9: Submit and track through Sircon.

## Application Steps Through NIPR

NIPR follows similar steps:

Step 1: Create or log into your NIPR account at nipr.com.

Step 2: Select Pennsylvania as your target state.

Step 3: Complete the Uniform Application form.

Step 4: Specify your line(s) of authority.

Step 5: Answer disclosure questions.

Step 6: Upload documentation through NIPR's Attachments Warehouse.

Step 7: Pay the application fee.

Step 8: Submit and track through NIPR.

## What the Pennsylvania Insurance Department Reviews

After submission, the Department reviews:

Your exam completion. PSI reports your exam results electronically.

Your fingerprint background check. FBI national criminal history results flow to the Department.

Your disclosure answers. Cross-referenced against available records.

Your application completeness. Missing information triggers requests for more.

Your documentation. For disclosure items, supporting materials are evaluated.

## Processing Times

Online applications (Sircon, NIPR, or testing center kiosk) typically process within 2-5 business days.

Applications with disclosures typically take longer depending on complexity.

Complex cases can take significantly longer as the Department evaluates whether to approve, deny, or approve with conditions.

You can track status using the Producer Application Status Lookup Tool at insurance.pa.gov.

## Common Application Mistakes

Name inconsistencies. Your name on application, exam registration, and fingerprint records must all match exactly. Discrepancies cause delays.

Incomplete disclosure answers. Answering "no" when "yes" is the honest answer creates serious problems.

Missing documentation. "Yes" disclosure answers typically require supporting documents.

Wrong IdentoGO Service Code. Using incorrect fingerprint Service Code (must use 1KGBGJ for Pennsylvania) invalidates fingerprints.

Submitting fingerprints before applying. Fingerprint results received without a corresponding license application will not be accepted.

Using cash for fingerprinting. IdentoGO does NOT accept cash or personal checks. Must use credit card, debit card, certified check, or money order.

## If Your Application Is Denied

If the Pennsylvania Insurance Department denies your application, you receive written notice explaining the reason. You have rights including:

Requesting a hearing to contest the denial

Appealing through Pennsylvania's administrative process

Consulting with an attorney familiar with Pennsylvania insurance licensing

Denial isn't always permanent. Some applicants successfully appeal or reapply after addressing concerns.

For applicants worried about criminal history, the Pre-Licensing Eligibility Review program allows assessing eligibility before formal application.

## If Your Application Is Approved

Approved applications result in an active Pennsylvania producer license. You'll receive:

Notification (typically electronic — Department no longer issues physical licenses)

Your license number

Your license effective date and renewal date

You can verify license status and print your license through the Pennsylvania Insurance Department website at insurance.pa.gov.

## Pennsylvania Reciprocity Through Act 147 of 2002

Pennsylvania has streamlined non-resident licensing through Act 147 of 2002 (effective June 4, 2003). Under this Act:

Non-resident producer applicants applying under reciprocity provisions are exempt from the Pennsylvania exam (in most cases)

Non-resident applicants are exempt from fingerprinting requirements

Streamlined application process

This makes Pennsylvania accessible for producers already licensed in other states.

## The Pennsylvania Department's "No More Letters of Certification" Policy

A useful Pennsylvania feature: the Department no longer requires nor issues Letters of Certification. If another state requires a Letter of Certification from Pennsylvania, the licensee can obtain it electronically through Sircon. This eliminates a common bureaucratic burden for multi-state producers.

## Making Changes After Approval

Once licensed, you must report changes to the Department:

Address changes. Update through Sircon, NIPR, or directly with the Department.

Name changes. Submit appropriate documentation.

Employer changes. Update through the standard channels.

Other changes. Various procedures depending on change type.

## 5 Frequently Asked Questions

- How long does the Pennsylvania Insurance Department take to process license applications? Typically 2-5 business days for online applications. Applications with disclosures take longer.
- How much is the Pennsylvania license application fee? $55 for resident producers, $110 for non-resident producers (plus small transaction charges). Business entity fees are similar.
- Can I apply at the testing center after passing my exam? Yes. Pennsylvania allows kiosk submission through Sircon or NIPR immediately after passing the exam at the testing center. Bring a credit card (Visa, MasterCard, or American Express) to enable this.
- Does Pennsylvania mail physical licenses? No. The Pennsylvania Insurance Department no longer mails physical licenses. Once your license is issued, you can print it from the Department's website at insurance.pa.gov.
- Are Letters of Certification required for Pennsylvania licensing? No. Pennsylvania no longer requires nor issues Letters of Certification. If another state requires one from Pennsylvania, obtain it electronically through Sircon.
