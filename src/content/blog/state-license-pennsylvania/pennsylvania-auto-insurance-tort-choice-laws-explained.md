Pennsylvania has one of the most distinctive auto insurance regulatory environments in the United States — operating as a "choice no-fault" state where drivers must choose between Limited Tort and Full Tort options when purchasing auto insurance. This Tort Choice system is unique to Pennsylvania (and a small number of other states) and fundamentally affects what compensation drivers can receive following accidents. For Pennsylvania producers serving auto insurance clients, understanding the Tort Choice system is essential.

Here's what Pennsylvania agents must know about Pennsylvania auto insurance Tort Choice laws.

## Pennsylvania's Choice No-Fault System

Pennsylvania operates as a "choice no-fault" state — distinct from both pure no-fault states and pure tort states. This hybrid system requires drivers to make important decisions about their coverage that have lasting consequences.

Key distinctive features:

Drivers MUST choose between Limited Tort and Full Tort options when purchasing auto insurance

Choice is governed by [75 Pa. C.S. §1705](https://codes.findlaw.com/pa/title-75-pacsa-vehicles/pa-csa-sect-75-1705/)

Default if no choice made: Full Tort

Choice affects ability to sue for non-economic damages (pain and suffering)

## Pennsylvania Auto Insurance Minimum Requirements

Pennsylvania's auto insurance minimums are among the lowest in the country, commonly referred to as "15/30/5":

Bodily Injury Liability:

$15,000 per person (one of the lowest minimums in the country)

$30,000 per accident

Property Damage Liability: $5,000 per accident

First Party Benefits (FPB) / Medical Benefits: $5,000 per person (Pennsylvania's version of PIP)

Uninsured/Underinsured Motorist (UM/UIM): NOT required by Pennsylvania law, but insurers MUST offer it. Rejecting requires written waiver.

These minimum requirements provide baseline legal compliance but leave drivers significantly underinsured for serious accidents. Many recommend at least 50/100/50 plus full tort.

## The Full Tort Option

Full Tort coverage preserves your full legal rights:

What it means:

Unrestricted right to seek financial compensation for ALL injuries caused by other drivers

Right to sue for medical expenses, lost wages, AND non-economic damages (pain and suffering)

No threshold required for non-economic damages

Default option if no choice is made

Premium impact:

Approximately 15-20% higher premium than Limited Tort

For average Pennsylvania driver, ~$200-300 annually more

When Full Tort is recommended:

Most drivers (insurance professionals strongly recommend Full Tort)

Those concerned about lifelong impact of injuries

Those who prefer predictable legal rights

Those willing to pay slightly more for full legal protection

## The Limited Tort Option

Limited Tort coverage restricts your legal rights in exchange for lower premiums:

What it means:

Right to recover medical expenses and out-of-pocket costs

NO right to sue for non-economic damages (pain and suffering) UNLESS injury meets "serious injury" threshold

Lower premium (typically 15-20% less than Full Tort)

Must specifically be requested

Serious injury exceptions (Limited Tort holders CAN sue for non-economic damages if):

Injury results in death

Injury results in serious impairment of bodily function

Injury results in permanent serious disfigurement

Injury caused by uninsured driver

Injury caused by driver under influence of alcohol or drugs

Injury caused by driver convicted of DUI or similar

Injury caused by vehicle defect (design, manufacturing, repair, maintenance)

Owner of uninsured vehicle injured as pedestrian

Driver of car not registered in Pennsylvania

Children (unlimited tort protection by public policy)

Risk of Limited Tort:

Significant injuries that don't meet the threshold receive no compensation for pain and suffering

Long-term complications may arise that aren't compensable

Insurance settlements can differ by tens of thousands of dollars based solely on tort selection

## How the Tort Choice Election Works

Per 75 Pa. C.S. §1705:

At policy issuance:

Insurers MUST provide written notice describing both Tort options

Notice is in standardized form adopted by the Commissioner

Applicant must be given opportunity to elect a tort option

Policy may not be issued until applicant has been provided opportunity to elect

At renewal:

Insurers MUST notify each insured of the availability of both options

Notice required [at least 45 days prior to first renewal](https://codes.findlaw.com/pa/title-75-pacsa-vehicles/pa-csa-sect-75-1705/)

Standardized form adopted by the Commissioner

If no choice is made:

A second notice is sent

If no response 10 days prior to renewal date

Insured is [conclusively presumed to have chosen Full Tort](https://codes.findlaw.com/pa/title-75-pacsa-vehicles/pa-csa-sect-75-1705/)

Multiple insureds:

[Any named insured may make the election for all insureds on the policy](https://codes.findlaw.com/pa/title-75-pacsa-vehicles/pa-csa-sect-75-1705/)

Choice binds all named insureds

Children's protection:

Children enjoy unlimited tort law protection by public policy

Cannot be bound to Limited Tort election

## Why Limited Tort Costs Less

Limited Tort premiums are lower because:

Insurers face less litigation exposure

Pain and suffering claims are common and expensive

Limiting lawsuits limits insurer payout exposure

Settlement amounts are often dramatically reduced

The 15-20% savings reflects this reduced exposure.

## Why Full Tort Is Often Recommended

Most insurance professionals recommend Full Tort because:

Cost is modest. $200-300 annually = less than $1/day for full legal protection.

Compensation difference is substantial. Identical injuries can produce settlements differing by tens of thousands of dollars based solely on tort selection.

Limited Tort exceptions are restrictive. "Serious injury" threshold is narrow — many real injuries don't meet it.

Long-term considerations matter. Injuries that develop over time (chronic pain, soft tissue) typically can't be sued for under Limited Tort.

You can't predict accidents. When you do need legal options, Limited Tort restrictions become apparent.

## Pennsylvania's "Serious Injury" Threshold

The two-part test for "serious injury" under Limited Tort:

Step 1: Determine whether the injury caused permanent serious impairment of bodily function.

Step 2: Apply objective standards considering:

Nature and extent of impairment

Duration of impairment

Treatment required

Effect on lifestyle and earning capacity

Many real-world injuries (whiplash, soft tissue injuries, temporary impairments) don't meet this standard.

## Coverage Considerations Beyond Tort Choice

Beyond Tort Choice, Pennsylvania auto insurance involves:

Increased Liability Limits. Pennsylvania's $15,000/$30,000/$5,000 minimums are dangerously low. Many recommend at least $50,000/$100,000/$50,000 with $100,000/$300,000/$100,000 ideal for clients with assets.

First Party Benefits (FPB). Pennsylvania's medical benefits coverage. $5,000 minimum but higher amounts often available and recommended.

Uninsured/Underinsured Motorist (UM/UIM). Not required but strongly recommended given approximately 1 in 8 Pennsylvania drivers is uninsured. Stacking available for multi-vehicle policies.

Collision and Comprehensive Coverage. Required by lenders for financed vehicles. Optional for owned vehicles.

Stacking. UM/UIM stacking available for multi-vehicle policies — important consideration for households with multiple vehicles.

## Producer Compliance Considerations

Pennsylvania auto insurance producers must:

Provide tort choice notice. Required notification at policy issuance and renewal under 75 Pa. C.S. §1705.

Use standardized forms. Pennsylvania-required tort choice forms adopted by the Commissioner.

Document choices. Maintain records of clients' tort choice elections.

Explain tradeoffs. Help clients understand cost/benefit of each option.

Ensure 45-day renewal notification. Insurers must provide notice at least 45 days before renewal.

Present second notice if no response. Required when client doesn't respond to first notice.

## Common Pennsylvania Auto Insurance Pitfalls

Selling Limited Tort to clients who don't understand restrictions. Most clients don't realize how restrictive Limited Tort is until they have a claim.

Not providing required tort notice. Required notice at issuance and renewal.

Selling minimum coverage to clients with assets. $15,000/$30,000/$5,000 is dangerously low for most clients.

Missing UM/UIM coverage. Not required but strongly recommended given Pennsylvania uninsured driver rates.

Inadequate explanation of FPB vs. health insurance coordination. First Party Benefits work differently than health insurance.

Failing to discuss stacking. Important consideration for multi-vehicle households.

## 5 Frequently Asked Questions

- What's Pennsylvania's auto insurance minimum requirement? $15,000 per person / $30,000 per accident bodily injury liability ("15/30/5"), $5,000 property damage liability, and $5,000 First Party Benefits per person. Uninsured/Underinsured Motorist coverage is offered but not required.
- What's the difference between Limited Tort and Full Tort? Full Tort preserves unrestricted right to sue for all damages including pain and suffering. Limited Tort restricts pain and suffering lawsuits unless injury meets "serious injury" threshold. Full Tort costs approximately 15-20% more but provides full legal protection.
- What happens if I don't make a tort choice? If no choice is made within the timeframe specified by 75 Pa. C.S. §1705, you're conclusively presumed to have chosen Full Tort. This default protects your full legal rights.
- Can children be bound to Limited Tort? No. By public policy, children enjoy unlimited tort law protection regardless of the parent's tort election.
- What injuries meet the "serious injury" threshold under Limited Tort? Death, serious impairment of bodily function, or permanent serious disfigurement. Common minor injuries (whiplash, soft tissue injuries, temporary pain) typically do NOT meet this threshold.
