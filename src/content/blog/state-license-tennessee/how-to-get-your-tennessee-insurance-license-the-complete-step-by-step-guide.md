Getting a Tennessee insurance license is a four-step process: pass the state exam, complete fingerprinting and a background check, submit your application through NIPR, and receive your license from the Tennessee Department of Commerce and Insurance. What makes Tennessee's process distinctive is what is absent from that list — there is no mandatory [prelicensing](/tennessee/prelicensing) education requirement. As of March 21, 2023, Tennessee eliminated its formal prelicensing hour requirement, making it one of a small number of states where candidates can go directly to the exam without completing a prescribed course. That structural difference makes Tennessee one of the fastest paths to an insurance license in the country for a well-prepared candidate. This guide covers every step in the exact sequence you need to follow, every fee you will pay, every deadline you need to know, and the decisions that determine how long the process takes.

## Before You Begin: Choose Your Line of Authority

Tennessee issues producer licenses by individual line of authority. Before you schedule an exam or take any other step, you need to decide which line or lines you intend to pursue — because each line requires a separate exam and a separate license application with a separate fee.

The major lines available in Tennessee:

Life — Covers life insurance products including term, whole, universal, variable, and annuities. Required for producers who sell any life insurance or annuity product.

Accident and Health (A&H) — Covers health insurance, disability income, long-term care, and accident products. Required for producers who sell individual or group health insurance, Medicare supplement, or disability income products.

Property — Covers insurance for physical property — homeowners, dwelling, commercial property, inland marine, fire. Required for producers who sell any property coverage.

Casualty — Covers liability insurance — auto liability, commercial general liability, workers' compensation, umbrella. Required for producers who sell liability products.

Personal Lines — A streamlined alternative to separate Property and Casualty licenses for producers who intend to sell personal auto and homeowners only. Does not authorize commercial lines placement.

Variable Products — Required in addition to Life authority for producers who sell variable life insurance and variable annuities. Requires a separate FINRA Series 6 or Series 7 registration as well.

The most common combinations:

Most producers pursue one of three combinations at initial licensing:

Life and A&H together — for producers focused on health insurance, life insurance, disability income, Medicare products, and employee benefits

Property and Casualty together — for producers focused on personal lines auto and homeowners, commercial lines, and workers' compensation

All four major lines — for producers who want the full commercial and personal lines practice from day one

Why the combination decision matters at the start: Tennessee charges $50 per line for the license application. Taking all four lines at initial licensing costs $200 in application fees — but saves the time and cost of adding lines later. Adding a line of authority after initial licensing requires passing an additional exam and paying an additional application fee. Producers who are certain of their career direction should apply for all intended lines at the start rather than in stages.

## Step 1: Prepare for and Schedule the Pearson VUE Exam

Tennessee administers all insurance licensing exams through Pearson VUE — not PSI, which is used in many other states. This is a Tennessee-specific distinction that matters when selecting study materials, because exam prep courses built around PSI's platform differ slightly in interface and format from Pearson VUE's platform. Confirm that any prep course or [practice exam](/tennessee/practice-exam) you use is specifically designed for Pearson VUE and for Tennessee.

### Exam Format by Line

Pretest questions: Nine questions on every Tennessee insurance exam are unscored pretest questions — they are being evaluated for future exam use and do not affect your score. You cannot identify which questions are pretest. Answer every question as though it is scored.

The pass score: You must score 70% or higher on the scored questions to pass. On an exam with 68 scored questions, you must answer at least 48 correctly. Scores are reported on a scaled basis — Pearson VUE converts your raw score to a standardized scale, so the exact number of questions you can miss varies slightly by exam version.

Score report: Pearson VUE delivers your score report immediately after you complete the exam. You know your result before leaving the testing center or closing your remote testing session. There is no waiting period for results.

Retakes: Tennessee allows unlimited retakes — there is no cap on the number of attempts — but a mandatory waiting period applies between attempts: 10 days after your first failure and 30 days after each subsequent failure. Each retake also requires paying the full exam fee again — $59 per attempt per line per attempt. The financial cost of repeated attempts is a genuine incentive to prepare thoroughly before your first attempt. First-time [pass rates](/pass-rates) for the Tennessee exam are approximately 55–65%, reflecting the challenge of exam preparation without mandatory structured prelicensing.

### Taking Multiple Exams in One Sitting

Tennessee allows candidates to sit for multiple line exams in a single testing session. A candidate pursuing both Property and Casualty licenses can take both exams back to back in one sitting — paying separate exam fees for each line but completing both in a single scheduling and travel event. This is the most efficient approach for candidates who want a full P&C license.

### Scheduling the Exam

Remote (OnVUE): Available from any location with a compatible computer, webcam, and reliable internet connection. Fee: $49 per line. You must verify your computer's compatibility with Pearson VUE's OnVUE system before scheduling — visit home.pearsonvue.com/tn/insurance to run the compatibility check. If your computer is not compatible, you must take the exam at a Pearson VUE test center.

In-person test centers: Available at Pearson VUE testing locations throughout Tennessee. The Public Adjuster exam is only available at the Knoxville, Memphis, and Nashville test centers — not at all locations. Fee: $59 per line.

Scheduling: Visit pearsonvue.com/tn/insurance or call (800) 274-4957. Payment is required at the time of scheduling by credit card, debit card, voucher, or electronic check.

Cancellation and rescheduling: Cancel or reschedule at least 48 hours before your appointment to avoid forfeiting the exam fee. Cancellations or changes within 48 hours of the scheduled exam result in forfeiture of the full fee.

### Exam Content: What Is Tested

Every Tennessee insurance licensing exam has two sections:

General section: Tests knowledge of insurance concepts, products, and principles that apply across all states — policy structure, types of coverage, underwriting concepts, claims handling, risk management fundamentals, and the insurance contract.

State law section: Tests knowledge specific to Tennessee insurance law, regulations, and the Tennessee Department of Commerce and Insurance — the Tennessee Code Annotated (TCA) Title 56 provisions that govern insurance in the state, producer conduct requirements, licensing rules, unfair trade practices, and Tennessee-specific coverage requirements.

The state law section is frequently the section where candidates who studied general insurance concepts but not Tennessee-specific law encounter difficulty. Studying Tennessee's specific statutory framework — the TDCI's regulatory authority, the unfair trade practices provisions, the auto insurance minimum requirements, and the workers' compensation threshold rules — is as important as studying general insurance concepts.

## Step 2: Complete Fingerprinting and Background Check

Tennessee requires all resident producer applicants to submit fingerprints and consent to a criminal background check through both the Tennessee Bureau of Investigation (TBI) and the FBI. This step must be completed after you pass the state exam and before or concurrent with submitting your license application.

The fingerprinting provider: IdentoGO (not a Pearson VUE function — a separate agency). Schedule your appointment at identogo.com.

How to schedule:

Visit identogo.com

Select "Schedule a New Appointment"

Select "Department of Commerce and Insurance"

Select "Insurance Producers" from the dropdown

Enter your zip code to find the nearest location

Confirm the Service Code and ORI number: TN920680Z (Transaction Type: IP)

The fingerprinting fee: $37.15, paid at the time of the appointment.

Timing requirement: Fingerprints must be submitted at least 2 business days before you submit your license application. Do not submit your NIPR application before your fingerprinting appointment has been completed and the 2-business-day window has passed.

The Fingerprint Policy and Acknowledgement Form: In addition to scheduling your IdentoGO appointment, you must read the Fingerprint-Based Criminal History Record Check Policy and submit the signed Fingerprint Policy and Acknowledgement Form (a one-page document) to the TDCI Agent Licensing Section. Submit this form by email to ce.agent.licensing@tn.gov (confirm whether a fax option is currently available on the TDCI's agent-producer resources page). You can do this while or after submitting your NIPR application — but the names on your NIPR application and the Fingerprint Policy and Acknowledgement Form must match exactly.

Criminal history: Any misdemeanors, felonies, or current criminal proceedings may affect the outcome of your licensing application. If any of these apply to you, you are required to file your application directly with the Tennessee Department of Commerce and Insurance rather than through the standard NIPR electronic process. Contact the TDCI Agent Licensing Section at (615) 741-2241 before proceeding if your background includes any criminal history.

## Step 3: Submit Your License Application Through NIPR

After passing the exam and completing fingerprinting, submit your Tennessee resident producer license application through the National Insurance Producer Registry (NIPR) at nipr.com.

The mandatory waiting period: You must wait at least 48 hours after passing the exam before submitting your NIPR application. Do not attempt to submit immediately after your exam — the 48-hour window is a TDCI requirement and applications submitted before it expires will not be processed correctly.

Application fee: $50 per line of authority. A candidate applying for both Property and Casualty pays $100. A candidate applying for all four major lines pays $200. The $5.60 NIPR transaction processing fee applies in addition to the state application fee.

The NIPR application process:

Create or log in to your NIPR account at nipr.com

Select Tennessee as your resident state

Select the line(s) of authority for which you are applying

Complete the required disclosures and background questions

Pay the application fee by credit or debit card

Submit — your application enters the TDCI's processing queue

Processing time: Applications are typically processed within 2–5 business days when submitted online through NIPR or the TDCI portal. Some applications take up to 15 days depending on background check status and TDCI workload. You will receive confirmation of your license issuance by email once your application is approved.

What your license number looks like: Tennessee licenses are issued with a National Producer Number (NPN) assigned by NIPR that is used for all future licensing transactions including CE reporting and renewal.

## Step 4: Receive Your License and Get Appointed

Once the TDCI approves your application, your Tennessee producer license is active. You can verify your license status at the TDCI's online license lookup tool at tn.gov/commerce/insurance.

The appointment requirement: A Tennessee insurance license authorizes you to be appointed by insurers — it does not by itself authorize you to transact insurance business. Before you sell, solicit, or negotiate insurance for any specific carrier, that carrier must file an appointment with the TDCI on your behalf. The carrier handles the appointment filing electronically; you need to coordinate with your agency or directly with each carrier to ensure appointments are in place before you transact business.

Timing of appointment: Tennessee requires that an insurer file a notice of appointment within 15 days of the date the agency contract is executed or the first insurance application is submitted, whichever is earlier.

The employer or agency relationship: Most new producers enter insurance either through a captive agency system (which handles appointments with the carrier automatically at hiring) or through an independent agency (which manages appointments across multiple carriers). Confirm with your employer or agency principal that all required appointments are filed before you begin writing business.

## The Complete Cost Summary

Optional but strongly recommended: a Pearson VUE exam prep course. Given Tennessee's first-time pass rate of approximately 55–65%, the cost of a prep course — typically $100–$350 depending on provider and tier — is substantially less than the cost of one or more retake exam fees plus the time cost of delayed licensure.

## The Complete Timeline

Typical timeline for a well-prepared candidate:

The fastest path for a candidate who has studied thoroughly, passes the exam on the first attempt, and has no background check complications is approximately 10–14 business days from exam day to license issuance. Candidates who retake the exam or whose background check requires additional review can expect 4–6 weeks or longer.

## Tennessee's Unique Characteristics Worth Knowing

No prelicensing requirement — but study anyway: Tennessee eliminated mandatory prelicensing in March 2023. This means you can sit for the exam tomorrow if you choose. It does not mean the exam is easy without preparation — first-time pass rates of 55–65% reflect the real difficulty of the material. The candidates who pass on the first attempt are almost universally those who completed a structured prep course even though it was not required. The financial and time cost of a retake exam fee — plus the delay in getting licensed — makes voluntary exam preparation the economically rational choice.

Separate exams for each line: Tennessee does not offer a combined Life and Health exam or a combined Property and Casualty exam. Each line requires a separate exam session with a separate fee. Candidates pursuing multiple lines must schedule and pay for each line individually, though they can complete multiple exams in a single testing day.

Pearson VUE — not PSI: Most insurance exam prep resources in the country are built around PSI, which administers exams in the majority of states. Tennessee uses Pearson VUE. Confirm that your prep course is Pearson VUE-aligned before purchasing. Pearson VUE's interface, question presentation style, and timing system differ from PSI's, and preparation specifically calibrated to Pearson VUE's format improves your test-day experience.

Broad reciprocity with other states: Tennessee maintains reciprocity agreements with other states, so producers licensed elsewhere — including in neighboring Georgia, North Carolina, Kentucky, Alabama, Arkansas, Missouri, and Virginia — can generally obtain a Tennessee non-resident license without retaking the Tennessee exam, provided their home state license is active, in good standing, and covers the matching lines of authority. Confirm your specific home state's reciprocity status with the TDCI before applying.

No state income tax: Tennessee eliminated its Hall income tax on investment income in 2021 and has no personal income tax on wages or salaries. This makes Tennessee an attractive state for high-income professionals and creates a specific market opportunity for life insurance and annuity products designed for tax-advantaged savings — because residents who pay no state income tax on investment income are particularly receptive to tax-deferred and tax-advantaged financial products.

## Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need to complete a prelicensing course before taking the Tennessee insurance exam?

No. As of March 21, 2023, Tennessee does not require any prelicensing education hours before sitting for the state exam. You can schedule your Pearson VUE exam immediately without completing any course. However, the absence of a requirement does not mean preparation is unnecessary. First-time pass rates for the Tennessee exam are approximately 55–65%, meaning roughly one in three candidates fails on their first attempt without adequate preparation. Every failed attempt costs you the full exam fee again and delays your licensure. Most candidates who approach the exam without structured preparation discover on test day that the material — particularly the Tennessee state law section — is more complex than expected. A prep course, while optional, is strongly recommended and typically produces the highest return on investment of any step in the licensing process.

I am currently licensed in Georgia. Do I need to retake the Tennessee exam to get a Tennessee non-resident license?

No, in most cases. Tennessee maintains reciprocity agreements with other states, and as a Georgia resident producer in good standing you can generally obtain a Tennessee non-resident license through NIPR without retaking the Tennessee exam, provided your Georgia license covers the same lines of authority you are applying for in Tennessee. The process is the same as for a Tennessee resident except that non-resident applicants do not go through the TDCI fingerprinting requirement — that applies to resident applicants only. Confirm your current reciprocity status with the TDCI before applying, since terms can vary by line.

Can I hold both a Property and a Casualty license without a Personal Lines license, and do I need Personal Lines separately?

Personal Lines is a separate, streamlined license for producers who sell only personal auto and homeowners — it is not required when you hold both Property and Casualty. If you hold Property and Casualty authority, you are authorized to transact both personal and commercial lines within each. The Personal Lines license is the appropriate choice only for producers who are certain they will never place commercial lines business and who want a lower-cost initial license. Most producers who intend to build any commercial book are better served by obtaining full Property and Casualty authority from the start rather than a Personal Lines license that they will later need to upgrade by passing additional exams.

Tennessee's insurance licensing process is genuinely accessible — no mandatory prelicensing, immediate score results, multiple lines testable in one sitting, and a 2–4 week total timeline for well-prepared candidates. The combination of Tennessee's no-prelicensing structure, Pearson VUE exam format, and 48-hour application window creates a process that rewards preparation and penalizes improvisation. Candidates who study thoroughly, schedule their exam with confidence, complete fingerprinting promptly after passing, and submit their NIPR application within the 48-hour window reach their license faster and at lower total cost than those who approach any step without preparation.
