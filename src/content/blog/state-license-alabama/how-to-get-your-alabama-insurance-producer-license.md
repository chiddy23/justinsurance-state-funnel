Getting your Alabama insurance producer license opens the door to one of the Southeast's steady insurance markets. The Alabama Department of Insurance (ALDOI) has a clear licensing process — and thanks to recent regulatory changes, it's now one of the more streamlined paths in the country.

Here's exactly how to get your Alabama insurance producer license.

## Step 1: Meet the Basic Eligibility Requirements

Before starting, confirm you meet Alabama's baseline requirements:

You must be at least 18 years old

You must be an Alabama resident, or qualify for a non-resident license

You must be able to pass a fingerprint-based background check

You must truthfully complete the license application and disclose any relevant history

There's no degree requirement, no prior insurance experience needed.

## Step 2: Prelicense Education Is No Longer Required

This is important and different from most states: Effective January 1, 2024 (under Alabama Act No. 2023-104), Alabama no longer requires prelicense education for insurance license applicants.

That said, prelicense education is still highly recommended because:

Alabama's exams are challenging without structured preparation

Pass rates are significantly higher for candidates who complete prelicense courses

Understanding content thoroughly helps your actual career, not just passing the exam

Most candidates still complete a prelicense course — they just aren't required to by state law.

## Step 3: Register for and Pass the State Exam

Alabama's insurance licensing exams are administered through The University of Alabama (this is unique to Alabama — most states use Pearson VUE or PSI).

Register at training.ua.edu/insurance-testing

Call (205) 348-3000 or email insurance@ua.edu for support

Schedule at least 7 days (5 business days) before your desired exam date

Pay the exam fee at registration by credit or debit card

Remote online testing may be available for certain exams.

## Exam Formats by License Type

Life Insurance: 125 questions, 2 hours 30 minutes

Health Insurance: 100 questions, 2 hours

Combined Life & Health: 150 questions, 3 hours

Property Insurance: 100 questions, 2 hours

Casualty/P&C combined exams: varies

Passing score: 70% on every exam.

Your examination is graded immediately upon submission. If you pass, you receive a passing certificate. Your passing results are electronically reported to the ALDOI within 3-5 days.

## Step 4: Complete Fingerprinting and Background Check

All Alabama individual resident applicants must complete a fingerprint-based background check.

Important timing: Alabama recommends getting fingerprinted after passing the exam and one day before applying for the license.

Why? Fingerprints only remain in the ALDOI system for 30 days after being taken. If you get fingerprinted too early and delay applying, you'll have to get re-fingerprinted.

Fingerprints completed for any reason outside pursuing an Alabama insurance license won't be accepted. You need insurance-specific fingerprinting through the ALDOI's designated vendor.

## Step 5: Submit Your License Application

Alabama license applications are typically submitted through:

NIPR (National Insurance Producer Registry) at nipr.com

SBS (State Based Systems) for certain license types

Your application includes:

Confirmation of exam completion (reported electronically from the University of Alabama)

Background check clearance via fingerprinting

Disclosure of any criminal history, regulatory actions, or bankruptcies

Application fee payment

Answer disclosure questions honestly. The ALDOI cross-references against background check results.

Your passing exam certificate is valid for one year — you must file your online license application within that window.

## Step 6: ALDOI Reviews and Issues Your License

Once the ALDOI has your complete application, exam results, and background check, they review everything together.

After 5 days from submitting the application, you can check aldoi.gov and go to License Search under Producers/Agents to confirm issuance.

Once issued, you can print your license directly from the ALDOI website.

## Step 7: Get Appointed by Insurance Carriers

Your Alabama producer license authorizes you to be licensed, but you need appointments with insurance carriers before you can sell their products. Carriers typically handle the appointment process once you've contracted with them.

## Total Timeline

From starting exam preparation to holding your active license, most Alabama candidates finish in 3 to 8 weeks. Without mandatory prelicense requirements, Alabama is one of the faster licensing paths in the country.

## Alabama License Lines of Authority

Common Alabama insurance producer license types include:

Life — life insurance products

Accident and Health — health, disability, long-term care

Life and Accident & Health combined

Property — property insurance coverage

Casualty — liability and related coverage

Personal Lines — limited P&C for personal (non-commercial) insurance

Variable Life and Variable Annuity — requires existing Life license plus securities credentials

## 5 Frequently Asked Questions

- Do I really not need prelicense education in Alabama? Correct — as of January 1, 2024, Alabama no longer requires prelicense education (per Alabama Act No. 2023-104). However, prelicense courses are still highly recommended because Alabama's exams are challenging without structured preparation.
- Why does Alabama use the University of Alabama for exams? Alabama partners with the University of Alabama to administer insurance licensing exams rather than using private testing providers. It's a long-standing arrangement unique to Alabama.
- What happens if I fail the Alabama state exam? You can retake it. Alabama allows multiple retake attempts, though each requires new registration and fee payment. Review your score report to focus your preparation on weak areas.
- How long is my Alabama license valid? Alabama producer licenses renew every 2 years on a biennial cycle tied to your birth month and birth year (odd/even).
- Does my Alabama passing exam score expire? Yes. You must file your license application within 1 year of your exam date. Otherwise, you'll need to retake the exam.
