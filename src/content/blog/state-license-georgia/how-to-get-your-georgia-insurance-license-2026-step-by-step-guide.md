If you line up Georgia’s producer steps in the wrong order—exam before fingerprints, or an application missing the notarized citizenship affidavit—you can burn weeks while appointments sit in limbo. Resident applications now run through Sircon (Vertafore), exams run through Pearson VUE, and [prelicensing](/georgia/prelicensing) hour totals changed under a July 2025 rule package. Carriers will not let you sell until Sircon shows an active license number, so treating this like a compliance project—not a weekend cram session—protects your comp cycles. Below is the sequence Georgia’s Office of Commissioner of Insurance and Safety Fire (OCI) expects for a typical life, accident and sickness, or property and casualty resident producer path, with numbers you can verify before you spend money. Keep OCI’s pages and Pearson’s handbook PDF open in two browser tabs while you work; they change when the legislature tweaks Title 33 rules.

## Choose the Georgia lines that match what you’ll sell

Georgia separates authority into distinct courses and exams. OCI lists common resident paths such as Life, Accident and Sickness (often paired with a combination course), Property and Casualty (which Georgia treats as a paired property and casualty authority), Personal Lines, Credit (with its own narrow education path), and others on its [resident licensing overview](https://oci.georgia.gov/get-insurance-agent-license). Credit is unusual: OCI specifies **5 hours** of self-study, a $**120** application fee for that class, electronic fingerprints, and a proctored final exam through the school before you move to the exam vendor. Temporary resident licenses run **6 months** on first issue, renewable in **3**-month extensions, capped at **15 months** total from the first issue date, with an **$80** fee on the OCI fee schedule for that path.

Most producers targeting retail life or health work focus on Life, Accident and Sickness, or the Life + Accident combination track. Property producers should note Georgia’s pairing requirement for property and casualty under the same overview.

## Finish prelicensing under the current 8.0-hour rule

OCI’s education team publishes the controlling language in the Georgia Administrative Code. Rule **120-2-3-.08(1)** now sets **8.0** clock hours of prelicensing for each major line, and **120-2-3-.08(2)** mirrors **8.0** hours for limited subagents on life, accident and sickness, or property and casualty lines, as summarized on OCI’s [education providers page](https://oci.georgia.gov/agents-agency-licensing/education-providers-courses-and-instructors). That page also links the official rule text on the Secretary of State rules site for auditors who want the verbatim citation.

If you started coursework under the older hour model, finish the exam quickly: OCI states exam scores remain valid for **12 months** according to NIPR’s [Georgia licensing overview](https://nipr.com/licensing-center/state-information/georgia), and Pearson VUE issues the score report you upload through Sircon.

## Schedule Pearson VUE and budget the $63 exam fee

Georgia contracts with Pearson VUE for agent testing. OCI’s licensing page lists a **$63** examination fee payable by card, debit, or prepaid voucher at scheduling time, and requires two forms of ID that match registration exactly. The same page links the [Georgia Insurance Licensing Candidate Handbook](https://www.pearsonvue.com/content/dam/VUE/vue/en/documents/publications/121100.pdf) for seat rules, tutorial timing, and line-specific exam lengths (OCI notes **1 or 2** hour administrations depending on the test you sit for).

Arrive **30** minutes early; if you forget acceptable ID, you forfeit the fee. After you pass, keep the score report—Sircon uploads depend on it.

## Apply on Sircon, then complete fingerprints on Georgia’s timeline

OCI states that, as of **April 1**, resident applications must be submitted through [Sircon’s Georgia portal](https://www.sircon.com/landingPages/states/georgia/content.jsp). Non-residents may still use Sircon or NIPR, but residents no longer initial-apply through NIPR’s LicenseHub.

Fingerprint instructions on OCI’s licensing page point to Identogo with service code **2TGJ6B** once your application is in motion. Do not schedule prints before you understand OCI’s sequencing—OCI warns that incomplete packets stall review and that staff communicates through Sircon, not random email attachments.

You must also upload the notarized citizenship affidavit package OCI links from the same page; missing affidavits are a frequent delay source for first-time applicants.

## Compare Georgia’s hour load with neighbors so you plan study correctly

Georgia’s **8.0**-hour-per-major-line rule sits far below Ohio’s **20**-hour-per-line classroom, distance learning, or self-study requirement documented on the Ohio Department of Insurance [pre-licensing education page](https://insurance.ohio.gov/wps/portal/gov/odi/agents-and-agencies/agent-education/pre-licensing-education). Illinois sits in the middle: the Illinois Department of Insurance states **20** hours per line with **7.5** hours in a classroom or live web class for most major lines on its [resident producer instructions](https://idoi.illinois.gov/producers/licensescertificationsfaqs/become-resident-producer.html). Use those contrasts to budget study time if you recruit producers moving between states—you cannot assume one course satisfies another state’s clock-hour rule.

## Know the waiver paths OCI spells out so you do not repeat coursework

OCI lists narrow waivers that catch people by surprise. Life and accident producers may skip prelicensing and testing if they hold **CLU** or **FLMI** and attach proof, or if they held the same lines in another state, have an active license, attach an original clearance letter, and apply within **90** days of moving to Georgia. Personal lines waivers mention **CPCU**, and property and casualty waivers mirror the same out-of-state move rules. If you think you qualify, assemble the designation certificate or clearance letter before you touch Sircon—OCI rejects piecemeal mailed documents once the portal workflow starts.

## Variable products and personal lines have extra guardrails

OCI’s page reminds variable applicants they need either a passing Georgia life score or an active Georgia life license before variable authority makes sense. Personal lines applicants face the same out-of-state waiver documentation rules as property and casualty producers. If you are building a recruiting plan for a multiline agency, sequence life first when variable business is on the roadmap, then add personal lines once property and casualty authority is in place.

## Use OCI’s published phones when education data looks wrong in a vendor catalog

Pearson VUE lists **800-274-8969** on OCI’s licensing page as the first-line help desk for agent education questions, with a separate **888-274-1212** education line referenced on the OCI education providers page for agent inquiries. Providers also maintain a dedicated inbox for course approvals. When a school still advertises outdated hour totals after the **120-2-3-.08** change, send the candidate to OCI’s published contacts rather than trusting a PDF footer from last year.

## After issuance: CE still gates renewals

This article stops at issuance, but Georgia renewals still ride on OCI’s [continuing education](/georgia/continuing-education) rules, which the same education team updates alongside prelicensing. Once licensed, track OCI’s CE bulletins—OCI’s education page notes CE rule tweaks (for example, caps on self-study vs classroom credits) that differ from prelicensing. Our [Georgia continuing education](https://justinsuranceco.com/georgia/continuing-education) page tracks how producers report credits, while the national [CE hub](https://justinsuranceco.com/continuing-education) explains how reciprocity works when you add nonresident states.

## Build a study plan that survives retakes and background review

Pearson VUE gives you an immediate pass/fail printout, but OCI still reviews criminal history hits tied to your Identogo submission. Budget time between exam day and your first appointment so you are not promising commissions to a carrier while Sircon still shows “pending.” If you fail an exam, OCI’s page is explicit: you return to prelicensing completion, secure a fresh certificate if your school requires it, and pay another **$63** attempt fee when you reschedule. That is why schools push proctored finals—Georgia wants proof you finished the clock hours before you burn exam fees.

Layer vendor-neutral reading on top of your provider’s slides. OCI’s linked Pearson handbook explains exam mechanics (tutorial time, break rules, on-screen calculators if any). Pair that with our [study guide](https://justinsuranceco.com/study-guide) so you are not seeing insurance concepts for the first time inside a Pearson item set. If you already hold another state license and only need Georgia as a nonresident, NIPR’s workflow still demands that Georgia has proof of the right exam waivers—double-check the special instructions block on NIPR’s [Georgia page](https://nipr.com/licensing-center/state-information/georgia) before you assume reciprocity clears testing.

## Bottom line

Map your Georgia lines, finish **8.0**-hour (or credit **5**-hour) prelicensing from an OCI-approved provider, pass Pearson VUE (**$63** per attempt), then drive the Sircon application, affidavit, and Identogo code **2TGJ6B** in the order OCI lists.
