South Carolina coastal homeowners insurance involves specific rules, coverage structures, and consumer protections that differ significantly from standard inland homeowners coverage. For producers serving coastal clients — and for anyone building a coastal insurance practice — understanding these rules is essential to provide appropriate coverage and avoid costly compliance mistakes.

Here's what South Carolina agents need to know about coastal homeowners insurance rules.

## Where "Coastal" Starts in South Carolina

South Carolina's coastal insurance rules primarily affect properties in designated coastal territories, generally in these counties:

Horry County (Myrtle Beach, North Myrtle Beach, Conway, Little River, Surfside Beach)

Georgetown County (Pawleys Island, Murrells Inlet, Georgetown)

Charleston County (Charleston, Mount Pleasant, Isle of Palms, Sullivan's Island, Folly Beach, Kiawah Island, Seabrook Island)

Colleton County (Edisto Beach area)

Beaufort County (Hilton Head Island, Beaufort, Fripp Island, Harbor Island)

Jasper County (along the Savannah River coastal area)

The specific boundaries of "coastal" for insurance purposes are defined by SCWHA territory designations and individual carrier underwriting territories.

## Coverage Structure for Coastal Properties

Coastal homeowners insurance typically involves multiple components:

Homeowners Policy (Standard HO-3 or similar). Covers the dwelling, other structures, personal property, liability, and additional living expenses. In coastal areas, typically excludes wind/hurricane and flood damage.

Windstorm Coverage. Obtained separately through SCWHA or, where available, from private carriers. Covers damage from wind, hurricanes, and related perils.

Flood Insurance. Separate NFIP or private flood policy covering flood damage, which homeowners policies universally exclude.

Dwelling Form Variations. For rental properties, second homes, or seasonal use, different policy forms may apply.

This layered coverage structure catches many coastal property buyers off guard. Helping clients understand what they need — and don't need — is central to a coastal agent's role.

## SCWHA Eligibility and Coverage

When private carriers won't provide wind coverage on a coastal property, SCWHA becomes the placement option:

Eligibility. Properties in designated SCWHA territories that cannot obtain wind coverage in the private market.

Coverage limits. SCWHA has maximum coverage limits that may be lower than the property's value. High-value coastal properties may need additional private excess coverage.

Rating. Based on specific SCWHA rate structures reflecting territory, construction, mitigation features, and property characteristics.

Exclusions. SCWHA excludes perils it doesn't cover (anything other than wind and hail), specific property types, and other defined exclusions.

Renewal. Annual renewal with specific underwriting review.

## The Wind and Hail Challenge for Private Market

Many private homeowners insurers limit or exclude wind coverage for South Carolina coastal properties due to:

Concentrated catastrophic exposure

Difficulty modeling hurricane risk accurately

Reinsurance market dynamics

Historical loss experience

This creates a coverage gap that SCWHA fills — but agents must navigate this landscape to construct appropriate coverage for coastal clients.

## Flood Coverage Requirements

Flood coverage is essential for most South Carolina coastal properties:

Special Flood Hazard Areas (SFHAs). Properties in designated flood zones (typically beginning with "A" or "V") with federally-backed mortgages are generally required to maintain flood insurance.

V zones (Velocity zones). Coastal areas subject to wave action — highest-risk flood zones with specific construction and rating considerations.

A zones. Other high-risk flood areas including inland flooding.

X zones. Lower-risk areas where flood insurance isn't typically mandated but may still be advisable.

Coverage limits. NFIP has maximum coverage limits that may be insufficient for higher-value properties. Private flood insurance or supplemental coverage may be needed.

Waiting periods. NFIP flood insurance typically has a 30-day waiting period before coverage begins.

## Hurricane Deductibles

South Carolina coastal policies typically include hurricane-specific deductibles:

Percentage deductibles. Often 1% to 5% of dwelling coverage value (rather than fixed dollar amounts) applying specifically to hurricane losses.

Triggering events. Hurricane deductibles typically apply when losses result from a named storm, with specific triggers based on watches, warnings, or landfall.

Financial impact. On a $500,000 dwelling with a 3% hurricane deductible, the client faces $15,000 out of pocket before insurance pays. Clients must understand and plan for this exposure.

Disclosure obligations. Producers must clearly disclose hurricane deductibles and help clients understand their practical implications.

## Pre-Loss Consumer Protections

South Carolina has specific consumer protections for coastal policyholders:

Notice requirements for cancellation or non-renewal. Insurers must provide proper notice before cancelling or non-renewing coverage, with specific rules for coastal areas.

Restrictions on pre-storm coverage changes. Insurance coverage generally cannot be changed or newly bound once a hurricane watch or warning is issued. Specific timing rules apply.

Moratorium provisions. During active storm threats, insurers typically implement moratoriums on new coverage binding.

Continuation rights. Policyholders have rights to continue coverage under specific circumstances.

## Post-Loss Rights and Procedures

After a covered loss, South Carolina policyholders have specific rights:

Prompt claims handling. Insurers must respond to claims within reasonable timeframes under South Carolina unfair claims settlement practices rules.

Right to adjuster. Policyholders have rights regarding the adjuster assigned to their claim.

Documentation rights. Access to claim information and documentation.

Appeal rights. If a claim is denied or underpaid, specific appeal and dispute resolution procedures apply.

Complaint filing. Policyholders can file complaints with the SCDOI when they believe their claims aren't being handled properly.

## Coverage Reviews for Coastal Clients

Coastal insurance needs change over time. Producers should conduct regular reviews covering:

Rebuild cost updates. Coastal construction costs have risen significantly. Coverage limits should reflect current replacement costs, not original coverage amounts.

Mitigation updates. Any property improvements affecting mitigation credits should be documented.

Flood zone changes. FEMA flood maps update periodically. Properties may move between zones, affecting required coverage.

Deductible reviews. Clients' financial circumstances may change, affecting what deductible levels make sense.

Coverage gap reviews. Ensuring homeowners, wind, and flood coverage work together without gaps.

Annual reviews — particularly before hurricane season — serve clients well and strengthen the agent-client relationship.

## Common Coastal Insurance Pitfalls

Underinsuring. Coverage limits that don't reflect actual rebuild costs leave clients exposed at claim time.

Missing flood coverage. Assuming flood isn't needed because the property isn't in a designated flood zone. Many coastal floods occur outside mapped high-risk zones.

Ignoring mitigation opportunities. Missing premium discounts for which clients qualify.

Pressure sales before storms. Trying to bind coverage during moratorium periods or overstating coverage in pre-storm panic.

Inadequate disclosure. Not explaining hurricane deductibles, SCWHA limitations, or flood exclusions clearly.

## 5 Frequently Asked Questions

- Does standard homeowners insurance cover hurricane damage in South Carolina? In coastal areas, standard homeowners policies typically exclude wind/hurricane damage. Separate wind coverage through SCWHA or private carriers is typically required.
- Is flood insurance required for South Carolina coastal properties? Properties in Special Flood Hazard Areas (SFHAs) with federally-backed mortgages are generally required to maintain flood insurance. Flood insurance is also strongly recommended for many coastal properties outside formally designated flood zones.
- What's the typical hurricane deductible on South Carolina coastal policies? Hurricane deductibles commonly range from 1% to 5% of dwelling coverage value, applied specifically to hurricane losses. The exact percentage depends on the carrier, property location, and coverage structure.
- Can I buy insurance for a coastal property as a hurricane approaches? No. Insurance moratoriums typically prevent new coverage from being bound once a hurricane watch or warning is in effect. Flood insurance has a standard 30-day waiting period before coverage begins.
- Do wind mitigation features really reduce premiums significantly? Yes. Qualifying mitigation features (impact windows, shutters, hurricane straps, reinforced roof-deck attachment) can generate substantial premium discounts. Documentation is required to obtain credits.
