Oregon's wildfire risk has fundamentally changed the state's insurance landscape — and created one of the most significant specialty opportunities for Oregon insurance producers. Properties in higher-risk wildfire zones face substantial coverage challenges, and the producers who develop expertise in this area become indispensable to clients in affected communities.

Here's what Oregon producers should know about building a wildfire insurance specialty practice.

## Why Wildfire Has Become a Specialty

Several factors have made wildfire insurance a distinct specialty:

Increasingly intense fire seasons. Oregon has experienced increasingly severe wildfire seasons in recent years, with the 2020 Labor Day fires causing devastating damage to communities including Talent, Phoenix, Detroit, and others.

Significant insurance market disruption. Many carriers have reduced or eliminated coverage in higher-risk areas. Some have non-renewed policies in wildfire-vulnerable zones.

Premium increases. Wildfire-exposed properties have seen substantial premium increases — sometimes multiples of previous premiums.

Coverage availability challenges. Some Oregon homeowners can't obtain coverage in the standard market and must seek surplus lines coverage or specialty solutions.

Mitigation requirements. Properties may need specific mitigation efforts to qualify for or maintain coverage.

Regulatory complexity. Oregon's Wildfire Hazard Map, building code requirements, and insurance regulation interact in complex ways.

This combination has elevated wildfire insurance from a routine consideration to a specialty area requiring genuine expertise.

## Areas Most Affected

Oregon wildfire risk affects properties throughout the state, but particularly:

Southern Oregon. Jackson, Josephine, and Klamath counties face significant wildfire exposure. Areas around Medford, Ashland, and Grants Pass have substantial risk.

Cascade foothills and mountains. Properties in or near forested areas of the Cascades, including parts of Lane, Linn, Marion, Clackamas, and other counties.

Eastern Oregon. Some eastern Oregon areas face significant wildfire risk, particularly in the Blue Mountains and surrounding areas.

Coast Range properties. Properties in or near Coast Range forests have wildfire considerations.

Interface properties throughout the state. Wildland-urban interface properties (where developed areas meet forested or undeveloped land) face elevated risk virtually statewide.

## What Wildfire Insurance Specialists Do

Wildfire insurance specialists work with clients to:

Assess current coverage. Identifying gaps, inadequate limits, exclusions, or other issues with existing coverage.

Find appropriate coverage. Including standard market options, surplus lines, specialty insurers, and other coverage paths.

Navigate non-renewal situations. Helping clients whose carriers have non-renewed find replacement coverage.

Coordinate mitigation efforts. Helping clients understand and implement mitigation features that improve insurability and may reduce premiums.

Document mitigation features. Ensuring proper documentation of mitigation work for insurance purposes.

Manage Wildfire Hazard Map issues. Helping clients understand their Wildfire Hazard Map classification and any related disclosure or mitigation requirements.

Coordinate multiple coverages. Property insurance, earthquake (separate), flood (separate), umbrella, and other coverages.

Provide ongoing service. Annual reviews, claim support, and adapting to changing conditions.

## Building Wildfire Insurance Expertise

To genuinely serve clients in this specialty:

Master coverage details. What's covered, what's not, deductible structures, exclusions, mitigation provisions, and other coverage details.

Know carrier landscapes. Which carriers write what types of properties in what areas. Standard market and specialty market knowledge.

Understand surplus lines. Many wildfire-exposed properties require surplus lines coverage. Understanding this market is essential.

Stay current on Wildfire Hazard Map. The map has been controversial with multiple revisions. Understanding current status and how it affects clients matters.

Know mitigation requirements. What mitigation features qualify for credits or improved availability. Defensible space, ember-resistant construction, etc.

Track regulatory developments. Oregon's wildfire regulatory landscape continues to evolve.

Build carrier relationships. Specialty carrier relationships are essential for placing difficult risks.

## Common Wildfire Mitigation Features

Mitigation features that may improve insurability and pricing include:

Defensible space. Cleared vegetation around structures (typically 0-30 feet of minimal vegetation, 30-100 feet of reduced fuel).

Fire-resistant building materials:

Metal or tile roofing

Fiber cement siding

Tempered or dual-pane windows

Fire-resistant decking

Ember-resistant construction:

Mesh-screened vents

Sealed eaves and soffits

Closed crawlspace and attic openings

Spark arrestors on chimneys and stove pipes

Multiple evacuation routes

Adequate water supply for firefighting

Maintained landscaping with appropriate plant selection and spacing

Compliance with local building codes for wildfire-prone areas

## The Surplus Lines Market

For properties that can't obtain coverage in the standard market, Oregon producers often need access to surplus lines insurance:

Surplus lines basics. Insurance from non-admitted (state-unregulated) insurers used when standard market coverage isn't available.

Higher premiums typically. Surplus lines coverage often costs more than standard market.

Different consumer protections. Some standard market consumer protections don't apply to surplus lines.

Producer requirements. Selling surplus lines typically requires specific surplus lines licensing or working through a surplus lines broker.

Specialty markets for wildfire. Specific surplus lines markets focus on wildfire-exposed properties.

For producers serving high-risk Oregon areas, surplus lines knowledge is essential.

## Coverage Coordination

Wildfire-exposed Oregon properties typically need coordination of multiple coverages:

Standard or surplus homeowners insurance. Covering most perils including wildfire.

Earthquake insurance. Separate coverage for earthquake damage (Oregon's significant seismic risk).

Flood insurance. Separate NFIP or private flood policy for flooding (separate peril).

Umbrella coverage. Excess liability for higher-value properties.

Other specialty coverages. As needed for specific properties.

Helping clients understand and coordinate this layered protection is core to specialty practice.

## Oregon Wildfire Hazard Map

Oregon's Wildfire Hazard Map classifies properties by wildfire risk level. The map:

Affects disclosure requirements. Properties in higher-risk zones may have specific disclosure requirements.

Affects insurance. Carriers consider classifications in underwriting.

Affects building requirements. Higher-risk zones may face stricter building code requirements.

Has been controversial. Multiple revisions and ongoing political debates affect the map's implementation.

Specialists need to track current map status and how it affects their clients.

Surplus lines compliance if you place coverage outside admitted markets.

## 5 Frequently Asked Questions

- Why is wildfire insurance becoming a specialty in Oregon? Increasingly severe wildfire seasons, market disruption, premium increases, and regulatory complexity have elevated wildfire from routine consideration to specialty area requiring genuine expertise.
- Are there enough wildfire-exposed clients to support a specialty practice? Yes, particularly in Southern Oregon, Cascade foothills, and wildland-urban interface areas. The market need significantly exceeds the supply of genuine specialists.
- What's the Oregon Wildfire Hazard Map? Oregon's official classification of properties by wildfire risk level. The map affects disclosure requirements, insurance availability, and building codes in some areas.
- Do I need surplus lines licensing to be a wildfire specialist? Often yes, since many high-risk properties require surplus lines coverage. Even if you don't have surplus lines authority, partnering with surplus lines brokers can serve clients.
- How long does it take to develop genuine wildfire insurance expertise? Typically 2-3 years of focused work — including CE, carrier relationship building, and serving affected clients — to develop true specialty-level expertise.
