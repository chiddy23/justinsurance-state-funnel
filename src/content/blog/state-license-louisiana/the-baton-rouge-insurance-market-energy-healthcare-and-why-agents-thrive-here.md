If you're a licensed insurance agent looking for a market that combines Fortune 500 commercial clients, a major research university, one of the most active energy and petrochemical corridors in the country, and a growing professional services sector — Baton Rouge deserves serious consideration. Louisiana's capital city is often overshadowed by New Orleans in conversations about the state's economy, but for insurance professionals, Baton Rouge offers a commercial market that is both more accessible and more diverse than its cultural neighbor to the south. This guide profiles Baton Rouge's major employers, the insurance needs they generate, and how to position yourself to build a real career in the Capital City.

## Baton Rouge's Economic Identity

Baton Rouge is defined by three economic pillars that give it a commercial insurance market profile unlike any other city in the region. The first is energy and petrochemicals — Baton Rouge sits at the northern end of the chemical corridor that runs along the Mississippi River south toward New Orleans and west toward Lake Charles, one of the most concentrated industrial zones in the country. ExxonMobil operates one of the largest oil refineries in the United States in Baton Rouge. Dow Chemical, BASF, and dozens of other petrochemical manufacturers have major operations in the Baton Rouge area. This concentration of industrial operations generates property, environmental liability, workers' compensation, and commercial auto insurance needs at a scale that supports highly specialized commercial lines careers.

The second pillar is higher education and healthcare. Louisiana State University (LSU) is one of the South's flagship research universities and one of Baton Rouge's largest employers, with thousands of faculty, staff, and researchers. LSU Health Sciences Center in New Orleans is affiliated with the university's broader academic health enterprise. The Baton Rouge healthcare market — including Our Lady of the Lake Regional Medical Center, Baton Rouge General, and the Mary Bird Perkins Cancer Center — employs thousands of healthcare professionals and generates group benefits, professional liability, and individual coverage opportunities across a well-educated workforce. Baton Rouge General and Our Lady of the Lake are among the city's largest private employers.

The third pillar is a growing technology and professional services sector. IBM built a major technology services center in downtown Baton Rouge. Albemarle Corporation, a global specialty chemicals company, is headquartered in Baton Rouge. Raising Cane's Chicken Fingers, one of the fastest-growing restaurant chains in the country, is headquartered in Baton Rouge and generates hospitality, workers' comp, and commercial property needs across its expanding operations.

## Blue Cross and Blue Shield of Louisiana

Blue Cross and Blue Shield of Louisiana (BCBSLA) — headquartered in Baton Rouge — is the largest health insurer in Louisiana and one of the city's most significant employers. For insurance agents, BCBSLA's Baton Rouge presence means two things. First, the company's thousands of employees represent a sophisticated, benefits-aware prospecting base for individual life insurance, disability income, LTC planning, and voluntary benefits. Second, agents who develop expertise in BCBSLA's group and individual health product lines are well-positioned to serve the large portion of Baton Rouge's employer market that uses BCBSLA coverage.

## ExxonMobil and the Petrochemical Corridor

ExxonMobil's Baton Rouge refinery is one of the largest refineries in the United States and one of Baton Rouge's largest employers. The company's operations generate substantial commercial insurance needs across property, general liability, environmental liability, commercial auto, and workers' compensation. Like most Fortune 500 operations, ExxonMobil's direct insurance needs are handled through national programs. The accessible opportunity for local producers lies in ExxonMobil's supplier and contractor network — the hundreds of engineering, construction, maintenance, and service companies that work on and around the Baton Rouge refinery and the broader petrochemical corridor.

Commercial producers who develop expertise in contractor programs, wrap-up insurance (OCIP/CCIP), pollution liability, and workers' compensation for the petrochemical sector access a client base that is both substantial and underserved by general-market agents who lack the specialized product knowledge.

## LSU and the University Market

Louisiana State University is one of Baton Rouge's largest institutions, employing thousands of faculty, staff, and research professionals. The university's workforce represents an excellent prospecting base for individual life insurance, disability income, LTC planning, and voluntary benefits. Academic professionals at a research university typically receive strong employer-provided benefits but carry significant gaps in disability income coverage and long-term care protection relative to their income levels and professional longevity.

LSU's student population — tens of thousands of young adults making their first insurance decisions — also represents a market for renters insurance, individual health marketplace plans, and student-oriented financial products that agents who establish campus relationships can serve effectively.

## The Flood and Natural Hazard Reality in Baton Rouge

The August 2016 Baton Rouge floods — a historic inland flooding event that damaged more than 60,000 homes in the Baton Rouge area, with many lacking flood coverage — illustrated the critical importance of NFIP flood insurance in Louisiana's capital city. Baton Rouge's inland location makes it easy for both clients and agents to underestimate flood exposure, but the 2016 event demonstrated that catastrophic flooding does not require a named hurricane to occur in the Baton Rouge market.

Louisiana's ongoing 3-hour NFIP flood training requirement every renewal period reflects the state's recognition that flood knowledge must be continuously refreshed. Producers in Baton Rouge who integrate flood coverage conversations into every homeowners and commercial property client review are serving their clients in a genuinely important way.
