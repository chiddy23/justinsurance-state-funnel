You might feel both excited and a bit overwhelmed as you prepare for your [California insurance broker license](/california) exam. After all, the license opens the door to new career opportunities, but there are several steps to follow before you can expand your professional horizon. In this guide, you will find each stage clearly explained, from understanding basic qualifications to putting your best foot forward on exam day. By the end, you will feel more confident, organized, and ready to ace the test.

## Understand the licensing requirements

Before you even think about study materials, check that you meet the basic requirements outlined by the [California Department of Insurance](/california/requirements). You typically have to be at least 18 years old, reside in or intend to do business in California, and have a clear criminal record. These standards help ensure that you will be well-prepared to serve clients ethically and responsibly.

Background checks are often part of the application. You will need to submit your fingerprints, which are then screened by the Department of Justice and the FBI. If you have any past infractions, do not assume automatic rejection. Instead, openly disclose the issues and provide documentation if requested. Often, transparency is appreciated and can speed up the review process.

## Complete your pre-licensing education

Once you confirm you meet the basic qualifications, you need to fulfill the [pre-licensing education requirement](/california/prelicensing). As of January 1, 2026, California Assembly Bill 943 eliminated the line-specific product hour requirement — there’s no longer a different hour count for Property versus Casualty versus Life versus Health. The only mandatory [prelicensing](/california/prelicensing) course is now the 12-hour Code and Ethics course from a CDI-approved provider, and that single course satisfies the prelicensing requirement for every line of authority. Most broker candidates still add voluntary, line-specific exam prep on top of the required Ethics course, because the state exam itself still tests the full body of policy provisions, state regulations, insurance ethics, and broker responsibilities.

Course providers offer both online and classroom options. Online platforms can be particularly helpful if you balance work or family obligations. You can set your own pace and revisit lessons on more challenging topics. Keep track of your progress by reviewing any quizzes or self-assessments that come with the course. These checkpoints highlight areas where you may need extra study time before the real exam.

## Register for the licensing exam

After finishing your required education, the next step is to register for the state licensing exam. You do this through California’s authorized testing service, which will have a list of available testing centers and dates. Book your spot well in advance, especially during peak periods when seats fill up quickly.

Exam fees vary, so check the most current rate on the California Department of Insurance website or through your exam registration portal. This fee covers your test appointment and often includes scheduling changes if you need to make adjustments to your test date. Make sure you read the confirmation email thoroughly. It typically includes important details such as identification requirements, testing time, and permissible materials (like a basic calculator).

## Develop a strategic study schedule

A clear plan will reduce stress and improve retention of the vast amount of information you will see on the California insurance broker license exam. Start by breaking your study materials into logical sections, such as insurance concepts, relevant state laws, and specific product knowledge like Life or Property coverage. Create a realistic timeline, focusing on one or two major topics each week.

• Allocate time for small review sessions, such as reading course notes for 15 minutes before bed each night.  
• Take practice quizzes to track your progress in areas like policy definitions or client disclosures.  
• Use flashcards to memorize key regulations or terms.

As your test date draws closer, do more comprehensive reviews of each topic. Any [practice exam](/california/practice-exam) or timed quiz you complete will help you become comfortable working under time pressure.

## Master exam-day strategies

Even if you know the material well, exam-day nerves can throw you off-track. Planning ahead to stay calm and clearly focused can be just as essential as learning important concepts. Here are a few strategies to boost your confidence:

1.  Arrive early. Plan to get to the testing center 30 minutes before your scheduled slot so you have enough time to park, check in, and find your seat.
2.  Pack the right items. Bring a valid, government-issued photo ID—plus a backup ID if possible—along with a pen or pencil if needed.
3.  Read carefully. Each question may contain subtle differences between possible answers, so take a moment to double-check before you commit.
4.  Manage your time. The exam is usually multiple-choice and timed. If a question stumps you, flag it and move on. Return at the end when you have spare minutes.

Remember, you have prepared diligently. Trust your study process, and do not let a tricky question derail your focus for the rest of the exam.

## Apply for your license

Assuming you pass, your next step is to officially apply for your California insurance broker license. You will need to complete the required application forms through the California Department of Insurance. Be ready to present proof of your exam results and your pre-licensing education completion certificate.

The state will verify your score, fingerprints, and any other paperwork you submitted. If everything is in order, you will receive your license within a few weeks, either by mail or electronically. At this point, you can legally operate as a licensed broker in California and begin assisting clients with policies that meet their needs.

## Maintain your license going forward

Receiving your license is a major accomplishment, but your journey does not end there. In California, brokers renew their licenses every two years and need to meet [continuing education](/california/continuing-education) (CE) requirements. CE courses help keep you updated on new insurance regulations, product changes, and industry trends. Being diligent with these ongoing requirements ensures that your skills remain fresh and that you continue to offer knowledgeable guidance to clients.

To stay on top of CE deadlines, set up reminders a few months before your license expires. This cushion of time helps you complete CE courses at a comfortable pace, rather than frantically rushing at the last minute. Many approved providers offer flexible schedules, remote learning, or in-person classes, so choose whatever suits you best.

## Frequently Asked Questions

1. What score do I need to pass the California insurance broker license exam? The California Department of Insurance requires a 60 percent score to pass, and each line's examination — Life, Accident and Health, Property, or Casualty — has 75 questions with a 1.5-hour time limit. You can review the full breakdown on our [California license requirements](/california/requirements) hub before you register.
2. Who administers the California insurance exam and how much does it cost? PSI Services LLC delivers the examination on behalf of the California Department of Insurance. The state examination fee is $55, plus a $43 PSI convenience fee when you test at a PSI center or by remote online proctoring, for a total of $98 per scheduled exam date; a separate $188 filing fee applies when you submit your license application.
3. Do I need to finish any coursework before I can be licensed in California? Yes — the 12-hour Code and Ethics prelicensing course from a CDI-approved provider is required before your California license can be issued, and under Assembly Bill 943 (effective January 1, 2026) it is the only prelicensing education required for every line of authority. Our [California prelicensing](/california/prelicensing) hub explains how the course fits alongside your exam prep.
