Understand your [continuing education](/florida/continuing-education) requirements

If you have your [Florida insurance license](/florida), completing continuing education (CE) is essential to maintain it. [Florida insurance continuing education](/florida/continuing-education) ensures you stay updated on new regulations and industry best practices, so you can provide clients with the best possible service. You’ll find that the [Florida Department of Financial Services](/florida/requirements) typically requires a set number of credit hours every two years, which usually includes an important ethics component.

Along with standard refresher courses, you can explore specialized topics like annuities, life insurance benefits, or health coverage updates. The goal is to deepen your industry knowledge and maintain a valid license in good standing.

## Learn your credit-hour breakdown

Florida scales your CE total to how long you have been licensed, so the first thing to establish is which tier you fall into. Under Section 626.2815, Florida Statutes:

-   **Licensed fewer than 6 years:** 24 hours per two-year cycle — a 4-hour law and ethics update specific to your license type, plus 20 elective hours.
-   **Licensed 6 years or more:** 20 hours per cycle — the same 4-hour update, plus 16 elective hours.
-   **Licensed 25 years or more,** and holding a CLU or CPCU, or a bachelor's degree or higher in risk management or insurance with 18 or more semester hours of insurance coursework: 10 hours per cycle — the 4-hour update plus 6 elective hours.

The 4-hour law and ethics update is required in every cycle no matter which tier you are in. If you hold multiple insurance-related licenses, check the requirements for each line of authority, as there could be additional or overlapping obligations.

The tier change is the detail most agents miss. It is common to keep buying 24-hour packages for years after crossing the 6-year mark simply because nobody mentioned the number had dropped.

Completing these credits can be easier when you have a plan from day one. Some professionals choose to space out their courses over the full two-year period, while others prefer to tackle them in fewer sittings. You might also find online providers that let you move at your own pace, which can be helpful if you have a balancing act of work, family, and additional study obligations.

## Keep an organized schedule

Your Florida CE deadline is the last day of your birth month — not your license anniversary and not your original issue date. Florida Administrative Code Rule 69B-228.220 requires you to complete all required hours “by the end of the licensee’s birth month after being licensed 24 months and every 2 years thereafter.” Your appointments run on that same birth-month clock, renewing every 24 months.

That makes your deadline personal to you. A colleague’s due date tells you nothing about yours, and calendaring your license anniversary instead will point you at the wrong month entirely. Missing the real deadline gets your appointments cancelled — which stops you writing business — and returning to compliance means a negotiated fine plus reinstatement fees. Note your birth month and set reminders at least a few months in advance.

Staying organized can be as simple as setting aside a weekend to knock out a few credits at once. If you worry about forgetting, you might create alerts on your phone or email. This extra reminder system can help you avoid rushing to complete credits at the last minute.

## Confirm your credits actually posted

Florida agent licenses are perpetual — there is no renewal application to submit and no renewal fee for you to pay. What you do instead is verify. Once you’ve earned the required credits, log into the Florida Department of Financial Services MyProfile portal and confirm the hours have posted to your compliance record, that the 4-hour law and ethics update is among them, and that your total matches your tier. Always save your course completion certificates or transcripts as backup documentation.

If you notice any discrepancies, such as credits not showing up properly, contact your course provider directly. They can often fix the issue by resubmitting your course completion data to the state’s system. Persistence is key here, so don’t hesitate to follow up—the state’s record is the one that counts, not your own folder of certificates.

## Explore next steps beyond the basics

After you’ve met your Florida insurance continuing education requirements, consider going further to strengthen your career prospects. Look into advanced topics like long-term care, estate planning, or emerging risks. These specialized courses can expand your expertise, letting you offer broader services and connect with more clients.

It might also be worthwhile to check out what other professionals in your field are doing. You’ll stay aware of industry trends that could impact your business, and you’ll show clients you are committed to excellence. Sometimes, these extra courses provide a fresh perspective that sets you apart in a competitive market.

## Common questions about Florida insurance continuing education

1.  What is the minimum number of CE hours required?  
    It depends on how long you’ve been licensed: 24 hours per two-year cycle for your first 6 years, 20 hours once you’ve been licensed 6 years or more, and 10 hours for licensees at 25 years or more who hold a CLU, CPCU, or a qualifying insurance or risk-management degree. Every tier includes 4 hours of Florida law and ethics.
2.  When is my CE deadline?  
    The last day of your birth month, every two years, beginning after you have been licensed 24 months. It is not tied to your license anniversary or issue date. Your appointments also renew every 24 months during your birth month. Confirm your exact status in the MyProfile portal.
3.  What if I miss my CE deadlines?  
    Your appointments get cancelled, which means you cannot write business — even though the license itself is perpetual and does not expire on a schedule. Regaining compliance means completing the outstanding hours, paying a fine under a negotiated Settlement Stipulation, and covering appointment reinstatement fees. If you’re running late, contact the state immediately for options.
4.  Do I need specific ethics courses each cycle?  
    Yes. Florida requires a 4-hour law and ethics update course, specific to the license you hold, in every compliance cycle regardless of which hour tier you fall into.
5.  Where can I find approved CE providers?  
    You can search on the Florida Department of Financial Services website or ask for referrals from colleagues. JustInsurance also offers courses designed specifically for Florida licensees.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I carry over unused CE credits to my next compliance cycle?**
Yes. Florida allows you to carry forward up to 24 excess CE hours into your next two-year compliance cycle. Keep in mind that carried-over ethics hours only count as general CE credit — you must still complete 4 new Florida law and ethics hours during each cycle.

**What if a course I completed disappears from the approved provider list?**
If a course loses its approval status after you completed it, your credits may still count as long as they were approved at the time you took them. Keep your completion certificates as documentation in case questions arise during an audit.

**Are there any reductions to the CE requirement?**
Florida's requirement scales down with experience rather than up. Your total drops from 24 hours to 20 once you have been licensed 6 years, and to 10 hours at 25 years or more if you hold a CLU, CPCU, or a bachelor's degree or higher in risk management or insurance with 18 or more semester hours of insurance coursework. New licensees are in the highest tier, not a reduced one — though CE does not begin until you have been licensed 24 months. Certain license types are treated differently, so contact the Florida Department of Financial Services directly to confirm what applies to your specific license.

**How do I know my course completions have been reported to the state?**
Most approved providers report completions electronically within a few days. You can verify your credits by logging into the MyProfile portal on the Florida Department of Financial Services website. If credits are missing, contact your course provider promptly to have them resubmit the records.
