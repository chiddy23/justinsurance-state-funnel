North Carolina recently joined a group of states that don't require [prelicense](/north-carolina/prelicensing) education for insurance producer licensure — a change that took effect October 1, 2025 under House Bill 737 (Session Law 2025-45). This update fundamentally changes how North Carolina candidates approach licensure, removing what was previously a 20-hour-per-line prelicense requirement (40 hours for combined Life/Accident & Health). Understanding what this change means and how to prepare effectively without mandatory PLE is essential for new candidates.

Here's everything you need to know about North Carolina insurance prelicense education requirements.

## The Major Recent Change: HB 737 / SL 2025-45

North Carolina House Bill 737 (Session Law 2025-45), effective October 1, 2025, eliminated the prelicense education (PLE) requirement for all North Carolina insurance producer license lines. The change reflects:

North Carolina's view that exam-only licensing is sufficient given quality study materials available

Reduction of barriers to entry for new producers

Alignment with growing number of states eliminating PLE (Pennsylvania, Massachusetts, South Carolina, Alabama, Nevada, and now North Carolina)

Modernization of North Carolina's licensing process

What this means practically:

New producer applicants no longer need to complete formal prelicense courses

Candidates can study independently using textbooks, online materials, or prep courses

North Carolina exam can be scheduled directly (after NIPR application) without PLE certificate

Reduced upfront cost (no required course fees)

Potential for faster path to licensure

## What North Carolina PLE Used to Require

Before October 1, 2025, North Carolina required prelicense education by line:

Life Insurance: 20 hours

Accident & Health or Sickness: 20 hours

Combined Life and Accident & Health or Sickness: 40 hours

Property: 20 hours

Casualty: 20 hours

Personal Lines: 20 hours

Medicare Supplement/Long-Term Care: 10 hours

Variable Life and Variable Annuity Products: Already exempt from PLE (still must hold underlying Life license)

These requirements no longer apply to applicants licensing after October 1, 2025.

Lines that never required PLE:

Title Insurance

Auto Physical Damage

Adjuster (separate licensing process)

Crop Hail Adjuster

## PLE Exemptions That Existed Before October 2025

Even before HB 737, certain professional designations exempted candidates from PLE:

CFP (Certified Financial Planner)

CIC (Certified Insurance Counselor)

RHU (Registered Health Underwriter)

CEBS (Certified Employee Benefits Specialist)

FLMI (Fellow Life Management Institute)

These exemptions are now moot since PLE is no longer required for any candidates.

## Why Many Candidates Still Take Prelicense Courses

Even though PLE is no longer required, most successful North Carolina candidates still complete prelicense study:

[Pass rate](/pass-rates) matters. Quality prelicense courses substantially improve first-attempt pass rates. North Carolina reports a scaled score from 0 to 100 with a passing score of 70 — and the Pearson VUE candidate handbook is explicit that the scaled score "is neither the number of questions you answered correctly nor the percentage of questions you answered correctly." Because Pearson VUE equates each exam form to correct for differences in difficulty, there is no fixed number of questions you are allowed to miss. You cannot study to a target miss count; you have to actually know the material.

Multiple separate exams. North Carolina has separate exams for each line — no combined L&H or P&C options. Multiple exams require multiple preparation efforts. Each exam costs $45 — failing wastes money.

Content coverage. Each North Carolina exam covers extensive material including general insurance principles, specific product knowledge, and North Carolina-specific laws.

[Practice exam](/north-carolina/practice-exam) access. Quality prelicense courses include practice exams that mirror the actual state exam format and content.

Study structure. Self-study without structure often leads to inefficient learning. Quality courses provide systematic content delivery.

North Carolina-specific content. State-specific laws and regulations need focused study. Generic national materials may miss North Carolina content.

Confidence building. Practice exams and quality content build confidence going into the actual exam.

While not legally required, structured study remains a strong recommendation for first-attempt success.

If you choose self-study without formal courses, here's how to structure your approach:

For each separate exam:

Step 3: Cover content systematically.

General insurance principles

Specific product knowledge for the line

Step 4: Practice questions.

Minimum 200-300 practice questions per line

Focus on weak areas revealed by practice

Step 5: Take full-length practice exams.

At least 2-3 practice exams per line under timed conditions

Aim for 80%+ scores consistently before scheduling

Step 6: Final review.

Light review of weak areas

Final practice questions for sharpness

Schedule and pass exam

## Time to Prepare for Each Exam

Without PLE requirements, time to prepare depends on your study approach:

Highly motivated, full-time self-study:

Single line (Life, A&H, Property, Casualty, Personal Lines): 1-2 weeks per line of intensive study

Medicare Supplement/LTC: 3-7 days

Working professional, part-time study:

Single line: 3-4 weeks per line

Medicare Supplement/LTC: 1-2 weeks

With formal prelicense course:

Single line: 2-3 weeks per line

Medicare Supplement/LTC: 1 week

Multi-line strategy: Most candidates pursuing multiple lines (e.g., Life and Health) study sequentially rather than simultaneously. Pass one exam, then prepare for the next. This approach allows focused preparation per line.

The required total preparation time hasn't changed substantially — you still need adequate preparation for each separate exam. What's changed is the legal requirement; learning still takes time.

## Common Mistakes Without PLE Requirements

Underestimating the exam. PLE elimination doesn't make the exam easier — content and difficulty remain.

Insufficient practice. Reading isn't practice. You need substantial practice question volume.

Skipping practice exams. Practice exams test calibration and timing.

Ignoring North Carolina-specific content. State law content remains tested.

Taking the exam too early. Without PLE forcing minimum preparation time, candidates may schedule exams before truly ready.

Overconfidence. "If PLE isn't required, the exam must be easy" is wrong thinking.

Multi-exam fatigue. Trying to take all exams in one week often produces poor results.

## Connecting Study to Exam Success

Whatever your approach (formal course or self-study):

Practice opportunities. Substantial practice question volume per line.

Final exam preparation. Mirror exam-style thinking and conditions.

North Carolina-specific focus. Don't neglect state law content.

Don't treat the lack of PLE as license to skip preparation — it's just removed the legal requirement, not the practical need.

## What Comes After Preparation

Once you're prepared:

Apply through NIPR first. $82 base + $50 per line.

Receive authorization to test. Email from Pearson VUE with reserved NPN.

Schedule and pass exam(s). $45 per exam attempt, scaled score of 70 required.

Complete fingerprinting. Through local law enforcement (Livescan), within 30 days of application.

Receive license. Plan on weeks, not days. NCDOI's own instruction to applicants is to "allow 60 days for submitted license applications," after which "you will receive an email advising you that your license has issued and providing instructions for a one time free print of your license." Most files clear well inside that window, but 60 days is the number the Department publishes — build your start date around it rather than around a same-week turnaround.

## 5 Frequently Asked Questions

- Does North Carolina still require prelicense education? No. North Carolina House Bill 737 (Session Law 2025-45), effective October 1, 2025, eliminated the prelicense education requirement for all North Carolina insurance producer license lines.
- Should I still take a prelicense course even though it's not required? Yes — most candidates do, and pass rates are substantially higher with structured preparation. While not legally required, prelicense courses provide systematic content delivery, practice questions, and North Carolina-specific focus.
- How does North Carolina compare to other states without PLE? North Carolina now joins Pennsylvania, South Carolina, Massachusetts, Alabama, Nevada, and several other states without mandatory PLE. The combined effect of no PLE requirement and a short exam format for the major lines (Life, Accident & Health, Property, and Casualty are each 55 scored questions plus 5 unscored pretest questions in 1 hour 15 minutes) makes North Carolina relatively accessible — though Personal Lines and Medicare Supplement/LTC run longer, and the per-line application pricing structure increases costs for multi-line licenses.
- Can I take all my North Carolina exams in one day? Theoretically yes if testing capacity allows, but most candidates space exams across days or weeks. Each exam takes 1 hour 15 minutes plus check-in time. Taking multiple exams in one day creates significant fatigue.
- What's a realistic preparation timeline for North Carolina exams? Working part-time, plan 3-4 weeks of preparation per single-line exam (Life, A&H, Property, Casualty, Personal Lines). Multi-line pursuit typically extends timeline as candidates prepare for and pass exams sequentially.
