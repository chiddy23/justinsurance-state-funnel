If there's one section of the Georgia insurance exam that catches unprepared candidates off guard, it's the state-specific law portion. General insurance concepts are tested alongside Georgia-specific laws, and unprepared candidates often lose enough points on state content to drag their overall score below 70%.

Here are the Georgia-specific insurance laws you need to know for the state exam.

## The Georgia Insurance Code

The Georgia Insurance Code is the statutory framework governing insurance in Georgia. It's administered by the Office of Commissioner of Insurance and Safety Fire (OCI), led by the Commissioner of Insurance — an elected position in Georgia.

The exam doesn't expect you to memorize Code citations, but it does expect you to understand key provisions around:

- Agent licensing and conduct

- Unfair trade practices

- Replacement rules

- Consumer protections

- Claims handling standards

- Enforcement authority

## Georgia Replacement Regulations

Georgia has specific rules for replacing existing life insurance and annuity contracts. Expect multiple exam questions on replacement.

Key points tested:

- What constitutes a replacement — when existing coverage is lapsed, surrendered, forfeited, or materially changed in connection with a new sale

- Required forms — Notice Regarding Replacement signed by agent and applicant

- Existing insurer notification — the new insurer must notify the existing insurer of the pending replacement

- Disclosure requirements — what the agent must communicate about the impact of replacement

- Conservation period — the existing insurer's opportunity to retain the business

Replacement-related questions often present scenarios and ask which action the agent must take or which disclosure is required.

## Georgia Unfair Trade Practices

Georgia law prohibits specific conduct considered unfair or deceptive. The exam tests whether you can recognize these practices when described in scenario questions:

Misrepresentation. Making false statements about policies, benefits, premiums, dividends, or insurer financial condition.

Twisting. Using misrepresentation to induce a policyholder to replace existing coverage.

Churning. Repeatedly replacing a client's own policies for commissions without client benefit.

Rebating. Offering anything of material value outside of policy terms as an inducement to purchase, with narrow permitted exceptions.

Defamation. Making false or maliciously critical statements about other insurers or agents.

Boycott, coercion, and intimidation. Anti-competitive practices in the insurance marketplace.

False financial statements. Misrepresenting an insurer's financial condition.

Know each by name and be able to distinguish them in scenario questions.

## Georgia Free-Look Period

Georgia requires free-look periods on life insurance and annuity contracts:

- Minimum free-look period typically 10 days from policy delivery for standard life insurance

- Longer periods often required for replacement policies (commonly 20–30 days)

- During free-look, policyholder can cancel for full premium refund

- Starts upon policy delivery to the policyholder

Expect questions on free-look duration, when it begins, and what the policyholder is entitled to if they cancel during the window.

## Georgia Licensing Requirements

The exam tests understanding of Georgia's own licensing rules:

- Age requirements (18 years old)

- Residency requirements

- [Prelicense](/georgia/prelicensing) education (16 hours for combined Life, Accident, and Sickness — 8 hours per line)

- Exam requirements

- Background check and fingerprinting

- CE requirements (24 hours biennially with 3 hours ethics)

- License renewal cycle

- Grounds for license suspension or revocation

Licensing-related questions are often straightforward factual recall. Know the numbers.

## Georgia Claims Handling Standards

Georgia has laws governing how insurance claims must be handled:

- Timely investigation requirements

- Reasonable response to communications

- Good faith settlement obligations when liability is clear

- Explanation of denial if a claim is denied

- Prompt payment after acceptance

- Prohibition on unfair claims settlement practices

While these primarily apply to insurers and adjusters, agents need to understand them to advise clients when claims go sideways.

## Georgia's Insurance Commissioner

Unlike many states where the Insurance Commissioner is appointed, Georgia's Commissioner is an elected official. This distinction can appear on the exam.

The Commissioner's authority includes:

- Licensing insurance producers

- Regulating insurance companies operating in Georgia

- Enforcing the Insurance Code

- Investigating complaints

- Imposing administrative penalties

- Issuing cease and desist orders

## Georgia's Guaranty Association

Georgia, like other states, has insurance guaranty associations that protect policyholders if an insurer becomes insolvent:

- The Georgia Life and Health Insurance Guaranty Association covers life and health policies

- Coverage limits apply for death benefits, cash values, and annuity amounts

- Agents cannot reference or advertise the guaranty association as a sales inducement

Questions about the Guaranty Association often test what agents cannot do with it (use it in marketing) rather than just what it covers.

## Georgia Small Employer Health Insurance

Georgia regulates small employer health insurance with specific rules:

- Guaranteed issue for small employers in defined size ranges

- Rating restrictions based on employer size and employee characteristics

- Specific disclosure requirements

- Coordination with federal ACA rules

Health-focused exam questions may cover small group rules.

## Georgia Long-Term Care Rules

Georgia has specific rules for long-term care insurance sales:

- Training requirements for agents selling LTC

- Consumer disclosures required at point of sale

- Policy benefit requirements

- Free-look provisions specific to LTC

Agents planning to sell LTC should know these requirements for both the exam and ongoing compliance.

## How to Study Georgia-Specific Content

Use Georgia-specific prelicense materials. Generic national study guides don't cover Georgia law in the depth the exam requires. Use OCI-approved courses built specifically for Georgia.

Focus on agent obligations. Exam questions often frame the law in terms of what the agent must do or disclose. Study from that angle.

Practice scenario questions. State law questions are frequently scenario-based. Practice applying rules to situations rather than memorizing definitions.

Don't leave state law for the final week. State-specific content is dense. Integrate it throughout your study period.

## 5 Frequently Asked Questions

1. How much of the Georgia exam is state-specific law? On the combined Life, Accident, and Sickness exam, Georgia-specific law accounts for 25 of the 125 scored questions — about a fifth of the exam, and the single largest content block on the outline. That's substantial enough to fail the exam on its own if you skip it.

2. Can national study materials cover Georgia law? Partially. National materials cover general insurance concepts well but rarely go deep on Georgia-specific rules. Use Georgia-specific prelicense courses to cover this portion.

3. Do I need to memorize specific Georgia statute numbers? No. The exam tests concepts and agent obligations, not citations. Focus on understanding what the rules require.

4. Which Georgia law topic is most heavily tested? Replacement rules, unfair trade practices, and licensing requirements are consistently among the most tested.

5. Does the Georgia exam cover federal laws too? Yes. HIPAA, ERISA, and Medicare rules are tested alongside Georgia-specific content. Understanding both the federal framework and Georgia's specific rules is essential.
