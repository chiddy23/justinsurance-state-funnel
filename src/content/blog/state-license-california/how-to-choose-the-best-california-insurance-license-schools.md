## Understand the licensing requirements

Choosing the best [California insurance license schools](/california/prelicensing) starts with knowing the prerequisites for your insurance license. In California, you generally need to be at least 18 years old and clear any required background checks before you can earn a license. Since the state wants to ensure you fully grasp insurance processes and regulations, you must take a [pre-licensing](/california/prelicensing) education course approved by the [California Department of Insurance](/california/requirements) (CDI). This course covers essential topics like insurance ethics, state regulations, and consumer protection.

Under California's AB 943 (effective January 1, 2026), the only mandatory prelicensing course is the 12-hour Code and Ethics course — the line-specific product hour requirement was eliminated. Most candidates still choose to add structured exam prep on top of the required Ethics course to pass the state licensing exam with confidence. Once you complete the Ethics course, you’ll receive a certificate that allows you to sit for the official licensing exam.

It’s best to confirm current regulations directly through the CDI’s official resources. Requirements sometimes change, so staying updated means you won’t waste time or choose a course that doesn't meet the latest standards.

## Know the different license types

Before settling on a school, clarify which license you're aiming for, because each license requires a distinct course focus and exam. California typically offers:

-   Life-Only: For those who plan to sell life insurance and annuities
-   Accident and Health: For health policies, disability, and similar coverage
-   Property and Casualty: For auto, home, and business insurance
-   Personal Lines: For personal property needs like auto or home

When selecting from various California insurance license schools, confirm that the institution caters to your specific license type. Some programs bundle Life and Health courses together, while others specialize in Property and Casualty. If you decide later to add more licenses, check whether the school offers [continuing education](/california/continuing-education) or extra courses to expand your credentials.

## Explore course formats

California insurance license schools typically offer three main types of learning: live classroom instruction, online self-study, and on-demand video courses. Each format has benefits, so choose the one that best fits your schedule and preferred learning style.

If you learn best with real-time feedback, live classes—either in person or via webinars—may be the most engaging. You can ask questions as they arise and bounce ideas off classmates. On-demand video courses let you study anytime, at your own pace, which is ideal if you’re juggling a full-time job or family commitments. Meanwhile, online self-study options often let you progress quickly if you’re already familiar with certain topics and prefer reading textbooks or digital modules.

Whichever route you pick, ensure the school includes practice quizzes or exam prep resources. The state licensing exam tests for both theoretical knowledge and practical application, so having a variety of question formats improves your confidence when exam day arrives.

## Compare costs and available scholarships

It’s normal to feel price-conscious when exploring California insurance license schools. While some programs look more expensive than others, they might offer extras like personalized tutoring, thorough review sessions, or study materials that really pay off. On the other hand, a basic self-study course may be more budget-friendly and still provide a solid foundation.

Because licensing programs can start anywhere from a few hundred to over a thousand dollars, check whether the school lets you pay in installments or offers discounts. Some companies sponsor their employees’ education, and industry associations occasionally provide scholarships or partial reimbursements. If you’re tight on budget, investigate any scholarships, group rates, or payment plans. You might be surprised how affordable a reputable program can be.

## Evaluate student support and resources

Choosing a school that offers robust support makes all the difference in how quickly you earn your license. Ask about:

-   Instructor availability: Does the program give you direct contact with an instructor?
-   Study materials: Do they provide up-to-date textbooks, practice exams, and review guides?
-   Customer service: How fast does the school respond if you have technical issues or questions about course content?

A program that features dedicated mentors or responsive help can ease you through challenging topics. Some programs even organize study groups or weekly Q&A sessions that boost motivation and let you clarify test-related doubts. Check for testimonials from recent graduates to see how helpful the support truly is.

## Follow a step-by-step plan

It’s easy to feel overwhelmed, especially if this is your first time navigating state regulations. Use the following quick-reference plan to streamline your path to a California insurance license:

1.  Determine your desired license type (Life-Only, Health, Property and Casualty, etc.).
2.  Verify the official pre-licensing hours required by the CDI for that specific license.
3.  Research California insurance license schools and pick one offering the license track you need.
4.  Decide how you prefer to study: in-person, on-demand videos, or online self-paced.
5.  Look for discounts, scholarships, or payment plans to make the course more affordable.
6.  Complete your pre-licensing hours and gather your course completion certificate.
7.  Schedule your state licensing exam, then use the course’s practice materials to prepare thoroughly.
8.  Once you pass, apply for your official license through the CDI.

In addition to the state-mandated steps, remember that consistent study habits go a long way. Setting aside daily review sessions keeps new information fresh in your mind. And if you get stuck on any topic, reaching out to your instructors or classmates can clarify confusing details faster than powering through alone.
