## Understand the Minnesota exam basics

Before diving into practice tests, it helps to know what you are dealing with. Minnesota offers several kinds of insurance licenses, the most common being property and casualty (P&C) and life and health (L&H). Both require you to complete a state-approved pre-licensing course, which covers the essential principles and regulations you will face in your exam. Topics range from understanding policy provisions to learning key state regulations enforced by the Minnesota Department of Commerce.

The state exam usually consists of multiple-choice questions, with passing scores typically around 70%. Although each license track has slight variations in content, the core format remains the same. You will see questions that test not just your recall of facts, but also your ability to apply those facts to real-life scenarios. Having a grasp of these fundamentals makes it easier to decide where to channel your study energy, so you can feel more in control when test day arrives.

## Know why it is easier

## Plan your study routine

A well-structured study plan is crucial if you want to pass quickly. Think of it in multiple steps, each reinforcing the other:

1.  Start with an overview. First, skim through your textbooks or online modules to get a sense of the main topics. This top-level approach sets the stage for more in-depth study.
2.  Dive into targeted lessons. Once you know what the exam covers, focus on one area at a time. Spend time on property, casualty, or specific types of life insurance, reviewing the technical terms and the state’s required knowledge.
3.  Take practice tests regularly. Weave these tests into your weekly study schedule—even a small set of 20–30 sample questions can reveal the weak spots you need to work on next. Weekly drilling will also help keep you motivated.
4.  Review and adjust. After practice test sessions, ask yourself which topics slowed you down or caused mistakes. Revisit those areas in your textbook or course materials. Each cycle of test-review-adjust refines your understanding until you are exam-ready.

A balanced plan also reduces the chance of burnout. Alternate between reading, answering practice-test questions, and discussing tricky topics with peers or instructors for a well-rounded approach that keeps your mind fresh.
