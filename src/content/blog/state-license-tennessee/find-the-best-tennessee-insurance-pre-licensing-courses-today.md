## Understand your licensing requirements

Before you dive into choosing a pre-licensing course, take time to review the official licensing requirements from the [Tennessee Department of Commerce and Insurance](/tennessee/requirements). Each line of authority in Tennessee has its own set of pre-licensing hours you must complete in order to sit for the state exam. That means your study plan may vary depending on whether you’re going for life & health, property & casualty, or both.

You’ll also want to verify that you meet the general eligibility criteria, which typically include being at least 18 years old and passing a background check. Different Tennessee insurance pre-licensing courses are designed to align with these official rules, so once you understand which licenses you want, you’ll have a clearer view of the exact courses needed.

## Key features to consider in a Tennessee pre-licensing course

Not every course is the same, and the best fit often comes down to the teaching methods, schedule, and resources offered. Look for these elements when comparing programs:

-   Comprehensive material: Ideally, the course will outline every topic evaluated on the state exam, from policy terminology to regulatory guidelines. Check if the curriculum is frequently updated to reflect any legislative changes that might affect your licensure.
-   Practice exams and quizzes: Mock exams help you absorb information faster by highlighting strengths and revealing where you need more practice. Multiple quizzes throughout the course allow you to review progress in real time.
-   Engaging instructors and support: Some people thrive under direct interaction with instructors, while others prefer independent study. If you suspect you’ll have questions along the way, look for programs that feature live Q&A sessions or quick-response support channels.

Selecting a course with these features can remove guesswork from your study process. You’ll also be more likely to stay motivated, which is half the battle when juggling work and personal commitments.

## Choosing between online and in-person study

You’ll come across two main types of pre-licensing courses: online-based programs and traditional classroom settings. Both can help you prepare effectively, but one might suit your schedule or learning style better than the other.

Online study typically offers flexible pacing, letting you log in at any time and go through modules from the comfort of home. This option is perfect if you have a full-time job or family responsibilities. You can also repeat or pause modules whenever you need a refresher. However, if you feel you learn best by interacting face-to-face with classmates and instructors, an in-person course might be ideal. Classroom settings tend to stick to a strict timetable, which can help with accountability if you thrive on structure but may be challenging if you need schedule flexibility.

## Cost considerations

Because each provider sets its own fees, prices for Tennessee insurance pre-licensing courses can vary widely. Higher-cost programs typically include extra tools like study guides, exam simulators, and instructor support, whereas more budget-friendly options may rely on self-paced material without as many extras. Neither is necessarily better or worse; it depends on how much guidance you feel you’ll need.

Keep in mind that investing in high-quality study materials can reduce your chances of having to retake the exam. The cost of re-examination, plus lost time, can exceed any savings you might see from a bare-bones course. It’s better to pick a program that gives you complete confidence on test day, even if it means paying a little more up front.

## Frequently asked questions

### 1\. How long does it take to complete a pre-licensing course?

It depends on your area of study and the minimum hours required by the state. For example, you may need 20 to 40 hours per major line of authority. If you choose an online course, you might finish faster by dedicating extra study time each day, whereas in-person classes follow a set schedule.

### 2\. Can I combine multiple lines of authority in one course?

Yes, some providers offer combined courses for life & health or property & casualty. If you plan to pursue multiple licenses, a bundled program can be a convenient way to meet all of your pre-licensing hours in a single package instead of taking two separate courses.

### 3\. What happens if I don’t pass the exam on my first try?

It’s not unusual to need a second attempt. You’ll simply schedule another exam slot, but you may need to pay an additional fee. To reduce the risk of having to retake, consider a program that offers robust practice tests and personalized feedback.

### 4\. Are there any ongoing education requirements after I’m licensed?

Yes, once you hold your Tennessee insurance license, you need to complete [Tennessee continuing education](/tennessee/continuing-education) (CE) credits regularly to maintain it. Different lines of authority have different CE requirements, so it’s wise to keep track of these deadlines to avoid any license lapses.

### 5\. How do I determine if a course is state-approved?

Always verify that your chosen provider is recognized by the Tennessee Department of Commerce and Insurance. Most websites will clearly state this if they’re officially approved. If you’re unsure, you can contact the department directly to confirm.
