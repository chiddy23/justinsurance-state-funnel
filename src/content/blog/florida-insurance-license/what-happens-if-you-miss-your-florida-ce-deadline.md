Missing your Florida CE deadline is one of the most expensive mistakes a licensed agent can make. It's also one of the most preventable. Understanding what actually happens when you miss the deadline — and how to fix it if you do — helps you avoid unnecessary damage to your license and your career.

First, clear up the single biggest misconception: Florida agent and adjuster licenses are **perpetual**. There is no renewal application and no renewal fee. Your license does not expire on your CE due date, and DFS does not suspend it for missing CE. What CE non-compliance actually costs you is your **appointments** — and without an appointment, you cannot transact insurance business at all.

Here's what happens when a Florida CE deadline gets missed and how to recover.

## The Real Consequence: Your Appointments, Not Your License

Florida's Department of Financial Services takes CE compliance seriously, but the enforcement lever is spelled out in statute. Fla. Stat. § 626.2815(9) provides that the department "[may immediately terminate or refuse to renew the appointment of an agent or adjuster](https://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0600-0699/0626/Sections/0626.2815.html) who has been notified by the department that his or her continuing education requirements have not been certified, unless the agent or adjuster has been granted an extension or waiver by the department."

That's the whole mechanism: appointment termination, not license suspension.

The sequence looks like this:

1. Your CE compliance period ends at the end of your birth month.

2. Roughly 45 days later, if your hours aren't certified, DFS posts a **Preliminary Notice of Non-Compliance** and a **Settlement Stipulation** to your MyProfile account. The stipulation buys you additional time to finish the hours in exchange for agreeing to its terms, including a fine.

3. If you don't sign and comply with one of the options — a good-cause extension or the stipulation — your appointments are cancelled.

DFS correspondence about CE compliance goes to your MyProfile inbox. If you never log in, you will never see the notice, and the clock keeps running anyway.

## What Losing Your Appointments Actually Means

An unappointed license is a license you can't use:

- You cannot sell, solicit, or negotiate insurance policies for that class of business

- You cannot write new business, so there are no new commissions to earn

- Carriers are notified when appointments are terminated, and re-establishing them takes their cooperation, not just yours

- DFS may not issue you a new appointment of the same or similar type until your delinquent CE is actually completed

- If you keep transacting business without an appointment, you face enforcement action — including fines and exposure for unlicensed activity

This isn't a theoretical risk. DFS monitors CE certification against its own records, and writing business while unappointed creates serious legal exposure.

## The Financial Cost of Missing Your Deadline

Florida charges no renewal fee, because there is no renewal — but non-compliance still has a price tag:

Settlement Stipulation fine. Signing the stipulation means agreeing to a negotiated fine. That's the real "late fee" in Florida, and it exists whether or not your appointments ever get cancelled.

Appointment reinstatement costs. Getting a cancelled appointment back means a new appointment submission — roughly $25 in appointment reinstatement charges plus the $60 appointment fee. When the requested effective date is more than 45 days old, Florida law adds a [$250 delinquent-appointment fee](https://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0600-0699/0626/Sections/0626.371.html) (Fla. Stat. §626.371) — but that one is billed to the appointing carrier, which by statute may not pass it on to you.

Completion of missed CE. You'll still need to complete the full CE requirement — the hours don't go away just because you're past your due date, and DFS will not clear the non-compliance until the hours are reported.

Lost income while unappointed. Depending on how long it takes to get appointments restored, the income lost can far exceed every fee above combined.

## The Timeline Matters

How quickly you fix the situation determines how bad the damage gets.

Days to weeks past your due date. If you act before the Preliminary Notice window closes, you can usually finish the missing hours, resolve the notice, and keep your appointments intact. The business impact is minor if you move fast.

Months past your due date. Once appointments are cancelled, restoring them requires each carrier to submit a new appointment, and if the effective date is more than 45 days stale Florida bills the appointing carrier a $250 delinquent-appointment fee under Fla. Stat. §626.371 — a fee the carrier cannot charge back to you, but one more reason a carrier may be slow to re-appoint. Carriers are not obligated to re-appoint you.

Four years unappointed. This is the only scenario where the license itself goes away: a Florida license expires if it goes 48 consecutive months without an appointment. At that point you don't reinstate anything — you have to qualify as a first-time applicant again, which means current [prelicensing](/florida/prelicensing) coursework, the state exam, and the full application over again.

Moving quickly is almost always cheaper than waiting.

## How to Fix It If You've Missed Your Deadline

Step 1 — Check MyProfile. Log into the DFS portal and see exactly where you stand. Your CE transcript, outstanding hours, appointment status, and any Preliminary Notice or Settlement Stipulation all live there.

Step 2 — Complete your missing CE immediately. Enroll in DFS-approved courses to fulfill the outstanding hours, including the [4-hour Law and Ethics Update](https://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0600-0699/0626/Sections/0626.2815.html) if you haven't completed it.

Step 3 — Respond to the notice. Either request a good-cause extension or waiver, or sign the Settlement Stipulation and pay the fine it specifies. Ignoring both is what triggers appointment cancellation.

Step 4 — Confirm your CE shows as certified. Once your provider reports the hours, verify in MyProfile that your compliance period reads as satisfied before you rely on it.

Step 5 — Get your appointments back. If appointments were cancelled, contact your appointing carriers — only they can submit the new appointment. Budget for the appointment fee; if the effective date is more than 45 days old, the carrier — not you — owes a separate $250 delinquent-appointment fee under Fla. Stat. §626.371.

## How to Avoid This Situation

Prevention is dramatically easier than cure. A few practices keep you safe:

Mark your deadline everywhere. Your CE due date falls at the end of your birth month, every two years. Put it on your calendar, your phone, your planner — wherever you'll actually see it.

Complete CE early. Don't wait until the final 60 days. Build a habit of knocking out CE hours throughout the cycle.

Check MyProfile regularly. Log in at least twice per cycle to confirm hours are being reported correctly.

Use DFS-approved providers. Non-approved courses don't count, and discovering that in your final month is a disaster.

Set reminders 90 and 60 days out. Plenty of time to complete any remaining hours and resolve issues before deadline.

## 5 Frequently Asked Questions

1. Where does DFS send CE compliance notices? To your MyProfile account. That's where the Preliminary Notice of Non-Compliance and Settlement Stipulation appear, roughly 45 days after your compliance period ends. Don't rely on an email landing in your inbox — log into MyProfile and check for yourself.

2. Can I still complete CE after my due date passes? Yes. Missed CE can still be completed, and completing it is the only way to clear the non-compliance. But you'll be doing it under a Settlement Stipulation with a fine attached, and if you drag it out, your appointments get cancelled in the meantime.

3. What if I miss the deadline because of an emergency or medical issue? DFS can grant an extension or waiver for good cause. Contact DFS directly through MyProfile, explain the situation, and ask about an extension before the notice period runs. Document your circumstances thoroughly.

4. Does missing CE suspend my Florida license? No. Florida licenses are perpetual and DFS does not suspend them for CE non-compliance. What DFS does under Fla. Stat. § 626.2815(9) is terminate or refuse to renew your appointments — and without an appointment you can't write business, which produces the same practical result. The license itself only expires after 48 consecutive months with no appointment.

5. How long does it take to get back in good standing? Once your CE hours are reported and the notice or stipulation is resolved, your compliance record updates in MyProfile within days. Getting cancelled appointments back takes longer, because each appointing carrier has to submit a new appointment on your behalf.
