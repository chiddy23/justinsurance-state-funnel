Getting your [Florida insurance license](/florida) can feel like a big step, but it all begins with a clear understanding of the costs. Whether you want to sell life, health, or property and casualty insurance, the total expense typically goes beyond just the exam fee. By knowing each of the fees involved, you can create a realistic budget and set yourself up for success.

## Understand Florida insurance licenses

Before diving into the different costs, let’s clarify what Florida insurance licenses are and why they matter to you. In Florida, you usually choose from various license types, such as the 2-15 (Life, Health, and Annuities) or the 2-20 (General Lines), depending on the kind of insurance products you plan to sell. Each license has a few unique requirements, but their cost structures are fairly similar.

You’ll go through [Florida pre-licensing courses](/florida/prelicensing), fingerprinting, the state exam, application paperwork, and sometimes additional expenses like study materials or any optional workshops. Once you have a handle on these costs, you can develop a timeline that fits your personal and financial schedule.

## Meet Florida’s licensing requirements

The [Florida Department of Financial Services](/florida/requirements) (DFS) wants to ensure you’re properly educated and prepared to serve clients. Their requirements typically include:

-   Being 18 years or older and a legal resident of Florida.
-   Completing a state-approved [pre-licensing](/florida/prelicensing) course (the number of hours depends on the license type).
-   Passing the licensing exam and a background check.
-   Submitting an application with all necessary fees.

Approach these requirements with a clear plan. Many providers offer in-person or online pre-licensing courses. You’ll also want to schedule your fingerprinting appointment early on, so you don’t run into delays right before your exam date.

## Break down the pre-licensing costs

Pre-licensing courses can vary in price, but you can expect a range of around $100 to $300 or more. The fee usually includes digital study materials, practice exams, and sometimes additional support like instructor Q&A. Some schools also bundle exam prep resources into the course cost, which can be an advantage if you want everything in one package.

Keep an eye on the course length as well. You might find a budget-friendly option that’s completely self-paced, or a slightly pricier option with real-time classes and personalized feedback. The most important factor is choosing a course that fits your learning style, so you feel fully prepped on exam day.

## Fingerprinting and background checks

Florida law requires you to complete a fingerprinting session so the state can run a background check. The typical cost for fingerprinting is around $45 to $60, though prices may vary slightly if you choose a mobile fingerprinting service.

You’ll want to schedule an appointment through a state-approved vendor to ensure your fingerprints are processed quickly. It’s a smooth process that only takes a few minutes, and you can often find a location near your home. Once the background check is complete, the results are automatically shared with the Florida DFS, so you don’t have to submit additional paperwork.

## Exam fees and scheduling

After finishing your pre-licensing course, you’ll be ready to take the state exam, which is administered by Pearson VUE. Plan to spend approximately $44 for the exam fee. These fees go directly to the testing center, and you’ll likely pay them when scheduling online.

Give yourself enough time to study before picking a test date. As of February 2024, Florida no longer offers remote or online-proctored exams — all Florida insurance licensing exams must be taken in person at a Pearson VUE testing center, so plan to choose a testing location near you. The exam itself usually includes multiple-choice questions on topics like Florida insurance laws, ethics, and the specific lines of insurance you intend to sell. Passing requires a solid understanding of these areas, so consider brushing up with practice tests and review sessions before test day.

## Submit your license application

Once you’ve passed the exam, it’s time to apply for your Florida insurance license. The application fee is typically around $50 to $60 and is paid directly to the Florida DFS. You’ll submit this application online through the state’s licensing portal, providing the required documents and verifying your eligibility.

At this stage, it’s crucial to double-check everything. Make sure you have your course completion certificate, exam pass confirmation, and fingerprinting results on file. Carefully review all your personal information, and pay attention to any deadlines the Florida DFS provides. An incomplete or incorrect application can slow you down, so it’s worth taking an extra moment to ensure every detail is accurate.

## Plan for future fees

Obtaining your Florida insurance license is only the beginning. Throughout your career, you’ll need to maintain your license by completing [Florida continuing education](/florida/continuing-education) (CE) credits. Florida agent licenses are actually perpetual — there’s no license-renewal application and no renewal fee. What recurs is your CE obligation: you must complete your credits every two years, by the end of your birth month, to keep the license active. New agents complete 24 CE hours per two-year cycle, which drops to 20 hours once you’ve been licensed more than six years, and each cycle must include the state’s required Law & Ethics update course. CE courses often come with fees to cover instruction and any materials, so be sure to budget for this recurring cost.

Another cost you might encounter is Errors & Omissions (E&O) insurance, especially if you open an independent agency or transition into a role that requires personal liability coverage. E&O insurance premiums can range widely, so it’s always good to review your options and speak with a trusted insurance provider or consult your employer for group rate discounts.
