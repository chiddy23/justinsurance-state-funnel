Massachusetts has specific workers' compensation requirements that affect virtually every employer in the Commonwealth — and producers serving business clients need to understand these rules to provide appropriate coverage and avoid compliance gaps. Massachusetts workers' compensation is regulated through the Department of Industrial Accidents (DIA) and has distinctive features that differ from many other states.

Here's what Massachusetts producers should know about workers' compensation insurance requirements.

## Massachusetts Workers' Compensation Basics

Massachusetts workers' compensation is a no-fault insurance system providing benefits to employees who suffer work-related injuries or illnesses. Key principles:

No-fault coverage. Employees receive benefits regardless of who caused the injury (with limited exceptions).

Exclusive remedy. Workers' compensation is generally the exclusive remedy for work-related injuries — employees cannot typically sue employers for additional damages.

Mandatory coverage. Most Massachusetts employers must provide workers' compensation coverage for employees.

Specific benefit categories. Coverage includes medical expenses, lost wages, disability benefits, and death benefits.

Department of Industrial Accidents. Massachusetts agency administering workers' compensation.

## Who Must Carry Workers' Compensation

Under Massachusetts General Laws Chapter 152 (the Workers' Compensation Act), Massachusetts employers must provide workers' compensation coverage:

General rule. Most Massachusetts employers with one or more employees must carry workers' compensation insurance.

Specific exceptions:

Employers of domestic servants working fewer than 16 hours per week

Some agricultural workers

Real estate salespersons (under specific circumstances)

Some independent contractors (with specific tests determining contractor vs. employee status)

Coverage requirements include:

Insurance through licensed carrier, OR

Self-insurance with state approval (typically larger employers), OR

Coverage through assigned risk pool (for employers unable to obtain standard market coverage)

The "one or more employees" threshold is lower than many states, making Massachusetts workers' compensation broadly applicable.

## Massachusetts Workers' Compensation Benefits

Massachusetts workers' compensation provides several benefit categories:

Medical Benefits.

Medical expenses for work-related injuries and illnesses

Generally unlimited coverage for medically necessary treatment

Includes hospitalization, doctor visits, medications, physical therapy, etc.

Temporary Total Incapacity Benefits.

Wage replacement when worker cannot work due to injury

Generally 60% of average weekly wage (with maximum and minimum thresholds)

Continues during recovery period

Temporary Partial Incapacity Benefits.

For workers who can work but earn less due to injury

Calculated based on wage difference

Permanent Partial Incapacity Benefits.

For workers with permanent partial disabilities

Specific schedules for various injuries

May be paid as lump sum or ongoing benefits

Permanent Total Incapacity Benefits.

For workers permanently unable to work

Ongoing wage replacement benefits

Death Benefits.

For dependents of workers killed in work-related incidents

Includes burial expenses and ongoing dependent benefits

Vocational Rehabilitation.

Training and assistance for workers unable to return to previous occupations

These benefits are extensive compared to many states.

Self-Insurance. Larger employers may self-insure with DIA approval.

## Independent Contractor vs. Employee

Massachusetts workers' compensation involves the perennial independent contractor vs. employee question:

Massachusetts ABC Test. Massachusetts uses an "ABC test" to determine independent contractor status:

(A) Worker is free from control and direction in performance of services

(B) Service is performed outside the usual course of employer's business

(C) Worker is customarily engaged in independently established trade, occupation, or business

All three prongs must be satisfied for independent contractor status. This is a strict test that classifies many workers as employees rather than independent contractors.

Implications. Employers misclassifying employees as independent contractors face significant workers' compensation liability and other consequences.

For producers, helping employer clients properly classify workers reduces compliance risk.

## Penalties for Non-Compliance

Massachusetts has substantial penalties for workers' compensation non-compliance:

Stop Work Orders. DIA can issue stop work orders against non-compliant employers.

Civil Penalties. Significant fines for non-compliance.

Personal Liability. Officers and owners may face personal liability for non-compliance.

Criminal Penalties. In some circumstances, criminal charges may apply.

Worker Recovery Rights. Non-compliant employers face direct liability to injured workers.

For producers, helping clients maintain compliance avoids these severe consequences.

## 5 Frequently Asked Questions

- Who must carry workers' compensation in Massachusetts? Most Massachusetts employers with one or more employees must carry workers' compensation coverage, with limited exceptions for domestic servants working fewer than 16 hours per week, some agricultural workers, and certain other specific situations.
- What's the Massachusetts ABC test for independent contractor status? Massachusetts uses a strict ABC test requiring all three prongs: (A) worker is free from control and direction, (B) service is outside the usual course of employer's business, and (C) worker is engaged in independently established trade. All three must be satisfied for independent contractor status.
- What benefits does Massachusetts workers' compensation provide? Medical benefits, temporary total/partial incapacity benefits, permanent partial/total incapacity benefits, death benefits for dependents, and vocational rehabilitation.
- What agency administers Massachusetts workers' compensation? The Department of Industrial Accidents (DIA) administers Massachusetts workers' compensation, including claim adjudication, fraud investigation, and rule enforcement.
- What happens if a Massachusetts employer doesn't carry workers' compensation? Penalties include stop work orders, significant civil penalties, personal liability for officers and owners, possible criminal charges, and direct liability to injured workers. Massachusetts enforces workers' compensation requirements actively.
