New York insurance continuing education (CE) courses are a crucial piece of staying licensed and confident in your insurance career. Whether you are just beginning your journey toward becoming an insurance professional or you already hold a New York license and want to renew, understanding the requirements for both pre-licensing and ongoing CE will help you meet state guidelines. Below, you will find clear steps to follow, practical tips to stay motivated, and insider pointers on making the most of your learning experience.

## Understand New York CE requirements

Growing your insurance expertise starts with knowing the rules. In New York, the Department of Financial Services typically requires you to complete a set number of hours of continuing education every two years to keep your license active. CE courses ensure you stay updated on critical industry changes, emerging trends, and new regulations.

By taking the right courses, you will be better prepared to offer informed guidance to your clients, stand out to employers, and ultimately protect your license from lapsing. New York agents and brokers need 15 credit hours per two-year licensing period — but you cannot fill all 15 with general coursework. For licenses renewing on or after April 1, 2022, DFS requires at least one hour each of insurance law, ethics and professionalism, and diversity, inclusion and elimination of bias. If you are licensed for one or more property/casualty lines, add at least one hour of flood insurance instruction, and if you sell flood coverage through the NFIP, at least three hours of enhanced flood insurance instruction. Plan those mandated credits first, then fill the balance — a general course will not satisfy them.

## Complete your license pre-work

If you are brand new to the insurance field, you will begin with New York prelicensing courses. This is distinct from ongoing CE but sets the foundation for your entire career. During these initial courses, you will focus on core insurance principles and specialized content matching the line of authority you intend to pursue, such as Life, Accident & Health, or Property & Casualty.

Plan to invest time in studying the basics. By building a strong foundation, you will grasp more advanced topics later on without feeling overwhelmed. Prelicensing hours vary widely by line of authority, so budget your time against the line you actually intend to write: DFS sets Life at 20 hours and Accident & Health at 20 hours, combined Life, Accident & Health at 40 hours, Personal Lines at 40 hours, and Property/Casualty at 90 hours. The agent and broker versions of each license carry the same hour requirement — a Property/Casualty Broker course is 90 hours, same as the agent course. If you are heading for a P&C license, plan for a serious commitment, not a long weekend.

## Pick the right CE courses

Once you have your license, you will turn your attention to the continuing education credits for your renewal. While you have plenty of New York insurance CE courses to choose from, picking high-quality ones that match your needs can make a real difference. If you are unsure where to start, consider the following:

1.  DFS approval first: This is the non-negotiable one. New York CE credit only comes from a provider DFS has approved and from courses DFS has approved. A course that is excellent but unapproved earns you nothing toward your 15 credits. Verify approval before you pay.
2.  Topic relevance: Look for courses that address your day-to-day practice or areas you want to expand into. For example, if you work mostly with auto policies, a course on commercial auto coverage might sharpen your expertise.
3.  Mandated topic coverage: Make sure the courses you choose actually carry the insurance law, ethics and professionalism, and diversity/inclusion designations — and the flood credit if you hold a P&C line.
4.  Format and flexibility: If you have a busy schedule, self-paced online courses can give you the freedom to complete modules at your own speed without sacrificing quality.

## Plan and finish your studies

The best way to stay on track with CE is to schedule your study periods in advance. Working through your courses in small chunks over time is often more effective than trying to power through in one sitting. Aim to finish your requirements well before your renewal deadline. That way, if you face an unexpected hiccup, you will still have some breathing space.

You can keep motivation high by setting short goals. Choose a certain number of modules or chapters to complete each week, and reward yourself when you hit each milestone. Not only does this help you stay focused, but it also reduces stress when renewals loom closer.

## Take and pass your exam

Some New York insurance CE courses require an exam at the end. Usually, these tests confirm that you understand the main concepts and can apply them in real-life scenarios. Before test day, review key points, summaries, or glossaries included in your study material.

Consider creating a short cheat sheet for yourself. Summarize the top 10 to 15 points you think are most likely to surface on the exam, such as key definitions, important regulations, or best practices. Stating them in your own words helps you retain information and refines your focus.

## Renew your license on time

After completing your coursework and any required exams, it is time to renew your license. In New York, you typically renew every two years. The process usually involves paying a renewal fee and submitting proof of your completed CE hours. The easiest way to avoid lapses or penalties is to set a reminder at least a month before your renewal date so you can double-check that everything is in order.

If you wait until right before your license expires, you risk running out of time to fix any errors or retake a missed examination. Renewing promptly also keeps you safe from any fines and ensures you can keep working without interruption. Plus, if you discover you need additional credits, there is still a window to finish them without stress.

## Budget for necessary costs

When planning for your insurance career, assume you will need to set aside funds for course fees, exam registration, and state renewal fees. These costs can vary widely:

• Course fees: Some online courses are packaged by topic or license line, while others allow you to pay per credit hour.  
• Exam registration: Paid directly to the testing centers or state agencies, depending on the exam.  
• Renewal fees: A state fee you pay during the renewal process as part of maintaining your license.

As you calculate these expenses, remember that investing in quality courses often pays off through increased knowledge, higher client satisfaction, and fewer retakes.
