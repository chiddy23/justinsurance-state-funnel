Missouri's license application process is the simplest of all comparison states — no PLE requirement, no fingerprinting vendor, a flat $100 fee that covers all lines on the same application, and 5-10 business day processing. The primary variables are exam preparation time (self-directed), the two-section exam format requiring 70% on each section separately, and the 24-48 hour wait before applying after passing. With no temporary license available, the 5-10 business day processing timeline matters — but it's among the fastest of the comparison states. Here's the precise walkthrough.

## The MO Licensing Sequence

Most streamlined of all comparison states:

(Optional) Study preparation

Schedule and pass Pearson VUE exam (both sections at 70%+)

Wait 24-48 hours for results to load in DCI system

Submit NIPR application with $100 fee

Wait 5-10 business days for processing

License issued

No PLE, no fingerprinting, no vendor appointments — exam + application.

## Step-by-Step Walkthrough

STEP 1: Choose Your Line(s) of Authority

Major lines in Missouri with exam format:

Life: 100 questions; 2 hours; $29

Accident & Health: ~100 questions; 2 hours; $29-$35

Life & A&H (combined): 170 questions; 3 hours; $35

Property: 105 questions; 2 hours; $29-$35

Casualty: 105 questions; 2 hours; $29-$35

P&C (combined): 165 questions; 3 hours; $35

Personal Lines: 125 questions; 2.5 hours; $35

Combined exam advantage: Missouri offers combined L&H (170 questions, 3 hours, $35) and P&C (165 questions, 3 hours, $35). Both cost $35 — for P&C, that's $35 vs. $58-$70 for two separate exams; for L&H, similar savings.

STEP 2: Schedule Pearson VUE Exam

Go to pearsonvue.com/us/en/mo/insurance.html

Schedule at least 24 hours in advance (5 days for electronic check)

Pay exam fee ($29-$35 depending on exam type)

All exams in-person only — effective May 7, 2025 (no OnVUE)

Pearson VUE test centers in Missouri: St. Louis area (multiple), Kansas City area (multiple), Springfield, Columbia, Jefferson City, Joplin, Cape Girardeau.

STEP 3: Take and Pass Both Sections

Arrive 30 minutes early; two government-issued IDs (one with photo); no personal items.

Two-section format: National section scored separately from Missouri state section. Each must reach 70%. If fail one section: wait 24 hours → retake only failed section.

Results: Immediate pass/fail notification. Diagnostic report available in Pearson VUE account.

STEP 4: Submit NIPR Application (After 24-48 Hours)

After passing both sections:

Wait 24-48 hours for results to load in DCI system

Go to nipr.com or apply online at insurance.mo.gov

Complete Uniform Application

Pay $100 application fee + $5.60 NIPR transaction fee

Answer all background disclosure questions accurately

Paper alternative: Download Uniform Application → mail with $100 check to DCI PO Box 4001, Jefferson City, MO 65102.

Processing: 5-10 business days. Apply within 1 year of passing exam.

## Costs Summary

Missouri's total licensing cost is among the lowest of comparison states despite the $100 application fee, because the exam fee is exceptionally low.

## Non-Resident Process

Non-residents apply through NIPR with home state license in good standing — same $100 fee. No PLE, no MO exam required for residents of states with reciprocity agreements.

## 5 Frequently Asked Questions

- Why do I have to wait 24-48 hours after passing before applying? After the Pearson VUE exam, results need 24-48 hours to transfer to the DCI licensing database. If you submit the NIPR application before results appear in DCI's system, the application may be delayed or returned incomplete. Wait the full 24-48 hours before submitting.
- Can I apply for multiple lines of authority on the same $100 application? Yes — Missouri's $100 application fee covers all lines included on the same application submission. Unlike Indiana ($40 per line) or MN ($50 per line + $20 tech fee), Missouri charges a flat $100 regardless of how many lines are on the application. For candidates applying for both Life/Health AND P&C, the same $100 application covers all lines.
- What is the fee waiver and who qualifies? In accordance with Missouri statutory laws, certain low-income individuals and veterans can apply for a License Fee Waiver with DCI — potentially waiving the $100 application fee. Contact DCI at insurance.mo.gov for current fee waiver application procedures and eligibility criteria.
- What test centers are available in Missouri? Pearson VUE has test centers throughout Missouri including the St. Louis metro area (multiple locations), Kansas City metro area (multiple), Springfield, Columbia, Jefferson City, Joplin, and Cape Girardeau. With no OnVUE option as of May 7, 2025, candidates must travel to a physical test center. Most Missouri population centers have nearby Pearson VUE access.
- What happens if I pass one section but fail the other on the combined exam? Missouri's section-specific retake is one of its most candidate-friendly features. If you pass the national section but fail the Missouri state section (or vice versa), you can retake ONLY the failed section after a 24-hour wait — paying the retake fee for only the one section rather than the entire combined exam. This is more efficient than states where failing any portion requires retaking the entire exam.
