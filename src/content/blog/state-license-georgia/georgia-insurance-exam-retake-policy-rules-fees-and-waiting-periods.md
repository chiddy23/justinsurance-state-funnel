Failing the Georgia insurance exam is disappointing — but it's not the end of your career path. Plenty of now-successful Georgia agents failed on their first attempt. What matters is understanding exactly what comes next so you can regroup quickly and pass on your retake.

Here's everything you need to know about Georgia insurance exam retake rules.

## First: What Happens When You Fail

When you finish the Georgia exam at a Prometric testing center, your pass/fail result appears on screen immediately. If you don't pass, you receive a printed score report showing your performance broken down by content area.

This score report is valuable — it's the most efficient diagnostic tool you have for figuring out what to study before your next attempt. It shows where you lost the most points, letting you prioritize those sections specifically.

Keep the score report. Don't discard it. You'll want it for your focused retake preparation.

## The Waiting Period

Georgia requires a 24-hour minimum waiting period before you can retake the exam. You can schedule your retake as early as the following day.

However, retaking immediately is rarely a good idea. Here's why:

One day isn't enough time to address weak areas. You need focused study to improve.

Test-day mindset needs recovery. Walking in still frustrated from the previous attempt affects performance.

Patterns repeat without intervention. Taking it again with the same preparation level typically produces the same result.

Most candidates who succeed on retake wait at least 1–2 weeks and use that time strategically.

## Retake Fees

Each Georgia exam attempt requires:

- New Prometric registration at prometric.com

- Full exam fee payment (approximately $63 per attempt, confirm current fee at registration)

The retake fee is the same as the initial attempt fee — no discounts for retakes. This is motivation to prepare thoroughly before each attempt.

## Number of Attempts Allowed

Georgia does not cap the total number of times you can retake the exam. You can keep attempting until you pass.

However:

- Each attempt costs money

- Each attempt requires waiting at least 24 hours

- Multiple failed attempts can indicate deeper preparation issues

- OCI may eventually review applicants with repeated failures

Treating each attempt as potentially your last is smart psychology. Prepare seriously and don't rely on unlimited retakes as a backup plan.

## How to Come Back Stronger

A failed attempt hurts — but it also gives you information you didn't have before. Use that information:

Step 1 — Review your score report carefully.

Look at the content areas where you performed poorly. These are your priorities. If your Life Insurance Provisions score was 50% but your General Concepts score was 80%, spend most of your retake preparation on Life Insurance Provisions.

Step 2 — Don't just reread your notes.

If rereading notes was enough, you would have passed the first time. Switch to:

- Active [practice questions](/georgia/practice-exam) — work through as many exam-style questions as possible on your weak content areas

- Teaching out loud — explain concepts to yourself or someone else without looking at notes

- Writing out explanations — force yourself to articulate concepts rather than just recognize them

Step 3 — Take full-length practice exams.

If you didn't take practice exams before your first attempt, take them now. If you did, take more. Practice exams:

- Build endurance

- Identify areas where you fade late in the exam

- Expose you to question styles you may not have seen

- Calibrate how long each question takes

Step 4 — Give yourself adequate time.

Don't rush back. One to two weeks of focused preparation beats one day of cramming. Three to four weeks is often ideal for candidates who struggled significantly with their first attempt.

Step 5 — Address test anxiety if it was a factor.

Some candidates know the material but freeze during exams. If that's you:

- Take more practice exams under realistic conditions

- Work on deep breathing and reset techniques

- Arrive early so you're settled before starting

- Remember that you can flag and revisit questions

## Common Patterns in Failed Attempts

Understanding why candidates typically fail helps you avoid the same mistakes:

Underprepared for Georgia-specific content. Generic national study materials don't cover state law in enough depth. Fix: use Georgia-specific [prelicense](/georgia/prelicensing) materials and focus on state law for retake.

Insufficient practice questions. Reading material isn't the same as practicing application. Fix: do significantly more practice questions before retake.

Weak on health insurance or annuities. These sections trip up candidates who focused mostly on life insurance. Fix: dedicate specific retake time to these areas.

Poor test-taking strategy. Rushing, not flagging, leaving questions blank, misreading. Fix: practice with realistic conditions and deliberate strategy.

Cramming. Trying to learn material in days. Fix: spread study over weeks for actual retention.

## Scheduling Your Retake

Re-register through Prometric. Same process as your initial registration — go to prometric.com, register, choose your retake date and testing center.

Choose your date carefully. Don't book too soon. Don't book too far out either — too much waiting can cause you to lose momentum. One to four weeks after your first attempt is typically the sweet spot.

Consider a different testing center. Some candidates report doing better at different Prometric locations. If one location felt uncomfortable, try another nearby.

Pay the fee. Confirm payment and save the registration confirmation.

## What Happens If You Keep Failing

If you're on attempt three or four without passing:

Seriously reconsider your preparation approach. The strategy that didn't work once, twice, three times isn't going to work on the fifth attempt.

Consider a different prelicense course. If your original course didn't get you to pass, a different structure might work better.

Invest in exam prep specifically. Structured exam prep programs focus more on practice questions than initial learning.

Work with a tutor or mentor. Sometimes having someone explain weak areas personally makes the difference.

Check that you're using Georgia-specific materials. Too many candidates fail because they're studying generic national content for a state-specific exam.

## When Your Exam Results Expire

Georgia exam results don't last forever. If you pass but delay submitting your license application:

- Your exam results are typically valid for 1 year from the date you passed

- You must submit your license application within that window

- If you wait beyond the valid period, you may need to retake the exam

Don't pass and then sit on your results. Submit your application quickly after passing.

## 5 Frequently Asked Questions

1. How soon can I retake the Georgia insurance exam after failing? After a minimum 24-hour waiting period. But waiting 1–2 weeks for focused preparation typically produces much better results.

2. Do I have to redo my prelicense course if I fail the exam? No. Your prelicense completion remains valid. You only need to re-register with Prometric and pay the retake fee.

3. Will a failed attempt show up on my license application? No. OCI's license application process considers your most recent passing result, not your full attempt history.

4. Is there a limit on how many times I can retake? Georgia doesn't cap attempts. You can keep trying until you pass, though each attempt requires time and money.

5. What if I'm running out of time on my valid exam results? Submit your license application immediately. Exam results are typically valid for 1 year from the date you passed. Don't risk having to retake.
