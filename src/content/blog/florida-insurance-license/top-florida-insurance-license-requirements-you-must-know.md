You may have heard that the [Florida insurance license requirements](/florida/requirements) can feel overwhelming at first. The good news is that the process becomes much easier once you know what to expect. Whether you plan to sell life insurance, health insurance, property and casualty, or a combination of these, understanding the steps in advance helps you move forward with confidence. Below, you will find a friendly, step-by-step guide to meeting Florida’s key requirements and earning your license.

## Check basic eligibility criteria

You need to make sure you meet Florida’s most fundamental requirements before moving on to any applications or exams. This first step saves time and prevents surprises that might delay your license.

Generally, you must be at least 18 years old and a legal resident of Florida or a full-time resident for at least half of the year. Non-residents must often follow other specific guidelines, but if you plan to live and work in Florida, you should provide a valid ID, Social Security Number, and a physical address in the state. Be prepared to prove residency if asked, usually by supplying a driver’s license or utility bill.

## Complete your pre-licensing education

Florida has separate [pre-licensing requirements](/florida/prelicensing) for different lines of insurance, such as life and health. These courses equip you with the knowledge needed to pass the exam and serve your future clients responsibly. You can take these courses online or in a classroom, depending on your learning style and schedule.

## Gather the necessary documents

Before filling out your licensing application, take time to assemble all the relevant documents and personal information in one place. Having everything on hand makes the process faster and keeps you from scrambling at the last minute.

If you have taken your pre-licensing course, gather proof of course completion, usually a certificate provided by your course instructor or online platform. You may also need standard identification (such as a driver’s license or passport) and official documents with your current address, along with a record of any legal name changes if you have them. Even though it might feel like extra work, this prep step can help you avoid any roadblocks during your application submission.

## Submit your application

With your paperwork in order, you are ready to submit your formal application through the Florida Department of Financial Services website. You will create an account and answer specific questions about your background, education, and any criminal history. The application also prompts you to list the lines of authority you plan to pursue, such as life insurance or property and casualty.

Florida charges an application fee, which may vary depending on the type of license. You can pay this fee online at the time of submission. Expect the cost to range from around 50 to 100 dollars. If you decide to add multiple lines of authority, that might affect your total fee. Once you have submitted your application, you can keep track of its progress online.

## Schedule and pass your exam

After your application is processed, you can schedule your licensing exam. Florida typically partners with third-party exam administrators who offer multiple testing sites statewide. When you book your date, consider what time of day you perform best to give yourself the greatest chance of passing on the first try.

Each exam focuses on your knowledge of insurance laws, ethics, and product details for the line of insurance you intend to sell. The number of multiple-choice questions depends on the license type: a single-line Life (2-14) or Health (2-40) exam has 95 questions with a two-hour limit, the combined Life, Health & Annuity (2-15) exam has 165 questions over two hours and forty-five minutes, and the 2-20 General Lines property and casualty exam has 175 questions over three hours. Every exam mixes in a set of unscored pretest questions that do not count toward your result — 10 on each single-line exam (85 scored out of 95) and 15 on the 2-15 and 2-20 exams (150 and 160 scored, respectively). To prepare effectively, review your pre-licensing course materials, use practice exams, and create a study schedule that begins at least a few weeks ahead of your test date. Once you pass, you will receive an official exam score, which you will use to complete your licensing requirements.

## Expect a background check

Florida is careful about who receives an insurance license, and part of that security involves a background check. After you pass your exam, you will typically go through fingerprinting and a review of your criminal history. This procedure is standard and ensures that you meet state regulations and demonstrate trustworthy conduct in a role that involves personal financial details.

If you have any past legal issues, do not panic. Being transparent and submitting all required documentation is often the best way to address potential concerns. In many cases, minor infractions do not necessarily block your license approval, especially if you have completed any required follow-up or restitution.

## Maintain your license

Getting your [Florida insurance license](/florida) is only the beginning of your professional journey. To stay in good standing with the state, you need to meet [continuing education](/florida/continuing-education) (CE) requirements. CE courses ensure that your knowledge remains current and that you are aware of changes in insurance laws, rules, and industry trends.

You may be required to complete a certain number of CE hours every two years. For instance, life and health license holders might take a short course on emerging medical coverage options, while property and casualty licensees might have to study updated flood insurance regulations. Keeping your license active is simpler if you plan your [continuing education](/florida/continuing-education) throughout the two-year compliance cycle, rather than rushing toward the deadline.
