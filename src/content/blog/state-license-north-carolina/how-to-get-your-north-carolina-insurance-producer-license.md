Getting your North Carolina insurance producer license opens doors to one of the fastest-growing insurance markets in the Southeast. Charlotte's substantial banking and financial services concentration, the Research Triangle's technology and healthcare growth, the booming coastal real estate market, and North Carolina's substantial population growth create real opportunity for licensed agents. North Carolina's licensing process recently became significantly more accessible — as of October 1, 2025, North Carolina eliminated the [prelicense](/north-carolina/prelicensing) education requirement, making the path to licensure faster and more flexible.

Here's exactly how to get your North Carolina insurance producer license.

## Step 1: Meet the Basic Eligibility Requirements

Before starting, confirm you meet North Carolina's baseline requirements:

You must be at least 18 years old

You must have a Social Security Number (SSN) or Federal Employer Identification Number (FEIN)

You must provide proof of citizenship and residency

You must be a North Carolina resident (for resident license) — non-residents apply through reciprocity

You must be able to pass a state and federal criminal history background check

You must truthfully complete the license application and disclose any relevant history

These eligibility requirements are governed under the North Carolina General Statutes (Chapter 58 — Insurance) and administered by the North Carolina Department of Insurance (NCDOI).

## Step 2: Pre-Licensing Education (NOT Required as of October 1, 2025)

Here's a major recent change: North Carolina House Bill 737 (Session Law 2025-45), effective October 1, 2025, eliminated the prelicense education (PLE) requirement for all North Carolina insurance producer license lines. North Carolina now joins states like Pennsylvania, South Carolina, Massachusetts, Alabama, and Nevada in not requiring formal prelicense courses.

However, prelicense education is strongly recommended. Here's why:

The North Carolina state exam covers substantial content

Each exam covers both general insurance topics and North Carolina-specific laws

Most candidates significantly improve their [pass rate](/pass-rates) by completing structured study

Quality prelicense courses include practice exams that mirror the actual state exam

Previous PLE requirements (no longer in effect):

Life: 20 hours

Accident & Health or Sickness: 20 hours

Property: 20 hours

Casualty: 20 hours

Personal Lines: 20 hours

Medicare Supplement/LTC: 10 hours

Combined Life/A&H or Sickness: 40 hours

While you can technically skip prelicense study and go straight to the exam, doing so reduces your chances of passing on the first try. Most successful candidates complete a prelicense course even though it's not legally required.

## Step 3: Submit Your License Application Through NIPR FIRST

Important: North Carolina uses an application-first approach. You must submit your license application BEFORE taking the state exam. This is different from most states.

Apply through: National Insurance Producer Registry (NIPR) at nipr.com

Application steps:

Step 1: Go to nipr.com and click "Apply for a license."

Step 2: Click the New Insurance License button within the "New Insurance Licenses" section.

Step 3: Choose "Resident," then "Individual."

Step 4: Complete the application with:

Legal name (matching government-issued ID exactly)

Date of birth

SSN or FEIN

North Carolina home address

Lines of authority you intend to obtain

Disclosure question answers

Step 5: Pay application fees:

Base application fee: $82 (includes $38 fingerprint fee for first-time applicants)

Plus $50 per line of authority

Step 6: Submit and receive confirmation.

Step 7: Receive an email from Pearson VUE within an hour containing:

Your reserved National Producer Number (NPN)

Authorization to test

Information needed to schedule your exam

Step 8: Receive an email from NIPR with fingerprinting instructions.

NIPR application validity: 6 months from submission. You must complete all requirements within this window.

## Step 4: Schedule and Take Your State Exam Through Pearson VUE

After your NIPR application generates your authorization to test, schedule your exam:

Pearson VUE contact:

Website: pearsonvue.com or home.pearsonvue.com/nc/insurance

Phone: 866-265-6329 or (800) 274-0668

Pay the $45 exam fee per exam at registration

## North Carolina Exam Structure

North Carolina has a distinctive exam format compared to most states. Passing score is 70% (at least 39 of 55 scored questions correct).

Common NC exam types:

Important North Carolina specifics:

Each line is a SEPARATE exam ($45 each)

NO combined Property and Casualty exam — Property and Casualty are two distinct exams ($90 total)

NO combined Life and Health exam — Life and Accident & Health or Sickness are two distinct exams ($90 total)

All exams are 55 questions and 1 hour 15 minutes (shorter than most states)

Identification requirements:

Valid photo ID (driver's license, passport, or other government-issued ID)

Names must match registration exactly

After passing each exam, Pearson VUE electronically transmits your passing score to NIPR to match with your pending application.

## Step 5: Submit Your Fingerprints

North Carolina requires fingerprinting through a distinctive process — done through local law enforcement rather than a centralized provider like IdentoGO.

Important: Submit fingerprints within 30 days of submitting your NIPR application.

Fingerprinting process:

Step 1: After submitting your NIPR application, you'll receive an email with a link to print the Fingerprint Submission Packet-Releases Forms.

Step 2: Print the forms:

Cover page

Pages 1 and 3 (you complete these)

Page 2 (law enforcement signs)

Step 3: Call your local law enforcement agency:

Confirm they perform Livescan fingerprints for non-criminal reasons

Schedule an appointment

Note: Fingerprint cards are NOT accepted — must be Livescan electronic

Step 4: Attend your fingerprinting appointment:

Bring valid photo ID

Bring completed forms (with pages 1 and 3 filled out)

Have law enforcement sign page 2

Pay any local fingerprinting fee

Step 5: Submit completed forms to North Carolina Licensing Office of Pearson VUE:

Fingerprints transmitted electronically to NC State Bureau of Investigation (SBI)

SBI conducts state and federal criminal history background check

SBI sends hard copy to NC Licensing Office at Pearson VUE

Fingerprinting fee: $38 (included in the $82 base application fee for first-time applicants).

Fingerprinting Exemption: Insurance producers licensed before October 1, 2010 are exempt under legacy provisions.

## Step 6: NCDOI Reviews and Issues Your License

Once Pearson VUE has your complete application, exam results, and fingerprint background check, processing typically takes:

Standard processing: 48-72 hours after all requirements are met.

Applications with disclosures: May add 4-6 weeks for additional review.

You'll receive an email notifying you to print your license once it's approved.

Important: NCDOI processes licenses electronically — print your license from the system.

## Step 7: Get Appointed by Insurance Carriers

North Carolina requires producers to be appointed by carriers to sell their products. Most carriers handle the appointment process once you've contracted with them. The carrier files the appointment with the NCDOI.

## Total Timeline

From starting study to holding your active North Carolina license, most candidates finish in 2-5 weeks:

Self-study (recommended even though not required): 1-3 weeks

Application submission: same day

Exam scheduling and completion: 1-2 weeks (multiple exams may extend this)

Fingerprinting: 1-2 weeks within 30 days of application

Background check processing: 1-3 weeks

License processing: 48-72 hours after all requirements met

The elimination of PLE requirements has made North Carolina one of the faster states for licensing.

Common North Carolina insurance producer license types include:

Casualty — liability and related coverage

Personal Lines — limited P&C for personal (non-commercial) insurance only

Variable Life and Variable Annuity Products — variable products requiring underlying Life license; exempt from prelicense and exam (must hold Life license + securities licensing)

Important: Each line of authority requires a separate exam (where exam is required) and an additional $50 application fee per line.

## License Renewal

North Carolina producer licenses are valid for 2 years from issuance:

Renewal cycle: Biennial, on the last day of birth month

Year: Determined by birth year (even-year birth = even-year expiration, odd-year birth = odd-year expiration)

CE requirement: 24 hours every 2 years (3 hours Ethics)

CE deadline: Must be completed at least 60 days before license expiration

No renewal fee for major lines licenses

Late renewal: 4-month grace period with $75 reinstatement fee

After 4 months: Must restart entire licensing process

Fingerprinting: Required EVERY OTHER renewal period ($38 fee)

This periodic re-fingerprinting requirement is distinctive to North Carolina among insurance licensing states.

## Special Path: Moving to North Carolina From Another State

If you're already licensed as an insurance producer in another state and you move to North Carolina:

Reciprocity available for licensed agents in good standing.

Application process:

Apply through NIPR

Provide home state license verification

Pay non-resident application fee

Submit any required documentation

This streamlined path makes North Carolina accessible for already-licensed producers from other states.

## 5 Frequently Asked Questions

- Does North Carolina require pre-licensing education? No — as of October 1, 2025. North Carolina House Bill 737 (Session Law 2025-45) eliminated the prelicense education requirement for all insurance producer lines. However, structured study is strongly recommended given the exam difficulty.
- Does North Carolina require fingerprinting for insurance producers? Yes. All initial resident producer license applicants must complete fingerprinting through local law enforcement (Livescan), with results processed by the NC State Bureau of Investigation. Insurance producers licensed before October 1, 2010 are exempt under legacy provisions.
- How much does the North Carolina insurance exam cost? $45 per exam, paid to Pearson VUE at registration. Each line of authority requires a separate exam.
- How long is my North Carolina insurance license valid? 2 years from the last day of your birth month, with even-year birthday = even-year expiration and odd-year birthday = odd-year expiration. Renewals require completing CE at least 60 days before expiration.
- What's the application fee for a North Carolina resident producer license? $82 base fee (includes $38 fingerprint fee for first-time applicants) + $50 per line of authority. Adding all four major lines totals approximately $282 in application fees plus $180 in exam fees.
