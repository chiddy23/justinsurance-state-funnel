Wisconsin's license application process follows a clear five-step sequence: OCI-approved PLE (20 hours/line, and if you take it online or by self-study it has to end with a proctored course exam), Fieldprint fingerprinting (about $35), PSI exam ($75/exam), NIPR application ($10 license fee), and 1-3 business day processing. The 180-day score validity creates the outer timing constraint. No combined exams are available, making each line a separate investment of PLE time and exam fees. What is *not* per-line is the state application fee: Wisconsin's license application fee is a flat $10 per application no matter how many lines you carry, so even a full multi-line build (Life + Health + Property + Casualty) adds only about $16 in application cost. Here's the precise walkthrough.

## The WI Licensing Sequence

Five-step sequence with 180-day score window:

Complete 20-hour PLE per line (8 hrs shared + 12 hrs line-specific) → pass the proctored course exam if your PLE is online/self-study → Certificate of Completion → 180-day clock starts upon passing PSI exam

Complete Fieldprint fingerprinting (about $35) — can do before or after exam

Schedule and pass PSI exam ($75/line) — bring Certificate of Completion + ID

Wait 48-72 hours after passing → submit NIPR application ($10 license fee + $5.60)

Wait 1-3 business days for OCI processing → license issued

## Step-by-Step Walkthrough

STEP 1: Choose Your Line(s) of Authority

Lines with PLE structure and exam format:

Life: 8 hrs (common) + 12 hrs (Life-specific) = 20 hrs; 100 questions; 2 hours; $75

Accident & Health: 8 hrs + 12 hrs = 20 hrs; 100 questions; 2 hours; $75

Property: 8 hrs + 12 hrs = 20 hrs; 100 questions; 2 hours; $75

Casualty: 8 hrs + 12 hrs = 20 hrs; 100 questions; 2 hours; $75

Personal Lines: 8 hrs + 12 hrs = 20 hrs; 100 questions; 2 hours; $75

Multi-line efficiency: The 8-hour common section (principles + WI laws + ethics) need not be repeated within 12 months. A candidate completing Life PLE first and then adding Health PLE within 12 months only needs 12 additional hours (not 20).

The proctored course exam most candidates don't budget for: this is the step people miss. Under Wis. Admin. Code Ins 26.09, any correspondence, self-study, audio, video, or online prelicensing course has to end with a proctored examination before the school can award credit — a minimum of 25 questions on the 8-hour common section and 50 questions on each line-specific section, with a passing score of 70% or better on each. Your proctor must be an impartial, disinterested third party (or a currently licensed agent) with no family or financial relationship to you; membership in a professional association doesn't count as a financial relationship. The proctor verifies your identity and signs an affidavit that you received no outside assistance, and that affidavit goes back to the approved school along with your exam. OCI and PSI both state the point flatly: this exam is prior to and in addition to the PSI licensing examination. No proctored exam and affidavit means no Certificate of Completion — and no Certificate of Completion means PSI turns you away at the test center and you forfeit your $75 exam fee. Line up your proctor while you're still working through the coursework, not the week you want to test.

STEP 2: Complete Fieldprint Fingerprinting

Schedule at fieldprintwisconsin.com using code FPWIOCIInsurance.

Fee: about $35 — OCI's fingerprinting page currently posts $34.75, while the Wisconsin candidate information bulletin lists $36.00. Confirm the exact amount at Fieldprint checkout

Bring two forms of signature ID (primary must be government-issued with photo)

Fingerprints valid for 180 days

Can complete before or after PSI exam — but must be done before OCI issues license

STEP 3: Schedule and Pass PSI Exam

Go to test-takers.psiexams.com/wiins

Create account or log in

Select exam (first-time: "General and State Specific" — e.g., "22-01 Life, General and State Specific")

Pay $75 exam fee

Choose in-person PSI test center or PSI Bridge remote testing (requires Chrome; check compatibility at syscheck.bridge.psiexams.com)

Exam day: Arrive 30 minutes early; bring government ID + Certificate of Completion (the certificate is valid for one year from issue — if you haven't passed within that year you retake the PLE course). Score displayed on screen immediately.

If fail: a roughly 2-calendar-day mandatory wait applies before rescheduling; pay another $75 exam fee.

STEP 4: Submit NIPR Application (After 48-72 Hours)

After passing exam:

Wait 48-72 hours for results to load in OCI system

Go to nipr.com

Complete Intermediary (Producer) Individual application

Pay the $10 license fee + $5.60 NIPR transaction fee

Answer all background disclosure questions accurately

Submit documentation for any affirmative background responses

Apply within 180 days of passing exam.

STEP 5: Wait for OCI Processing

Processing: 1-3 business days once OCI has a complete file — application, passing exam result, and fingerprint background check all in hand.

After 90 days from application submission, any required documentation not yet received causes application to expire.

## Costs Summary

What the state-side steps cost, per line of authority:

Pre-licensing education (20 hours per line): varies by provider

Proctor for the self-study/online course exam: you arrange the proctor yourself, so if you use a paid proctoring service that cost is yours — ask your school what it supplies before you assume it's covered

Fieldprint fingerprinting: about $35 — paid once, not per line

PSI exam: $75 per exam (each retake is another $75)

Application fee: a flat $10 per license application — this covers every line you've passed exams for, it is not charged per line — plus a $5.60 NIPR transaction fee

Because Wisconsin charges a separate $75 exam fee per line and offers no combined exams, a full multi-line build (Life, Accident & Health, Property, Casualty) runs $300 in exam fees before any coursework — but the state license application itself is a flat $10 (plus the $5.60 NIPR transaction fee), not a per-line charge.

## 6 Frequently Asked Questions

- Do I have to take a proctored exam on my prelicensing course itself? Yes, if your course is online, self-study, correspondence, audio, or video. Wis. Admin. Code Ins 26.09 requires those courses to conclude with a proctored examination — 25 questions minimum on the 8-hour common section and 50 questions on each line-specific section, 70% to pass each — before the school can give you credit. You supply an impartial, disinterested third-party proctor (or a currently licensed agent) with no family or financial relationship to you; that person verifies your ID and signs an affidavit that you had no outside help, and the affidavit goes back to the school. It is a separate exam from, and comes before, the PSI licensing exam. Classroom courses do not have this extra step.
- Can I submit the NIPR application before fingerprinting is complete? Yes — you can submit the NIPR application while fingerprinting is being processed through Fieldprint. OCI reviews the background check as part of processing. However, OCI will not issue the license until both the application and fingerprint background check are complete. Complete Fieldprint as early as possible to avoid delays.
- What is the 90-day documentation deadline? After submitting the NIPR application, you have 90 days to submit any required additional documentation (for affirmative background responses, etc.) before the application expires. OCI will request specific documentation if needed — respond promptly to avoid expiration.
- How does the 12-month PLE sharing window work in practice? If you complete Life PLE and pass your Certification Exam on March 1, the 8-hour common section is valid for any additional line's PLE through February 28 of the following year. During that window, adding A&H only requires 12 additional hours of A&H-specific PLE (not 20). After the 12-month window, the 8-hour common section must be repeated for a new line.
- Is the Fieldprint appointment mandatory before the PSI exam? No — you can schedule the PSI exam before completing Fieldprint. However, OCI requires fingerprint background check results before issuing the license. Most candidates complete Fieldprint concurrently with PSI exam preparation to minimize overall timeline.
- Can I apply for multiple lines of authority on the same NIPR application? Yes — if you have passed exams for multiple lines (Life and A&H, for example), you can submit a single NIPR application covering both lines. The $10 license fee is charged once per application, not per line — a single application submission covers every line you've passed exams for. This is different from Wisconsin's exam structure (no combined exams) — you take separate exams but can apply for multiple lines at once.
