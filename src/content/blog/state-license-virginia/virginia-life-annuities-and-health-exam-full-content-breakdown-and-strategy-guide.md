The Virginia Series 11-01 exam — Life, Annuities and Health — is the broadest and most content-dense exam in the Virginia licensing program. It has 140 scored questions plus 10 unscored pretest items spread across a 150-minute session, distributed across the 16 content sections of Prometric's Virginia outline — and it is scored as one overall percentage, with 70% of all scored questions, or 98 of 140, needed to pass. Virginia does publish a line-level first-time [pass rate](/pass-rates) for this exam: the SCC Bureau of Insurance posts annual Prometric demographic reports on its Licensing Exam Pass Rates page, and the report covering June 1, 2023 through May 31, 2024 shows 3,261 first-time candidates sat the Life, Annuities & Health exam with a 68.14% first-time pass rate. Roughly three in ten first-time candidates walked out without a license. Understanding exactly what is on this exam and how to allocate your preparation time is the most direct path to landing in the passing share.

## General Insurance Content: Where Most of the Questions Live

Most of the 140 scored questions test general insurance product knowledge that applies across all states. Prometric's Series 11-01 content outline publishes the exact percentage weight attached to each of its 16 sections — pull it from the Virginia exam page and use it as your study checklist rather than trusting any third party's summary of it. The heaviest territory looks like this:

### Life Insurance Products

The dominant section. Covers traditional whole life, universal life, variable life, term life (level, decreasing, increasing), and combination products. Key concepts: cash value accumulation, nonforfeiture options, policy loans, dividends (participating vs. non-participating), endowments. You must know the mechanics of each product type — not just what they are called, but how cash value builds, how dividends are treated, how loan provisions work, and how riders modify the base policy.

Annuities are tested as a standalone topic: immediate vs. deferred, fixed vs. variable, accumulation and distribution phases, annuity settlement options (life only, life with period certain, joint and survivor), and the tax treatment of annuity distributions (exclusion ratio concept).

### Life Insurance Policy Provisions, Options, and Riders (11% of the exam)

Grace periods, incontestability clauses, misstatement of age provisions, reinstatement, beneficiary designation types (revocable vs. irrevocable), assignment provisions, suicide clauses, and common riders: waiver of premium, accidental death benefit (double indemnity), guaranteed insurability, payor benefit. Know what each provision does and when it applies.

### Health Insurance Basics and Individual Policy Provisions

Individual health policy types: major medical, comprehensive, dread disease, hospital-surgical, critical illness. Key provisions: renewability types (noncancellable, guaranteed renewable, conditionally renewable, optionally renewable), coordination of benefits rules, subrogation, preexisting condition definitions and limitations, COBRA continuation rights, HIPAA portability provisions.

### Disability Income and Related Insurance

Own-occupation vs. any-occupation definitions of disability. Short-term vs. long-term disability. Elimination (waiting) periods. Benefit period options. Residual and partial disability benefits. Coordination with workers' compensation and Social Security. This section requires understanding specific definitions precisely — own-occupation vs. any-occupation is one of the most tested conceptual distinctions in all of life and health insurance.

### Long-Term Care Insurance

Benefit triggers (ADL-based and cognitive impairment). Benefit periods and daily benefit amounts. Elimination periods. Inflation protection riders. Qualified vs. non-qualified LTC policies. LTC Partnership program concept (expanded in the Virginia regulatory content below).

### Medical Plans and Managed Care

HMO structure (staff, group, network, IPA models), PPO, EPO, POS plans. Gatekeeper requirements. Primary care physician referral rules. Out-of-network coverage rules. Medicare Advantage (Part C) as a managed care product. Know the structural differences between HMO and PPO precisely — these are heavily tested in both the general and the Virginia-specific material.

### Medicare, Medicaid, and the Senior Market

Medicare Parts A, B, C, D: what each covers, enrollment periods, premium structure. Medicare supplement (Medigap) standardized plan letters. Coordination with other coverage. Medicaid eligibility concepts. This is an area where the general content and Virginia regulatory content overlap — Medicare supplement rules are tested from both angles.

### Federal Tax Considerations and Qualified Plans

Section 125 cafeteria plans. Health Savings Accounts (HSAs) and Health Reimbursement Arrangements (HRAs). Qualified retirement plans (401(k), IRA, SEP-IRA basics). Tax treatment of life insurance premiums and death benefits (§101 exclusion). Tax treatment of annuity withdrawals.

## Virginia Regulatory and Statutory Content

Virginia-specific material is not quarantined into a separate, separately-scored block of state-law questions. Prometric folds it into the same 16-section outline — concentrated in the Insurance Regulation section, which carries 10% of the exam (roughly 14 questions), and woven through the product sections wherever Virginia's rules depart from the national default. Prometric's own demographic reporting for the Bureau describes the exam as having a general-knowledge portion and a state-specific portion that are "scored together as a single examination." The Virginia material that most reliably appears tests Title 38.2 of the Code of Virginia and Bureau of Insurance regulations:

### SCC Bureau of Insurance Structure

How the SCC functions as the insurance regulator. The Bureau of Insurance's authority. The Commissioner's role (in Virginia, insurance authority resides in the SCC, not in a standalone commissioner). Title 38.2 as the governing statute.

### Producer Licensing Rules

Requirements to obtain and maintain a Virginia producer license. Grounds for license suspension or revocation. Reporting requirements (address changes within 30 days). Appointment requirements. Temporary license provisions. The 183-day exam score validity rule.

### Unfair Trade Practices

Virginia's unfair trade practices statute (Title 38.2, Chapter 5 — Va. Code §§ 38.2-500 et seq.). What constitutes an unfair method of competition or unfair or deceptive act. Misrepresentation, rebating, twisting, churning, defamation. The Bureau's enforcement authority.

### Virginia Life and Annuity Insurance Law

Virginia replacement regulations — disclosure requirements when replacing existing life insurance. Free-look period (10 days minimum for individual policies). Grace period requirements. Incontestability period. Beneficiary rights. Variable product licensing.

### Virginia Health Insurance Law

Individual market rules. Small employer health insurance requirements. Continuation of coverage (Virginia mini-COBRA for small employers). Virginia's Insurance Marketplace — the state-based ACA exchange operated by the Virginia Health Benefit Exchange within the SCC at marketplace.virginia.gov, which replaced HealthCare.gov for Virginians beginning with plan year 2024. Dependent coverage requirements. Mental health parity. HIPAA in the Virginia context.

### Medicare Supplement and LTC in Virginia

Virginia Medicare supplement requirements. Standardized plan letters. Open enrollment rights. Guaranteed issue situations. LTC Partnership program specifics — the initial 8-hour training requirement and its content, the Partnership policy's asset protection benefit.

## Strategy: How to Allocate Your Preparation Time

Weight your study time to the published section percentages in Prometric's outline, not to an even split across topics. The general insurance content accounts for the large majority of the 140 questions and covers far more total ground, so it needs the bulk of your hours. But the Virginia regulatory material deserves a disproportionate share relative to its question count: it covers a narrow, finite topic set drawn straight from Title 38.2, which means it rewards density of preparation rather than breadth, and it is the material generic national study guides cover least well.

The single highest-leverage thing you can do for the Virginia material: read the key statutes directly. The unfair trade practices chapter (Title 38.2, Chapter 5 — §§ 38.2-500 through 38.2-518, which is where misrepresentation, false advertising, defamation, unfair discrimination, rebating, and unfair claim settlement practices all live), the producer licensing chapter (Chapter 18), and the Virginia liability and auto insurance statutes (Chapter 22) are all available at law.lis.virginia.gov. The Virginia questions are drawn directly from statutory language — candidates who have read the source material answer these questions more reliably than those who have only read summaries.

## Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Series 11-01 exam scored section by section, or as one overall score?

As one overall score. The Bureau of Insurance's Virginia licensing information bulletin is explicit: your score report "indicates the overall score and grade," and "your total percentage score is computed by dividing the number of questions you answered correctly by the total number of questions in the examination." You need 70% of the scored questions correct — 98 of 140 — and it does not matter how those correct answers are distributed between national content and Virginia law. Your report does break out the percentage correct in each major content section, but the bulletin says those figures exist "to guide you, or your employer and/or trainer, about areas requiring additional preparation for retesting if you do not pass," and warns that "the section percentages will not average out to your total percentage score." There is no separate state-law pass threshold to clear. Experimental questions distributed throughout the exam are not scored at all, but they are not identified, so answer every question as if it counts.

Which part of the Series 11-01 exam costs candidates the most points?

Because Virginia scores the exam as one overall percentage, there is no section you can fail on its own — but the Virginia-specific regulatory questions are where candidates with solid general-content knowledge most often leak points, because those questions test Virginia statutes that general study resources cover less thoroughly than national insurance products. The health insurance statutes are the most technical part of the Virginia content — Virginia's small employer health insurance rules, continuation of coverage requirements, mental health parity provisions, and the rules governing Virginia's Insurance Marketplace (the state-based exchange at marketplace.virginia.gov) require specific statutory knowledge that generic study materials often provide only in summary form. Since every scored question counts the same toward your total, converting the Virginia questions is the cheapest way to move your overall percentage.

Is the annuity portion of the Series 11-01 exam heavily weighted?

Annuities are tested throughout the Series 11-01 exam at a meaningful weight — both in the general content (where annuity mechanics, settlement options, and tax treatment appear) and in the Virginia regulatory content (where the Virginia Annuity Best Interest training requirement, replacement regulations for annuities, and variable annuity licensing rules appear). Candidates who are taking the exam primarily to sell life insurance sometimes under-prepare the annuity material. The Virginia material in particular tests awareness of Virginia's annuity best interest training requirement (the one-time 4-hour course required before selling annuities) and the regulatory framework for variable products.

What is the free-look period requirement in Virginia, and will it be on the exam?

Virginia law requires a free-look period — a window after policy delivery during which the policyholder can return the policy for a full refund of any premium paid. For individual life insurance policies, Virginia requires a minimum 10-day free-look period. For Medicare supplement policies, Virginia aligns with the NAIC model, requiring a 30-day free-look period. For LTC insurance, Virginia also requires a 30-day free-look. The specific free-look periods for different policy types are tested as Virginia-specific content. The concept is also foundational in the replacement regulations section — a replacement sale triggers specific disclosure requirements and the free-look period is part of the regulatory framework protecting replaced policyholders.

How is disability income tested on the Virginia L/A/H exam?

Disability income is tested primarily in the general insurance content, covering the definitional and mechanical elements of individual and group disability products. The most important distinctions to know: the difference between own-occupation and any-occupation definitions of disability (and hybrid "modified own-occupation" definitions); short-term vs. long-term disability benefit periods; the elimination (waiting) period and how it affects premium; residual or partial disability benefits for partial income loss; and how disability income integrates with Social Security disability, workers' compensation, and group coverage under coordination of benefits rules. Virginia-specific disability content includes the small employer definition and group policy requirements. Disability income carries its own section in Prometric's outline — check the published weight before you decide how many hours it is worth — and candidates who know the definitional elements cold consistently answer these correctly.

The Virginia Life, Annuities and Health exam covers enormous ground in a single sitting, and every scored question counts the same toward one overall percentage. Preparation that treats both bodies of content seriously — national insurance fundamentals and Virginia statute specifics — is what puts that percentage over 70 on your first attempt.
