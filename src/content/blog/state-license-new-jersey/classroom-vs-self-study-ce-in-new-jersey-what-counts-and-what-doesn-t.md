If you have been told that half of your New Jersey insurance CE hours must come from live, instructor-led instruction, you have been told something New Jersey does not require. There is no classroom minimum in New Jersey's CE rule, and no cap on self-study hours. It is one of the most persistent pieces of misinformation in producer CE, and it costs New Jersey producers real money in courses they did not need to buy.

The classroom-versus-self-study distinction still matters in New Jersey — just not for the reason people think. What actually varies by delivery format is how the course proves you learned the material: whether it ends in a monitored exam, and who is allowed to monitor it. That is the real "what counts and what doesn't," and it is what this post covers.

## The Rule, As Written

N.J.A.C. 11:17-3.6(a) is short and there is nothing hiding in it:

"No resident individual insurance producer licensed with life, accident, and health or sickness, property, casualty, surplus lines, title, variable life and variable annuity, or personal lines authority shall be renewed unless the renewal applicant demonstrates that he or she has completed 24 credit hours of approved continuing education courses during the previous licensing term, including three credit hours related to professional ethics per licensing term, one credit hour of which may be substituted with a credit hour related to insurance fraud."

That is the requirement. Twenty-four credits per two-year license term, three of them ethics, one of which may be an insurance fraud credit. The Department of Banking and Insurance states the same thing on its producer licensing page: resident individual licensees complete 24 continuing education credits with at least three credit hours in an approved ethics course.

No delivery-format allocation appears anywhere in the section. All 24 credits may be completed through DOBI-approved self-study courses, and New Jersey-approved providers sell them exactly that way.

The other allocation rules that do exist in the section, and that are worth knowing:

Carry-over is capped at 12 credits. Producers who complete more than 24 credit hours in one licensing term may carry up to 12 credits into the next renewal term. Those credits may only be carried over once, and ethics credits may not be carried over at all. This 12-credit figure is almost certainly the source of the "12 hours must be classroom" myth — it is a carry-over limit, not a delivery split.

Prelicensing courses do not count toward CE, and you may only take credit for the same approved course once per licensing term.

Flood: with respect to the first renewal following initial issuance or initial renewal of a license on or after January 1, 2007, a producer with property or personal lines authority who writes flood insurance must complete a three-credit approved course on flood insurance and the NFIP — as part of the 24, not on top of it.

Professional designations: under subsection (e), a producer who fulfills the requirements of a Department-approved designation program receives 12 credit hours for each full year of the license period preceding renewal in which those requirements were met.

## What Qualifies as Classroom CE

Classroom delivery is entirely optional in New Jersey, but plenty of producers prefer it, and some employers require it. Here is what it means.

### In-Person Classroom Courses

Traditional in-person classroom CE is taught by a live instructor at a physical location. Students attend in person for the full scheduled duration. Completion is based on attendance and participation rather than a final exam — the instructor tracks attendance throughout the session, and under N.J.A.C. 11:17-3.6(b) a contact-course credit hour must provide no less than 50 minutes of instruction.

In-person classroom CE is increasingly uncommon as a primary CE format for most NJ producers, but it remains available through approved providers and industry associations throughout the state. Some producers prefer it for the structured environment and the opportunity to ask questions directly.

### Live Webinar / Classroom Equivalent

A live webinar taught by an instructor in real time is treated as classroom equivalent, provided the format enables real-time interaction between instructor and students: instructor and students interact remotely by video-conferencing or comparable technology in a classroom format. Producers attend at a scheduled time, the instructor presents material and responds to questions, and completion is tracked by the platform through attendance monitoring.

What qualifies as classroom equivalent:

Scheduled live sessions with a named instructor

Real-time Q&A capability between instructor and attendees

Attendance tracking for the full session duration

Listed as classroom or classroom equivalent by the approved provider

What does not qualify as classroom equivalent:

Pre-recorded webinar replays

On-demand video courses, even if originally recorded from a live session

Self-paced online modules with embedded video

None of this changes whether the hours count toward your 24 — they count either way. It changes what the course is called on your transcript, and whether you need to be somewhere at a specific time.

## What Qualifies as Self-Study CE — and the Part That Actually Matters

Self-study CE covers courses you complete independently on your own schedule. In New Jersey these can carry your entire 24-hour obligation.

Under N.J.A.C. 11:17-3.6(b), a self-study credit hour includes the time for reading and study that the Department has approved as reasonable for successfully passing the final examination; the time spent taking the exam itself does not count toward credit.

### Monitored vs. Monitor-Free: The Real New Jersey Distinction

This is the split producers should actually be planning around. N.J.A.C. 11:17-3.6(c) provides that self-study courses are approved if the program of instruction culminates in a monitored examination of the subject presented — but then carves out an exception:

"A monitored examination is not required for an interactive, online program that the Department determines has sufficient internal testing to validate the student's full comprehension of the material presented."

So there are two self-study categories in New Jersey, and they behave very differently:

Interactive online courses that DOBI has approved as having sufficient internal testing. No monitor. New Jersey-approved providers market these as "monitor-free" courses, and they are the reason a producer can complete a full 24-hour renewal without ever scheduling anything with anyone.

Non-interactive self-study — paper-based courses, and online courses without qualifying internal testing. These must end in a monitored final exam. The monitor must be a disinterested third party: an impartial person with no financial interest in the outcome, not a family member, not a friend, and not someone in your income stream, which rules out your supervisor and your subordinates. Providers typically expect a passing score of 70 percent or higher, and generally allow retakes until you pass.

The practical takeaway: before you enroll, ask the provider one question — is this course monitor-free, or does it require a proctored exam? That answer determines whether you need to arrange a monitor, not whether the hours count.

### Reporting

Under N.J.A.C. 11:17-3.6(g), the provider's insurance education director must report the names and license numbers of producers completing a course to the Department within 15 days of course completion or certification of the credits, whichever is later. Many providers report considerably faster than that as a service level — but 15 days is the rule, and it is the number to plan against if you are finishing CE close to your deadline. Do not assume same-day posting.

## Building a CE Plan That Actually Works

Because delivery format is unconstrained, New Jersey CE planning comes down to three things.

Start with the mandatory content, not the format. Three ethics credits every term, plus the flood course if it applies to you. Those are the pieces that can actually invalidate a renewal, and they are the ones producers leave until last.

Decide early whether you are willing to arrange a monitor. If you are not, filter for interactive, monitor-free online courses from the start. Discovering at hour 21 that your remaining courses require a proctored exam is the version of this that goes badly.

Give the reporting window room. Finish with more than 15 days to spare before your license term ends — New Jersey producer terms end at the end of your birth month, every other year — and confirm the credits actually posted rather than assuming they did. That check happens on PSI's CE site at ce.psiexams.com, which is DOBI's credit-banking system; the transcript lookup asks only for your last name and license number.

If you prefer live instruction, take live instruction. Just take it because you learn better that way, not because someone told you the state requires 12 hours of it.

## The Ethics Hours

Three of your 24 total credits must be in professional ethics. Ethics credits can be completed in any approved delivery format — there is no format requirement attached to the ethics component. One of the three may be substituted with a credit hour related to insurance fraud.

Ethics credits are the one category that cannot be carried over to a future renewal term, even if you complete more than three. Plan on completing them fresh every term.

## Frequently Asked Questions

Can I complete all 24 NJ CE hours online through self-study courses?

Yes. New Jersey does not impose a classroom minimum or a self-study cap. N.J.A.C. 11:17-3.6 requires 24 credits per licensing term including three ethics credits, and says nothing about delivery format, and DOBI's producer licensing page states the requirement the same way. A New Jersey resident producer who completes all 24 credits — ethics included — through DOBI-approved self-study courses has satisfied the requirement and renews normally. The only format-driven condition is the monitored-exam rule in subsection (c): non-interactive self-study courses must end in a proctored exam, while interactive online programs the Department has approved as having sufficient internal testing are monitor-free.

Does a recorded webinar count as classroom CE in New Jersey?

For labeling purposes, no. A recorded webinar — even one originally broadcast live — is treated as self-study once it becomes an on-demand recording. Classroom equivalent status requires live delivery: the instructor teaching in real time, with students able to interact during the session. But since New Jersey has no classroom minimum, this distinction does not affect whether the hours count toward your 24. It affects how the course is categorized and, if it is a non-interactive self-study course, whether you will need a monitor for the final exam.

Does the proctor for my self-study CE exam need to be physically present with me?

First, check whether you need a proctor at all — interactive online courses approved by DOBI as having sufficient internal testing do not require a monitored exam, and are sold in New Jersey as monitor-free. If your course does require monitoring, the monitor must be a disinterested third party: an impartial person with no financial interest in the outcome, not a family member or friend, and not a supervisor or subordinate. The regulation does not specify that the monitor must be in the same room, and online proctoring through an approved platform can satisfy the requirement for providers that offer it. Check your provider's proctoring options, as practices vary. What you cannot do is self-administer a monitored exam, or have a friend or family member serve as your monitor.

What happens if I complete 30 hours of CE in one renewal period — can I carry the extra over?

Yes, within limits. Under N.J.A.C. 11:17-3.6(a)4, a resident individual producer who completes more than 24 credit hours in a licensing term may carry over up to 12 credits to the next renewal term. Those credits may only be carried over once — they cannot be banked further — and ethics credits may not be carried over at all, even if you completed more than three. Carried-over credits count toward your 24-credit total in the following term with no format strings attached, because there is no format requirement to attach.

Where does the "12 classroom hours" rule come from, if it isn't real?

Two places, most likely. First, the only 12-credit figure in N.J.A.C. 11:17-3.6 is the carry-over cap in paragraph (a)4, which is easy to misread as a delivery split by anyone skimming. Second, several states genuinely do impose classroom or classroom-equivalent minimums, and requirement summaries get copied across state pages without being re-checked. New Jersey is not one of those states. If a provider tells you that you must buy live hours to renew in New Jersey, ask them to point at the rule — then read N.J.A.C. 11:17-3.6 and DOBI's producer licensing page for yourself.
