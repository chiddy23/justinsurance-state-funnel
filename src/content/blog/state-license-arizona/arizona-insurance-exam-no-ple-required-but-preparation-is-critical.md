Arizona's no-prelicense-education approach gives candidates flexibility in how they prepare — but it doesn't reduce what they need to know. Arizona's two-section independent scoring requirement (70% on each section separately — not averaged), a strict four-attempt annual limit per line, and an Arizona state law section that distinctly reflects A.R.S. Title 20 provisions all mean that "no PLE required" doesn't mean "no preparation needed." Understanding Arizona's exam structure and building a study approach that addresses both the general and Arizona-specific sections is essential.

Here's everything candidates need to know about Arizona's exam approach.

## Arizona Does Not Require PLE — Context and Implications

Arizona joined the growing group of states without mandatory prelicense education. Unlike Michigan (which maintains a 20-hour per-line PLE requirement), Arizona allows candidates to proceed directly from the decision to get licensed to scheduling the state exam.

States without PLE (context): Pennsylvania (April 2025), North Carolina (October 2025), Massachusetts, South Carolina, Alabama, Nevada, Arizona, and others have all eliminated or never required PLE. The trend reflects regulatory recognition that exam-only licensing is sufficient when quality study materials are available.

Arizona's specific reasoning: Arizona's insurance regulatory philosophy favors market accessibility, particularly given the state's substantial population growth and demand for insurance professionals.

What this means practically:

No minimum study hours required

No formal course completion certificate needed before exam

No waiting period between deciding to get licensed and scheduling the exam

Complete flexibility in how you prepare

What this does NOT mean:

The exam is easier or less comprehensive

Arizona-specific content is less important

Preparation is optional (given the 4-attempt annual limit and two-section independent scoring)

## The Two-Section Independent Scoring Requirement — Arizona's Most Important Exam Feature

Arizona's most distinctive and consequential exam feature: both the General Knowledge section and the Arizona State Law section must independently score 70% or higher. The two scores are NOT averaged.

Why this matters: Most states produce a single combined percentage score. A strong General Knowledge performance can compensate for weaker state-specific knowledge. Not in Arizona.

The failure scenario: Candidate scores 88% General Knowledge + 68% Arizona State Law = FAIL.

Despite strong general insurance knowledge, failing the state law section alone fails the entire exam. You must retake both sections.

What this means for preparation:

Arizona state law is not a minor section to skim — it's a separate pass/fail determination

Spending substantial study time on A.R.S. Title 20 isn't optional for Arizona exam success

Generic national study materials that focus primarily on general insurance content are insufficient alone

Score report after failure: Arizona provides a separate numeric score for each failed section. This tells you specifically where to focus for your retake.

## The Four-Attempt Annual Limit

Arizona's exam retake limit (A.R.S. § 20-284) is among the strictest nationally:

Maximum 4 attempts per line of authority within a 12-month period

After the 4th failed attempt: must wait one full year from the date of that last attempt before retesting

Each retake costs $59 — four failed attempts = $236 in exam fees alone

Why this limit matters: Unlike states with unlimited retakes (Michigan) or no cooling-off periods, Arizona's limit means candidates who approach the exam underprepared risk locking themselves out for a full year. This makes adequate preparation before each attempt — particularly the first — significantly more valuable.

PSI scheduling after failing: PSI's registration system takes 24-48 hours to update after a failed attempt. You can schedule a retake 1-2 days after your previous attempt.

Strategic implication: Take the exam when you're genuinely ready. Don't rush to schedule after two weeks of minimal study — the 4-attempt limit means each attempt matters.

## Arizona Exam Specifications

Current exam administrator: PSI Services (replaced the previous vendor effective September 1, 2025).

Single ID required: One valid government-issued photo ID with signature.

## Building Your Arizona Study Approach

Step 1: Choose a quality Arizona-specific prep course. Even without PLE requirements, Arizona-specific content requires dedicated study. Courses specifically addressing A.R.S. Title 20, DIFI regulations, and Arizona market-specific provisions produce better state section outcomes.

Step 2: Allocate study time proportionally. Both sections must independently hit 70%. If you have strong insurance background, weight Arizona state law heavily in your preparation. If you're new to insurance, allocate proportional time to both.

Step 3: Practice Arizona-specific questions specifically. Generic national practice question banks don't adequately cover A.R.S. Title 20 content. Seek practice questions specifically addressing DIFI authority, Arizona policy provisions, and Arizona Guaranty Association limits.

Step 4: Take full-length practice exams under simulated conditions. 2 hours (single line) or 2.5 hours (combined) under timed conditions.

Step 5: Target 80%+ on practice exams before scheduling. The 10-point buffer above 70% protects against exam day variance — particularly important given the 4-attempt annual limit.

Step 6: Don't schedule until ready. Given the 4-attempt annual limit, waiting one or two additional weeks to reach 80%+ practice exam confidence is always worth it.

## Common Arizona Exam Failures

Underestimating the state section. The most common Arizona exam failure pattern: strong general knowledge, weak Arizona-specific performance. Both sections must independently pass.

Using only national study materials. Generic national materials don't cover A.R.S. Title 20, DIFI authority, Arizona Guaranty Association limits ($300K life, $250K annuity, $500K health), or Arizona-specific free-look periods adequately.

Scheduling too quickly without reaching practice confidence. The 4-attempt annual limit means premature scheduling carries real consequences.

Ignoring Arizona's unique CE structure. Arizona's 48-hour/4-year CE cycle appears on exam questions — know this is different from most states' 24-hour/2-year frameworks.

## 5 Frequently Asked Questions

- Why doesn't Arizona require PLE? Arizona's regulatory philosophy favors market accessibility. The state has never required PLE for insurance producer licensure, reflecting confidence that exam-based competency assessment plus ongoing CE requirements are sufficient to maintain professional standards.
- What's the most important thing to know about Arizona's passing score? Both the General Knowledge section and the Arizona State Law section must independently score 70% or higher. Scores are not averaged. A strong General score cannot compensate for a failing State section.
- What are the most tested Arizona-specific topics? DIFI authority and licensing requirements, Arizona unfair trade practices, Arizona Guaranty Association limits ($300K life, $250K annuity, $500K health), Arizona free-look periods (10/20/30 days), replacement regulations, annuity suitability, and the 4-year/48-hour CE framework.
- What happens after my fourth failed attempt in a year? You must wait one full year from the date of your last attempt before retesting on that line of authority (A.R.S. § 20-284). Each attempt costs $59 — four failed attempts costs $236 in exam fees.
- Can I take the Arizona exam online? Yes. Arizona offers remote proctored testing through PSI Bridge. Check system compatibility at syscheck.bridge.psiexams.com before scheduling. Remote testing requires a compatible computer, webcam, and quiet private environment.
