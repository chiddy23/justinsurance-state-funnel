Passing the Oregon insurance exam on the first try saves you time, money, and frustration. Oregon's exam is genuinely passable with proper preparation, but candidates who try to shortcut the process typically fail at least once before passing. Understanding what actually works — and what doesn't — sets you up for first-attempt success.

Here's how to pass the Oregon insurance exam on the first try.

## Why First-Try Success Matters

Beyond the obvious benefits, passing first means:

Lower total cost. Each retake means paying another exam fee.

Faster career start. Each failed attempt delays your ability to start earning as a licensed producer.

Stronger confidence. First-time success starts your career on solid footing.

Less stress. Avoiding the cycle of failure, regroup, and retry.

Better job prospects. Some employers consider exam history when evaluating candidates.

The investment in proper preparation is significantly less than the cost of multiple attempts.

## The First-Try Success Formula

Successful first-time Oregon candidates consistently combine:

Quality prelicense education (the required 40 hours treated as actual learning, not paperwork)

Substantial practice questions (hundreds of practice questions worked through carefully)

Full-length practice exams (multiple timed practice exams under realistic conditions)

Targeted review of weak areas (study time concentrated where you actually need it)

Strategic test-taking (proper exam-day strategy for time management and question handling)

Skip any of these elements and your first-try chances drop significantly.

## Step 1: Take Prelicense Education Seriously

Oregon requires 40 hours of prelicense education for the combined Life and Health exam (or 20 hours per individual line). This isn't busy work — it's the foundation of your knowledge.

What works:

Spend the full required hours actively engaged with material

Take notes by hand for better retention

Don't skip ahead to the end-of-chapter quizzes

Review challenging material multiple times

Ask questions when concepts don't click

What doesn't work:

Treating prelicense as something to rush through

Trying to "test out" by skimming and taking quizzes

Leaving entire content areas for later

Cramming the full course into one weekend

The hours requirement exists because the content takes time to absorb properly.

## Step 2: Build Strong Foundational Knowledge

Before attempting practice exams, build solid knowledge in foundational areas:

Life Insurance Basics. Term, whole, universal, variable. Premium structures. Cash value behavior. Policy provisions and riders.

Annuities. Fixed, variable, indexed. Immediate vs. deferred. Tax treatment. Suitability.

Health Insurance Plan Types. HMO, PPO, POS, EPO. Networks, gatekeepers, referrals.

Health Insurance Provisions. Deductibles, coinsurance, copays, out-of-pocket maximums. Coordination of benefits.

Medicare. Parts A, B, C, D. Eligibility. Enrollment periods. Coverage scope.

Federal Laws. HIPAA, ERISA, ACA basics.

Oregon Specifics. DFR structure, licensing requirements, replacement rules, producer conduct.

These foundations underlie virtually every exam question.

## Step 3: Use Comparison Tools

Comparison charts and frameworks help you remember distinctions:

Life Insurance Product Comparison Chart:

Term: temporary, level premium, no cash value, low cost

Whole Life: permanent, level premium, guaranteed cash value, dividends possible

Universal Life: permanent, flexible premium, cash value with interest credit

Variable Life: permanent, level premium, cash value invested in subaccounts

Variable Universal: permanent, flexible premium, cash value invested in subaccounts

Health Plan Type Comparison Chart:

HMO: closed network, PCP required, referrals needed, low cost

PPO: open network, no PCP, no referrals, moderate cost

POS: hybrid, PCP required, referrals for specialists, moderate cost

EPO: closed network, no PCP needed, no referrals, low to moderate cost

Build these mental frameworks early in your preparation.

## Step 4: Practice Questions Aggressively

Practice questions are the single most valuable tool for first-try success.

How to use practice questions effectively:

Work through hundreds of practice questions across all content areas

Don't just check answers — understand why each correct answer is correct

Review every wrong answer carefully

Track your performance by content area

Re-test weak areas after additional study

Where to find quality practice questions:

Your prelicense course usually includes practice questions

Quality exam prep providers offer additional question banks

PSI may provide sample questions through their candidate handbook

Some industry websites offer free practice questions (verify quality)

Volume matters. Most successful first-time Oregon candidates work through 500-1,000+ practice questions before their exam.

## Step 5: Take Full-Length Practice Exams

Individual practice questions build knowledge. Full-length practice exams build endurance and calibration.

Take at least 2-3 full-length practice exams before your real exam:

Simulate exam conditions (no phone, no notes, full time limit)

Complete the entire 150 questions without breaks

Review every missed question afterward

Note patterns in the types of questions you miss

Target 80%+ scores on practice exams before scheduling your real exam.

If you're scoring below 80% in practice, more preparation is needed.

## Step 6: Address Weak Content Areas

Practice exams reveal weaknesses. Address them systematically:

Identify the weak area. Practice exam scoring should show performance by content area. Focus on the lowest-scoring areas.

Return to source material. Re-read prelicense content for weak areas.

Work additional practice questions specifically on weak topics.

Build reinforcement tools. Flashcards, comparison charts, or mental frameworks for difficult concepts.

Re-test. Take additional practice questions to verify improvement.

Don't ignore weak areas hoping they won't appear on the exam. They will.

## Step 7: Master Oregon-Specific Content

Approximately 15-20% of the Oregon exam covers state-specific content. Generic study materials underprepare you for this.

Focus areas:

DFR structure and authority

Oregon licensing requirements

Oregon replacement rules

Oregon unfair trade practices

Oregon producer conduct standards

Oregon-specific consumer protections

Annuity Best Interest standard

LTC training requirements

CE requirements

Use Oregon-specific prelicense materials to cover this content properly.

## Step 8: Schedule Your Exam Strategically

Don't schedule too early. Booking the exam before you're ready creates pressure to take it before you're prepared.

Don't schedule too late. Indefinitely delaying scheduling can cause prelicense exam validity issues (12-month window).

Choose a good time. Morning appointments often produce better focus than late afternoon.

Choose a good location. PSI offers multiple Oregon testing centers. Choose one convenient and reliable.

Consider online vs. in-person. Online testing offers convenience but requires good home environment. Physical centers offer structured environment but require travel.

## Step 9: Develop Test-Taking Strategy

How you take the exam matters as much as what you know.

Read every question carefully. Particularly watch for modifiers like "EXCEPT," "NOT," "LEAST LIKELY," "BEST," "MUST," and "MAY."

Manage your time. With 2 hours 40 minutes for 150 questions, you have just over 1 minute per question. Don't spend 5 minutes on a hard question while easier ones remain.

Flag uncertain questions. Don't waste time on hard questions while easy ones remain. Flag them and return.

Trust your first instinct. Second-guessing correct answers is a common cause of changed-right-to-wrong answers.

Never leave questions blank. Oregon doesn't penalize guessing. Always answer.

Review flagged questions after completing the rest, if time permits.

## Step 10: Manage Exam Day Logistics

Sleep well the night before. Cramming until 2 AM hurts more than helps.

Eat a real breakfast. Blood sugar affects focus.

Arrive early. 30 minutes for in-person testing. Start online testing process early.

Bring required materials. Two forms of ID, your prelicense Certificate of Completion, registration confirmation.

Use the bathroom before starting. Avoid distractions.

Breathe. If anxiety hits during the exam, pause for three deep breaths and reset.

## What to Do the Final Week

The week before your exam should be focused on:

3-5 days before: Practice questions and targeted review of weak areas.

1-2 days before: Light review only. Notes, flashcards, comparison charts.

Day before: Stop studying by early afternoon. Prepare your materials. Get good sleep.

Day of: Eat breakfast. Arrive early. Trust your preparation.

## What NOT to Do

Don't cram. Trying to learn material in days produces poor retention.

Don't skip practice exams. Practice exams are too valuable to skip.

Don't ignore Oregon-specific content. It's 15-20% of the exam.

Don't book the exam too early. Schedule when you're ready, not when you hope to be ready.

Don't second-guess at the testing center. Trust your preparation.

## 5 Frequently Asked Questions

- How long should I study to pass on the first try? Most successful first-time Oregon candidates put in 50-80 hours of total preparation, including the 40-hour prelicense education plus practice questions and review.
- How many practice exams should I take? At least 2-3 full-length practice exams under timed conditions. More if your scores aren't consistently 80%+.
- Should I study every day or take rest days? Daily consistency matters. One rest day per week is fine. Complete burnout hurts more than helps.
- What's the best way to handle Oregon-specific content? Use Oregon-specific prelicense materials. Don't rely on generic national content. Practice scenario-based Oregon law questions.
- Should I take the combined Life and Health exam or separate exams? Most candidates take the combined exam (12-03) for efficiency. Single exam, single fee, single test day.
