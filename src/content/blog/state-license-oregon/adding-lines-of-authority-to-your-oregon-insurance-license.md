Once you have your initial Oregon insurance producer license, you may want to add additional lines of authority over time. Adding lines lets you serve broader client needs, increase your revenue potential, and develop deeper expertise. Oregon's process for adding lines is more streamlined than initial licensing, but it still requires specific steps.

Here's how to add lines of authority to your Oregon insurance license.

## What "Lines of Authority" Means

A line of authority is a specific category of insurance you're licensed to sell, solicit, or negotiate. Oregon's main lines of authority include:

Life Insurance — life insurance products

Health Insurance — health, disability, long-term care

Property Insurance — property coverage for personal and commercial

Casualty Insurance — liability and related coverage

Personal Lines — limited P&C for personal (non-commercial) insurance only

Variable Life and Variable Annuity — variable products requiring securities credentials

Crop Insurance — specialty agricultural coverage

Each line of authority requires its own qualifying education and exam (with some exceptions for industry designations).

## Why Add Lines of Authority?

Adding lines of authority offers several benefits:

Broader client service. A combined Life and Health license lets you serve a client's life insurance, health insurance, disability, and Medicare needs comprehensively rather than referring out.

Higher per-client revenue. Multi-line clients generate more total revenue than single-line clients.

Stronger client retention. Clients who get multiple types of insurance from one agent are stickier and more likely to refer others.

Career flexibility. As your career develops, you may want to specialize in different products. Multi-line licensing gives you that flexibility.

Cross-sell opportunities. Existing clients often need products you weren't originally licensed for.

Bundling and discounts. Many clients value the convenience of one-agent service across all their insurance needs.

## Most Common Combinations

Oregon insurance professionals typically pursue these multi-line combinations:

Life and Health combined. Most common for agents focusing on life insurance, health insurance, Medicare, long-term care, and disability. Allows comprehensive personal coverage.

Property and Casualty combined. Standard for agents focusing on auto, home, commercial property, and liability coverage.

Full personal lines coverage. Life, Health, and Personal Lines (P&C) — provides coverage of all major personal insurance categories.

Full producer. Life, Health, Property, and Casualty — comprehensive coverage of personal and commercial markets.

The right combination depends on your career goals and target markets.

## Step 1: Decide What Line(s) You Want to Add

Before starting, decide:

Which lines align with your career goals? A Medicare specialist might add Health if they originally licensed only for Life. A residential real estate-focused agent might add Personal Lines to their existing Life license.

What's your timeline? Adding multiple lines simultaneously means more upfront commitment but gets you to full licensing faster.

What's your current capacity? Each line requires study time and exam preparation. Don't bite off more than you can chew.

## Step 2: Complete the Required Prelicense Education

Each new line of authority requires its own [prelicense](/oregon/prelicensing) education:

Life: 20 hours

Health: 20 hours

Property: 20 hours

Casualty: 20 hours

Personal Lines: 20 hours

If you're adding two lines that combine, you can take a combined course:

Life and Health combined: 40 hours total

Property and Casualty combined: 40 hours total

Confirm the prelicense provider is Oregon-approved before enrolling.

JustInsurance's Oregon prelicense catalog currently covers Life, Health, and the combined Life & Health course. If you're adding Property, Casualty, or Personal Lines, you'll need to complete that prelicense education through another Oregon-approved provider.

## Step 3: Schedule and Pass the Required Exam(s)

Each new line requires passing the corresponding state exam through PSI:

Register with PSI

Pay the exam fee

Schedule at an Oregon PSI center or use online remote-proctored testing

Pass with 70% or higher

Most Oregon line-specific exams are 100 questions over 2 hours. The combined Life and Health exam is 150 questions over 2 hours 40 minutes.

## Step 4: Submit Your Application to Add Lines

Adding lines to an existing Oregon license is done through NIPR at nipr.com:

Log into your NIPR account

Select the option to add lines to your existing Oregon license

Specify the new lines of authority

Confirm exam completion

Pay the application fee for adding lines

## Step 5: No Additional Background Check Required

This is one of the conveniences of adding lines to an existing active Oregon license: no additional fingerprinting or background check is required.

Your initial fingerprinting and background check covered your character and fitness for licensure. Adding lines doesn't require recheck — saving time and fees.

This applies only to active licenses. If your license has lapsed or expired, the situation is different and may require a new background check.

## Step 6: DFR Processes Your Application

The DFR reviews your application to add lines. Processing is typically faster than initial licensing because:

Your character/fitness has already been verified

Your basic identity and information is on file

Only the new line(s) need processing

Typical processing for adding lines: 1-2 weeks for most applications.

## Step 7: Confirm Your Updated License

Once approved, your Oregon license shows the additional lines of authority. You can:

Conduct insurance business in the new line(s)

Pursue carrier appointments for products in the new line

Update your marketing and business cards to reflect expanded capabilities

## Strategic Considerations for Adding Lines

Timing matters. Adding lines too early (before you've established expertise in your initial line) can spread you thin. Adding lines too late means missing opportunities.

Build expertise before expanding. Focus on becoming genuinely good at one line before pursuing the next. Generalists who lack depth often underperform specialists.

Consider your market. A rural Oregon agent serving farms might benefit from Property and Casualty plus Crop Insurance. A Portland agent serving young professionals might focus on Life and Health.

Plan [continuing education](/oregon/continuing-education). More lines mean more CE consideration. Oregon's standard CE requirement applies, but specialty training requirements (LTC, Annuity Best Interest) may affect your CE planning.

Carrier appointments. Each line requires appointments with carriers writing that line. Building those appointments takes time beyond just adding lines to your license.

## Common Mistakes When Adding Lines

Adding lines without completing prelicense education first. Each line requires its own qualifying education before you can sit for the exam.

Submitting application before exam completion. Wait until you've passed the exam before submitting to NIPR.

Wrong combination of lines. Some line combinations require specific exam paths. Verify before enrolling in courses.

Missing fee payment. Application fees apply even for adding lines to existing licenses.

Letting prior exam validity expire. Your prelicense exam result is valid for 12 months. Add the line within that window.

## Adding Variable Products

Adding variable life or variable annuity products has additional requirements beyond standard prelicense education:

You must already hold a Life line of authority

You must hold securities credentials (typically Series 6 or Series 7 plus Series 63 or 66)

You must be associated with a registered broker-dealer

The application process verifies your securities credentials

This combination provides comprehensive ability to serve clients with both insurance and securities needs.

## Adding Lines as a Non-Resident

If you hold a non-resident Oregon license, adding lines follows similar processes:

Verify what lines are eligible based on your home state license

Complete any required education

Submit application through NIPR

Pay applicable fees

Non-resident reciprocity often simplifies adding lines if you're already licensed for those lines in your home state.

## Continuing Education for Multi-Line Licenses

Oregon's CE requirement is 24 hours per 2-year cycle including 3 hours of ethics. Holding multiple lines doesn't double your CE requirement — your total is still 24 hours.

However, you may want to:

Take CE covering each of your lines (life CE, health CE, P&C CE, etc.)

Complete specialty training (Annuity Best Interest, LTC, NFIP) for any specialty products you sell

Plan your CE to cover the breadth of your licensed activities.

## 5 Frequently Asked Questions

- Do I need to take another background check when I add lines to my Oregon license? No. No additional background check is required to add lines to an existing active Oregon license.
- How much time do I need to add a new line? Typically 4-8 weeks: 2-4 weeks for prelicense education, 1-2 weeks for exam scheduling, and 1-2 weeks for DFR processing.
- Can I add multiple lines simultaneously? Yes. You can take a combined Life and Health course (40 hours) and combined exam, or take individual line exams. Plan based on your study capacity.
- Does adding lines affect my CE requirements? Your total Oregon CE remains 24 hours every 2 years even with multiple lines. However, you should take CE covering all your licensed lines.
- What if I take prelicense for a new line but don't pass the exam? You can retake the exam multiple times. Your prelicense completion is valid for 12 months — make sure to pass and submit your application within that window.
