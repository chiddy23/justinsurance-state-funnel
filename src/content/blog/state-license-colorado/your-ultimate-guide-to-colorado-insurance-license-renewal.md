[Colorado insurance license](/colorado) renewal can feel like one more administrative chore, but keeping your license active is a crucial part of maintaining a successful insurance career in Colorado. By staying on top of renewal requirements and deadlines, you ensure that your clients receive the best possible guidance. Plus, when you fully understand the process, you can focus on helping others, rather than stressing over paperwork.

Below, you will find a practical overview of what goes into renewing your license, from key [continuing education](/colorado/continuing-education) steps to common deadlines. This friendly guide will walk you through each detail and leave you with a clear plan of action.

## Why renewal matters

Your insurance license is your gateway to advising and supporting clients. In Colorado, it also represents your commitment to ethical and professional standards. Renewing shows that you are staying updated on changes in industry regulations, and it signals that you are ready to help customers make sound decisions about their coverage. If you let your license expire, you risk more than an inconvenience. You lose the ability to conduct business legally, which could interrupt your livelihood and reputation.

Making time to renew before it expires gives you a sense of security. You will not need to worry about lapses in your authority to sell or advise on insurance products. You also protect your career growth, because actively maintained credentials show employers and clients that you take your professional advancement seriously.

## Know your CE requirements

In Colorado, you are required to complete [Colorado continuing education](/colorado/continuing-education) (CE) hours to remain up to date on relevant industry regulations and best practices. Resident producers must complete 24 CE credits every two-year continuation cycle — but the state is specific about how those 24 credits break down, and that detail is where most compliance problems start.

Per the Division's Candidate Handbook, the 24 credits must include:

| Component | Credits |
| --- | --- |
| Courses in a line of authority you hold | 18 |
| Ethics category | 3 |
| Category of your choice | 3 |
| **Total** | **24** |

Two additional rules matter:

**Homeowners requirement.** If you hold a Personal Lines or Property line of authority, at least 3 of your credits must be in the Homeowners category.

**First cycle is exempt.** New producers are not required to complete CE during their first continuation cycle — the obligation starts with your second cycle. Credits you take during that first cycle do not carry forward and will not be applied to any future requirement, so there is no benefit to taking CE early as a first-time licensee.

If you hold multiple lines that require CE, you still only owe 24 credits total. At least 18 must fall in one or more of the lines you actually hold, so you can distribute them across the Life/A&H category, the Property/Casualty category, or both.

When choosing CE courses, be sure they are approved by the [Colorado Division of Insurance](/colorado/requirements). The courses should address relevant topics for your licensed line of authority, but you can also add electives to expand your knowledge. There are many ways to earn these hours, including in-person classes, online modules, and self-study options.

## Complete the renewal steps

1.  Fulfill your CE hours  
    Make a plan so you accumulate the required CE credits well before your license expires. Ideally, spread these learning hours throughout the two-year period rather than rushing at the last minute.
2.  Submit your continuation application and fee  
    Colorado calls renewal "license continuation," and you file it online at sircon.com/colorado or nipr.com — not through the Division's own website. Pearson VUE administers licensing on the Division's behalf, and everything must be received by Pearson VUE no later than 10:00 p.m. Mountain Time on your license expiration date. The state continuation fee is $27 per line of authority for residents and $40 per line for nonresidents, per the Division's fee schedule effective April 1, 2026; NIPR or Sircon transaction fees are charged on top of that.
3.  Confirm your status  
    After filing, watch for confirmation and print your continued license from sircon.com/colorado. Keep your renewal receipts and proof of CE completions. Having these records on hand helps in case of an audit or if you need to verify your renewal status.

Try to organize your documentation in a single folder. When everything is stored together, you can easily check if your renewal is complete and which CE credits were claimed.

## Stay on deadline

You must continue your license every two years in Colorado, and the expiration date is tied to your birth month. One wrinkle catches new producers: your *first* license period is not a full two years. Depending on your date of birth and the month you were licensed, that first expiration can land anywhere from 13 to 24 months after issue. Every cycle after that is a full two years. Pearson VUE emails a continuation notice 90 days before expiration, but failing to receive it does not excuse a late continuation — check your own date at sircon.com/colorado.

A good way to avoid last-minute panic is to set a recurring calendar reminder a few months before your deadline. That way, you can complete your CE courses and arrange payment without feeling rushed. Marking the due date in multiple places, such as on your phone and on your desk calendar, doubles your chances of remembering to follow through.

## Frequently asked questions

1.  How do I find out how many CE hours I still need?  
    Log into sircon.com/colorado to see the courses your providers have reported to Pearson VUE. You will also receive a CE notification by email 120 days before your continuation date listing the credits required and the credits already applied, broken out by category, with a status that changes to "Compliant" once each category is satisfied.
2.  What happens if I do not complete my CE on time?  
    Colorado does not charge a late fee and does not offer a late-renewal window. If your CE is not complete and reported by your continuation date, the license simply expires, and you may not sell, solicit, or negotiate insurance in Colorado until it is restored. Getting it back means filing a new license application and paying the initial application fee of $44 per line of authority — and if the license has been expired for more than a year, repeating prelicensing and the state exam.
3.  Can I carry over extra CE hours to the next renewal period?  
    Yes, within limits. You may carry a maximum of 12 credits earned above the required 24 into your next cycle, and only if those courses were completed within the final 120 days of the cycle. Hours beyond that 12-credit cap, or earned earlier in the cycle, do not carry. Carry-over does not apply to first-time licensees, since credits taken during your exempt first cycle are not applied at all. Note also that you cannot receive credit for the same course repeated within a two-year period.
4.  Do I need separate CE for each line of authority?  
    No. If you hold multiple lines that require CE, you owe 24 credits total, not 24 per line. At least 18 of them must be in one or more of the lines you actually hold, so you can take them in the Life/A&H category, the Property/Casualty category, or a mix — whatever matches your authorities.
5.  Is there a grace period if my license expires?  
    No. A Colorado license expires at the end of your birth month in your renewal year, with no grace period and no ability to late-renew. Once that date passes the license is inactive, and transacting insurance on it violates state law. The only way back is reinstatement through a new application, so renew inside the 90-day window before your deadline.
