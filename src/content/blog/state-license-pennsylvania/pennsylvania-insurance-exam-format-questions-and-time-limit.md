Understanding the format of the Pennsylvania insurance exam — exactly how many questions, how much time, how questions are structured, and what testing options exist — helps you prepare strategically rather than guessing. Pennsylvania exam format follows specific standards published by PSI in the Pennsylvania Candidate Information Bulletin, and knowing these details lets you study effectively and choose the right exam path.

Here's a detailed look at Pennsylvania insurance exam format.

## Where to Find Official Format Information

PSI publishes the Pennsylvania Candidate Information Bulletin containing official exam format and content information. This is the authoritative source for what's tested.

The bulletin includes:

Exam format specifications

Detailed content outlines

Information about the testing process

Eligibility and licensing application process information

ID requirements and testing rules

Access through PSI's website at psiexams.com. Read the bulletin before scheduling your exam — knowing exactly what's covered, weighted, and formatted helps you prepare effectively.

## Exam Format Basics

All Pennsylvania insurance license exams share common format characteristics:

Computer-based. Exams are taken on computers at PSI testing centers or through online proctored testing.

Multiple choice. All questions are multiple choice with four answer options.

Combined sections. General Knowledge and State Law sections combined into a single test.

Single combined score. You receive one overall score reflecting performance across both sections.

70% passing requirement. Standard across all exam types.

Immediate or rapid results. Most candidates learn pass or fail within 24 hours, often within minutes.

One question at a time. PSI displays one question on screen at a time.

## Question Counts and Time by Exam Type

Pennsylvania exam specifications fall into two main categories:

Single-Line Exams (100 questions, 2 hours):

Producer's Life Insurance (16-01)

Producer's Accident and Health Insurance (16-02)

Combined Life and Health Insurance

Producer's Property Insurance

Producer's Casualty Insurance

Personal Lines Insurance

Combined Property and Casualty Exam (150 questions, 2 hours 50 minutes):

Producer's Property and Casualty Insurance

The single-line vs. combined-P&C distinction is important — combined P&C is the only Pennsylvania exam with 150 questions and the extended time limit.

## Time per Question

Understanding pacing helps:

100-question exams over 120 minutes: Approximately 72 seconds per question on average

150-question P&C combined over 170 minutes: Approximately 68 seconds per question on average

Most prepared candidates complete with time to spare. Time becomes an issue mainly for unprepared candidates who get stuck on multiple questions.

## Question Types

Pennsylvania exam questions follow standard multiple choice patterns:

Recall questions. Test factual knowledge.

"What is the standard provision regarding the grace period in a life insurance policy?"

Application questions. Test ability to apply knowledge to scenarios.

"An applicant for life insurance has a history of high blood pressure. The agent should..."

Analysis questions. Test deeper understanding.

"Which of the following best describes the purpose of the Notice Regarding Replacement?"

Calculation questions. Test mathematical computation.

"If a policy has a face amount of $250,000 and a 90-day grace period premium of $200, what is the policy's pro-rated grace period charge?"

Comparison questions. Test ability to distinguish between concepts.

"Which of the following statements is true about HMO plans but not PPO plans?"

Most questions are application or analysis style — pure recall is less common.

## Question Modifier Words to Watch

Pay close attention to specific modifier words:

EXCEPT. Asks for the answer that is NOT true.

"All of the following are required to be disclosed in a Notice Regarding Replacement EXCEPT..."

NOT. Asks for the false statement.

"Which of the following is NOT a standard policy provision?"

LEAST LIKELY. Asks for the lowest probability.

"Which client is LEAST LIKELY to need long-term care insurance?"

BEST. Asks for the optimal among reasonable options.

"What is the BEST way to handle this client's request for replacement?"

MUST. Asks for required behavior.

"An agent MUST do which of the following before recommending an annuity?"

MAY. Asks for permitted (not required) behavior.

"An insurer MAY require which of the following at policy issuance?"

Misreading modifiers is one of the most common sources of preventable wrong answers.

## Single-Line vs. Combined Exam Decision

A key choice for many Pennsylvania candidates: take individual single-line exams or the combined exam?

For Life and Health:

Combined Life and Health Exam (100 questions, 2 hours):

Single exam covering both content areas

Most efficient path for combined Life and Health licensure

Separate Life and Health Exams:

Two separate single-line exams (16-01 and 16-02)

Each 100 questions, 2 hours

Allows focused preparation per area but doubles exam fees

For Property and Casualty:

Combined Property and Casualty Exam (150 questions, 2 hours 50 minutes):

Single exam covering both content areas

More content but single test event

Separate Property and Casualty Exams:

Two separate single-line exams

Each 100 questions, 2 hours

Smaller question count per exam but two test events

Recommendation for most candidates: Take the combined exam if you're pursuing both lines. The cost and time savings are typically substantial.

Recommendation for cautious candidates: Take single-line exams if you have stronger preparation for one area than the other, or if you want to space out your testing.

## Online Proctored Testing

Pennsylvania permits online proctored testing through PSI for many insurance exams. If you choose this path:

Equipment requirements: Compatible computer or laptop, webcam, reliable internet, microphone, compatible operating system.

Environment requirements: Quiet, private testing space with no other people, clear desk/workspace, adequate lighting.

Identification requirements: Same valid ID requirements as physical testing centers.

Testing experience: Live proctor monitors throughout, cannot leave camera view, same time limits and scoring.

If you don't have a compatible computer for online testing, schedule an in-person exam at a PSI testing center.

## Format Comparison: Pennsylvania vs. Other States

Pennsylvania's exam format is similar to many states using PSI:

Question counts. Comparable to other PSI states for single-line and combined exams.

Time limits. Standard across most state insurance exams.

Passing score. 70% standard across most state insurance exams.

Format. Computer-based multiple choice consistent with most state exams.

State-specific weighting. ~15-25% Pennsylvania content typical.

No retake limit. Pennsylvania allows unlimited retakes (though each costs another fee).
