You may be surprised by how straightforward it can be to earn your [Florida insurance adjuster license](/florida) once you understand the process. The single most useful thing to know up front: unlike Florida's agent licenses, the 6-20 All-Lines Adjuster license has **no state-mandated prelicensing course**. The Florida Department of Financial Services gives you two ways to qualify — hold one of the credentials that exempt you from the state exam, or hold none of them and sit for the exam. Below, you will find a clear, friendly roadmap that guides you from the first moment you decide to become an adjuster to the day you land your official license.

## Know the role of insurance adjusters

Before diving into the requirements, it helps to understand exactly what an insurance adjuster does. In Florida, adjusters investigate claims, evaluate damages, and negotiate settlements on behalf of insurance companies and policyholders. Your job involves talking with clients, inspecting properties or vehicles, taking photographs, and documenting your findings. By assessing each claim accurately and fairly, you help safeguard people’s livelihoods and bring peace of mind during tough times.

For many adjusters, job variety is one of the biggest benefits. Each day can look different as you move between field inspections, phone interviews, and claim reviews. If you enjoy problem-solving and working with people, this profession could be remarkably rewarding.

## Check if you qualify

Acquiring your Florida insurance adjuster license requires meeting a few key prerequisites. Per the DFS requirement sheet for the 6-20 Resident All-Lines Adjuster license, you must be a natural person at least 18 years of age, a resident of Florida, a United States citizen or a legal alien with work authorization, and you must not hold a resident license in another state. A clean criminal background matters too — prior offenses are reviewed case by case rather than automatically disqualifying. Verify the current eligibility rules directly with DFS so you know where you stand.

From there, it is a matter of deciding which qualifying route fits you and making room in your schedule. The entire process can often be completed in just a few weeks, though exact timelines differ depending on which route you take and how quickly the department processes your application.

## Choose your qualifying route

This is the step most guides get wrong, so it is worth slowing down on. Florida does **not** require a prelicensing education course for the 6-20 All-Lines Adjuster license. Instead, Step 2 of the DFS requirement sheet asks whether you already hold one of the qualifying prerequisites. Each one is marked "Exempt from State Examination" — meaning it replaces the exam rather than qualifying you to take it.

You are exempt from the state exam if you:

-   Are currently licensed as an all-lines public adjuster.
-   Hold one of the department's recognized adjuster designations, awarded by the specific institution DFS names: ACA, AICS, AIC, CA, CALA, CCA, CPCU, CACP, PCA, PIC, PPIA, RCA, or UCC. (A UCC designation additionally requires proof of precertification course and exam completion.)
-   Earned an insurance degree that includes at least 18 semester hours of college credit in property, casualty, health, and commercial insurance from an accredited college or university — official transcript required.
-   Are currently licensed as a Florida General Lines (Property and Casualty) agent.
-   Are transferring in: obtain a Letter of Clearance showing you held an all-lines adjuster license for at least one year immediately preceding your application, and apply within 90 days of becoming a Florida resident.

If you hold none of the above, you take the state examination instead. That is the entire choice. Designation programs are voluntary — people take them because they prefer studying to test day, not because Florida requires a course.

If you go the designation route, choose a provider from the DFS list rather than any course that markets itself as "Florida adjuster prelicensing," because only a designation from a named institution triggers the exemption. Send proof of your prerequisite to AgentLicensing@MyFloridaCFO.com; some providers transmit it for you within 21 days of your passing date.

A note on what we do and do not offer: JustInsurance's Florida catalog covers agent [prelicensing](/florida/prelicensing) for the Life and Health lines — 2-14, 2-40, and the combined 2-15. We do not offer a 6-20 adjuster designation course, and Florida would not require one of you in any case. If the adjuster path is where you are headed, work directly from the DFS requirement sheet.

## Take the state exam

If you do not hold one of the exemptions above, the state examination is your route. Florida's All-Lines Adjuster exam (Pearson VUE code InsFL-ALJ16, covering the 6-20, 7-20, and 70-20 licenses) is 110 questions — 100 scored plus 10 unscored pretest items — with a two-hour time limit. The passing score is 70%, and in Florida that is a true percentage: the handbook computes it by dividing the number of questions answered correctly by the total number of examination questions.

You schedule online through Pearson VUE and test at a center in Florida. The examination fee is $44 per attempt, payable at the time of reservation and forfeited if you miss your appointment. Bring proper identification. There is a limit of five examination attempts for the same examination type in any twelve-month period, and a passing score stays valid for one year — if you have not finished the licensing process within that year, you have to retake and pass the exam again.

Note the sequence, because it is the reverse of what most people assume. The DFS 6-20 requirement sheet puts the application at Step 3 and the examination at Step 6: once your application is approved, the department emails you to check your MyProfile messages, where you will find your examination authorization and scheduling instructions. Follow that sequence rather than trying to book a seat before you have applied.

## Apply for your license

The application itself is Step 3, and it comes before the exam. You handle it online through the [Florida Department of Financial Services](/florida/requirements) (DFS) MyProfile portal. You will answer the application questions, pay the fees, and — if you are qualifying by designation, degree, license, or letter of clearance — send proof of that prerequisite to AgentLicensing@MyFloridaCFO.com. Fingerprinting is a separate step with its own vendor fee, paid directly to the vendor rather than bundled into the application.

Once you have submitted everything accurately, DFS reviews your application, and any deficiencies show up under the pending license type in MyProfile. Approval timelines can vary, but many applicants receive a decision within a few weeks. Keep an eye on your MyProfile messages and your inbox for requests for additional documents that could delay final approval. When everything clears, you print your license from the "Wallet" and "Letter" links in MyProfile.

One thing that surprises new adjusters: the license needs an appointment to be active, and an adjuster license can hold only one appointment at a time. All-lines adjusters are appointed by an insurance company or adjusting firm, or self-appointed through eAppoint. A 6-20 license expires if it goes unappointed for 48 months, so do not let it sit idle.

## Maintain your license

Earning your Florida insurance adjuster license is a major accomplishment, and you will want to keep it in good standing. Florida requires ongoing [continuing education](/florida/continuing-education) (CE) to ensure you stay current with any shifts in regulations or best practices. For the 6-20 license, DFS sets the requirement at 24 hours due every two years by the end of your birth month, under section 626.2815, Florida Statutes. Once the requirement generates, you can view it in your MyProfile account.

Often, you will focus part of your CE on specific topics such as law and ethics, and the rest can be on subjects that boost your professional development. Thankfully, many of these courses are offered online, so you can fit them around your schedule. Staying on top of CE not only keeps your license active, it also helps you deliver top-notch service to clients.

## Understand timelines and costs

Acquiring your adjuster license does involve both time and money, but a little planning makes it all manageable. Most people can finish their application, fingerprinting, and exam in under two or three months if they commit to a steady study pace. If other responsibilities limit your availability, you can spread out the process — just remember that a passing exam score is only good for one year.

When it comes to expenses, here is a quick rundown of what you might expect:

-   No mandatory course cost. A voluntary designation program is optional and priced by the provider, so shop it like any other purchase.
-   The state examination fee is $44 per attempt, per the Pearson VUE Florida candidate handbook.
-   The DFS license application fee is $50, plus a $5 license identification fee.
-   Fingerprinting is paid directly to the vendor and is not included in the application fee — DFS publishes the current rate on its fingerprinting information page.

Fees change, so confirm all current costs through the DFS fee schedule and the Pearson VUE Florida handbook before you commit.

## Start your journey

Now that you know the steps, you are one decision away from pursuing a career as a Florida insurance adjuster. Start by pulling up the DFS 6-20 Resident All-Lines Adjuster requirement sheet and answering one question honestly: do you already hold one of the qualifying prerequisites, or are you taking the exam? That answer determines everything that follows. Keep a notebook on hand to jot down questions as you work through the DFS steps — those notes will come in handy whether you are preparing for the exam or gathering proof of a designation.

Once you have your Florida insurance adjuster license, you open the door to a field that offers stability, opportunities for growth, and the gratification of assisting individuals in need. And if your plans eventually extend beyond claims into selling coverage, that is a different license with a different path — Florida's agent lines do require prelicensing hours, and our [Florida prelicensing courses](/florida/prelicensing) cover the 2-14, 2-40, and 2-15 Life and Health lines.
