If you’re ready to become a licensed insurance professional in Arizona, you’re in the right place. Understanding [Arizona insurance license requirements](/arizona/requirements) from the start can help you avoid confusion and set a steady path toward your new career. Whether you plan to offer life and health coverage, property and casualty services, or both, each license demands specific steps. Here’s how to get organized and move forward with confidence.

## Understand Arizona licensing rules

Arizona’s Department of Insurance (DIFI) oversees the licensing process to ensure that you meet all regulatory standards. You’ll need to decide which lines of authority you intend to pursue. Common license types include Life, Accident & Health, and Property & Casualty. If you’re relocating from another state and already hold a resident license there, read the FAQ below before you do anything else — Arizona waives the exam for relocating producers who apply within 90 days of inactivating the prior resident license, and that clock starts whether you know about it or not.

Stay aware of any updates to Arizona regulations. Although major changes don’t happen frequently, it’s always smart to confirm key guidelines on the official DIFI website. Double-check as you plan your studies so you don’t overlook any new or updated prerequisites.

## Complete your pre-licensing

Arizona does not legally require [Arizona insurance prelicensing](/arizona/prelicensing) education before you register for the exam — it's optional test prep. That said, these classes help you learn basic insurance concepts, policy structures, and state regulations that you’ll need to serve clients effectively, and most candidates find them well worth the time.

If you plan to offer multiple lines of coverage, you can choose to complete [pre-licensing](/arizona/prelicensing) prep for each line. Studying thoroughly also benefits you in the long run. Not only will a complete understanding of insurance fundamentals help you pass the exam, but it will also prepare you for real-life client scenarios.

## Pass your insurance exam

Unless you qualify for the relocating-producer exam waiver described in the FAQ below, this is your next step: whether or not you took a pre-licensing course, you’ll register for the state licensing exam. Securing a passing score proves that you understand [Arizona insurance license](/arizona) requirements and can apply them in practice. Arizona’s exams are administered by PSI, so you’ll need to schedule in advance and pay a registration fee.

When you sit for the exam:  
• Arrive early with a valid government-issued photo ID.  
• Bring a copy of your pre-licensing completion certificate if you took a course and the testing site asks for it.  
• Expect multiple-choice questions covering state regulations, policy details, client scenarios, and more.

The exam format typically lasts a few hours, but you’ll find out if you’ve passed before you leave. Be prepared for a range of questions. Some focus on state rules regarding licensure, while others test your understanding of complex coverage concepts. Solid preparation is key, so dedicate appropriate time to reviewing the materials you learned in your classes.

## Submit your application

Once you’ve passed the licensing exam — or, if you’re a relocating resident producer using the 90-day exam waiver, as your very first step — you submit a formal application to Arizona’s Department of Insurance. You’ll need to fill out the required forms, pay the applicable fees, and provide proof of your exam results (or your clearance letter, if you’re transferring). DIFI will also conduct a background check to confirm that you meet all professional standards.

Don’t let the paperwork intimidate you. Keep digital copies of everything you collect during the licensing process, including pre-licensing certificates and exam results. Having them in one place speeds up the submission and helps you seamlessly complete each step.

After you submit your application, you may wait several weeks for approval, although the actual time frame can vary. Once approved, you’ll receive electronic confirmation of your license. Then you can legally represent insurance carriers and advise clients on the best coverage for their needs.

## Frequently asked questions

Below are answers to some questions people often have about meeting Arizona insurance license requirements and starting a career in this field.

1.  Do I need separate licenses for life and health?  
    Yes, technically — Life and Accident & Health are separate lines of authority, though they can be combined into a single exam. Arizona doesn't require pre-licensing hours for either, but if you take an optional course, double-check with your provider to make sure you’re covering the right topics for both lines.
2.  Can I use my out-of-state license in Arizona?  
    Your old license doesn’t automatically become an Arizona license — you still apply to DIFI. But if you’re relocating and you held a resident license in another state, Arizona will waive the exam under A.R.S. § 20-288, provided your application reaches the Department within 90 days after your former resident license was cancelled, expired, or converted to nonresident status. You’ll need a clearance letter from your prior state showing that date, the lines of authority you held, and that you were in good standing (DIFI can skip the letter if it can verify all of that on the Producer Database). Two catches: Arizona must already issue those exact license types to residents, and not every line of authority is eligible for transfer. Miss the 90-day window and you do have to sit for the Arizona exam — which is why relocating producers should file first and unpack later.
3.  How many hours of study do I really need?  
    Arizona doesn't set a mandatory minimum — there's no state-required prelicensing hour count. Most candidates still benefit from a structured course (JustInsurance's optional Life and Health prelicensing courses run about 20 hours per single line, or around 40 for the combined Life & Health track) plus additional self-study to feel ready for the exam.
4.  Is self-study an option or do I need formal classes?  
    Arizona doesn't require a pre-licensing program — you can self-study and register for the exam directly. That said, a structured course can make self-study more efficient by pointing you toward the topics the exam actually covers.
5.  Will I need to renew my license at some point?  
    Yes. All Arizona insurance licenses must be renewed periodically. You’ll have [Arizona continuing education](/arizona/continuing-education) (CE) requirements to maintain your license, specific to each line of authority you hold.
