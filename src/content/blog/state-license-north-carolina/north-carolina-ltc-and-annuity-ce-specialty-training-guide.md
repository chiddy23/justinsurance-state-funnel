Two specialty training requirements stand out for North Carolina producers pursuing higher-revenue practice areas: the Long-Term Care Partnership training requirement and the Annuity Best Interest certification. Both are one-time-plus-ongoing investments that open access to substantial product markets — and both reflect North Carolina's commitment to consumer protection in areas where client harm can be significant. Understanding exactly what's required, when it applies, and how it connects to your CE compliance helps you plan your specialty training efficiently.

Here's what North Carolina producers need to know about LTC and Annuity specialty training.

## Why These Specialties Matter in North Carolina

Before diving into specifics, context matters:

North Carolina's senior population. North Carolina has a substantial and growing senior population — both from the state's own aging demographics and from significant retiree in-migration. Charlotte's suburbs, the Research Triangle, western NC mountain communities, and coastal areas all attract retirees from across the country.

LTC cost reality. North Carolina's long-term care costs are substantial. Nursing facility and home care costs create real financial risk for seniors without LTC coverage. This creates genuine client demand for properly structured LTC products.

Annuity market depth. North Carolina's growing wealth concentrations — Charlotte banking professionals, Research Triangle tech and healthcare workers, coastal affluent retirees — create substantial annuity market opportunity.

Consumer protection concern. Both LTC and annuity products carry meaningful risk of unsuitable sales. North Carolina's training requirements reflect regulatory concern about ensuring producers understand these products before selling them.

For producers willing to develop genuine expertise, both specialties offer substantial North Carolina market opportunity.

## North Carolina LTC Training: The Full Framework

Who must complete LTC training: All producers wishing to sell, solicit, or negotiate Long-Term Care Partnership (LTCP) products in North Carolina.

License prerequisites (before training):

Active North Carolina Accident & Health or Sickness license

Active North Carolina Medicare Supplement/Long-Term Care limited lines license (requires a 10-hour [prelicense](/north-carolina/prelicensing) course and separate exam — or was required before PLE elimination effective October 1, 2025)

Training requirements:

[Initial 8-hour LTCP Certification Training](https://www.ncdoi.gov/documents/agent-services/long-term-care-partnership-licensees-faqs/open):

Required before selling any LTCP products

One-time requirement (not repeated)

State-approved course

Counts toward CE hours in the period completed

Covers LTC products, LTCP-specific provisions, North Carolina regulations, and Medicaid/asset protection coordination

[4-hour Ongoing LTCP Training](https://www.ncdoi.gov/documents/agent-services/long-term-care-partnership-licensees-faqs/open):

Required every biennial compliance period after initial 8-hour completion

Continues for the producer's entire LTCP sales career

State-approved course

Counts toward CE hours in the period completed

Important reciprocity note: Resident licensees who satisfy training requirements of another state that are substantially similar to North Carolina's law will be considered compliant — but only if [the insurance company chooses to recognize and accept the training completed in another state](https://www.ncdoi.gov/documents/agent-services/long-term-care-partnership-licensees-faqs/open). This reciprocity isn't automatic.

## Why LTC Partnership Specifically (vs. Non-Partnership LTC)

North Carolina producers should understand the distinction:

Non-Partnership LTC: Standard LTC insurance without the Partnership asset protection feature. Producers may sell this without the 8-hour LTCP training — though the Medicare Supplement/LTC limited lines license is still required.

LTC Partnership (LTCP): Policies that include the asset protection benefit connecting insurance benefits paid to Medicaid asset protection. Requires LTCP training.

Most NC LTC producers pursue full LTCP training since it opens access to both Partnership and non-Partnership products while providing the most complete client protection.

## North Carolina Annuity Best Interest Training

Who must complete: All North Carolina licensed producers who sell, solicit, or negotiate annuity products.

Requirement:

One-time 4-hour Annuity Best Interest certification course

Must be completed before selling annuities

Effective date: January 1, 2023

Counts toward CE hours in the period completed

Reciprocity: Completion in any other state with laws substantially similar to North Carolina's (NAIC Annuity Best Interest framework) satisfies the NC requirement. This is true reciprocity — automatic recognition, not carrier-dependent.

## Practical Planning: Fitting Specialty Training Into CE

Both LTC and Annuity specialty training count toward your [standard 24-hour CE requirement](https://www.ncdoi.gov/licensees/insurance-producer-and-adjuster-licensing/continuing-education-insurance-producers-and-adjusters):

Example CE plan for new L&H producer in first compliance period:

3 hours Ethics (mandatory) ✓

4 hours Annuity Best Interest (one-time, counts as CE) ✓

8 hours LTC Partnership initial training (one-time, counts as CE) ✓

9 hours additional general CE topics ✓

Total: 24 hours ✓

In subsequent periods for the same producer:

3 hours Ethics (mandatory) ✓

4 hours LTC Partnership ongoing ✓

17 hours general CE topics ✓

Total: 24 hours ✓

Specialty training integrates efficiently into the 24-hour requirement — you're satisfying regulatory training while also filling CE hours.

## Military/Disability Waiver Note for LTC

An important distinction: if you receive a CE waiver for military service or long-term medical disability, your LTC training requirement is NOT waived. The CE waiver only covers the general 24-hour [continuing education](/north-carolina/continuing-education) requirement. LTC training must still be maintained even if CE is waived.

This means LTC producers on military deployment still need to address their LTC ongoing training during waiver periods.

## 5 Frequently Asked Questions

- What licenses do I need before completing North Carolina LTC Partnership training? [Both an active Accident & Health or Sickness license AND an active Medicare Supplement/Long-Term Care limited lines license are required before completing LTCP training](https://www.ncdoi.gov/documents/agent-services/long-term-care-partnership-licensees-faqs/open) and selling LTCP products.
- Does the Annuity Best Interest training apply to all annuity types in North Carolina? Yes. The 4-hour Annuity Best Interest certification applies before selling any annuity products — fixed, indexed, or variable. Required effective January 1, 2023.
- Does annuity training completed in another state satisfy North Carolina? Yes — if the other state has laws substantially similar to North Carolina's (NAIC Annuity Best Interest framework). Reciprocity is automatic for substantially similar states, unlike LTC reciprocity which depends on carrier acceptance.
- Do specialty training hours count toward my 24-hour CE requirement? Yes. Both LTC Partnership training and Annuity Best Interest training count toward your 24-hour CE requirement in the period completed.
- If I receive a military CE waiver, do I still need to complete LTC ongoing training? Yes. The CE waiver only covers the general 24-hour continuing education requirement. LTC training requirements must be maintained even during military service CE waivers.
