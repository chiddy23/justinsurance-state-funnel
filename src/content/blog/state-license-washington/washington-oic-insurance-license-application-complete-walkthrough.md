The Washington Office of the Insurance Commissioner manages producer licensing with a combination of OIC direct services and NIPR integration, a distinctive application-before-fingerprinting sequence, and an increasingly modernized electronic licensing system. Washington's 2025 and 2026 updates — including the January 2026 electronic transition for core licensing transactions — mean that the process looks somewhat different from what older guides describe, and the application-before-fingerprinting sequence trips up candidates who follow generic national advice. Here's a current, accurate walkthrough of how Washington's OIC application process works.

## Who the OIC Is

The Washington Office of the Insurance Commissioner (OIC) is Washington's sole insurance regulatory authority — unlike DIFI (Arizona) or DIFS (Michigan), Washington's OIC focuses exclusively on insurance. It does not regulate banking or mortgage lending, which are handled by separate Washington state agencies.

OIC's insurance authority includes:

Licensing producers, adjusters, and insurance companies

Examining insurer financial condition and market conduct

Investigating consumer complaints

Enforcing RCW Title 48 (Revised Code of Washington) and WAC Title 284

Rulemaking through the Washington Administrative Code

OIC is modernizing: A January 2026 bulletin announced that OIC's Producer & Licensing Oversight (PLO) program is transitioning core licensing transactions from the OIC Online system to electronic handling. This modernization is ongoing — some transactions that previously required OIC's specific portal are moving to more streamlined electronic processing.

## Decision 1: Which Lines to Apply For

Washington's line-of-authority structure shapes both exam requirements and application fees:

Life and Disability (L&D): Most common path for financial services, Medicare, and health-adjacent producers. Washington's terminology: "Disability" not "A&H."

Property and Casualty (P&C): Standard path for home, auto, and commercial lines producers.

Combined Life & Disability + Property & Casualty: Broadest market access. Requires two exams but one application covers all lines.

Line-specific exam fees (PSI Washington Candidate Information Bulletin, revised 3/6/2026):

Single line — Life Producer, Disability Producer, Property Producer, Casualty Producer, Personal Lines Producer: $38 per exam (100 questions)

Combined exam — Life and Disability Producer Combo, or Property and Casualty Producer Combo: $55 per exam (150 questions)

Application fee: Approximately $55 covers all lines in a single application — applying for multiple lines simultaneously doesn't multiply the application fee in the same way some states charge per-line.

Washington's "Disability" terminology: When selecting lines, note that Washington licenses the health/disability line as "Disability" — not "Accident & Health" or "A&H" as most other states call it. This distinction appears throughout the application and on your license.

## The Critical Application Sequence

Washington's fingerprint process requires specific sequencing that many candidates and older guides describe incorrectly. Both OIC and PSI are explicit: you must submit your license application *before* you can schedule fingerprinting, which OIC and PSI attribute to an FBI requirement.

Correct sequence:

Pass your exam (or plan to pass first, then follow steps 2-6)

Submit license application through NIPR (nipr.com) or OIC (insurance.wa.gov) — pay ~$55 fee

Receive transaction number — in confirmation email or NIPR order history

Schedule fingerprint appointment using your transaction number — through IDEMIA IdentoGO (identogo.com or 888-771-5097)

Complete fingerprinting — at one of Washington's 30+ IDEMIA IdentoGO locations ($49.00, which OIC describes as the background check fee plus IdentoGO's service charge)

OIC processes — Washington State Patrol forwards background check to OIC

Why application-first? OIC needs a submitted application on file before it can receive and match fingerprint results. The transaction number links your fingerprint submission to your application.

Timing flexibility: You can apply after passing the exam or coordinate the application and fingerprint timing around your exam. The key constraint is that fingerprinting cannot be scheduled until after the application is submitted.

One thing PSI does not do: fingerprint you. Fingerprinting is handled only by IDEMIA IdentoGO, at a separate appointment from your exam — PSI's bulletin directs candidates to "schedule an appointment with Idemia (IdentoGO)" and to select the Washington Office of the Insurance Commissioner as the receiving state agency. Candidates do not need to be fingerprinted before taking the exam.

## NIPR Application Steps

Step 1: Go to nipr.com, create or log into account.

Step 2: Select "Apply for a License" → Washington.

Step 3: Select "Resident Individual."

Step 4: Select lines of authority.

Step 5: Complete personal information:

Legal name (must exactly match government-issued ID — discrepancies cause delays)

Date of birth

Social Security Number

Washington address

Contact information

Step 6: Answer background disclosure questions:

Criminal history

Prior license actions in any state

Regulatory actions

Financial history disclosures

Step 7: Upload passing score report (fax or attach as scan).

Step 8: Pay ~$55 application fee.

Step 9: Submit — receive transaction number.

## Background Disclosures

Washington OIC takes background disclosures seriously. Specific criminal offenses can disqualify a candidate, particularly:

Disqualifying concerns:

Felony convictions, especially breach-of-trust offenses

Prior license revocations or suspensions in any state

Financial crimes and fraud convictions

Not automatically disqualifying:

Traffic misdemeanors and similar minor offenses

Many older convictions with demonstrated rehabilitation

Pre-application eligibility review: If you have something serious in your background — particularly a breach-of-trust felony — OIC offers a pre-application eligibility review. Complete the OIC's eligibility form and allow approximately 30 days for a response before investing in exam fees. This protects candidates from paying for exams before knowing if they're eligible.

"If you have a criminal offense" webpage: OIC maintains specific guidance on their website for candidates with criminal histories — consult this resource before applying.

## After Submitting Your Application

OIC review period: Clean applications typically process within a few weeks after receiving complete information (application, exam score, and fingerprint background check results).

Tracking your application: Use your transaction number at insurance.wa.gov to check status.

License printing: Once OIC issues your license, log back into the OIC online portal to print your license document.

Appointment and affiliation: Washington requires producers to be affiliated with a carrier or agency to actively sell. The carrier appointment process occurs separately from licensing.

Affiliation begin date: Washington requires licensees to supply their affiliation begin date when submitting initial applications (effective January 18, 2024).

## The December 2025 Business Rule Updates

December 12, 2025 rule changes affected several Washington licensing procedures:

Reinstatements now allowed: Washington updated rules to process license reinstatements electronically.

Reinstatement fees: Specific fee structure established for reinstatement applications.

DRLP (Designated Responsible Licensed Producer) requirements: Updated business entity licensing rules regarding who serves as DRLP.

Affiliation requirements: Updated rules governing producer affiliation with insurers and agencies.

These 2025-2026 changes are part of OIC's broader modernization program. When in doubt about current requirements, verify with OIC directly at LicInfo@oic.wa.gov.

## Non-Resident Application Path

For producers already licensed elsewhere:

No exam required — in good standing in home state.

No fingerprinting required — Washington's fingerprint requirement only applies to resident applicants.

Apply through NIPR — reciprocal application process.

Fee: ~$55 application fee.

Timeline: Non-resident applications process quickly given no exam or fingerprint processing.

Washington non-resident licensing is among the fastest and most streamlined in the country — no exam, no fingerprints, NIPR application, and relatively low fee.

## 5 Frequently Asked Questions

- What is Washington's OIC and what does it regulate? The Washington Office of the Insurance Commissioner regulates insurance producers, insurers, and related entities under RCW Title 48 and WAC Title 284. Unlike Arizona's DIFI or Michigan's DIFS, Washington's OIC focuses exclusively on insurance — banking and mortgage are handled by separate state agencies.
- Why do I need to apply before getting fingerprinted in Washington? Washington requires the license application to be submitted first — OIC and PSI both cite an FBI requirement — so OIC has a record on file to match with your fingerprint results. As OIC puts it, you won't be able to schedule your appointment until they've received your license application. Your transaction number from the application is what lets you book the IDEMIA IdentoGO appointment.
- Can I take the Washington insurance exam online from home? Yes. Washington has offered remote testing since 2020 and is a leader in remote insurance exam proctoring. All major line exams are available through PSI Bridge remote testing. A compatible computer, webcam, and stable internet connection are required.
- How long does Washington license processing take? Clean applications typically process within a few weeks after OIC receives all required components — application, passing score report, and fingerprint background check results. Non-resident applications without fingerprint requirements are typically faster.
- What makes Washington non-resident licensing so straightforward? Non-resident producers in good standing in their home state don't need to take the Washington exam and don't need fingerprinting. A simple NIPR application with fee is all that's required. Washington's reciprocal licensing approach makes non-resident licensing very accessible.
