## What makes Texas different from most state exams

Unlike Florida's 40-hour mandate or California's 12-hour Code & Ethics course, **Texas has no mandatory prelicensing education requirement** for the Life or Health insurance producer license under [Tex. Ins. Code §4001.105](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/?link=IN). The only gates between you and a Texas producer license are:

- Pass the Texas Life, Health, or Life & Health exam administered by [Pearson VUE](https://home.pearsonvue.com/tx/insurance) — the current exam fee is $39
- Complete fingerprinting through IdentoGO using the Texas-specific service code **11G6QF** (without that exact code, your prints may not be routed to the Texas Department of Insurance)
- Submit your license application through [NIPR](https://nipr.com/licensing-center/apply) with the $50 application fee
- Pass the background review under [Tex. Ins. Code § 4001.103](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/?link=IN)

From there, the [Texas Department of Insurance](https://www.tdi.texas.gov/agent/) typically processes a resident license application in 1 to 2 business days after fingerprints clear. Real-time issuance is the norm for Texas — **most candidates go from exam-pass to active license number in under a week.**

Two things follow from this:

1. **Course quality matters more in Texas than in mandated-hour states.** There's no state-approved seat-time floor forcing providers to include a minimum content volume. Some Texas "prelicensing courses" are marketed as prelicensing but are really just exam-prep with a certificate of completion nobody at TDI is asking for. What you actually need is content that matches the Texas exam outline — not a paper credential.

2. **Your fingerprinting and NIPR application steps are where candidates stumble.** Most delays in Texas aren't the exam — they're wrong IdentoGO service codes, mismatched names between ID and application, or overlooked background disclosures. White-glove licensing support (NIPR walkthrough, fingerprint vendor help, TDI follow-up) is more valuable in Texas than in states where the prelicensing course is the longest step.

## What to verify before exam day (Texas-specific)

1. **Your name on the Pearson VUE exam registration matches your government-issued ID exactly** — middle names, suffixes, and maiden names all need to match. Texas-specific issue: candidates whose Pearson VUE registration uses a preferred name or nickname are regularly turned away at the testing center.
2. **Your IdentoGO service code is 11G6QF** — use this exact code when scheduling. A different code will route your prints to a different state agency and delay your application.
3. **Your NIPR application is submitted within 12 months of passing the state exam** — Texas allows 12 months between exam pass and application submission, after which the exam result expires and you'd have to retest.
4. **Sircon or MyProfile** — both portals work for ongoing license maintenance after issuance. Pick one and use it consistently; using both interchangeably causes duplicate record issues.
5. **Annuity Suitability Training** — once licensed, Texas requires a one-time 4-hour annuity suitability training course before you can sell annuity products under [Tex. Ins. Code §4004.051](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/?link=IN) requirements. Not required for licensure, but required before first annuity sale.
