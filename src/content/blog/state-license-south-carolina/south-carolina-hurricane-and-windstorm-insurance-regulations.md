South Carolina's extensive coastline and hurricane exposure create one of the most complex property insurance environments in the Southeast. For producers working with coastal clients, understanding hurricane and windstorm insurance regulations is essential — both to serve clients effectively and to stay compliant with South Carolina-specific rules.

Here's what South Carolina producers need to know about hurricane and windstorm insurance regulations.

## South Carolina's Hurricane Exposure

South Carolina sits in one of the most hurricane-vulnerable regions of the U.S. Atlantic coast:

Over 180 miles of coastline

Major coastal population centers in Charleston, Myrtle Beach/Grand Strand, Hilton Head, Beaufort, and surrounding areas

Historic hurricane strikes including Hugo (1989), Matthew (2016), Florence (2018), and others

Significant hurricane risk extending well inland through wind and flooding

This exposure has shaped South Carolina's insurance regulatory framework around specific programs to ensure coastal property owners can obtain coverage.

## The South Carolina Wind and Hail Underwriting Association (SCWHUA)

The South Carolina Wind and Hail Underwriting Association (SCWHUA) — also referred to as the Joint Underwriting Association and known informally as the "wind pool" or "Beach Plan" — is South Carolina's residual market mechanism providing windstorm and hail coverage for properties in coastal areas where private market coverage may be limited or unavailable.

Key SCWHUA features:

Coverage area. SCWHUA writes wind and hail coverage in designated territories within South Carolina's five coastal counties — Beaufort, Charleston, Colleton, Georgetown, and Horry. Territories are defined by specific boundaries.

Coverage scope. SCWHUA provides wind and hail coverage only — not comprehensive homeowners or dwelling coverage. Policyholders typically maintain a standard homeowners policy (which excludes wind in coastal areas) alongside SCWHUA wind coverage.

Eligibility. Properties in designated coastal areas that cannot obtain wind coverage in the private market may obtain SCWHUA coverage.

Premium and claims. SCWHUA operates with its own rate structure, underwriting guidelines, and claims handling. Premiums often exceed standard wind coverage rates due to the high-risk nature of covered properties.

Market mechanism. All insurers writing property insurance in South Carolina participate in supporting SCWHUA, sharing in both premiums and losses.

Understanding how SCWHUA works is essential for agents serving coastal clients.

## The Coastal Insurance Landscape

For South Carolina coastal properties, insurance typically involves multiple coverages:

Standard Homeowners Policy. Covers most perils, but in coastal areas typically excludes wind/hurricane coverage.

Windstorm Coverage. Obtained through SCWHUA or, where available, through private carriers. Covers damage from wind, hurricanes, and related perils.

Flood Insurance. Separate policy through the National Flood Insurance Program (NFIP) or private flood market. Required for mortgaged properties in Special Flood Hazard Areas.

Umbrella/Excess Coverage. For higher-value coastal properties, additional liability and excess property coverage may apply.

Helping clients understand this layered coverage structure is a core service for coastal agents.

## Mandatory Hurricane Deductibles

South Carolina allows insurers to apply hurricane deductibles on coastal properties:

Percentage-based deductibles (often 1%, 2%, 3%, or 5% of dwelling value) apply for losses from named hurricanes rather than fixed dollar amounts

Higher deductibles apply to higher-risk coastal properties

Specific rules govern when hurricane deductibles apply (typically based on named storm warnings/landfall)

Clients should understand their hurricane deductible clearly — it can represent significant out-of-pocket exposure

Producers must disclose hurricane deductibles clearly and ensure clients understand their financial exposure before a storm.

## Wind Mitigation Credits

South Carolina property insurance policies often include wind mitigation credits for properties with protective features:

Impact-resistant windows or storm shutters

Hurricane straps and structural reinforcement

Roof-deck attachment reinforcement

Secondary water resistance

Fortified construction standards

Properties with documented mitigation features may qualify for substantial premium discounts. Agents should help clients:

Understand available mitigation credits

Document existing mitigation features

Consider mitigation improvements that pay back through premium savings

## Pre-Storm and Post-Storm Considerations

Pre-storm obligations:

Insurance cannot be purchased or modified once a hurricane watch or warning is issued (specific timing rules apply)

Clients should review coverage annually, particularly before hurricane season (June 1 - November 30)

Agents should proactively contact coastal clients before major storms approach

Post-storm obligations:

Claims filing procedures and timelines

Coordination of multiple policies (homeowners + wind + flood)

Additional Living Expenses coverage during displacement

Debris removal and contents coverage

## Agent Responsibilities in Coastal Markets

South Carolina coastal insurance requires specific producer attention:

Complete disclosure. Clients must understand what's covered, what's not, deductibles, and coverage limits.

Coverage coordination. Helping clients obtain and coordinate homeowners, wind, and flood coverage appropriately.

Documentation. Thorough records of client conversations, recommendations, and coverage decisions.

Compliance with advertising rules. South Carolina regulates how coastal insurance is marketed, particularly regarding representations about coverage availability and pricing.

Ethical sales practices. High-pressure selling in pre-storm periods is scrutinized heavily. Maintain ethical practices regardless of timing.

## Common Mistakes Coastal Agents Make

Assuming homeowners covers everything. In coastal areas, wind and flood typically require separate coverage. Clients who believe they're fully protected by homeowners alone may face catastrophic uninsured losses.

Not explaining hurricane deductibles. Percentage-based hurricane deductibles create significant out-of-pocket exposure. Clients must understand this before a storm.

Missing flood coverage. Many coastal properties need flood insurance even when not in designated flood zones. Storm surge and heavy rainfall cause flooding in areas not always mapped as high-risk.

Inadequate coverage limits. Coastal rebuilding costs exceed standard inland rebuild estimates. Coverage limits must reflect actual replacement costs.

Poor mitigation documentation. Failing to document qualifying mitigation features means clients pay higher premiums than necessary.

## 5 Frequently Asked Questions

- What is the South Carolina Wind and Hail Underwriting Association (SCWHUA)? SCWHUA — also called the Joint Underwriting Association, and known as the "wind pool" or "Beach Plan" — is South Carolina's residual market for wind and hail coverage in designated coastal areas. It provides wind coverage for properties that cannot obtain it in the private market.
- Do standard homeowners policies cover hurricane damage in South Carolina? In coastal areas, standard homeowners policies typically exclude wind/hurricane damage. Separate wind coverage through SCWHUA or private carriers is typically required.
- What is a hurricane deductible? A percentage-based deductible (often 1-5% of dwelling value) that applies specifically to losses from named hurricanes. It's separate from and typically higher than the standard policy deductible.
- When must flood insurance be in place to cover a hurricane? Flood insurance typically has a 30-day waiting period before coverage begins. You cannot purchase flood insurance as a storm approaches and have it cover that storm's damage.
- What wind mitigation features qualify for premium discounts? Qualifying features typically include impact-resistant windows, storm shutters, hurricane straps, reinforced roof-deck attachment, and secondary water resistance. Documentation is required to obtain credits.
