Virginia's workers' compensation system operates entirely through the competitive private market. There is no state fund, no government insurer of last resort for workers' compensation (beyond the NCCI-administered assigned risk pool), and no monopolistic carrier structure. Every covered Virginia employer obtains workers' compensation insurance from a licensed private carrier or qualifies for self-insurance. This market structure distinguishes Virginia from states like Ohio and Washington, which have exclusive state funds, and makes Virginia workers' compensation a genuinely open commercial insurance market — accessible to P&C producers with the right carrier appointments. This post covers the complete workers' comp framework: who must carry coverage, what the penalties are for non-compliance, and what the Virginia Workers' Compensation Commission does.

## Who Must Carry Workers' Compensation in Virginia

Under Va. Code § 65.2-300 (the Virginia Workers' Compensation Act), employers must carry coverage when they regularly employ more than two employees — meaning coverage is required when total employment reaches three or more workers.

Employee threshold details:

All employees count: Full-time, part-time, seasonal, and temporary workers all count toward the three-employee threshold

Subcontractors count: If a business hires subcontractors to perform the same trade, business, or occupation as the employer's work, the subcontractor's employees are included in the employee count — even if those subcontractors carry their own coverage

Executive officers count: Corporate officers count as employees for threshold purposes, though they may individually elect to reject coverage

LLC managers count: Managers of LLCs are treated as employees under the Virginia Workers' Compensation Act; they may elect to reject coverage by filing a Rejection of Coverage form with the Virginia Workers' Compensation Commission (VWC)

Agricultural employers: Must cover employees who regularly employ more than three full-time workers; seasonal/migrant workers count only if employed more than 30 days in a calendar year

Domestic workers in private homes: Exempt — households need not cover domestic workers regardless of number employed

Sole proprietors with no employees: Not required to carry workers' comp — but the moment they hire anyone (including a subcontractor), the rules change

## The Virginia Workers' Compensation Commission (VWC)

The Virginia Workers' Compensation Commission is an administrative court that adjudicates workers' compensation claims. It is not the SCC Bureau of Insurance — these are two entirely separate agencies with different functions:

VWC: Administers and adjudicates workers' comp claims; located at workcomp.virginia.gov; handles claim disputes, benefit determinations, and compliance enforcement

SCC Bureau of Insurance: Regulates the insurance companies writing workers' comp coverage; handles rate filings, policy form approvals, carrier compliance

A Virginia P&C producer dealing with workers' comp insurance contracts with the Bureau of Insurance for carrier oversight matters and directs claim disputes to the VWC.

## Virginia Has No State Fund

Virginia does not have a state-operated workers' compensation insurance fund. Employers obtain coverage through:

Licensed private carriers — the most common route; standard market workers' comp from licensed insurers

Self-insurance — available to larger employers who demonstrate financial ability to self-fund claims; requires VWC approval

Group self-insurance associations — industry groups pool risk; requires VWC and Bureau approval

Assigned risk (NCCI pool) — employers who cannot obtain voluntary market coverage can access coverage through the National Council on Compensation Insurance (NCCI) assigned risk pool; phone: 1-800-622-4123

Important for producers: Monopolistic state funds from other states (Ohio, North Dakota, Washington, Wyoming) cannot provide Virginia workers' comp coverage. If an out-of-state employer's workers' comp policy is with a carrier not licensed in Virginia, that policy cannot be endorsed to cover Virginia operations. A Virginia-licensed policy with a Virginia-approved carrier is required for Virginia-based employees.

## Penalties for Non-Compliance

Virginia's penalties for operating without required workers' compensation coverage are among the most significant compliance risks for employers:

The Uninsured Employers' Fund (UEF), administered by the VWC, can pay benefits to injured workers whose employers were uninsured — and the fund then pursues reimbursement from the uninsured employer.

## Virginia Workers' Compensation Benefits

The Virginia Workers' Compensation Act provides four categories of benefits:

Medical benefits: All reasonable and necessary medical treatment for the compensable injury — 100% covered with no deductible.

Disability income:

Temporary Total Disability (TTD): 66⅔% of average weekly wage for up to 500 weeks

Permanent Total Disability: 66⅔% of average weekly wage for lifetime

Permanent Partial Disability (PPD): Compensation for specific body part losses per a statutory schedule

Temporary Partial Disability: Compensation for reduced earning capacity

Vocational rehabilitation: If the injured worker cannot return to their original job, the Act provides vocational rehabilitation benefits.

Death benefits: $10,000 funeral expenses plus 66⅔% of decedent's average weekly wage to eligible dependents.

## How Premium Is Calculated

Virginia workers' compensation premium is calculated using three factors:

Classification code (NCCI risk classification by industry/occupation type)

Payroll (premium is per $100 of payroll)

Experience modifier (based on the employer's own claims history relative to expected claims for their class)

The Bureau of Insurance regulates the filing and approval of workers' comp rates and forms. NCCI files rate recommendations for Virginia, and the Bureau reviews and approves them.

## Frequently Asked Questions

What should a Virginia producer do if a small business client asks whether they need workers' compensation?

The threshold question is employee count: does the business regularly employ three or more workers (including subcontractors performing similar work)? If yes, workers' compensation is required. If the answer is borderline — for example, the employer uses 1099 contractors extensively — the producer should explain that Virginia applies a common-law test to determine whether a worker is truly an independent contractor or is legally an employee. The test looks at whether the employer controls the means and method of the worker's activities. Misclassifying employees as contractors to avoid workers' comp exposure is a significant compliance risk. The producer's role is to ensure the client understands the threshold and the classification rules — not to provide legal advice on specific worker classifications. When in doubt, the client should consult with a Virginia employment attorney.

How does the assigned risk pool work for Virginia employers who cannot find voluntary market coverage?

The NCCI Assigned Risk Plan (also called the residual or involuntary market) provides workers' comp coverage to Virginia employers who cannot obtain it in the voluntary market due to their risk profile, claims history, or industry type. The NCCI's Virginia Assigned Risk plan is accessed by contacting NCCI at 1-800-622-4123. Assigned risk coverage is typically more expensive than voluntary market coverage because it is serving employers that the voluntary market has declined. Producers serving high-hazard industries or employers with significant claims history should be familiar with the assigned risk option as a last resort for clients who cannot otherwise secure compliance.

Can Virginia executive officers opt out of workers' compensation coverage?

Yes. Corporate officers of Virginia corporations may individually elect to reject workers' compensation coverage by filing a Rejection of Coverage form with the Virginia Workers' Compensation Commission. The business must have valid coverage in place, and the officer must file the rejection form with both the VWC and the insurer. Once the rejection is accepted, the officer is not covered under the workers' comp policy for their own injuries. Importantly, rejecting coverage does not remove the officer from the employee count for threshold purposes — the business still needs coverage for all other employees. LLC managers may also elect to reject coverage through a similar process. Executive officers who reject coverage should be advised that they bear personal injury risk that workers' comp would otherwise cover.

Does Virginia's workers' compensation exclusive remedy doctrine protect employers from civil lawsuits by injured employees?

Yes — the exclusive remedy doctrine means that workers' compensation is the exclusive legal remedy for an employee's workplace injury against the employer. An injured employee covered under a valid workers' comp policy cannot sue the employer in civil court for the same workplace injury (with narrow exceptions). However, the exclusive remedy protection applies only to insured employers — an employer who is required to carry workers' comp but fails to do so loses the exclusive remedy protection. Uninsured employers can be sued in civil court by injured workers for the full range of tort damages, including pain and suffering, which workers' comp benefits do not cover. This is a powerful incentive for employer compliance beyond the direct civil penalties.

How should a Virginia producer advise a client contractor about workers' comp requirements for their subcontractors?

Virginia's workers' comp law requires contractors to count subcontractors' employees in their own employee threshold if the subcontractors are performing the same trade, business, or occupation as the contractor. This means a general contractor with 2 direct employees who hires a subcontractor with 5 workers has a total of 7 employees for threshold purposes — and must carry workers' comp for all of them. Additionally, the contractor's insurer can charge premium for any subcontractor payroll, even sole proprietor subcontractors with no employees. The best practice: require all subcontractors to provide certificates of workers' comp insurance as a condition of engagement, and maintain those certificates for audit purposes. Contractors who fail to obtain certificates may find their workers' comp premium audit retroactively increased to include uninsured subcontractor payroll.

Virginia's workers' comp framework — no state fund, competitive private market, firm employer threshold, and severe non-compliance penalties — creates clear commercial insurance opportunities for producers who serve the Commonwealth's business community. Understanding the VWC, the NCCI assigned risk pool, and the coverage requirements for contractors and their subcontractors is the foundation for advising Virginia employers accurately.
