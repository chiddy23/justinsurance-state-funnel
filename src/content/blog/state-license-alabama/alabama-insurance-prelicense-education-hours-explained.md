Here's a unique situation: As of January 1, 2024, Alabama no longer requires [prelicense](/alabama/prelicensing) education hours for insurance license applicants. Under Alabama Act No. 2023-104, the state removed the formal prelicense education requirement.

But that doesn't mean prelicense education is useless — in fact, for most candidates, it's still essential to passing the exam. Here's the full picture of Alabama's current prelicense education landscape.

## The Big Change: Alabama Act No. 2023-104

Effective January 1, 2024, Alabama Act No. 2023-104 amended Alabama's insurance licensing framework to remove the mandatory prelicense education requirement. The ALDOI adopted corresponding amendments to Insurance Regulation No. 147 to implement this change.

Before this change, Alabama required specific prelicense education hours by license type. After the change, the state no longer regulates prelicense courses for applicants.

## What This Means Practically

You can now schedule your Alabama licensing exam without completing prelicense education first. You still need to pass the exam, complete fingerprinting, and submit a complete application — but prelicense education is no longer a state-mandated step.

The University of Alabama (which administers Alabama's insurance exams) reflects this — you don't need to present a prelicense certificate to sit for the exam.

## Why Prelicense Education Is Still Recommended

Despite not being required, prelicense education remains strongly recommended for most candidates. Here's why:

Alabama's exams are challenging. The Life and Health combined exam is 150 questions over 3 hours. The Property and Casualty combined exam has similar rigor. Passing rates for candidates who skip prelicense preparation are significantly lower than for those who complete it.

The 70% passing score is strict. There's no curve, no partial credit, no adjustments. You either hit 70% or you fail. Preparation matters.

Content coverage is comprehensive. Life insurance, annuities, health insurance, Medicare, disability, long-term care, federal laws, Alabama-specific laws — the range of topics tested requires structured study.

Poor exam performance costs time and money. Each failed attempt requires a new registration fee. Failing twice costs more than a quality prelicense course.

Your career depends on actual knowledge. Passing the exam is the minimum. Understanding the material prepares you to actually help clients and build a sustainable practice.

## How Long Prelicense Study Takes

Without a mandatory hour requirement, study time varies based on:

Your prior background. Candidates with insurance, finance, or related experience often move through material faster than those with no background.

Your study pace. Some candidates spend 40-60 hours preparing thoroughly. Others dedicate less time.

The format. Online self-paced allows faster completion than scheduled classroom sessions.

The exam you're taking. Combined Life & Health has more content than Life-only or Health-only.

Most serious candidates invest 30-60 hours of preparation before attempting their Alabama exam.

## Alternative Preparation Paths

If prelicense courses aren't your preference, alternatives include:

Exam prep courses. More focused on passing the exam specifically, less comprehensive than full prelicense courses.

Self-study with manuals and practice tests. Cheaper but requires more self-direction.

Mentor-guided preparation. If you're starting with an agency, they may provide training and guidance.

Classroom instruction. Some schools still offer in-person prelicense-style courses.

Regardless of path, your preparation should include extensive practice questions in Alabama's exam format.

Build familiarity with exam format

Calibrate your readiness

Build test-taking stamina

Most successful candidates take multiple full-length practice exams before their real exam, aiming for consistent 80%+ practice scores.

## For Variable Life and Variable Annuity

Alabama has a specific exception for Variable Life and Variable Annuity Products. Candidates for this line of authority are:

Exempt from both prelicensing courses and state examinations

Must already hold a current Life line of authority

Must have passed securities Series 6 or Series 7

Must have passed securities Series 63 or Series 66

Must be licensed with a registered broker-dealer through the Alabama Securities Commission

Candidates provide their CRD number to verify securities credentials during the application process.
