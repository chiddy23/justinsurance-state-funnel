If you're a licensed insurance producer in Kentucky approaching your renewal deadline — or you're newly licensed and want to understand what's ahead — this guide covers everything you need to know about the state's [continuing education](/kentucky/continuing-education) requirements. The Kentucky Department of Insurance (KDOI) requires all active producers to complete 24 hours of approved CE every two years, including a mandatory ethics component. Missing the deadline costs you money and can put your license at risk. Here is exactly what you need to do, when you need to do it, and how to do it without surprises.

## The Basic CE Requirement

Every Kentucky resident insurance producer must complete 24 hours of approved continuing education during each two-year renewal period. Of those 24 hours, at least 3 must be devoted to an approved ethics course. This 24/3 structure is consistent with most comparison states in the region. Indiana requires 24 hours including 3 ethics. Wisconsin matches at 24 hours/3 ethics. Missouri is the lightest CE state in the region at 16 hours/3 ethics — a meaningful contrast for producers who hold licenses in multiple states.

The 24-hour Kentucky requirement applies regardless of how many lines of authority you hold. A producer with Life, Accident & Health, and Property & Casualty licenses does not need 72 hours — 24 total hours including 3 ethics satisfies the obligation across all lines for a given renewal period. That is one of the more producer-friendly aspects of Kentucky's CE structure.

## Renewal Period and Deadline

Kentucky insurance producer licenses expire on the last day of the licensee's birth month, every two years. You may begin the renewal process up to 90 days before your expiration date. All required CE must be completed before you submit your renewal application — the KDOI does not accept renewal applications with outstanding CE hours.

If you miss your renewal deadline, a late renewal fee of $80 applies — double the standard $40 renewal fee. Contact the KDOI directly at (502) 564-3630 if your license lapses significantly beyond the deadline to understand your reinstatement options. Do not assume the problem resolves itself. The longer you wait, the more complex the reinstatement process becomes.

## How CE Hours Are Reported

Approved CE providers report course completions to the KDOI electronically. As of July 1, 2024, PSI Services LLC manages CE provider approvals and course oversight for Kentucky, having taken over that function from Prometric. In most cases you do not need to manually submit CE certificates — your provider's electronic reporting satisfies the requirement. However, it is always your responsibility as the licensee to verify that your CE credits are reflected correctly in your eServices account before submitting your renewal. Providers are reliable, but discrepancies happen and they are yours to resolve.

## Specialty Training Requirements That Count Toward CE

One of the most practical aspects of Kentucky's CE structure is that required specialty product training counts toward your 24-hour total. This means you are satisfying multiple regulatory obligations with the same study time rather than stacking requirements on top of each other.

The 4-hour Annuity Best Interest training under 806 KAR 12:120 is a one-time requirement for agents who sell annuities and counts toward CE in the renewal period in which it is completed. For long-term care insurance, 806 KAR 9:025 requires an 8-hour initial LTC training course before your first LTC sale, followed by 4 hours of ongoing LTC training in each 2-year renewal period — both are approved CE courses and credit toward the 24-hour total. Do not confuse the ongoing 4 hours with the one-time 8-hour certification; agents who complete only the shorter course are not authorized to sell LTC. For NFIP flood insurance, the standard 3-hour one-time course is similarly creditable toward your CE requirement.

## Non-Resident Producer CE Exemption

Non-resident producers licensed in Kentucky are generally exempt from Kentucky's CE requirements as long as they maintain their CE obligations in their home state and their home state's requirements are substantially similar to Kentucky's. If you hold a non-resident Kentucky license, verify this exemption applies to your specific situation by checking with the KDOI or your home state regulator before assuming you have no Kentucky CE obligation.

## CE Comparison: Kentucky vs. Neighboring States

Wisconsin adds a $1.00-per-credit-hour reporting fee that Kentucky does not impose, making Kentucky's CE process slightly more cost-efficient among comparison states.

## The Mistake Most Kentucky Agents Make With CE

The most common CE mistake is waiting until the month before renewal to complete all 24 hours. Last-minute course completion creates real risk — provider reporting can be delayed, discrepancies in your eServices record take time to resolve, and a single administrative hiccup between your completion date and your renewal deadline can result in an $80 late fee or a lapsed license. Build your CE hours throughout the renewal period. Eight hours per year is a far more manageable pace than 24 hours in the final weeks before your birthday month deadline. The total is the same regardless of how many lines of authority you hold — 24 hours covers all lines for the renewal period.
