Arizona's exam requires a study plan built around its distinctive structure: no PLE requirement (but strong prep still matters), a 70% passing score on an exam that tests both national insurance content and Arizona-specific law, a four-attempt annual limit, and a September 2025 vendor transition that means existing accounts don't transfer to PSI. Whether you're pursuing Life and A&H for financial services practice, Property and Casualty for personal and commercial lines, or all four major lines for maximum market access, the plan below aligns with how Arizona's exam actually works.

Here's a comprehensive study plan for the Arizona insurance license exam.

## How Long Should Your Study Plan Be

Without a PLE requirement, study duration is entirely self-directed. Most successful Arizona candidates spend:

Life only or A&H only:

No prior insurance experience: 3-4 weeks

Some insurance/finance background: 2-3 weeks

Life and A&H (separate exams — recommended):

Life first, then A&H: 6-8 weeks total

Allow more time for A&H than Life (consistently harder)

Combined L&H (one exam):

No prior experience: 4-6 weeks

Some background: 3-4 weeks

Property only or Casualty only:

No prior experience: 3-4 weeks

Casualty typically takes more time than Property (more abstract liability concepts)

Combined P&C:

No prior experience: 5-7 weeks

Some experience: 3-5 weeks

All four major lines (pursuing both L&H and P&C):

Sequential approach: 10-14 weeks

Better to complete and license one combination before starting the other

## Phased Study Plan Structure

Phase 1: Content Acquisition (60% of study time)

Build foundational knowledge through systematic study. For each exam:

Work through all major content areas systematically

Take chapter or section quizzes after each major topic

Do not rush to practice exams before covering all content

Phase 2: Arizona State Law Focus (25% of study time)

Dedicated state law study — proportionally more than most states require:

Study A.R.S. Title 20 provisions relevant to your line

Memorize specific Arizona numbers (Guaranty Association limits, free-look periods, CE hours)

Practice Arizona-specific scenario questions

Review DIFI authority structure and enforcement tools

Phase 3: [Practice Exam](/arizona/practice-exam) Intensive (15% of study time)

Full-length practice exams under timed conditions:

At least 3-4 full-length practice exams per line

Simulate the full exam, tracking your general-content and Arizona state law performance separately

Target 80%+ overall — with strong Arizona state law performance — before scheduling the real exam

## Arizona State Law Study Allocation

Because the Arizona state law content is heavily tested and commonly under-prepared, it deserves dedicated, proportionally large study time:

For Life or A&H candidates: 25-30% of total study time on Arizona state law For P&C candidates: 25-30% of total study time on Arizona state law

Arizona-specific numbers to memorize (test yourself until automatic):

Arizona Life and Disability Insurance Guaranty Fund limits (A.R.S. § 20-682): $300,000 in life insurance death benefits, but no more than $100,000 in net cash surrender and withdrawal values; $250,000 in the present value of annuity benefits; $300,000 for disability income; $300,000 for long-term care; $500,000 for health benefit plans — with a $300,000 aggregate cap per individual, except $500,000 where health benefit plans are involved

Annuity free look (A.R.S. § 20-1233): 10 days generally, 30 days if the contract holder is 65 or older on the date of application

Exam rules: 70% to pass, four attempts per line of authority in any 12-month period, and you must apply for your license within one year of passing (A.R.S. § 20-284)

CE: 48 hours every 4 years, including 6 hours of ethics, with no carry-forward

Product-specific training: 4 hours of Arizona-approved annuity training before selling annuities; 8 hours of initial long-term care training plus 4 hours every two years

Arizona auto liability minimums (A.R.S. § 28-4009): 25/50/15

These figures should be automatic — exam questions ask for them directly, with no ability to calculate or reason through the answer.

## Practice Question Volume

Minimum recommended per line: 400-500 practice questions Solid preparation per line: 600-800 practice questions Strong preparation (especially A&H): 800-1,000+ practice questions

Quality over quantity: Each wrong answer requires careful review. Understanding why the correct answer is correct (not just what it is) builds the knowledge depth the Arizona state law content demands.

Arizona-specific practice questions: At least 25-30% of your practice question volume should specifically test Arizona state law content. Generic national question banks are insufficient on their own.

## Practice Exam Strategy for Arizona

Take at least 3-4 full-length practice exams per line before scheduling your real exam.

Track your general and state-law performance separately. Most practice exams produce a single combined score. Before using a practice exam, identify which questions come from general content vs. Arizona state law, and track your performance on each so you can catch a weak state-law area early.

Score interpretation for Arizona:

Either content area below 70% on practice: Not ready — more preparation needed

Both areas 70-75%: Marginal — additional practice strongly recommended

Both areas 76-84%: Good readiness — likely to pass

Both areas 85%+: Strong readiness — very likely to pass

Target 80%+ on both your general and Arizona state law practice performance before scheduling. The 10-point buffer above the 70% passing score accounts for exam-day variance.

## Common Arizona Exam Mistakes

Treating Arizona state law as a minor supplement. The Arizona-specific content is heavily tested and can make or break your overall score. Inadequate state law preparation is the #1 cause of preventable Arizona exam failures.

Using only generic national study materials. National materials don't cover Arizona Guaranty Association limits ($300K/$250K/$500K), Arizona free-look periods (10 and 30 days), DIFI's specific authority structure, or Arizona's 48-hour/4-year CE framework.

Rushing to the exam after two weeks of minimal study. Arizona's 4-attempt annual limit means each attempt matters. Adequate preparation before each attempt is always worth it.

Not knowing the specific numbers. Arizona state law questions ask for exact figures. "$250,000 for annuity values" requires knowing that specific limit — there's no calculation pathway.

Scheduling a retake without using PSI diagnostic results. After a failure, PSI's score report breaks your performance down by content area. If your Arizona state law score was weak — say 58% on that content — the retake calls for focused state law study, not general content review.

Not accounting for Arizona's vendor change. PSI replaced the previous Arizona exam vendor on September 3, 2025. Old portal accounts don't transfer. Start fresh at test-takers.psiexams.com/anzins.

## Timing Your Exam Schedule

Apply through NIPR first. PSI verifies NIPR applications before allowing exam scheduling. Have your NIPR application submitted before scheduling.

Schedule when: ✅ Practice exam scores consistently 80%+ on both general and Arizona state law content ✅ Arizona-specific numbers are automatic (no hesitation) ✅ You've completed 3-4 full-length practice exams ✅ You've specifically reviewed all Arizona state law content areas

Don't schedule when: ❌ Either content area consistently below 75% on practice ❌ Arizona Guaranty Association limits or free-look periods are uncertain ❌ You haven't completed full-length timed practice exams

Exam validity: You must apply for your license within 1 year of passing. Don't schedule so far in advance that you risk the window expiring during the application/fingerprint process.

## 5 Frequently Asked Questions

- How long should I study for the Arizona insurance exam without prior experience? 3-4 weeks for single-line exams (Life, A&H, Property, Casualty). Allow 6-8 weeks for Life then A&H separately, or 5-7 weeks for combined P&C. A&H consistently requires more preparation time than Life.
- How should I split my study time between general and state law content? Approximately 70% general content / 30% Arizona state law — but with equal performance targets. Both content areas are tested on the same 70% exam, so whichever one is weaker deserves more time regardless of the general allocation.
- How many practice questions should I complete per line? Minimum 400-500 per line, with 600-800 for solid preparation and 800-1,000+ for A&H specifically. Quality matters as much as quantity — review every wrong answer carefully.
- What practice exam score should I target before scheduling? 80%+ overall, and 80%+ on your Arizona state law practice questions specifically. The 10-point buffer above the 70% passing score provides meaningful protection against exam-day variance.
- Should I study for Life and A&H simultaneously or sequentially? Sequential is recommended for most candidates. Pass Life first (lower content volume, generally considered more accessible), then give A&H focused attention (more complex health/disability content requiring deeper study).
