Virginia's 65/20 CE exemption is a grandfather rule, and nothing more. The exemption was repealed effective January 1, 2019. If you had already applied for and been granted the permanent exemption by December 31, 2018, you keep it — subject to strict continuity requirements that void it permanently if your license lapses. If you reach age 65 with 20 years of continuous licensure after that date, there is no exemption available to you, and you owe your full 16 or 24 hours of CE every biennium like every other Virginia resident agent. This post explains exactly who still holds the exemption, what it provides, and what destroys it.

Be careful with what you read elsewhere on this subject. A great deal of insurance CE marketing still describes the 65/20 exemption in the present tense, as though a producer turning 65 today can claim it at renewal. That description is of a statute that no longer exists.

## The 65/20 Rule: One Surviving Category

Va. Code § 38.2-1871(B) lists the licensees exempt from Virginia's CE requirement. The only age-and-tenure entry in that list is agents "who have applied for and received a permanent exemption from the continuing education course requirements set forth in this article by December 31, 2018." There is no second path, no ongoing eligibility test, and no renewal-by-renewal evaluation. Either the exemption was granted to you on or before that date, or you do not have one.

If you hold the grandfathered exemption, it remains in force as long as:

You continue to submit a renewal application and pay the renewal fee on time

Your license is never allowed to lapse

You do not fail to renew or reinstate within 12 months of the renewal date

### The qualifying tests that used to apply

The pre-2019 statute granted the permanent exemption on either of two showings. These are worth knowing only because they explain why you were granted the exemption, and because you may be asked to document that history — they cannot be used to obtain an exemption now:

Criterion A: A resident agent at least 65 years old who had held any combination of resident or nonresident Virginia licenses of equivalent type continuously for at least 20 years.

Criterion B: A resident agent at least 65 years old who had held a Virginia resident license continuously for the preceding 4 years and could furnish proof of equivalent license authority held continuously in other states for an additional 16 years — 20 years of continuous licensure in any combination.

Both tests required continuous licensure with no gaps. That continuity requirement did not end with the grant: a lapse today still eliminates a grandfathered exemption, and it cannot be recovered.

## What the Exemption Provides

Producers who hold the grandfathered exemption are exempt from completing CE hours as a condition of license renewal. They must still:

Submit a renewal application through NIPR or Sircon by their renewal deadline (end of [birth month in their odd/even year](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title38.2/chapter18/section38.2-1825.1/))

Pay the [$10 per-line renewal fee](https://www.scc.virginia.gov/regulated-industries/bureau-of-insurance/current-agents-agencies/license-renewals/)

The CE exemption eliminates the 16 or 24-hour CE obligation. It does not eliminate the renewal fee or the renewal application requirement.

## The 2019 Change: What It Means If You Are Turning 65 Now

The Virginia General Assembly repealed the 65/20 CE exemption effective January 1, 2019, with the exemptions already granted preserved on a grandfathered basis. There is no replacement, no successor exemption, and no "evaluated at each renewal" version of the rule.

Practically: if you reach age 65 after December 31, 2018 — no matter how many decades you have been continuously licensed — you are not exempt. You complete the same 16 or 24 credit hours every biennium as every other Virginia resident agent, including the 3 hours of ethics. Under [Va. Code § 38.2-1866](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacodefull/title38.2/chapter18/article7/), a producer holding a Life & Annuities license, a Health license, or both completes 16 hours; a producer holding licenses in two or more categories completes 24 hours with at least 8 hours applicable to each category held.

Get this wrong and there is no soft landing. Virginia has no CE grace period — the license expires on the renewal date. A 65-year-old producer who assumes the old exemption still applies and skips his hours does not get a warning letter; his renewal is refused and he cannot legally transact insurance until he completes the CE and reinstates.

## How Licensure Continuity Is Measured

For agents who hold the grandfathered exemption, "continuously" means without any gap in licensure. The 20 years counted toward the original grant could be assembled across:

Virginia resident licenses

Virginia nonresident licenses of equivalent type (Criterion A)

Licenses held in other states, for up to 16 of the 20 years (Criterion B)

So an agent who held a Life & Annuities license in Maryland for 16 years, relocated to Virginia, and had held a Virginia resident Life & Annuities license for 4 consecutive years by the time he turned 65 would have satisfied Criterion B — provided he applied for and received the exemption on or before December 31, 2018. The same fact pattern arising today produces no exemption at all.

## Specialty Training Requirements: Not Exempt

CE exemption does not eliminate specialty training obligations. A producer who holds the grandfathered 65/20 CE exemption and sells LTC Partnership policies must still complete the initial 8-hour LTC Partnership training and the ongoing 4-hour training every 24 months. The annuity best interest one-time training and the one-time NFIP flood training similarly apply to exempt producers who sell those products. The CE exemption covers only the general biennial CE hour requirement — specialty product training requirements are separate obligations rooted in product authorization, not CE compliance.

## Limited Lines License Holders

Virginia producers who hold only limited lines licenses (credit life/health, travel accident, travel baggage insurance) are also exempt from CE requirements. Limited lines are narrow product authorizations that do not require CE compliance under Virginia's current framework.

## Frequently Asked Questions

If I received a permanent CE exemption before 2019 and my license recently lapsed, can I reinstate and reclaim the exemption?

No. The grandfathered permanent exemption continues only while you renew, and it is no longer valid if you fail to renew or [reinstate within 12 months of the renewal date](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title38.2/chapter18/section38.2-1825.1/). Once that window closes the exemption is gone for good — and because the 65/20 rule was repealed effective January 1, 2019, there is no path to earning a new one. You would be reinstating into the full 16- or 24-hour CE obligation. Even inside the 12-month reinstatement window, a lapse puts the exemption at risk; confirm your status with the Bureau of Insurance at AgentLicensing@scc.virginia.gov before you file the reinstatement.

I turn 65 next year and I have been licensed in Virginia for 30 years. Do I qualify for the exemption?

No. This is the single most common misunderstanding about the 65/20 rule, and it is the one that costs licenses. The exemption had to have been applied for and granted by December 31, 2018. Age and tenure reached after that date confer nothing, regardless of how far past 65 and 20 years you are. Plan on your full CE — 16 hours if you hold Life & Annuities and/or Health, 24 hours with at least 8 per category if you hold two or more license categories, including 3 hours of ethics either way — and complete it well before your renewal date, because Virginia grants no CE grace period.

Does the CE exemption apply to non-resident Virginia license holders?

Non-resident producers are generally not required to complete Virginia CE regardless of age or tenure — Virginia accepts CE compliance from the producer's home state for non-resident license holders. The 65/20 exemption is specifically a resident producer exemption. Non-resident producers do not need to claim or qualify for the 65/20 exemption because the non-resident CE waiver applies across the board. The relevant non-resident CE obligation is compliance with their home state's CE requirements.

How does an exempt producer handle the CE certification at renewal?

If you already hold the grandfathered exemption, your renewal is a renewal application and a fee — the Bureau's record already reflects your exempt status, and the exemption continues so long as you renew on time. What you should not do is treat the exemption as something to newly claim: no first-time exemption has been available since January 1, 2019, and certifying CE compliance you do not have is a licensing problem, not a paperwork one. If you are unsure whether the Bureau shows you as exempt, ask before you renew — contact the Bureau of Insurance at AgentLicensing@scc.virginia.gov rather than guessing at the renewal screen.

Can a producer who qualifies for the CE exemption choose to take CE courses anyway?

Yes. The exemption is a permission, not a prohibition. An exempt producer who wants to stay current with industry changes, complete specialty training, or maintain professional development can take CE courses and have them credited to their record, even though the credits are not required for renewal. Virginia does let a non-exempt agent carry excess CE credit hours forward to the immediately following biennium ([Va. Code § 38.2-1867(D)](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacodefull/title38.2/chapter18/article7/)), but that carryover only helps a producer who actually owes CE. A grandfathered-exempt producer owes none, so voluntary courses don't offset any future requirement — the educational value and regulatory currency the courses provide are independent of the exemption status.

The 65/20 CE exemption is a closed class. For the shrinking group of Virginia producers who were granted it by December 31, 2018, it is a real benefit that requires careful maintenance — timely renewal, unbroken licensure, and ongoing specialty training compliance for applicable products, since a single lapse permanently voids an exemption that may represent decades of earned tenure. For everyone else, including producers reaching 65 with 20 years of service today, the honest answer is that the exemption is gone and the CE is owed.
