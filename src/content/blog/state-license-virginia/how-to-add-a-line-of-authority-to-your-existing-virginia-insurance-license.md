If you already hold a Virginia insurance producer license and want to expand what you can sell — adding Property & Casualty to an existing Life & Health license, or adding Health to a Life & Annuities license — you are adding a line of authority. Virginia's process for adding a line follows the same general framework as initial licensing, with a few important distinctions around fingerprinting, application fees, and the CE implications of expanding your license portfolio. Here is exactly how the process works and what changes when you already hold a license.

### What Is a Line of Authority?

Virginia issues producer licenses by line of authority — the specific type of insurance you are authorized to sell. The major lines requiring a Prometric exam are:

Life and Annuities

Accident and Health or Sickness

Property

Casualty

Personal Lines

Title (requires 16-hour prelicensing course)

Each line is a separate qualification on your license. You can hold one, several, or all of them. Adding a line means obtaining authorization to transact an additional insurance type you do not currently hold.

### Step 1: Pass the Prometric Exam for the New Line

You must pass the Prometric licensing exam for the new line of authority before applying to add it. There is no shortcut or waiver for this requirement (unless an exemption applies — see below).

Register with Prometric at prometric.com/virginia/insurance or call (866) 891-6396. The exam fee is $35 per exam. Your score is valid for 183 days — you must submit your add-line application within that window.

Exemption — Variable Products: The Variable Life and Variable Annuity Products line of authority does not require a Prometric exam. Instead, you must: already hold and maintain an active Life line of authority, have passed the FINRA Series 6 or Series 7 securities exams and the Series 63 or Series 66 exams, and be actively licensed with a registered broker-dealer under the Virginia Securities Commission. The application requires your CRD number to verify securities exam completion.

### Step 2: Determine Whether New Fingerprints Are Required

This is the step that most existing licensees get wrong.

You do NOT need new fingerprints if:

You have submitted fingerprints to Fieldprint in the last 12 months, AND

You are currently licensed in Virginia, AND

You are applying for the same license type you already hold

You DO need new fingerprints if:

Your prior fingerprints are more than 12 months old, OR

You are applying for a different license type than you currently hold

The definition of "same license type" versus "different license type" is critical here. Life & Annuities and Health are considered the same license type for this purpose — if you hold a Life & Annuities license and are adding Health, you are adding a new line of authority within the same license type. Property and Casualty are typically considered the same license type together. But if you hold a Life & Health license and want to add Property & Casualty, you are adding a different license type — new fingerprints are required regardless of when you last submitted.

If in doubt, contact the Bureau of Insurance at AgentLicensing@scc.virginia.gov before scheduling your Fieldprint appointment.

Fieldprint Code (if required): FPVABOIProducer at fieldprintvirginia.com. Cost: $34.95.

### Step 3: Apply Through NIPR or Sircon

Submit your add-line application electronically through NIPR (nipr.com) or Sircon (sircon.com/virginia). The fee is $15 per new line of authority being added, plus the $5.60 NIPR/Sircon processing fee per session.

Attach your CHRR (Criminal History Record Report from the Virginia State Police, no more than 90 days old) and any required disclosure documents for new or changed background information. If your background has not changed since your initial license application and you have been continuously licensed, the Bureau may not require a new CHRR — but confirm this before submitting. The safest approach is to obtain a fresh CHRR for any new application submission.

Applications are processed in date order. Allow approximately 15 business days.

### CE Impact of Adding a Line

Adding a line of authority in Virginia has a direct impact on your continuing education requirements going forward. This is not just an administrative note — it changes your CE obligation for every renewal cycle after the new line is added.

Single license type: 16 CE hours biennial (3 ethics hours required) Two or more license types: 24 CE hours biennial (3 ethics hours required, minimum 8 hours per line type held)

Life, Annuities, and Health are treated as a single license type for CE purposes. If you hold Life & Annuities and add Health (or vice versa), you remain at 16 hours biennial.

If you hold a Life & Health license and add Property & Casualty (or any P&C line), you now hold two license types — your CE obligation increases from 16 to 24 hours biennial, with a minimum of 8 hours in each license type.

Plan for this change before adding a line. Adding P&C to an existing L&H license is valuable for cross-selling but adds 8 CE hours per cycle and requires CE in a second subject area.

### Frequently Asked Questions

Can I add a line of authority at any time, or only at renewal?

You can apply to add a line of authority at any time — you do not need to wait for your renewal period. Pass the Prometric exam for the new line, complete any required fingerprinting, and submit your add-line application through NIPR or Sircon. The $15 per-line fee and the 183-day exam score validity window apply the same way as an initial application. There is no procedural advantage to waiting for your renewal date, and there is a meaningful disadvantage: if your exam score expires (183 days), you must retake the exam. Add your new line as soon as you are ready.

If I already hold a Life & Annuities license and want to add Health, do I need a new fingerprint check?

It depends on when you last submitted fingerprints. If your fingerprints were submitted within the last 12 months and you are currently licensed, and Life & Annuities and Health are considered the same license type under Virginia's rules, you may not need new fingerprints. Virginia treats Life, Annuities, and Health as a combined license category for some purposes. However, the safest practice is to confirm with the Bureau of Insurance at AgentLicensing@scc.virginia.gov before scheduling a Fieldprint appointment. The Bureau can tell you definitively whether new fingerprints are required for your specific situation. Do not assume — the $34.95 fingerprinting fee and the timing implications of a new fingerprint window make it worth one confirmation call or email.

What is the Personal Lines line of authority, and who should add it?

The Personal Lines line of authority in Virginia allows you to sell personal (non-commercial) Property and Casualty insurance products — primarily personal auto, homeowners, renters, and similar individual risk coverage. It is a narrower authorization than holding both Property and Casualty separately. Producers who want to sell only personal auto and homeowners — without commercial lines authority — can hold Personal Lines rather than the full Property and Casualty lines. However, most producers who intend to build a comprehensive P&C practice take the full Property and Casualty exam rather than the Personal Lines exam, because the commercial lines authority opens access to a much larger market. Personal Lines is appropriate for producers focused exclusively on personal insurance who want a more targeted (and slightly narrower) exam scope.

Does adding a line of authority restart my license's renewal cycle?

No. Adding a line of authority does not reset your existing license's renewal date. All lines on your Virginia producer license renew on the same statewide deadline — November 30 on even years. Your CE compliance must cover all lines you hold by that renewal date. The new line you add will be included in your next renewal cycle — if you add Property & Casualty in March 2025, it renews along with all your other lines on November 30, 2026. You may need to complete CE for the new line in a partial biennium if you add it close to the renewal date, so add lines early in the renewal cycle if possible.

Can I hold a Virginia insurance license without any active carrier appointments?

Yes. Your Virginia producer license is independent of your carrier appointments. You can hold an active license — including all the lines you are authorized for — without having any insurer appointments on file. An appointment is required before you can transact insurance on behalf of a specific carrier, but the license itself does not require any appointments to remain active. Producers who are between agencies, building an independent practice, or studying for additional lines routinely maintain active licenses without appointments. The Bureau of Insurance does not require appointment activity as a condition of license renewal.

Adding a line of authority in Virginia is a straightforward extension of the initial licensing process — same exam, same Fieldprint requirements (when applicable), same application portal. The one additional consideration is your CE obligation, which increases when you cross from one license type to two. Plan for that before you apply, and the rest is a matter of scheduling and paperwork.
