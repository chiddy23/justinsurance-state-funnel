## Understand property and casualty basics

To start off, it’s essential to grasp the fundamental differences between property and casualty insurance. Property insurance typically deals with protecting physical items such as homes, commercial buildings, or personal belongings. Casualty insurance, on the other hand, focuses more on liability coverage when someone is held responsible for causing damage or injury to others.

Because both property and casualty insurance policies come with a variety of endorsements, exclusions, and special rules, you’ll need to pay close attention to details in each coverage form. Terms like “replacement cost value,” “actual cash value,” and “liability limits” can easily blend together if you aren’t reviewing them regularly. A clear understanding of these definitions ensures you can tackle questions with confidence.

## Try these sample questions

Practice questions are an invaluable part of any study plan because they sharpen your problem-solving skills and give you firsthand experience with the exam’s style. Test yourself with the following three examples:

1.  Question: Which of the following best describes “actual cash value” in a property policy?  
    Answer: It is the cost to replace damaged property minus depreciation.  
    Explanation: Actual cash value (ACV) factors in wear and tear, so you only receive the depreciated amount rather than the full cost to replace an item.
2.  Question: What is an endorsement in a casualty policy?  
    Answer: A modification to the original terms of a policy.  
    Explanation: Endorsements add, remove, or change policy conditions. They are particularly useful if you want to tailor coverage to specific needs or risks.
3.  Question: If you own a small business and someone is injured on your property, which coverage would typically respond?  
    Answer: Commercial general liability (CGL) coverage.  
    Explanation: CGL is designed to protect your business from claims involving bodily injury or property damage, making it key for businesses of any size.

By practicing with questions of varying difficulty, you’ll learn to read each scenario carefully and pinpoint exactly which concept is being tested.

## Adopt effective exam strategies

Before you know it, test day will be around the corner, so it helps to establish a solid review routine early on. Consider setting aside smaller blocks of time each day instead of marathon sessions only on weekends. This consistent approach prevents burnout and makes it easier to recall the content you studied a few days prior.

It also pays to familiarize yourself with the format of the exam. Find out how many questions will be asked, how much time you’ll have, and whether you can go back to revisit any skipped questions. If you tend to feel anxious, practice relaxation techniques like deep breathing or brief stretching before diving into your test. On the big day, read all options carefully, eliminate clearly wrong answers, and remember that an educated guess is typically better than leaving the question blank.
