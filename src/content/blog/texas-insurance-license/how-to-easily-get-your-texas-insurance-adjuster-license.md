If you have ever considered working as an insurance adjuster in Texas, you may be pleasantly surprised by how straightforward the path can be. Obtaining a [Texas insurance adjuster license](/texas) does not have to be an intimidating process, even if you are brand new to the world of insurance. Texas also gives adjuster candidates something most states do not: you can qualify either by passing the state exam **or** by finishing a TDI-approved adjuster course, and either route can be done in a relatively short timeframe. The key is knowing each step in advance—and that is exactly what this article will show you.

Before you know it, you will be well on your way to adjusting claims, investigating accidents, and helping people navigate their insurance matters, all while enjoying a rewarding career. Below is a step-by-step breakdown of how to get your Texas insurance adjuster license, from picking your qualification route to maintaining your credential in the years to come.

## Understand license requirements

Texas offers several types of adjuster licenses, but the All-Lines Adjuster License is among the most comprehensive and popular. It typically allows you to handle a variety of claim types such as property, casualty, and even workers' compensation within the state. If you would prefer a narrower specialty, you can look into other options, like a Property and Casualty Adjuster License, although your job prospects might be broader with the all-lines designation.

Regardless of the license type you choose, you need to meet the same foundational requirements. Under Tex. Ins. Code §4101.053 you must be at least 18 years old, reside in Texas or in a state or country that permits a Texas resident to act as an adjuster there, and be trustworthy and have sufficient experience, education, or training in handling loss claims. (TDI also issues a designated home state all-lines adjuster license for producers whose own state does not license adjusters.) If you have a criminal history, you may also need to undergo additional scrutiny. By confirming your eligibility early on, you can avoid any hassles down the road.

The one requirement people most often get wrong is the exam. Texas adjusters do **not** have to test. The [Texas Department of Insurance](/texas/requirements) states plainly that you do not need to take an exam if you have completed a TDI-approved course or training program in adjusting losses within the last 12 months — the approved course is a legal substitute for the state exam, not just study material. TDI also waives the exam if you hold the CPCU or AIC designation, if you hold a license in good standing in a reciprocal state as a nonresident, or if your Texas license has been expired more than 90 days but less than a year.

## Choose your route: exam or approved course

Because of that rule, your first real decision is not which study guide to buy — it is which qualification route to take.

**Route 1: sit the exam.** Register with Pearson VUE and pass the Adjuster – All Lines exam (exam code InsTX-ALAdj16). Nothing is required before you test; Texas has no mandatory pre-exam course hours for adjusters, so plenty of candidates study on their own and go straight to the test center.

**Route 2: complete a TDI-approved adjuster course.** Finish a TDI-approved course or training program in adjusting losses, then apply within 12 months of completing it and skip the exam entirely. Pearson VUE's own Texas candidate handbook confirms this path for the All Lines, Property and Casualty, and Workers' Compensation adjuster licenses, and directs course completers to submit the application at sircon.com/texas, where TDI also publishes the list of approved courses.

Providers on the approved list must adhere to the guidelines set by the state, and you will find that many offer courses tailored for busy learners. Some allow you to study at your own pace, while others have structured, instructor-led sessions.

When comparing providers, you want to look into factors like price, course format, and student support. Classes can range from fully online to a hybrid of virtual and in-person learning. If you are balancing a job and family obligations, you might prefer an on-demand, online curriculum that fits into your schedule. On the other hand, if you prefer real-time guidance and accountability, a live or instructor-led option can help keep you on track. One caution: only a course on TDI's approved list carries the exam exemption. General exam-prep material, however good, does not — if you study with a prep-only product, you still have to test.

## Complete your adjuster coursework

Course length varies by provider and license type, but 40 hours is a common benchmark for all-lines adjuster programs in Texas. This time is structured to give you a broad understanding of essential topics, including policy provisions, endorsements, relevant Texas insurance laws, federal regulations, and ethical responsibilities.

You will find that most adjuster courses cover the following areas in detail:

-   Basic insurance terms and concepts.
-   Types of policies and coverages specific to Texas.
-   Claims handling procedures and best practices.
-   Laws and regulations set forth by the Texas Department of Insurance.

While every provider structures content a bit differently, you can expect a mix of reading assignments, quizzes, and possibly interactive modules designed to keep you engaged. If you are on the approved-course route, hang on to your certificate of completion — it is what qualifies you, and the 12-month window runs from that date. If you are testing instead, absorbing this material now gives you a major advantage on exam day.

## Register for your exam

If you are taking the exam route, the next step is to schedule with Pearson VUE, which administers every Texas insurance exam. You can book online or by phone, and you pay at the time you reserve your seat.

Fees are set in the Pearson VUE Texas Insurance Licensing Candidate Handbook and are lower than most people expect. The Adjuster – All Lines exam is **$49**, as is Adjuster – Property & Casualty. Adjuster – Workers' Compensation is $29, and the Public Insurance Adjuster exam is $39. The All Lines exam gives you 150 minutes for all 150 questions — and unlike the agent exams, the adjuster exams carry no unscored pretest items, so every question you answer counts toward your score. You need a scaled score of 70 to pass. Fees are non-refundable, so confirm the current figures in the handbook before you book. Taking practice exams offered by your study provider or other reputable sources is a great way to identify your strong points and any gaps in your knowledge.

When test day arrives, be sure to bring a valid, government-issued photo ID and your exam confirmation details. After you have completed the exam, your results are typically available right away, letting you know if you passed or need to re-test. Should you fall short on your first attempt, do not worry—Pearson VUE lets a candidate who failed schedule again as soon as the next day, and there is no limit on the number of attempts, though you pay the exam fee each time.

## Submit your application documents

Once you have passed the exam — or finished your TDI-approved course — the formal application to the Texas Department of Insurance comes next. Adjuster applications are submitted online through Sircon, and the resident adjuster application fee is $50. You will be asked to provide personal information, background details, and proof of whichever qualification route you used. Watch the clock on both routes: exam passers have one year from the exam date to complete the application, and course completers must apply within 12 months of finishing the course. In addition, a background check and fingerprints through IdentoGO are standard parts of the licensing process, and you can schedule your appointment online or by phone.

While many applicants sail through this part without any trouble, you do want to fill out all forms accurately to avoid processing delays. If something is missing or needs verification, the state will usually contact you, but it can slow down your timeline. It is best to gather all necessary documents and ensure every form is correctly completed before you submit your application.

## Maintain your license renewal

Congratulations, you are now an official Texas insurance adjuster—at least, you will be once your application is approved! However, your journey does not end there. Staying an active adjuster means keeping up with renewal deadlines, [continuing education](/texas/continuing-education), and any changes in state requirements. Generally, you will renew your license every two years, and specific continuing education hours are mandatory for renewal.

[Texas continuing education](/texas/continuing-education) helps you stay informed about evolving laws, coverage options, and best practices in claims handling. This allows you to serve your clients better and improves your long-term career prospects. Be sure to track your continuing education credits carefully so there are no surprises when you approach renewal. Many adjusters find a continuing education provider that fits their learning style and schedule, making it easier to meet deadlines and stay compliant.

## Final thoughts and next steps

Earning a Texas insurance adjuster license spells opportunity for you, whether you are hoping to switch careers or move up in the insurance industry. The steps—meeting the eligibility criteria, qualifying by exam or by TDI-approved course, and submitting your application with fingerprints—can be managed efficiently with good preparation. After you are licensed, staying current with renewals and continuing education ensures you remain in good standing and advance professionally.
