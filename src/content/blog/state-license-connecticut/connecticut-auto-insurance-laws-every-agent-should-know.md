Auto insurance is one of the most consistent product areas Connecticut producers work with — and Connecticut has specific laws affecting how auto insurance is sold, structured, and serviced. Understanding Connecticut auto insurance laws helps you serve clients effectively, avoid compliance issues, and recognize coverage gaps that affect client protection.

Here's what Connecticut agents need to know about auto insurance laws.

## Connecticut's Mandatory Auto Insurance Requirements

Connecticut requires drivers to carry minimum auto insurance coverage. The required minimum coverage includes:

Bodily Injury Liability: $25,000 per person / $50,000 per accident

Property Damage Liability: $25,000 per accident

Uninsured/Underinsured Motorist (UM/UIM): $25,000 per person / $50,000 per accident (typically required equal to or in excess of liability limits)

These minimums represent baseline requirements. Most clients should consider higher limits given the cost of medical care, vehicle repairs, and potential litigation.

## Why Minimum Coverage Often Isn't Enough

Connecticut's minimum requirements may not adequately protect clients:

Medical costs. A serious injury can easily exceed $25,000 in medical bills. Limits below actual exposure leave clients personally liable for the difference.

Vehicle replacement costs. New car prices regularly exceed $25,000. Property damage limits matching minimums may leave clients personally liable.

Wage loss. Bodily injury limits don't reflect the substantial wage loss claims associated with serious injuries.

Litigation. Connecticut allows lawsuits for damages exceeding insurance limits.

Net worth protection. Clients with substantial assets need higher limits to protect against significant losses.

Producers should help clients understand the gap between minimum requirements and adequate protection.

## Uninsured/Underinsured Motorist Coverage

Connecticut emphasizes UM/UIM coverage:

Mandatory minimum. Connecticut requires UM/UIM coverage at minimum levels.

Stacking provisions. Connecticut has specific rules about UM/UIM stacking (combining limits across multiple vehicles).

Underinsured motorist conversion coverage. Connecticut offers a unique "underinsured motorist conversion coverage" option that provides UIM benefits even when the at-fault driver has higher liability limits than the client's UIM limits.

Notification requirements. Specific notification requirements for UIM claims.

UM/UIM is particularly important given that Connecticut clients can be hit by drivers with minimum or no coverage.

## No-Fault Considerations

Connecticut uses a modified comparative fault system rather than a no-fault system:

Comparative fault. Damages are allocated based on each party's percentage of fault.

Connecticut's threshold: A party can recover damages if their fault is less than 51%.

No PIP requirement. Connecticut doesn't require Personal Injury Protection (PIP) coverage like true no-fault states.

Lawsuits permitted. Drivers can pursue lawsuits against other drivers for damages.

This system affects how claims are handled and what coverage clients should consider.

## Required Disclosures

Connecticut auto insurance involves specific disclosure requirements:

UM/UIM coverage disclosures. Required disclosures about UM/UIM coverage and conversion options.

Premium increase notifications. Required notifications for premium increases meeting specific thresholds.

Policy renewal notifications. Required notifications before policy renewal.

Cancellation requirements. Specific rules about when and how policies can be cancelled.

Non-renewal requirements. Specific rules about when and how non-renewals can occur.

These disclosures protect consumers from surprises and ensure informed decision-making.

## Coverage Considerations for Connecticut Clients

Beyond minimum requirements, Connecticut auto insurance typically involves:

Collision coverage. Covers damage to insured's vehicle from collision. Required by lenders for financed vehicles.

Comprehensive coverage. Covers damage from non-collision causes (theft, vandalism, weather, animals). Required by lenders for financed vehicles.

Medical payments coverage (Med Pay). Covers medical expenses for occupants of insured's vehicle. Optional but commonly purchased.

Rental reimbursement. Covers rental costs while insured's vehicle is being repaired.

Roadside assistance. Covers towing and roadside services.

Custom equipment coverage. Covers aftermarket modifications.

Gap coverage. Covers difference between vehicle's actual cash value and loan/lease balance after total loss.

Each coverage type has specific considerations producers should explain to clients.

## Connecticut Driver Discount Programs

Connecticut allows insurance carriers to offer various discounts:

Multi-policy discounts. Combining auto with home, renters, or other policies.

Multi-vehicle discounts. Insuring multiple vehicles.

Safe driver discounts. Drivers with clean records.

Anti-theft device discounts. Vehicles with approved anti-theft devices.

Defensive driving course discounts. Drivers who complete approved defensive driving courses.

Good student discounts. Student drivers maintaining good grades.

Mature driver discounts. Drivers over specific ages who complete approved courses.

Bundling discounts. Various combinations of products and services.

Producers should help clients identify all applicable discounts.

## Risk Rating Considerations

Connecticut auto insurance rates depend on multiple factors:

Driver characteristics. Age, gender, marital status, driving record.

Vehicle characteristics. Make, model, year, safety features, theft rates.

Location. Garage location affects rates significantly. Urban areas typically cost more than rural.

Usage. Annual mileage, commute distance, business use.

Coverage selections. Higher limits and additional coverages cost more.

Credit-based insurance scores. Connecticut allows insurance scoring (with specific consumer protections).

Claims history. Prior claims affect rates.

Insurance score. Consumer credit-based insurance score impacts rates.

Producers should help clients understand factors affecting their rates.

## Connecticut's Recent Flooding and Auto Insurance

In August 2024, Connecticut Governor Lamont declared a state of emergency in response to flooding affecting Fairfield, New Haven, and Litchfield counties. Auto insurance considerations from this event:

Flood damage to vehicles. Comprehensive coverage typically covers flood damage. Liability and collision do not.

Total loss situations. Severe flooding often results in total loss claims.

Salvage and rebuilding. Specific rules for total loss vehicle handling.

Claims surge management. Major weather events create claims surge requiring carrier resources.

This event highlights why comprehensive coverage matters even for clients who feel they don't need it.

## Specialty Auto Coverage

Connecticut also involves specialty auto coverage:

Commercial auto. For business vehicles. Different rules and rates than personal auto.

Classic and antique cars. Specialty coverage for collector vehicles.

Motorcycle coverage. Connecticut motorcycle insurance has specific provisions.

Recreational vehicle coverage. RVs, trailers, boats with motors.

Rideshare coverage. Specific coverage for rideshare drivers (Uber, Lyft, etc.). Standard auto policies typically exclude rideshare activities.

Delivery driver coverage. For drivers delivering food, packages, etc. Standard auto policies typically exclude delivery activities.

Specialty coverage requires specific carrier programs and producer expertise.

## Connecticut Auto Insurance Producer Compliance

Connecticut auto insurance producers must comply with:

Connecticut Insurance Code provisions. All Connecticut Code sections affecting insurance practice.

Consumer protection requirements. Including required disclosures and prohibited practices.

Replacement rules. When applicable to auto policy replacement.

Documentation standards. Maintaining records of recommendations, disclosures, and transactions.

[Continuing education](/connecticut/continuing-education). Auto/casualty CE may count toward Connecticut's 24-hour CE requirement (with 6-hour minimum per line for those holding Casualty or Personal Lines authority).

## Common Connecticut Auto Insurance Pitfalls

Selling minimum-only coverage to clients with assets to protect. Liability limits should reflect client's net worth, not just legal minimums.

Missing UM/UIM conversion coverage discussions. Connecticut's distinctive UIM conversion option provides important protection.

Inadequate explanation of comprehensive coverage value. Clients sometimes view comprehensive as optional when it provides important protection against weather, theft, and vandalism.

Missing rideshare or delivery exclusions. Clients participating in gig economy work need coverage their standard policies may not provide.

Neglecting annual reviews. Auto insurance needs change as vehicles, drivers, and circumstances change.

Inadequate discount identification. Failing to apply all discounts clients qualify for.

## 5 Frequently Asked Questions

- What's Connecticut's minimum auto insurance requirement? $25,000 per person / $50,000 per accident bodily injury liability, $25,000 property damage liability, and UM/UIM at typically equivalent levels.
- Does Connecticut have no-fault auto insurance? No. Connecticut uses modified comparative fault rather than a no-fault system. PIP coverage isn't required.
- What is underinsured motorist conversion coverage? A Connecticut-specific UIM option that provides UIM benefits even when the at-fault driver has higher liability limits than the client's UIM limits.
- Are insurance scores legal for auto rating in Connecticut? Yes. Connecticut allows credit-based insurance scoring with specific consumer protections.
- Does standard auto insurance cover rideshare driving? Typically no. Standard auto policies exclude rideshare activities. Rideshare drivers need specific rideshare coverage or carrier endorsements.
