Kansas City's insurance market — split across Missouri and Kansas — is defined by several anchors that create professional advisory demand in a growing Midwest metro: Cerner/Oracle Health (electronic health records; acquired by Oracle in 2022; largest private employer in KC metro), H&R Block (Fortune 500 tax preparation; significant professional financial awareness), Hallmark Cards (major private company; creative and corporate professionals), Burns & McDonnell (major engineering firm; employee-owned), Kansas City Life Insurance (regional insurer; financial community connections), and a growing technology and startup ecosystem along the Crossroads Arts District and the emerging KC tech corridor. Kansas City's location at the center of the continental United States creates commercial insurance opportunities in logistics, agriculture, and manufacturing that reward producers who understand both the metro professional market and the broader regional commercial economy.

## Cerner/Oracle Health — Kansas City's Healthcare Tech Anchor

Cerner Corporation (now Cerner, an Oracle company; North Kansas City, Missouri) was the largest private employer in the Kansas City metro area before the Oracle acquisition in 2022:

Electronic health records (EHR) technology — used by hospitals and health systems worldwide

Oracle acquired Cerner for approximately $28 billion in June 2022

Oracle's acquisition created significant career uncertainty and corporate transition activity — generating rollover advisory demand

Kansas City campus employs thousands of software engineers, product managers, healthcare IT consultants, and corporate staff: $80,000-$200,000+

Advisory for Cerner/Oracle Health professionals:

Oracle RSU compensation (publicly traded) creates equity advisory contexts

Career transitions during Oracle integration created 401(k) rollover advisory moments

Health IT professionals with specialized EHR knowledge need own-occupation disability protection

Kansas City's growing health IT ecosystem: Beyond Cerner/Oracle, Kansas City has attracted health IT companies including Netsmart Technologies, Crossroads Hospice, and various digital health startups — creating a health technology professional community growing beyond Cerner's dominance.

## H&R Block — Tax and Financial Awareness

H&R Block (Kansas City; Fortune 500) employs thousands of corporate professionals and runs the world's largest consumer tax preparation service:

Corporate headquarters professionals: $70,000-$200,000+

Tax professionals with above-average financial awareness

RSU compensation for senior H&R Block corporate staff

H&R Block's tax professionals are acutely aware of tax-advantaged insurance products (annuities, permanent life insurance) — creating a sophisticated client base that values tax planning integration

Advisory for H&R Block community:

Tax-aware clients appreciate producers who understand how life insurance and annuity products fit into tax planning

H&R Block senior executives with significant RSU and bonus compensation need life insurance and disability advisory beyond group coverage

## Burns & McDonnell — Employee-Owned Engineering

Burns & McDonnell (Kansas City; employee-owned engineering and construction firm):

Major engineering, architecture, and consulting firm; global operations

Employee-owned (ESOP): over 10,000+ employees are owners; significant equity accumulation at retirement

Engineers and project managers: $80,000-$200,000+

Employee ownership model creates specific ESOP liquidity advisory needs at retirement — employees who hold significant ESOP value need estate planning and diversification advisory

Advisory for Burns & McDonnell professionals:

ESOP retirement creates a specific advisory moment — at retirement or termination, employees receive ESOP distributions in company stock that must be diversified; this creates rollover advisory opportunities

Engineers with specialized skills need own-occupation disability income protection

## Kansas City Commercial Insurance Opportunities

Logistics and distribution: Kansas City is a major continental logistics hub — Interstate 70, Interstate 35, and multiple major rail lines converge in the KC metro. Major distribution centers, logistics companies, and warehousing operations create commercial general liability, cargo, and workers' comp advisory throughout the region.

Agricultural processing: Kansas City sits at the edge of the Midwest agricultural belt — grain elevators, food processing facilities, and agricultural businesses throughout the Kansas City corridor create commercial P&C advisory needs.

Construction: Kansas City's active development — the Crossroads Arts District, Power & Light District redevelopment, suburban residential development — creates active construction workers' comp advisory.

## 5 Frequently Asked Questions

- What makes the Cerner/Oracle acquisition a one-time insurance advisory opportunity? Oracle's $28 billion acquisition of Cerner in 2022 created one of the largest corporate transitions in Kansas City history. Thousands of Cerner employees experienced compensation structure changes, career uncertainty, and benefit plan transitions — all creating insurance advisory moments. Employees who left Cerner during or after the transition carry Cerner/Oracle 401(k) balances requiring rollover advisory. The acquisition's aftermath continues to create career transition advisory demand in Kansas City's health IT community.
- What is the ESOP advisory context for Burns & McDonnell employees? Burns & McDonnell is 100% employee-owned through an ESOP. Engineers and professionals who have been with the firm for decades accumulate significant ESOP value — often reaching $500,000-$2,000,000+ at retirement. At separation or retirement, this ESOP value must be distributed (in company stock, then typically diversified). The rollover and diversification advisory moment is among the most significant financial events in a Burns & McDonnell employee's career. Annuity Best Interest certification is required before any rollover advisory involving annuity products.
- How does Kansas City's logistics concentration create commercial insurance opportunities? Kansas City is often called "America's Crossroads" — the geographic center of the continental U.S. with superior rail and highway access. Major logistics companies (Coyote Logistics, YRC Worldwide, major distribution centers for Target, Home Depot, Amazon) operate large Kansas City facilities. Commercial general liability, cargo insurance, commercial auto fleets, and workers' comp for warehousing operations create active commercial insurance advisory markets in the Kansas City metro.
- What is the H&R Block tax professional community's insurance advisory context? H&R Block's tax professionals are among the most financially aware non-financial-services professionals — they help millions of Americans navigate taxes annually. When advising H&R Block employees on life insurance and annuity products, producers who understand the tax implications (tax-deferred growth, tax treatment of death benefits, 1035 exchanges) create distinctive advisory value. H&R Block professionals are likely to ask sophisticated tax questions that generic producers can't answer.
- Is Kansas City a metro area that spans two states? Yes — Kansas City spans Missouri and Kansas, with major suburbs in both states (Kansas City, MO; Overland Park, KS; Lenexa, KS; Shawnee, KS). Insurance producers advising Kansas City clients across both states should consider dual licensure in Missouri and Kansas. Kansas City's split-state geography also means commercial insurance advisory may span both state regulatory environments depending on where the employer operates.
