Getting your Pennsylvania insurance producer license opens doors to one of the most diverse insurance markets in the Northeast. Philadelphia's substantial healthcare and financial services concentration, Pittsburgh's healthcare and growing technology sectors, the Lehigh Valley's industrial markets, and Pennsylvania's substantial population create real opportunity for licensed agents. Pennsylvania's licensing process recently became more accessible — as of April 29, 2025, Pennsylvania eliminated the [prelicense](/pennsylvania/prelicensing) education requirement, making the path to licensure faster and more flexible.

Here's exactly how to get your Pennsylvania insurance producer license.

## Step 1: Meet the Basic Eligibility Requirements

Before starting, confirm you meet Pennsylvania's baseline requirements:

You must be at least 18 years old

You must have a Social Security Number (SSN) or Federal Employer Identification Number (FEIN)

You must be a Pennsylvania resident (for resident license) — non-residents apply through reciprocity

You must be able to pass a federal criminal history background check

You must truthfully complete the license application and disclose any relevant history

These eligibility requirements are governed under the Pennsylvania Insurance Code (Title 40 of the Pennsylvania Statutes), which defines the regulations and requirements for insurance producers in Pennsylvania.

## Step 2: Pre-Licensing Education (NOT Required as of April 29, 2025)

Here's a major recent change: Pennsylvania Act 142 of 2024, effective April 29, 2025, eliminated the prelicense education (PLE) requirement for Pennsylvania insurance producer licensure. Pennsylvania is now one of the more accessible states for new producers.

However, prelicense education is strongly recommended. Here's why:

The Pennsylvania state exam covers substantial content

The exam covers both general insurance topics and Pennsylvania-specific laws

Most candidates significantly improve their [pass rate](/pass-rates) by completing structured study

Quality prelicense courses include practice exams that mirror the actual state exam

So while you can technically skip prelicense and go straight to the exam, doing so reduces your chances of passing on the first try. Most successful candidates complete a prelicense course even though it's not legally required.

## Step 3: Pre-Licensing Eligibility Review (Optional)

Pennsylvania offers a distinctive optional step: the Pre-Licensing Eligibility Review for individuals with criminal records. This program:

Provides guidance on how specific circumstances may affect formal license application

Allows candidates to assess eligibility before investing in education and exam fees

Helps applicants understand likely application outcomes

Reduces risk for applicants with concerning history

Visit the Preliminary Insurance License Eligibility Review page through the Pennsylvania Insurance Department to submit a Pre-Licensing Review Request.

## Step 4: Schedule Your State Exam Through PSI

Pennsylvania insurance licensing exams are administered by PSI Services LLC. Schedule through:

PSI's website at psiexams.com/test-takers/

Phone: 1-888-818-5822

Pay the $43 exam fee per exam at registration

Schedule at a PSI testing center or through OnVUE remote online proctored testing

Bring valid identification on test day

Identification requirements:

A valid form of identification is required

Acceptable forms include U.S. government-issued documents (passport) or state-issued driver's license/ID card

Names must match registration exactly

## Pennsylvania Exam Structure

Pennsylvania offers exams for various lines of authority. Passing score is 70% on every Pennsylvania insurance license exam.

Common exam types:

The exam covers two sections: General (insurance principles applicable in any state) and State Law (Pennsylvania-specific laws, rules, and regulations).

Exam validity: Your passing score is valid for 12 months. You must apply for licensure within this window.

Most candidates receive results within minutes of completing the exam.

## Step 5: Submit Your License Application

After passing the state exam, apply for your license:

Pennsylvania license applications can be submitted through:

National Insurance Producer Registry (NIPR) at nipr.com (most common)

Sircon at sircon.com/pennsylvania

Kiosk in the testing center (immediately after passing the exam)

Application fees:

Resident Producer: $55 (plus small transaction charge)

Non-Resident Producer: $110 (plus small transaction charge)

Resident Business Entity: $55

Non-Resident Business Entity: $110

Important: Pennsylvania allows you to apply at the testing center kiosk through Sircon or NIPR immediately after passing the exam. Bringing a credit card to your exam (Visa, MasterCard, or American Express) lets you complete this step efficiently.

## Step 6: Submit Your Fingerprints Through IdentoGO

Pennsylvania requires fingerprinting for all initial resident producer license applicants. Important: Do NOT register for fingerprinting until after you have passed your exam and applied for licensure. Fingerprint results received without a corresponding license application will not be accepted, and you'll be required to resubmit and repay the fingerprinting fee.

Fingerprinting process:

Step 1: Apply for your license first.

Step 2: Register with IdentoGO:

Online at IdentoGO website

Phone: 844-321-2101

Use service code: 1KGBGJ for the Pennsylvania Insurance Department

Receive registration number — keep it for your appointment

Step 3: Schedule and attend appointment:

IdentoGO Live Scan (electronic fingerprinting, no ink)

Bring registration number and valid photo ID

Pay fingerprinting fee at the IdentoGO center

Acceptable payment: Credit card, debit card, certified check, or money order

NOT accepted: Cash, personal checks

Step 4: Background check processing:

IdentoGO transmits fingerprints to the FBI

FBI national criminal history check completed

Results sent to Pennsylvania Insurance Department

Fingerprinting Exemptions:

Non-resident producers applying under reciprocity provisions of Act 147 of 2002

Resident producers ADDING a line of authority to existing license

Motor vehicle physical damage appraisers

## Step 7: Pennsylvania Insurance Department Reviews and Issues Your License

Once the Pennsylvania Insurance Department has your complete application, exam results, and fingerprint background check, processing typically takes 2-5 business days for online applications.

You can check application status using the Producer Application Status Lookup Tool on the PA Insurance Department website (insurance.pa.gov).

Important: The Pennsylvania Insurance Department no longer mails physical licenses. Once your license is issued, you can print it from the Department's website.

## Step 8: Get Appointed by Insurance Carriers

Pennsylvania requires producers to be appointed by carriers to sell their products. Most carriers handle the appointment process once you've contracted with them. The carrier files the appointment with the Pennsylvania Insurance Department.

## Total Timeline

From starting study to holding your active Pennsylvania license, most candidates finish in 2-4 weeks:

Self-study (recommended even though not required): 1-3 weeks

Exam scheduling and completion: 1 week

Application submission and fingerprinting: 1-2 weeks

License processing: 2-5 business days after fingerprinting

The elimination of PLE requirements has made Pennsylvania one of the faster states for licensing.

## License Renewal

Pennsylvania producer licenses are valid for 2 years from issuance:

Renewal cycle: Biennial

CE requirement: 24 hours every 2 years (3 hours Ethics + 2 hours Flood for P&C/Personal Lines)

Reinstatement period: Up to 1 year after expiration without re-exam or re-fingerprinting required

After 1 year of expiration: Must re-apply as new license applicant including new exam and new fingerprinting

This generous one-year reinstatement window provides flexibility if circumstances cause a renewal delay.

## Special Path: Moving to Pennsylvania From Another State

If you're already licensed as an insurance producer in another state and you move to Pennsylvania:

Reciprocity through Act 147 of 2002. Non-resident producer applicants applying under reciprocity provisions don't need to:

Take the Pennsylvania exam (in most cases)

Submit fingerprints

Application process:

Apply through NIPR or Sircon

Provide home state license verification

Pay $110 non-resident application fee

Submit any required documentation

This streamlined path makes Pennsylvania accessible for already-licensed producers from other states.

## 5 Frequently Asked Questions

- Does Pennsylvania require pre-licensing education? No — as of April 29, 2025. Pennsylvania Act 142 of 2024 eliminated the prelicense education requirement. However, structured study is strongly recommended given the exam difficulty.
- Does Pennsylvania require fingerprinting for insurance producers? Yes. All initial resident producer license applicants must complete fingerprinting through IdentoGO using service code 1KGBGJ. Non-resident reciprocity applicants and producers adding lines to existing licenses are exempt.
- How much does the Pennsylvania insurance exam cost? $43 per exam, paid to PSI at registration.
- How long is my Pennsylvania insurance license valid? 2 years from the date of issuance. Renewals can include up to 1 year after expiration without re-exam or re-fingerprinting.
- What's the application fee for a Pennsylvania resident producer license? $55 (plus small transaction charge). Non-resident license fee is $110.
