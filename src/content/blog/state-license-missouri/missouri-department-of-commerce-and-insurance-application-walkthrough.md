Missouri's license application process is the simplest of all comparison states — no PLE requirement, no fingerprinting vendor, a flat $100 fee that covers all lines on the same application, and 5-10 business day processing. The primary variables are exam preparation time (self-directed), the exam's single combined scaled score (0-100, passing at 70), and the 24-48 hour wait before applying after passing. Missouri does have a temporary producer license under RSMo § 375.025 — up to 90 days, no exam — but it exists to keep an existing book of business serviced (a deceased or disabled producer's successor, for example), not to bridge a first-time applicant. So for a new candidate the 5-10 business day processing window is what stands between passing the exam and selling — still among the fastest of the comparison states. Here's the precise walkthrough.

## The MO Licensing Sequence

Most streamlined of all comparison states:

(Optional) Study preparation

Schedule and pass the Pearson VUE exam (one combined scaled score, passing = 70 on a 0-100 scale)

Wait 24-48 hours for results to load in DCI system

Submit NIPR application with $100 fee

Wait 5-10 business days for processing

License issued

No PLE, no fingerprinting, no vendor appointments — exam + application.

## Step-by-Step Walkthrough

STEP 1: Choose Your Line(s) of Authority

Major lines in Missouri with exam format:

Life: 100 questions; 2 hours; $32

Accident & Health: ~100 questions; 2 hours; $32

Life & A&H (combined): 170 questions; 3 hours; $40

Property: 105 questions; 2 hours; $32

Casualty: 105 questions; 2 hours; $32

P&C (combined): 165 questions; 3 hours; $40

Personal Lines: 125 questions; 2.5 hours; $32

Combined exam advantage: Missouri offers combined L&H (170 questions, 3 hours, $40) and P&C (165 questions, 3 hours, $40). Each combined exam is $40 versus $64 for the two single-line exams it replaces ($32 each) — a savings of about $24, plus one trip to the test center instead of two.

STEP 2: Schedule Pearson VUE Exam

Go to pearsonvue.com/us/en/mo/insurance.html

Schedule at least 24 hours in advance (5 days for electronic check)

Pay exam fee ($32 for a single-line exam, $40 for a combined exam)

All exams in-person only — effective May 7, 2025 (no OnVUE)

Pearson VUE test centers in Missouri, per the current candidate handbook: Cape Girardeau (2), Carthage, Columbia, Joplin (2), Kansas City (2), Maryville, Poplar Bluff, Springfield, St. Joseph (2), St. Louis (2), and West Plains. Locations and schedules change — confirm your site when you schedule. Candidates may also test at Pearson VUE centers in neighboring states.

STEP 3: Take and Pass the Exam

Arrive 30 minutes early; two government-issued IDs (one with photo); no personal items.

Exam content blends a general (national) portion with a Missouri state-specific portion, but both are combined into one result. Scoring is a single scaled score on a 0-100 scale, with 70 as the passing score — the reported score is neither the number nor the percentage of questions you answered correctly. If you fail, you must wait one day before scheduling a retake of the entire exam.

Results: Immediate pass/fail notification. Diagnostic report available in your Pearson VUE account.

STEP 4: Submit NIPR Application (After 24-48 Hours)

After passing the exam:

Wait 24-48 hours for results to load in DCI system

Go to nipr.com or apply online at insurance.mo.gov

Complete Uniform Application

Pay $100 application fee + $5.60 NIPR transaction fee

Answer all background disclosure questions accurately

Paper alternative: Download Uniform Application → mail with $100 check to DCI PO Box 4001, Jefferson City, MO 65102.

Processing: 5-10 business days. Apply within 1 year of passing exam.

## Costs Summary

Missouri's total licensing cost is among the lowest of comparison states despite the $100 application fee, because the exam fee is exceptionally low.

## Non-Resident Process

Non-residents apply through NIPR with home state license in good standing — same $100 fee. No PLE, no MO exam required for residents of states with reciprocity agreements.

## 5 Frequently Asked Questions

- Why do I have to wait 24-48 hours after passing before applying? After the Pearson VUE exam, results need 24-48 hours to transfer to the DCI licensing database. If you submit the NIPR application before results appear in DCI's system, the application may be delayed or returned incomplete. Wait the full 24-48 hours before submitting.
- Can I apply for multiple lines of authority on the same $100 application? Yes — Missouri's $100 application fee covers all lines included on the same application submission. Unlike Indiana ($40 per line) or MN ($50 per line + $20 tech fee), Missouri charges a flat $100 regardless of how many lines are on the application. For candidates applying for both Life/Health AND P&C, the same $100 application covers all lines.
- What is the fee waiver and who qualifies? In accordance with Missouri statutory laws, certain low-income individuals and veterans can apply for a License Fee Waiver with DCI — potentially waiving the $100 application fee. Contact DCI at insurance.mo.gov for current fee waiver application procedures and eligibility criteria.
- What test centers are available in Missouri? The current Pearson VUE candidate handbook lists Missouri test centers in Cape Girardeau (2), Carthage, Columbia, Joplin (2), Kansas City (2), Maryville, Poplar Bluff, Springfield, St. Joseph (2), St. Louis (2), and West Plains. With no OnVUE option as of May 7, 2025, candidates must travel to a physical test center. Most Missouri population centers have nearby Pearson VUE access, and candidates may also test at centers in neighboring states — but locations and schedules change, so confirm yours when you schedule.
- What happens if I fail the Missouri exam? Missouri reports one combined scaled score (0-100) with 70 as the passing score, so you pass or fail the exam as a whole — there is no separate pass/fail for the national and state portions. If you fail, you must wait one day before scheduling a retake, and you retake the entire exam and pay the exam fee again. Your score report includes a diagnostic breakdown so you can see which content areas to focus on before you retest.
