## Understand renewal basics

[Washington insurance license](/washington) renewal might sound straightforward, but if you miss a date or skip a key step, the consequences can be costly. Start with the one date that governs everything else: your Washington license expires on **the last day of your birth month, every two years**. It is not keyed to when you first got licensed, so don't count forward from your original issue date — count to your birthday.

The [Washington Office of the Insurance Commissioner](/washington/requirements) (OIC) lets you renew as early as 90 days before that expiration date, and sends email reminders at 90, 60 and 30 days out. Those reminders are a courtesy, not a safety net — put the last day of your birth month on your own calendar and work backward from it.

You want to keep your license active without interruption or penalties. That means having a clear picture of your renewal window, the fees you owe, and the [continuing education](/washington/continuing-education) (CE) hours you must complete. A short refresher course in the regulatory basics can do wonders for your confidence and ensure you’re always in compliance.

If any part of the process feels overwhelming, remember that it’s normal to have a few questions the first time around. Once you walk through the steps and set reminders for next time, you’ll move through renewals with speed and ease.

## Complete your continuing education

A major part of the renewal process is completing the required [Washington continuing education](/washington/continuing-education) hours. In Washington, most licensees need 24 hours of CE coursework every two years, which must include at least 3 hours of ethics training. If you hold several lines of authority, such as both Life & Disability and Property & Casualty, you’ll generally still only need a total of 24 hours. Be sure to confirm your specific obligations with the OIC to avoid any surprises.

Try to complete your CE courses well before your renewal date. Leaving it to the last few weeks can cause unnecessary stress. Look for state-approved providers that match your learning style. Some people thrive in classroom settings, while others prefer online programs that let them study anywhere, anytime. When you plan ahead, you can pick courses that align with your career goals instead of settling for whichever class has an open seat at the last minute.

## Submit your renewal application

Once you’ve satisfied the CE requirement, the next step is to submit your renewal application. You’ll do this through the Washington OIC website, where you’ll fill out forms, confirm your personal information, and pay the license renewal fee. Speed is key. If you delay even a few days past your deadline, you could face extra fees or, worse, a lapse in your license.

Consider using electronic submission to make the process faster. The online system often notifies you instantly if something is missing or if your payment didn’t go through. Keep a record of your confirmation details in case you need to reference them later.

There’s usually a short processing period before your updated license status is reflected in the official database. During this time, it’s smart to keep all proof of submission and payment at your fingertips. That way, if a client or employer asks for verification, you’ll be prepared.

## Avoid common pitfalls

Because renewals happen every two years, it’s easy to forget some of the details once the process is behind you. Here are a few common pitfalls you’ll want to avoid.

First, don’t skip ethics training. While you can often choose from a range of CE topics, ethics is a mandatory component. If you miss it, your entire renewal application may be rejected. Second, track your deadlines. Failing to renew on time can lead to heavy late fees or temporarily losing the right to transact insurance business in Washington. Nothing puts a slower start on your next renewal cycle than having to chase down paperwork to reinstate your license.

Lastly, keep a personal record of your CE certificates. Even if your provider reports your completed hours to the OIC, maintaining duplicates ensures you can prove compliance if a system error occurs. You’ll thank yourself later if you ever need to resolve a reporting discrepancy.

## Consider convenient renewal solutions

Sometimes, the biggest barrier to a smooth Washington insurance license renewal is simply finding a hassle-free way to manage it all. Before you pick a service, understand one rule that decides everything else: in Washington, only an **OIC-approved CE provider** can report credits, and the provider that issues your certificate of completion is the one that files them — within 10 days of completion. No tracking tool, reminder app or third-party service can report hours on your behalf.

So evaluate any provider on that single question first: are they on OIC's approved CE provider list for Washington? (JustInsurance is not currently OIC-approved in Washington, so our Washington offering is exam prep rather than resident CE credit.) Take your credits from an approved provider, then confirm the hours actually posted by pulling your Individual Education History in OIC online services before you submit your renewal. Verifying the transcript yourself is the step that catches a reporting failure while you still have time to fix it.

If you prefer a do-it-yourself approach, you can get most of the same benefit by setting digital reminders, storing your license information in a cloud folder, and checking the OIC website regularly for updates. One way or another, a little organization goes a long way.

In a nutshell, you have options. You can pursue formal CE courses through local associations, enroll in virtual classes that fit your schedule, or lean on a specialized company to do the heavy lifting. The key is to pick a route that keeps you compliant without disrupting your day-to-day business.

## Frequently asked questions

Below are some of the most common questions asked by individuals renewing a Washington insurance license. Reviewing these can help you avoid typical glitches and get your renewal in on time.

1.  What happens if I miss my renewal deadline?  
    Washington gives you a ladder rather than a cliff. You can still late-renew for 60 days after expiration, at $82.50 per line for days 1–30 and $110 per line for days 31–60, through OIC online services. From day 61 through day 365 you move into reinstatement — $165 for an individual full-lines producer under WAC 284-17-490. Past one year from expiration you have to apply as a new licensee and re-examine. What you will *not* have to do is take a prelicensing course: [Washington eliminated its prelicensing education requirement](/washington/prelicensing) effective July 23, 2023 (HB 1061), and there is no PLE to repeat at any point on that ladder. Throughout any lapse, you cannot legally transact insurance business in Washington.
2.  How many continuing education hours do I need?  
    Most licensees in Washington need 24 CE hours every two years, including 3 hours of ethics. Be sure to confirm your specific requirements with the OIC because rules can differ slightly based on your line of authority.
3.  Do I have to retake the licensing exam for renewal?  
    No. Renewal, late renewal and reinstatement all run on CE and fees — no exam. Re-examination only comes back into play if you let the license sit expired for more than a year, at which point you are applying as a new licensee rather than renewing.
4.  Can I move extra CE hours to my next renewal?  
    Washington does not usually allow carryover of unused CE hours. Any extra hours beyond the required 24 cannot be applied to future cycles, so plan your CE schedule wisely.
5.  Where can I find convenient solutions for CE courses?  
    Start from OIC's list of approved Washington CE providers — only an approved provider can report your credits to the state, and it reports them within 10 days of completion. Many approved providers offer self-paced online formats, and industry associations run in-person and virtual sessions tailored to specific lines of authority. Whichever you choose, verify the hours posted to your Individual Education History in OIC online services before you renew.

Wrapping up your renewal shouldn’t be a stressful process. By understanding the basics, keeping track of CE hours, and submitting your paperwork on time, you’ll avoid costly mistakes and keep your career rolling along. You’ve chosen a fulfilling path in insurance—make sure you protect the license that makes your work possible. And if you ever feel stuck, remember that resources are always at your fingertips. Good luck with your Washington insurance license renewal.
