Once you've passed Arizona's state exam and navigated the Fieldprint fingerprint process, understanding what happens next — and how long it takes — helps you plan your career launch accurately. Arizona also has distinctive provisions for producers relocating to and from Arizona that differ from many other states, including a 90-day window for relocation processing and specific Form L-CLR requirements. Understanding the full timeline from exam pass to active license also requires navigating Arizona's unique two-system fingerprint process, which is consistently the most time-consuming step for most applicants.

Here's a complete breakdown of Arizona insurance license processing and relocation procedures.

## Arizona's Standard Processing Timeline

For a clean application, Arizona licensing typically follows this timeline:

Day 0 — Pass exam PSI notifies DIFI within 48 hours of your passing score.

Days 1-3 — Submit your NIPR application Do this first. DIFI's own instruction is that you should not complete your Fieldprint appointment until *after* you have submitted your application at nipr.com. Application fee payment confirms submission.

Days 1-7 — Schedule and complete fingerprinting Create accounts on both the AZ DPS Portal and Fieldprint. Schedule the Fieldprint appointment (availability varies, typically 3-7 days from scheduling). Attend the appointment, receive the reference number starting with "A."

Days 7-10 — Complete and mail Form L-FPV Mail Form L-FPV with your Fieldprint reference number to DIFI. Allow mail transit time.

Days 10-25 — FBI background check processing DIFI receives Form L-FPV, processes FBI criminal history check. Background check processing typically takes 1-3 weeks.

Days 25-35 — DIFI review and license issuance DIFI reviews complete package — application, exam results, background check. Clean applications typically approved within a few business days of receiving all elements.

Total typical timeline: 4-6 weeks from exam pass to active license for clean applications.

The fingerprint process is typically the longest step — both due to the multi-system complexity and FBI background check processing time.

## What Slows Down Processing

Missing Form L-FPV. The most common stalling point — applicants complete fingerprinting but forget to mail Form L-FPV with the Fieldprint reference number. DIFI cannot match fingerprint submission to application without this step.

Wrong fingerprint vendor. Submitting through a generic IdentoGO page without DIFI's specific service code sends prints to the wrong queue. DIFI will not receive them, requiring resubmission.

Disclosure issues. Any "yes" answers on background questions requiring additional documentation add 1-4+ weeks depending on complexity.

Name inconsistencies. Name on NIPR application, PSI exam registration, ID, and Form L-FPV must all match exactly.

P.O. box address. DIFI does not accept P.O. boxes as resident or business addresses. Applications with P.O. boxes are returned for correction.

Missing Form L-152. Required for lawful presence verification unless fingerprints were submitted electronically (which satisfies the requirement).

## The Fingerprint Timeline in Detail

The Fieldprint/AZ DPS fingerprint process is the most complex step for most applicants:

Account creation: Creating both AZ DPS Portal and Fieldprint accounts can be done same day — allow 30-60 minutes.

Appointment availability: Fieldprint appointment availability varies by location. In the Phoenix metro area, appointments are typically available within a few days. In smaller Arizona cities, wait times may be longer.

Day of appointment: Livescan electronic fingerprinting — no ink. Bring required identification. Appointment takes approximately 15-30 minutes.

Reference number: Issued immediately after appointment — reference number starts with "A." Record it carefully.

Mailing Form L-FPV: Standard mail to DIFI takes 3-5 business days depending on proximity to Phoenix.

DIFI receipt and processing: After receiving Form L-FPV, DIFI initiates FBI background check. Processing: typically 1-3 weeks.

Total fingerprint process time: Plan for approximately 2-3 weeks from exam pass to DIFI receiving complete fingerprint package.

## If Your Application Is Denied

DIFI provides written notice if your application is denied. You have rights including:

Requesting a hearing to contest the denial

Appealing through Arizona's administrative procedures

Addressing the underlying concern and reapplying

Some denials are not permanent. Applicants who address the concerns raised may successfully reapply after demonstrating rehabilitation or resolving outstanding issues.

## After License Issuance

License issued electronically. Check your DIFI account and the email on file for notification.

CE planning. Your CE deadline is your license expiration date — the last day of your birth month — not an anniversary of the day your license was issued. With 48 hours required (including 6 ethics hours) before that date, planning early avoids last-minute rushes.

License renewal. Arizona licenses expire on the last day of your birth month and renew on a 4-year cycle through NIPR. One catch for first-time licensees: per DIFI, when the Department issues a license to someone who does not already hold one, "the license term is between three and four years and expires on the last day of the licensee's birth month." Only future renewals are a full four-year term.

Carrier appointments. Contact carriers for appointment paperwork.

Assumed name (if applicable). File Form L-193 with DIFI and register trade name with Arizona Secretary of State if operating under a DBA.

## The Relocating Producer Provisions — Moving TO Arizona

Arizona has a streamlined path for producers relocating from another state:

Exam exemption: If you held an active resident license in another state for the same line of authority, Arizona exempts you from the state exam. This is a meaningful benefit — no exam fee ($42-$59 depending on your line of authority), no exam prep, and immediate access to the state's insurance market.

Required steps for relocating producers:

Apply for the Arizona resident producer license through NIPR ($120 fee) — DIFI must receive this application within 90 days after your prior resident license is inactivated

Complete Arizona electronic fingerprinting through Fieldprint (DIFI's guidance is to schedule the Fieldprint appointment *after* submitting the NIPR application, not before)

Submit Form L-FPV to DIFI with Fieldprint reference number

Include a clearance letter from your former state showing the cancellation date, your lines of authority, and that you were in good standing (DIFI can waive the letter if it can verify the prior license and inactivation date on the Producer Database)

Submit Form L-152 (Lawful Presence) if not covered by electronic fingerprinting

The 90-day window — and where people get it wrong: the clock starts when your prior resident license is cancelled, expired, or converted to nonresident status. It does **not** start when the clearance letter reaches you. DIFI's bulletin requires the application to reach the Department within 90 days after you inactivate the former resident license, and A.R.S. § 20-288 conditions the exam exemption on the application being "received within ninety days after the cancellation of the resident license that the applicant held in another state." Because clearance letters are often issued and delivered days or weeks after cancellation, anchoring your deadline to the letter can quietly push you past the statutory window and cost you the exam waiver entirely. Count from the cancellation date on the letter, not the date you received it.

Same license type and line requirement: The exam exemption applies only when Arizona offers the exact same license type and line of authority you held. If you had a Life license in your prior state and Arizona offers Life, you're exempt from the Life exam. If your prior state had a license type Arizona doesn't issue, the exam may be required.

[Continuing education](/arizona/continuing-education) for relocating producers: Non-residents in good standing in their home state are exempt from Arizona CE. For new Arizona residents, CE requirements apply going forward.

## The Relocating Producer Provisions — Moving FROM Arizona

When moving from Arizona to another state:

Form L-CLR: Complete and email Form L-CLR (Request for Letter of Clearance) to insurancelicensing@difi.az.gov.

Timeline: Most states run the same 90-day rule Arizona does, and it is measured from the date your Arizona resident license is cancelled or converted to nonresident status — not from the day the clearance letter lands in your inbox. Request the letter early and file the new state's resident application promptly after cancellation. Confirm the destination state's exact trigger before you rely on it.

Retaining Arizona non-resident license: If you want to retain an Arizona non-resident license after moving, email a copy of your new resident license certificate to DIFI within 30 days of receiving it. This allows DIFI to convert your license to non-resident status without requiring a new non-resident application and fees.

Arizona as non-resident: Arizona non-resident licenses require the $120 fee through NIPR and DIFI verification of home state license status.

## CE Timing for New Licensees

Arizona's 4-year CE cycle creates planning considerations for new licensees, and the first term is the one people miscalculate:

Your first license term is not four years. DIFI states it plainly: when the Department issues a license to someone who does not already hold one, "the license term is between three and four years and expires on the last day of the licensee's birth month." Where your birth month falls relative to your issue date decides the length.

First CE deadline: your license expiration date — the last day of your birth month at the end of that first three-to-four-year term. All 48 hours (including 6 ethics) must be complete before it.

Planning your CE and renewal together: there really is a single date to track, and it's the expiration date on your license, not an anniversary of issuance. Look it up in NIPR or on DIFI's license lookup the week you're licensed and write it down.

Why this matters: a producer who counts four years forward from their issue date can be as much as a year past their real deadline. If DIFI hasn't received your renewal — including your CE — on or before the expiration date, you must stop transacting insurance business, and a late renewal within the following year costs a $100 late renewal fee on top of the renewal fee.

CE carryover warning: Arizona does not allow CE carryover. Excess CE hours cannot be applied to the next 4-year period. Plan your CE completion to land within the term without significant over-completion.

Use the 4-6 week processing window productively:

Complete carrier contracting paperwork. Many carriers allow [pre-licensing](/arizona/prelicensing) training and contracting preparation before formal license issuance.

Develop your initial contact list. Identify first 50-100 prospective clients before license arrives.

Study Arizona market dynamics. Arizona's distinctive retiree and snowbird markets, Phoenix's financial services concentration, and Tucson's unique market all reward advance research.

Complete securities licensing if needed. For variable products, securities licensing can be pursued concurrently.

## 5 Frequently Asked Questions

- What's the most common reason Arizona applications get stalled? Missing or incorrectly submitted Form L-FPV — the fingerprint verification form that must be mailed to DIFI with the Fieldprint reference number. Many applicants complete fingerprinting but forget this required mailing step.
- Do I need to take Arizona's exam if I'm moving from another state? Generally no — if you held an active resident license in another state for the same line of authority Arizona offers, you're exempt from Arizona's exam under A.R.S. § 20-288. DIFI must receive your application within 90 days after your prior resident license was cancelled, expired, or converted to nonresident status — the clock runs from that cancellation date, not from the day you receive the clearance letter.
- Can I keep my Arizona license if I move to another state? Yes. Email a copy of your new resident license to DIFI within 30 days of receiving it, and DIFI can convert your Arizona license to non-resident status. Otherwise, email Form L-CLR for clearance.
- When is my Arizona CE actually due? By your license expiration date — the last day of your birth month — not four years from your issue date. Your first term runs between three and four years depending on where your birth month falls; renewals after that are full four-year terms. You need 48 hours, including 6 ethics hours, completed before that expiration date.
