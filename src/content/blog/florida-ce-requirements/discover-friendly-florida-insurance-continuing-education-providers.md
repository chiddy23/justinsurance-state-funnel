Understand [Florida continuing education](/florida/continuing-education)

You might feel excited about becoming a licensed insurance agent in Florida, but staying compliant over the long haul can be just as crucial as passing your initial exam. Florida insurance [continuing education](/florida/continuing-education) providers offer the courses you need to maintain your license, keep your knowledge fresh, and protect your clients’ interests. If you are new to the ongoing requirements, you are in the right place. By the time you finish reading, you will have a clear path forward to meet your Florida continuing education (CE) obligations.

Every state writes its own compliance rules, and Florida's are straightforward once you know what to expect — starting with one that surprises a lot of new agents. Your Florida license is perpetual. The state does not make you renew it, and it has no expiration date. What you do have to keep up with is a set number of CE hours every two years on topics prescribed by the [Florida Department of Financial Services](/florida/requirements), plus an active appointment, which renews every 24 months during your birth month. Miss the CE and the appointments are what fall over. While it might seem daunting at first, the right approach and a friendly provider make the process manageable.

## Fulfill your credit requirements

Because Florida's rules differ from other states, it is important to understand exactly what your license mandates. Requirements vary depending on how long you have held your license and the type of coverage you sell. If you are a new licensee, you can expect to complete a set of core topics, including ethics and state-specific regulations.

How many hours you owe depends on how long you have been licensed. Under Fla. Stat. §626.2815, every licensee (other than a title agent) takes a 4-hour update course specific to the license held, and then adds elective hours on a sliding scale:

-   Fewer than 6 years licensed: 20 elective hours, for 24 hours total.
-   6 or more years licensed: 16 elective hours, for 20 hours total.
-   25 or more years licensed, and you hold a CLU or CPCU or a bachelor's degree or higher in risk management or insurance: 6 elective hours, for 10 hours total.

So the familiar "24 hours" number is really the new-licensee tier. If you have been licensed longer, you owe less — and you should verify any line-of-authority specifics for your license class before you buy. Keep in mind that the state also updates its rules periodically, so even seasoned agents need to stay alert.

## Compare providers for the best fit

With so many Florida insurance continuing education providers out there, how do you choose one? Your first step is to search for a provider that is approved by the Florida Department of Financial Services. If a provider lacks official approval, you cannot count their courses toward your CE credits.

Once you confirm their approval status, the provider’s course format and overall style become just as critical. You want a friendly, flexible program that fits into your schedule, especially if you juggle policyholder phone calls and appointments throughout the day.

Here are a few considerations to guide your decision:

-   Course variety: Choose a program with multiple course options relevant to your specific lines of authority.
-   Instructional style: Make sure the lessons and practice tests match your learning preferences, whether you like video, self-paced text, or interactive modules.
-   Customer support: Seek out providers who can address questions quickly, either via email or phone, so you are never left wondering about technical issues or deadlines.
-   Budget and value: Compare not just the price but also the quality of content, study tools, and availability of updates.

If you can, chat with colleagues or friends who have earned CE credits recently. Their firsthand experiences often reveal which providers deliver on their promises.

## Track and maintain compliance

Beyond just finding the right courses, you need a reliable way to track your credits. Florida typically asks providers to report your exam completions digitally, but it is always smart to keep personal records as well.

In most cases, you can create or log into a MyProfile account with the Florida Department of Financial Services to double-check CE progress. The provider you choose might also have a student dashboard where you can see your completed modules and upcoming deadlines. Paying attention to these details helps you avoid last-minute panics.

Try to spread out your courses so you do not rush to finish them all in a single week. That way, you will actually absorb important updates and enhance your skills, rather than merely checking off a requirement. By thinking long term, you will gain new strategies to serve your clients better while setting yourself up to close each compliance period without a scramble.

## FAQ about Florida insurance continuing education

1.  How often do I need to renew my [Florida insurance license](/florida)?  
    You don't. A Florida resident insurance license is perpetual — the Department of Financial Services does not issue license renewals and the license has no expiration date. Two things do run on a two-year clock: your appointment, which renews every 24 months during your birth month, and your CE compliance period, which is 24 months long and closes on the last day of your birth month. Fall short on CE and your appointments can be cancelled, and a license that sits 48 months without an appointment expires. So the biennial rhythm is real — it is just an appointment and CE deadline, not a license renewal.
2.  How many CE hours am I required to complete?  
    It depends on your tenure. Every two-year cycle you take a 4-hour license-specific law and ethics update course, plus 20 elective hours if you have been licensed fewer than 6 years (24 total), 16 elective hours once you hit 6 years (20 total), or 6 elective hours at 25 or more years if you hold a CLU, CPCU, or a bachelor's degree or higher in risk management or insurance (10 total). Always verify any line-of-authority specifics on the Florida Department of Financial Services website.
3.  Can I earn extra hours and roll them over to the next compliance cycle?  
    Yes. Florida allows you to carry forward up to 24 excess CE hours into your next two-year cycle. Carried-over ethics hours only count as general credit, so you still need to complete 4 new Florida law and ethics hours each cycle.
4.  What if I have multiple lines of insurance, like life and health?  
    If you hold multiple Florida licenses, check your exact credit requirements for each license type. Many times, core topics overlap, but you will need to ensure you meet the total credit hours for all lines rather than assuming one set covers everything.
5.  How do I confirm that my credits have been reported?  
    Your provider should submit your completed credits electronically to the Florida Department of Financial Services. You can confirm by logging into your MyProfile account. You can also keep personal records in case you need proof at any point.
