If you're studying for the Louisiana PSI insurance exam and want to pass on the first attempt — rather than paying the $36 exam fee twice and spending another week waiting to retake — this guide is for you. Louisiana's exam is intentionally challenging, and the absence of a required [prelicensing](/louisiana/prelicensing) course means many candidates sit the exam underprepared. Understanding the exam's structure, prioritizing the right content areas, and applying proven test-taking strategies on exam day makes the difference between walking out with a score report and walking out with a license.

## Why the Louisiana Exam Is Harder Than It Looks

Louisiana does not require prelicensing education, which creates a false sense of accessibility. Because you can schedule the PSI exam immediately without completing any coursework, many candidates underestimate the depth of knowledge being tested. The LDI intentionally designs its licensing exams to be difficult — their stated purpose is to protect Louisiana consumers from underprepared agents, and that purpose is reflected in the exam content.

The exam tests both general insurance knowledge and Louisiana-specific laws, regulations, and ethics. The state-specific content is where underprepared candidates most commonly lose points. If your study preparation has been limited to general insurance concepts without specific attention to Louisiana law — Title 22, the Insurance Code, LDI regulations, auto law, and workers' comp — you are walking in with a significant gap.

## Understanding the Two Content Areas

Every Louisiana licensing exam covers two sections in an integrated format: general insurance content and Louisiana state-specific content. Both contribute to your single overall 70% score. There are no separate passing thresholds for each section — you need 70% across the combined exam.

The general content covers principles that are consistent across states: policy types, coverage structures, insuring agreements, exclusions, endorsements, beneficiary rules, underwriting basics, and the general mechanics of insurance products for your line. This content is well-covered by quality exam prep courses and most candidates find it more approachable than the state content.

The Louisiana state content is where most failures happen. This section covers Title 22 of the Louisiana Revised Statutes (the Louisiana Insurance Code), LDI producer licensing requirements, unfair trade practices and producer conduct standards, Louisiana auto insurance law including the new modified comparative fault standard effective January 1, 2026 (Act 15), Louisiana workers' compensation basics, the Annuity Best Interest standard effective September 20, 2024, and Healthy Louisiana (Medicaid expansion) and Healthcare.gov marketplace structure.

## High-Priority Topics by Line

For the Life exam, prioritize: types of life insurance (term, whole, universal, variable, and their policy mechanics), annuity types and the Louisiana Annuity Best Interest standard (806 LAC 19:, effective September 20, 2024), beneficiary designations, producer licensing requirements and grounds for discipline under Title 22, and unfair trade practices.

For the Property & Casualty exam, prioritize: Louisiana auto minimums (15/30/25), the modified comparative fault system (51% bar, effective January 1, 2026), the No Pay No Play statute, UM/UIM coverage in Louisiana, workers' compensation (NCCI, LWCC, 1+ employee threshold, Louisiana Workforce Commission Office of Workers' Compensation Administration), NFIP flood insurance (ongoing every renewal period in Louisiana — not a one-time requirement), and LDI producer conduct obligations.

For the Health & Accident exam, prioritize: group vs. individual health insurance structures, COBRA continuation, Healthy Louisiana (state Medicaid program, expanded June 1, 2016, 138% FPL), Healthcare.gov marketplace (Louisiana uses the federal exchange, not a state-based exchange), LTC initial training requirements (8-hour initial, 4-hour ongoing per renewal), and disability income concepts.

## Test-Taking Strategy on Exam Day

Read every question completely before selecting an answer. Louisiana exam questions frequently contain partially correct distractors — answer choices that are true statements but do not answer the specific question asked. The correct answer must fully and precisely address the question as written. When two answers look similar, identify which is more technically precise under Louisiana law.

Use the exam's navigation features strategically. Mark questions you are uncertain about and continue through the exam before returning to them. You may find that later questions trigger recall that helps you answer earlier uncertain ones. Do not spend disproportionate time on any single question — every question carries equal weight.

There is no penalty for incorrect answers on the Louisiana exam. Guess rather than leave any question blank. If you have eliminated two of four options and genuinely cannot identify the correct answer between the remaining two, choose one and move forward. A 50% guess is better than a zero.

## Preparing for the Louisiana State Law Content Specifically

The state law section of the Louisiana exam is the area most commonly cited by failed candidates as the section that cost them their passing score. A few specific preparation strategies address this gap directly.

First, study Louisiana auto law in depth. The transition from pure comparative fault to modified 51% comparative fault effective January 1, 2026, the 15/30/25 minimum liability requirements, the No Pay No Play statute, the two-year PI statute of limitations (increased from one year effective July 1, 2024), and UM/UIM coverage rules are all testable. Second, understand the Annuity Best Interest standard's four obligations — care, disclosure, conflict of interest, documentation — and when it applies. Third, understand Healthy Louisiana as the state Medicaid program and Healthcare.gov as the exchange platform. Fourth, review producer licensing requirements, renewal obligations, and the grounds for LDI disciplinary action under Title 22.
