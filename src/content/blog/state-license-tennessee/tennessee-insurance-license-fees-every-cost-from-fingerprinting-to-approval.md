Getting a Tennessee insurance license has a defined, predictable cost structure — but the total depends on which lines you pursue, whether you book those exams in one session or separately, how many attempts you need to pass, and whether you choose to use a prep course. One thing that does *not* move the number: Tennessee charges the same exam fee whether you test at a Pearson VUE center or remotely through OnVUE. Candidates who understand every fee before they begin avoid the surprise of discovering mid-process that the costs are higher than expected. This post breaks down every cost in the Tennessee licensing process — exam fees, fingerprinting, application fees, and optional costs — with exact figures for every scenario, from a single-line candidate to a full four-line applicant, so you can budget accurately before you begin.

## The Four Fee Categories in Tennessee Licensing

The Tennessee insurance licensing process has four distinct cost categories. Every candidate pays fees in at least three of them:

Category 1 — Exam fees: Paid to Pearson VUE per exam per attempt. The cost depends on how many lines you sit for, whether you book them in a single session, and how many attempts you need.

Category 2 — Fingerprinting and background check: Paid to IdentoGO. A one-time cost per applicant regardless of how many lines you pursue.

Category 3 — License application fees: A single $50 filing fee paid to the Tennessee Department of Commerce and Insurance through NIPR per application — one flat fee regardless of how many lines of authority the application covers. Plus the NIPR transaction processing fee per application submission.

Category 4 — Optional preparation costs: Paid to a prep course provider. Tennessee does not require prelicensing education, so this category is genuinely optional — but because every failed attempt costs another full exam fee, it is the one category where spending can reduce your total. (Neither the TDCI nor Pearson VUE publishes a Tennessee first-time pass rate; our own student results are documented on our [pass rates](/pass-rates) page.)

## Category 1: Exam Fees

### Remote vs. In-Person

Pearson VUE offers two delivery formats for Tennessee insurance exams — a physical test center or OnVUE online proctoring — and for a single line the fee is identical either way. The multi-line combinations, however, are sold only on the OnVUE side; at a test center the handbook requires each single-line exam to be taken alone in one session. Here is the fee schedule published in Pearson VUE's Tennessee Insurance candidate handbook:

| Exam | Fee |
|---|---|
| Life (taken alone) | $55 |
| Accident & Health (taken alone) | $55 |
| Property (taken alone) | $55 |
| Casualty (taken alone) | $55 |
| Personal Lines (taken alone) | $55 |
| Title | $55 |
| Public Adjuster | $50 |
| Life and Accident & Health (one OnVUE session) | $80 |
| Property and Casualty (one OnVUE session) | $80 |
| Life, Accident & Health, and Title (one OnVUE session) | $125 |
| Property, Casualty, and Title (one OnVUE session) | $125 |

If you are testing one line, there is no price difference between formats, so the remote-versus-in-person decision should be made on your technical setup, your preference for the testing environment, and whether your system passes the OnVUE compatibility check — not on cost. Choosing remote and then discovering your internet connection is unstable on exam day is a bad trade with no offsetting savings. If you are testing two lines, remote carries a genuine $30 discount, but weigh it against a $55 retake fee: the saving is not worth a shaky connection.

Where money is genuinely on the table is scheduling. Pearson VUE prices certain multi-exam OnVUE sessions as bundles: Property and Casualty booked in one session is $80 rather than $110 for the same two exams booked separately, and the Life and Accident & Health pairing is priced the same way. Fees are non-refundable and non-transferable, so confirm the amount on your reservation screen before you pay.

### The Retake Cost Reality

Every failed attempt on any line requires paying the full $55 exam fee again for that line. There is no reduced retake fee in Tennessee and no partial credit for having previously paid. You can attempt as many times as needed — but each attempt carries the full cost, and Pearson VUE's Tennessee handbook adds two constraints worth budgeting around: you must wait ten days after a first failure and thirty days after each subsequent failure, and you are limited to two OnVUE attempts per line, after which additional attempts must be taken at a test center.

The retake cost argument for preparation: at $55 per attempt, a candidate who takes the Property exam three times before passing has spent $165 on exam fees for that single line alone, and the mandatory waiting periods stretch that across at least six weeks of delayed licensure and delayed commissions. We are not going to quote you a pass-rate lift here, because no one publishes a Tennessee first-time pass rate to compare against and we will not invent one. The arithmetic that does hold up is simpler: a couple of avoided retakes covers the cost of most prep courses, and our own Tennessee course is $199 for the combined Life and Health package.

### Cancellation and Rescheduling

If you need to cancel or reschedule your exam, Pearson VUE requires at least 48 hours advance notice. Cancellations or changes made less than 48 hours before your scheduled appointment result in forfeiture of the full exam fee — the same cost as a failed attempt. Calendar your exam appointment prominently and set a reminder for the 48-hour cancellation deadline so you do not inadvertently forfeit a fee for a scheduling conflict that could have been addressed earlier.

## Category 2: Fingerprinting and Background Check

### IdentoGO Fee

This is a flat fee paid at your IdentoGO appointment regardless of how many lines of authority you are pursuing. A candidate applying for one line pays $37.15. A candidate applying for all four major lines pays the same $37.15 — fingerprinting is per applicant, not per line.

What the $37.15 covers: Electronic fingerprint capture and submission to both the Tennessee Bureau of Investigation (TBI) and the FBI for criminal history record checks. The background check results are reported to the TDCI as part of your license application review.

Who pays this fee: Only Tennessee resident applicants. Non-resident applicants are not subject to Tennessee's fingerprinting requirement — they comply with their home state's background check requirements.

When to pay: At your IdentoGO appointment, which must be scheduled and completed before you submit your NIPR application. The $37.15 is paid directly to IdentoGO at the time of the appointment — it is not paid through NIPR.

Appointment timing: Fingerprinting must be completed at least 2 business days before you submit your license application. Schedule your IdentoGO appointment promptly after passing your exam to avoid unnecessary delays in application submission.

## Category 3: License Application Fees

### TDCI Application Fee

The $50 filing fee is charged per application, not per line of authority. A candidate applying for Property and Casualty in a single application pays one $50 TDCI fee — the same $50 whether that application covers one line or all four major lines. What multiplies the fee is filing at separate times: each separate application (including adding a line later by amendment) carries its own $50 filing fee.

The $5.60 NIPR transaction processing fee is a flat fee per application submission — not per line. When you submit a single NIPR application covering multiple lines at once, you pay $5.60 once for that submission, regardless of how many lines are included.

The efficiency of applying for multiple lines at once: Applying for all intended lines in a single NIPR submission is more cost-efficient than applying for lines in stages. A candidate who applies for Property and Casualty together in one submission pays one $50 TDCI filing fee and one $5.60 NIPR fee. A candidate who applies for Property first and then adds Casualty three months later in a separate submission pays the $50 filing fee twice and the $5.60 NIPR fee twice — an extra $55.60 for splitting the filing. Applying for all intended lines together is both cheaper and faster.

### Application Fee by Lines Pursued

Because the $50 TDCI fee is charged per application rather than per line, filing every line in a single NIPR application costs the same $50 no matter how many lines you include:

| Lines pursued (single application) | TDCI filing fee | NIPR transaction fee | Total |
|---|---|---|---|
| One line | $50 | $5.60 | $55.60 |
| Two lines (e.g., Property and Casualty) | $50 | $5.60 | $55.60 |
| Three lines | $50 | $5.60 | $55.60 |
| Four lines | $50 | $5.60 | $55.60 |

NIPR itemizes the state filing fee and its own transaction fee separately at checkout, so confirm the exact total on the payment screen before you submit — that screen, not any third-party summary, is the authoritative figure for your specific application.

### When to Pay

Application fees are paid through NIPR at the time of application submission — by credit card or debit card. The $5.60 NIPR transaction fee is added automatically by the NIPR system at checkout. There is no option to pay by check or cash through the NIPR portal.

### The 48-Hour Waiting Period

Tennessee requires applicants to wait at least 48 hours after passing the exam before submitting the NIPR application. This is a TDCI requirement — not a NIPR limitation. Do not attempt to submit your application immediately after passing. Wait the full 48 hours.

## Ongoing Costs After Licensure

The initial licensing cost is a one-time investment — but Tennessee producers face recurring costs throughout their licensing careers.

### License Renewal

Tennessee licenses renew biennially — every two years — on the last day of the birth month. The standard renewal fee is $60 for a license carrying at least one major line or Title ($30 for a limited-line-only license), paid through NIPR at the time of renewal, plus the NIPR transaction fee.

### Late Renewal Fees

Tennessee has no post-expiration grace period. A renewal submitted after the due date — but within one year of the expiration date — can still reinstate the license without retaking the exam, but Tenn. Code Ann. § 56-6-107(d) requires a penalty of double the unpaid renewal fee: $120 for a license carrying at least one major line or Title, and $60 for a limited-line-only license. After one year, the producer must retake the Pearson VUE exam and reapply — the full initial licensing cost applies again.

### Continuing Education Costs

CE costs vary by provider and course selection. Tennessee requires 24 hours of CE per biennial period including 3 hours of ethics. Self-paced online courses typically cost $5–$15 per hour of CE credit. A full 24-hour CE package from a reputable Tennessee provider typically costs $50–$150, depending on provider and format.

### Specialty Training Costs

Producers who sell specific product types incur additional, product-gated training costs. These are not optional — you cannot solicit the product until the training is on file:

Annuities: a state-approved annuity suitability and best interest course must be completed before you sell, solicit, or negotiate any annuity product, plus carrier-specific product training for each annuity you offer. The carrier product training is normally provided free by the carrier.

Long-term care: a state-approved long-term care training course is required before you sell LTC, with periodic ongoing training after that. Selling Long-Term Care Partnership policies requires TDCI-approved training specifically.

Flood: producers writing NFIP flood policies must meet the federal flood insurance training requirement.

Approved-provider catalogs currently list the annuity course at four hours and the initial long-term care course at eight hours with a four-hour refresher on a recurring cycle, but hour requirements change — confirm the current figures on the TDCI's producer resources page before you buy, since you are paying by the credit hour.

Specialty training courses approved by the TDCI count toward the 24-hour CE total — they are not additional hours on top of CE, so the marginal cost of these requirements is usually zero if you plan your CE around them.

## Non-Resident Licensing Costs

Producers licensed in other states who want a Tennessee non-resident license face a different cost structure than resident applicants.

Non-resident applicants do not pay Tennessee's fingerprinting fee — the IdentoGO requirement applies to resident applicants only. Non-resident applicants are subject to their home state's background check requirements.

Tennessee maintains reciprocity agreements with other states, so most non-resident applicants — including producers from neighboring Georgia, North Carolina, Kentucky, Alabama, Arkansas, Missouri, and Virginia — do not need to take the Tennessee exam, provided their home state license is active, in good standing, and covers the matching lines of authority. Confirm your specific home state's reciprocity status with the TDCI before applying.

For non-resident producers who qualify for reciprocity, the cost is only the application fee and NIPR transaction fee: $55.60 for the application (the $50 filing fee plus the $5.60 NIPR fee), regardless of how many lines that single application covers. Non-resident applicants whose home state does not satisfy Tennessee's reciprocity standard must pass the Tennessee Pearson VUE exam to obtain a Tennessee non-resident license.

## Frequently Asked Questions

I want to get licensed in Property and Casualty. If I take both exams on the same day and pass both, do I pay two NIPR transaction fees when I apply?

No. When you submit a single NIPR application covering both Property and Casualty, you pay one $50 TDCI filing fee and one $5.60 NIPR transaction fee — both are per-application charges, not per-line charges, so covering two lines in one application doubles neither. You pay $50 in state filing fees for Property and Casualty together, not $100. Submitting both lines in one NIPR application is both more cost-efficient (one $50 filing fee and one $5.60 fee, instead of two of each) and more time-efficient than submitting separate applications.

I failed the Casualty exam and need to retake it. Does retaking the exam require me to redo the fingerprinting or submit a new NIPR application?

No. Fingerprinting and the background check are completed once per applicant — not once per exam attempt. You do not need to return to IdentoGO or pay the $37.15 fee again for a retake. Simply reschedule through Pearson VUE, pay the retake exam fee ($55 for a single-line exam), and take the exam again — after the mandatory ten-day wait that follows a first failure. If you have already submitted your NIPR application for Property authority (which you passed) while awaiting your Casualty retake, you will submit a separate NIPR application for Casualty after passing that retake — paying the $50 TDCI fee and $5.60 NIPR fee for that separate submission. One fingerprinting covers your full licensing history in Tennessee regardless of how many lines or applications follow.

I am budgeting for the full licensing process. What is a realistic total budget to plan for?

Start with the fixed costs, because those are published and knowable. For a candidate pursuing Property and Casualty — the most common combination for new producers — the mandatory costs are $172.75 if you book both exams in a single OnVUE session ($202.75 booked separately): exam fees, the $37.15 fingerprint, a single $50 TDCI filing fee, and the $5.60 NIPR transaction fee. All four major lines runs $252.75 to $312.75 on the same basis. Then add your prep course, which is the one variable you choose — ours is $199 for combined Life and Health. Finally, budget $55 per failed attempt as a contingency; that is the exact retake cost, not a range. Budget for one retake per line as a reasonable contingency; if you do not need it, the money stays in your pocket.

Tennessee's licensing fee structure is transparent and fixed — every fee is published, every cost is predictable, and the total investment for any combination of lines can be calculated precisely before you begin. The two decisions that actually move your total are how you book your exams and how well you prepare for them. A prep course is not free money: at $55 a retake, a course only pays for itself in pure exam fees after several avoided attempts. The larger return is time — every failed attempt carries a ten- or thirty-day lockout before you can sit again, and that is income you are not earning while you wait.
