## Start your licensing journey

Landing your insurance license in Tennessee can unlock a variety of career paths, from life and health to property and casualty. If you’ve ever wondered how to get an insurance license in Tennessee, you’re in the right place. This thorough checklist walks you through everything you need to know, step by step.

You might be new to the insurance world or simply exploring a fresh opportunity. Either way, the process is more straightforward than you might think. With the right approach and preparation, you’ll be on your way to helping customers protect their most important assets in no time.

## Your step-by-step checklist

Below is a clear path to follow. It’s designed to make each crucial step in the licensing process feel more achievable and less overwhelming.

1.  Confirm you meet eligibility requirements  
    Tennessee typically requires you to be at least 18 years old, reside in or plan to conduct business in the state, and have a clean criminal record. Make sure you have valid identification and a Social Security number. It’s also wise to review the official guidelines from the [Tennessee Department of Commerce and Insurance](/tennessee/requirements) to confirm that you’re eligible.
2.  Choose the right license type for you  
    Before diving into pre-licensing courses, decide which lines of authority you want to sell. Popular options include Life, Accident & Health or Property & Casualty. You can get both, but note that each license may have distinct exam requirements. Picking the correct focus upfront will keep you from juggling multiple applications later.
3.  Complete your required [Tennessee prelicensing courses](/tennessee/prelicensing)  
    Tennessee mandates a specific number of study hours for each license type. This course work is designed to give you a strong foundation in policy provisions, insurance laws, and best practices. Many providers offer these classes in-person or online, so choose the format that fits your schedule. Be sure to hold onto the certificate you receive when you finish—this is often required to book your exam.
4.  Schedule your official exam  
    Once you’ve completed your pre-licensing prerequisites, select an approved testing center and schedule an exam date. It’s generally recommended to secure your spot early, as testing locations can fill up quickly. Double-check everything from required IDs to testing fees, so you’re not caught off guard on exam day.
5.  Pass the exam with confidence  
    On exam day, arrive at least 30 minutes early to handle any check-in procedures. You’ll typically answer multiple-choice questions covering terminology, state-specific regulations, and policy scenarios. Remember that partial knowledge can trip you up, so commit to a thorough study plan beforehand. Once you pass, you’ll receive an exam score report which you’ll need for your application.
6.  Submit your license application  
    With your exam passed, you can file your application online through the official state portal. Make sure to include all necessary documentation, such as your exam results and application fee. If everything is in order, your license should be issued soon afterward. At this point, you can legally pursue work as a [Tennessee insurance producer](/tennessee).
7.  Maintain your credentials long-term  
    Even after you’re licensed, the journey doesn’t end. Tennessee requires you to complete [continuing education](/tennessee/continuing-education) (CE) courses to keep your license active. Set calendar reminders to start your CE in plenty of time. Staying current with CE helps you keep up with industry changes, so you can continue providing top-quality service.

## Frequently asked questions

### 1\. Do I need to renew my license every year?

No. Tennessee generally requires renewals every two years, though certain license types may have different timelines. Always verify your specific renewal window to avoid accidental lapses.

### 2\. How long should I study for the exam?

That depends on how familiar you are with insurance concepts. Many first-time candidates spend several weeks reviewing their materials and taking practice exams. The key is consistency—regular study sessions will keep the information fresh in your mind.

### 3\. Can I hold more than one license type at once?

Absolutely. It’s common for agents to hold multiple licenses, such as Life & Health and Property & Casualty. Just remember that each line may come with extra fees, exams, or continuing education requirements.

### 4\. Do I need a sponsor to apply?

Not necessarily. While some states require a sponsoring insurer, Tennessee allows individuals to apply on their own. That said, if you already have an employer or agency lined up, their support can help you navigate the details.

### 5\. Is online or in-person pre-licensing better?

Both formats are effective, and the choice usually comes down to personal preference. Online classes offer flexible scheduling, while in-person sessions provide immediate instructor feedback. Pick the option that suits your learning style and availability.
