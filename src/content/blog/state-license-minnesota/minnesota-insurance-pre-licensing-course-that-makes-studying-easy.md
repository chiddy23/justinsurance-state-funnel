## Understand your license needs

Your first move is to confirm which type of Minnesota insurance license you want to pursue. Most new candidates focus on life & health (L&H) or property & casualty (P&C) lines. Each license serves a distinct client need, so it is worth thinking about your future career setting. Are you planning to sell policies that protect families, such as life insurance or health coverage? Or do you envision working with home and auto clients? The more clarity you have about your goals, the easier it is to pinpoint the correct pre-licensing course.

Minnesota requirements include completing a specified number of hours for each line of authority before you can sit for the state exam. That usually involves around 20 hours of formal instruction per line, though it can vary based on L&H, P&C, or other combined licenses. When you start reviewing different providers, make sure any course you choose meets or exceeds the mandated hourly requirement.

If you want to combine lines—for example, life & health plus property & casualty—seek a package that covers both sets of requirements. That often saves you money by bundling courses, and it also helps you keep a consistent study routine.

## Plan your study schedule

Finding the time to complete your pre-licensing program often feels like the trickiest part of the entire process. Yet, consistency is key. Think about your daily routine and block off a few hours each week for lessons, reading, and practice quizzes. Small, repeated sessions add up quickly and reduce the risk of cramming at the last minute.

Try to study at the same time each day if possible. You will settle into a pattern and remember content more easily because your mind gets used to focusing at that set hour. If life happens and you miss a study session, simply reschedule it. Getting off track for a day or two does not mean you cannot bounce back.

## FAQs about Minnesota insurance pre-licensing

1\. How many hours are required for Minnesota pre-licensing?  
It depends on your chosen line of authority. Most lines need at least 20 hours of study each, though some combined licenses may involve more. Verify the exact hour requirement through the [Minnesota Department of Commerce](/minnesota/requirements) or your course provider’s website.

2\. What is the difference between life & health and property & casualty licenses?  
A life & health license covers products such as term life insurance, whole life, and health insurance plans. A property & casualty license typically focuses on home, auto, and liability policies. Your career interests usually guide which license (or licenses) you pursue.

3\. Does my course provider need to be state-approved?  
Yes. Minnesota requires you to complete your hours through a state-approved or accredited provider. Always confirm that the course you select meets the licensing requirements, and keep your completion certificate for reference.

4\. How do I know if I am ready for the exam?  
Track your scores on practice tests. If you are consistently reaching or exceeding the passing threshold—usually around 70 to 75 percent—then it is a strong signal you are prepared. Your pre-licensing course may also offer final quizzes designed to replicate exam conditions.

5\. What if I want extra support beyond the course?  
Many providers, including JustInsurance, offer additional resources like live instructor sessions, additional practice exams, or one-on-one tutoring. If you find topics confusing, look for programs that offer personalized training or the ability to email questions to expert instructors.
