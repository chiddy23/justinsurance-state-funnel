Colorado's insurance [prelicensing](/colorado/prelicensing) requirement is one of the defining features of its licensing process — and one of the most frequently misunderstood. Unlike Virginia, where no prelicensing is required for most lines, or New Jersey, where the requirement is 20 hours per line, Colorado mandates 50 hours of state-approved education per line of authority before a candidate can sit for the Pearson VUE licensing exam. Those 50 hours are structured with a specific breakdown, must conclude with a proctored Certificate Exam, and are valid for exactly one year. Understanding how the prelicensing requirement works — what counts, what does not, and what to do after completion — is the foundation for getting through the licensing process efficiently.

## The 50-Hour Structure: What It Includes

Colorado's 50-hour prelicensing requirement for each major line of authority divides into two components:

40 hours — General insurance content: This portion covers the fundamental principles of insurance applicable to the specific line of authority being pursued. For a Life course, this includes policy types, riders, provisions, annuities, retirement planning concepts, and application/delivery procedures. For Property and Casualty, this includes property coverage concepts, liability principles, policy structures, and claims procedures. The general content is comparable across many states — it is the foundation of insurance product knowledge.

10 hours — Colorado-specific content: This portion is mandatory for every Colorado prelicensing course regardless of the line of authority and covers:

The Legal Concepts and Regulations component is the most directly exam-relevant of the three — it covers Title 10 of the Colorado Revised Statutes, the structure and authority of the DORA Division of Insurance, producer licensing requirements and obligations, and line-specific Colorado regulatory requirements that appear in the state law section of the Pearson VUE exam.

## Hours by License Type

Colorado's prelicensing structure varies by the license being pursued:

Life and Accident & Health are each a separate line of authority in Colorado — there is no combined course or combined exam for the two. Candidates pursuing both complete the full 50-hour Life course and the full 50-hour Health course separately (100 hours total) and pass two separate Pearson VUE exams. For Property and Casualty, the same principle applies — each is a separate line requiring its own 50-hour course, and there is no combined "P&C" prelicensing option either.

## The Certificate Exam: Required Before the State Exam

At the conclusion of each prelicensing course, every Colorado candidate must pass a Certificate Exam with a score of 70% or higher. This is not optional — it is a mandatory step between completing the prelicensing course and taking the Pearson VUE state exam.

Certificate Exam proctoring requirements:

The Certificate Exam must be administered by a disinterested third party who:

Is not a minor

Is not related to the candidate

Is not the candidate's immediate supervisor or employee

Must be physically present for the entire exam duration

Must follow all applicable Colorado state proctoring rules

This requirement distinguishes Colorado's prelicensing from online self-study programs in states with less rigorous prelicensing oversight. The disinterested third party requirement ensures that the Certificate Exam is a genuine assessment, not an unproctored formality.

Once you pass the Certificate Exam, your prelicensing provider:

Reviews and processes your Certificate of Completion

Reports your course completion to the state (typically via daily reporting to Sircon)

Issues your Certificate of Completion for your records

Your prelicensing provider will report your completion information to Pearson VUE. When scheduling your Pearson VUE state exam, you must provide your 5-digit training school code (each approved provider has its own code) at the time of booking.

## Certificate Validity: 1 Year

Your Certificate of Completion is valid for one year from the date you complete the course. You must take and pass the Pearson VUE state exam within this one-year window. If the certificate expires before you pass the exam, you must complete a new prelicensing course before taking the exam again.

This one-year clock is one of the most important deadlines to track in the Colorado licensing process. Candidates who complete prelicensing and then delay exam scheduling — due to work schedules, life events, or lack of urgency — occasionally find their certificate has expired when they finally go to schedule their exam. Schedule your exam promptly after completing your prelicensing course to avoid this situation.

## Online vs. Classroom Prelicensing

Colorado-approved prelicensing courses are available in both formats:

Online/self-paced: The most common choice. Candidates work through course content on their own schedule. Lessons must be completed sequentially — Colorado does not allow candidates to skip ahead or take the Certificate Exam before completing all lesson content. This sequential completion requirement is enforced by approved providers.

In-person classroom: Available through some providers. Classroom format can be beneficial for candidates who learn better in structured environments or who want direct instructor access for questions.

Both formats satisfy the prelicensing requirement equally — there is no distinction in the state exam or license application process based on how prelicensing was completed.

## Common Pitfalls to Avoid

Name mismatch: Register for your prelicensing course, your Pearson VUE exam, and your license application using exactly the same name as on your government-issued ID. Even minor discrepancies (middle name included in one registration but not another, nickname vs. legal name) can delay your certificate or invalidate progress.

Skipping the Colorado-specific content: The 10 Colorado-specific hours are tested directly in the state law section of the Pearson VUE exam. Candidates who rush through this section and focus only on general product knowledge consistently underperform on the state law portion and fail exams they otherwise would have passed.

Missing the certificate validity window: Do not complete prelicensing and then wait several months before scheduling your exam. Build a study plan that flows directly from completing prelicensing into exam preparation and scheduling.

Proctor logistics: Do not wait until the day before your Certificate Exam to identify and arrange a qualified proctor. The disinterested third party requirement eliminates most obvious candidates — plan for this in advance.

## Frequently Asked Questions

Can I complete Colorado's prelicensing course entirely online, or do any portions require in-person attendance?

The standard 50-hour prelicensing course for most lines of authority can be completed entirely online through an approved provider. Both the coursework and the Certificate Exam can be completed in an online environment, provided the Certificate Exam is administered by a disinterested third party who is physically present. The one exception that involves mandatory classroom attendance is the LTC Partnership training required before selling long-term care insurance — 8 of the 16 initial LTC training hours must be completed in a classroom or webinar setting. Standard prelicensing for Life, Accident & Health, Property, Casualty, and Personal Lines, however, can be completed fully online.

How sequential must I complete the prelicensing course? Can I take the Certificate Exam first?

No — Colorado requires that all lessons be completed sequentially before you can take the Certificate Exam. Approved providers enforce this sequential completion requirement in their learning management systems. You cannot access the Certificate Exam until all lesson content has been completed in order. This requirement exists to ensure that candidates have actually engaged with the complete course content before certifying competency. Attempts to bypass sequential completion requirements by using provider access credentials from someone else who completed the course are violations of both the provider's terms and Colorado's licensing regulations.

Does the prelicensing course automatically prepare me for the state exam, or do I need additional study?

Completing the prelicensing course satisfies the legal requirement but is not always sufficient alone to pass the Pearson VUE exam on the first attempt. The [pass rate](/pass-rates) on Colorado insurance licensing exams is approximately 60–65% for first-time candidates nationally. The prelicensing course covers the content, but the Pearson VUE exam tests that content under timed, multiple-choice conditions that require active recall rather than recognition. Most candidates who pass on the first attempt supplement their prelicensing course with practice exams, flashcard review, and focused study of the Colorado state law sections that are most heavily tested. Providers that include [practice exam](/colorado/practice-exam) simulators as part of their prelicensing package give candidates meaningfully better exam preparation than those who only provide study materials.

What happens if I fail the Certificate Exam — can I retake it?

Yes — if you fail the Certificate Exam at the end of your prelicensing course, you can retake it. The specific retake policy varies by provider, but Colorado regulations do not impose a waiting period or attempt limit on the Certificate Exam. Some providers allow immediate retakes; others may require a short waiting period or additional review of course content before retaking. Check your provider's retake policy before starting the course. The Certificate Exam requires a 70% passing score — the same threshold as the Pearson VUE state exam. Candidates who consistently score below 70% on practice questions or the Certificate Exam should dedicate additional study time before scheduling the state exam.

If I already hold an insurance license in another state, do I still need to complete Colorado's 50-hour prelicensing course?

The answer depends on whether you are applying as a resident or non-resident. If you are applying as a Colorado resident — meaning Colorado is your state of domicile — you must complete the full 50-hour prelicensing requirement regardless of licenses held in other states. Colorado does not grant resident prelicensing waivers based on out-of-state licensure. If you are applying as a non-resident — meaning you are licensed and in good standing in your home state — you can obtain a Colorado non-resident license without completing prelicensing education or taking the state exam, as long as your home state has reciprocity with Colorado. Non-resident applicants apply through NIPR or Sircon and pay the $47 per line application fee plus $5.60 NIPR transaction fee.

Candidates who engage seriously with the Colorado-specific content, use practice exams, and pass their Certificate Exam with comfortable margins consistently outperform those who rush through the material to check a compliance box.
