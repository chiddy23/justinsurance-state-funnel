Virginia candidates have two formats for the Prometric insurance licensing exam: in-person at a Prometric test center, or remote via Prometric's ProProctor platform from your own computer. Both cost the same ($35), deliver the same exam content, use the same scoring standard (70% each section), and produce the same score report. The difference is logistics — and the right choice depends entirely on your situation. This post covers every relevant detail about both formats so you can make the decision before you schedule.

## In-Person at a Prometric Test Center

Virginia has six Prometric test center locations within the Commonwealth. You can also test at any of the 300+ Prometric centers nationwide — Virginia does not require you to test in-state.

How to find and schedule: Go to prometric.com/virginia/insurance, click "Schedule Your Test," and enter your preferred location and dates. Centers in Virginia and neighboring states typically have available appointments within a few days of scheduling during normal demand periods.

What to bring:

Two valid government-issued IDs — primary must have photo and signature (driver's license, passport, or military ID); secondary must have valid signature

Your Prometric registration confirmation number (recommended)

No other items — bags, wallets, phones, food, and drinks are stored outside the testing room

Arrival: Report to the test center 30 minutes before your scheduled exam time. The check-in process includes identity verification, biometric intake (palm vein or fingerprint scan at the center), a review of testing rules, and a brief computer tutorial. Check-in takes approximately 15–20 minutes.

During the exam:

No breaks are permitted

No materials, phones, or watches allowed in the testing room

Test center proctors monitor the room in person but cannot answer content questions

If you have a technical issue, raise your hand — the proctor will assist

After the exam: Score appears on your screen immediately. A score report is emailed to you with section-by-section results.

Advantages of in-person testing:

Controlled, distraction-free environment

No technical compatibility concerns

Proctor physically present if issues arise

No risk of technical disqualification

## Remote via Prometric's ProProctor Platform

Virginia insurance exams are available via Prometric's ProProctor remote proctoring platform. You take the exam on your own computer, in your own space, monitored by a Prometric proctor via webcam and screen monitoring.

Technical requirements: Before scheduling, you must confirm your computer is compatible with ProProctor. Check compatibility at rpcandidate.prometric.com. Requirements include:

Desktop or laptop computer (tablets and Chromebooks are not compatible)

No dual-monitor configurations

Webcam and microphone

Stable internet connection

Compatible operating system (Windows or Mac — confirm current OS versions at the compatibility check page)

Test environment requirements:

A private room, alone for the entire exam duration

All doors in the room must be closed (including closet and bathroom doors)

Testing surface must be completely clear of all objects

No phone, smartwatch, or wearable devices

No food or drink

Room must have adequate lighting so the proctor can see you clearly

How to schedule remote: Go to prometric.com/virginia/insurance, click "Schedule Your Test," and select the remote proctored option. Available times are typically more flexible than physical centers — including evenings and weekends in some time slots.

On exam day (remote):

Check in 30 minutes before your appointment through the ProProctor application

The proctor connects via video and verifies your identity using your government-issued ID (hold it up to the webcam)

You perform a 360-degree room scan with your webcam per proctor instructions

The exam launches once the proctor approves your check-in

The proctor monitors you throughout via webcam and screen recording

Technical issues: If you experience technical difficulties during the exam, Prometric has technical support available via live chat. Contact information is in the ProProctor user guide. If the exam is interrupted through no fault of yours (connectivity issue, platform failure), Prometric will work with you to reschedule without additional charge.

Cancellation rule for remote exams: You must cancel at least three calendar days before your scheduled appointment to avoid forfeiting $40 of your exam fee. The same rule applies to in-person exams.

## The Decision Framework

The remote option is not inherently harder or easier — the exam is identical. The format choice is purely about which testing environment allows you to perform at your best.

Choose in-person if:

You don't have a private room at home

Your computer is not ProProctor-compatible

You prefer external accountability and structure

You want to minimize any technical risk on exam day

You live near a Prometric center

Choose remote if:

You have a compatible computer, webcam, and reliable internet

You have a private, distraction-free room available

You prefer testing from home to reduce commute and logistical stress

In-person scheduling is limited in your area or time window

## Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Virginia insurance exam harder in one format versus the other?

No. The exam content, question pool, scoring standard, and time limits are identical for both formats. Prometric delivers the exam using the same software whether you are at a test center or on your own computer at home — the user experience is designed to be the same. Anecdotally, candidates who are easily distracted in test center environments sometimes find remote testing less stressful, while candidates who have noisy or shared home environments or who lack technical confidence sometimes find in-person testing more reliable. Neither format produces systematically better results — performance depends on preparation, not format.

What happens if I have a technical problem during the remote ProProctor exam?

Prometric has a technical support process for remote exam issues. If you experience a problem during the exam — software failure, connectivity disruption, webcam issue — you should first try to resolve it using the resources in the ProProctor user guide (available at prometric.com before your exam). If the issue persists, contact Prometric's support team via live chat or phone. If the technical failure is on Prometric's side (platform issue, proctor-side failure), Prometric will typically reschedule the exam without additional charge. If the failure is on your side (internet outage, power failure, non-compliant testing environment), the outcome depends on the specific circumstances. The safest way to avoid technical issues is to run the ProProctor compatibility check at rpcandidate.prometric.com at least one week before your exam and address any compatibility issues before exam day.

Can I take the Virginia exam at a Prometric test center in another state?

Yes. Virginia allows you to sit for your licensing exam at any Prometric test center in the United States — not only in Virginia. When you schedule at prometric.com/virginia/insurance, you can select any state or region when searching for locations. This is useful for candidates who live near a border (Northern Virginia candidates who might find a Maryland or DC Prometric center more convenient), candidates who are traveling, or candidates seeking an earlier appointment date than Virginia centers have available. The exam delivered is Virginia's exam — the same content, same sections, same scoring standard, regardless of where the test center is physically located.

What if I arrive late to my Prometric in-person appointment?

Prometric's standard policy is that candidates who arrive late may be denied testing and forfeit their exam fee. The check-in process requires 30 minutes — if you arrive after your appointment start time, the center may not be able to complete check-in and still deliver your exam within its scheduled window. If you are running late and have not yet arrived, call Prometric at (866) 891-6396 immediately. Centers have some flexibility for minor delays, but there is no guarantee. The safest approach is to know your route, account for traffic, and arrive 30–45 minutes early rather than the minimum 30.

Does Prometric offer any accessibility accommodations for the Virginia insurance exam?

Yes. Prometric provides testing accommodations for candidates with documented disabilities under the Americans with Disabilities Act. Common accommodations include extended time (typically 1.5× the standard time), a private testing room, large-print materials, and a reader. To request an ADA accommodation, submit documentation to Prometric before scheduling your exam — the processing time is several days, and your exam appointment cannot be scheduled until the accommodation is approved. Prometric also offers English as a Second Language (ESL) extended time (also typically 1.5×) for candidates whose primary language is not English, subject to documentation from an instructor or employer on official letterhead. Request ESL time before scheduling, as it must be pre-approved before your appointment is made.

In-person and remote Prometric exams deliver identical content under identical standards. Choose the format that fits your environment, technology, and temperament — then prepare the same way regardless of how you plan to test.
