Passing the state exam gets you licensed. Staying compliant as a working South Carolina agent is a separate challenge — and the South Carolina Insurance Code (Title 38 of the South Carolina Code of Laws) is the master rulebook you need to navigate. It governs how you sell, market, and service insurance in South Carolina, and understanding its key sections protects your license, your clients, and your career.

Here are the most important areas of the South Carolina Insurance Code every producer needs to understand.

## What the South Carolina Insurance Code Is

The South Carolina Insurance Code is the statutory framework that regulates insurance in the state. It's found in Title 38 of the South Carolina Code of Laws, with producer-specific regulations primarily in Chapter 43 (governed in part by S.C. Code Ann. § 38-43-106, which governs [continuing education](/south-carolina/continuing-education)).

The Code is administered by the South Carolina Department of Insurance (SCDOI), led by the Director of Insurance.

The Code covers everything from licensing and agent duties to policy forms, claims handling, consumer protections, and enforcement. Every licensed agent, broker, adjuster, and insurance company operating in South Carolina is bound by it.

## Key Sections New Producers Should Know

Licensing and Producer Conduct (Chapter 43). These sections cover who can be licensed, what qualifications are required, standards of conduct expected, and SCDOI's authority to discipline licensees. This is the foundation of your legal authority as a South Carolina producer.

Unfair Methods of Competition and Unfair Practices. South Carolina prohibits specific conduct considered unfair or deceptive — misrepresentation, twisting, churning, rebating, defamation, and more. Violations here are the most common source of agent disciplinary action.

Replacement of Life Insurance and Annuities. Specific rules govern the replacement of existing life insurance and annuity contracts. Disclosure requirements, required forms, and notification obligations to the existing insurer are covered.

Unfair Claims Settlement Practices. Rules governing how claims must be handled by insurers and adjusters. Agents need to understand these to advise clients when claims don't go smoothly.

Free-Look Periods. South Carolina requires free-look periods on life insurance and annuity contracts, with longer periods for replacement policies.

Life Insurance Policy Lapse and Grace Periods. South Carolina has specific provisions governing grace periods, reinstatement rights, and the process for policy lapse.

Consumer Protection Laws. Provisions that protect policyholders during the sales process, policy delivery, and ongoing service.

Small Employer Health Insurance. South Carolina regulates the small employer health market with specific rules about guaranteed issue, rating, and disclosures.

Long-Term Care Insurance Standards. Specific rules for LTC sales, including the one-time 8-hour training requirement and 4-hour ongoing training every 24 months.

Annuity Best Interest Standard. South Carolina requires producers to complete a one-time 4-hour Annuity Best Interest training before selling annuities, reflecting enhanced suitability standards for annuity sales.

Coastal and Property Insurance Rules. Given South Carolina's coastal exposure, specific provisions address windstorm and flood insurance in coastal areas.

Privacy and Information Protection. How you collect, store, and share client information is regulated under both South Carolina law and federal frameworks like HIPAA and GLBA.

## The Role of the South Carolina Director of Insurance

South Carolina's insurance regulator is the Director of Insurance, appointed by the Governor. The Director leads the SCDOI and has substantial authority:

Licensing insurance producers and companies

Regulating insurance companies operating in South Carolina

Enforcing the Insurance Code

Investigating consumer complaints

Imposing administrative penalties

Issuing cease and desist orders

Adopting regulations to implement the Code

The SCDOI main office is at PO Box 100105, Columbia, SC 29202-3105, with the main phone line at 803-737-6205. Online access is through doi.sc.gov.

## Why Knowing the Code Matters

New producers sometimes treat the Insurance Code as academic — material to study for the exam and forget afterward. That's a serious mistake.

Every disciplinary action the SCDOI takes against a South Carolina producer is rooted in a specific section of the Code. Understanding the rules isn't just about passing the exam — it's about recognizing what you can and can't do, what you must disclose, and what conduct puts your license at risk.

The SCDOI also publishes bulletins, regulations, and guidance that clarify how specific sections are applied. Following these updates is part of being a compliant working producer.

## South Carolina's Regulatory Environment

Compared to some other states, South Carolina's regulatory environment is generally viewed as:

Well-established with clear, long-standing frameworks

Business-friendly while maintaining consumer protection

Detail-oriented with specific requirements in replacement, suitability, and flood/windstorm handling

Responsive to coastal insurance challenges given the state's hurricane exposure

New producers benefit from South Carolina's relatively clear rules — there's less ambiguity than in some states about what's required.

## Staying Current with Code Changes

The South Carolina General Assembly meets regularly, and insurance-related bills pass periodically. Recent areas of legislative focus have included:

Coastal and hurricane insurance markets

Annuity Best Interest standards

Long-term care rules

Flood insurance requirements

Privacy and data protection

Consumer protection expansions

Subscribe to SCDOI bulletins and review Code updates after each legislative session. Changes affecting your product area could impact how you sell, what you disclose, or what forms you use.

## Distinguishing Code from SCDOI Regulations

The South Carolina Insurance Code and SCDOI regulations are related but distinct:

The Insurance Code (Title 38) is statutory law passed by the South Carolina General Assembly

SCDOI regulations (found in Chapter 69 of the South Carolina Code of Regulations) are administrative rules adopted to implement and enforce the Code

Both are legally binding. "South Carolina insurance law" typically refers to both together.

## How to Stay Compliant

Know the rules for your products. If you sell life insurance, know replacement rules. If you sell annuities, know Best Interest. If you sell LTC, know the specific LTC requirements. If you sell flood or coastal property, know the specific coastal rules.

Document everything. Notes on client conversations, reasons for recommendations, forms signed and retained. Documentation is your primary defense if questions arise.

Follow SCDOI bulletins. Subscribe to SCDOI communications and review them periodically through doi.sc.gov.

Complete your CE seriously. Continuing education is where compliance knowledge stays current. Don't treat it as busywork.

When uncertain, ask. The SCDOI and reputable industry attorneys can provide guidance on specific situations. Getting clarification before acting beats explaining after the fact.

## 5 Frequently Asked Questions

- Do I need to read the entire South Carolina Insurance Code? No. Focus on the sections that apply to your practice — unfair practices, replacement rules, suitability, free-look periods, lapse and grace periods, and the specific product areas you sell.
- Where can I access the South Carolina Insurance Code? Title 38 of the South Carolina Code of Laws is available through the South Carolina Legislature's website (scstatehouse.gov). The SCDOI at doi.sc.gov provides guidance and bulletins about how the Code applies.
- How often does the Insurance Code change? Legislative changes happen periodically. The SCDOI also issues regulations and bulletins regularly. Significant changes are typically publicized through SCDOI notices.
- What happens if I unintentionally violate a Code provision? Intent matters in the SCDOI's evaluation, but it's not a complete defense. Penalties range from warnings to fines, suspension, or revocation depending on severity, history, and circumstances.
- Is the Code the same as the insurance laws tested on the exam? Yes — the state law portion of the South Carolina exam draws directly from the Insurance Code and SCDOI regulations. Studying this content prepares you both for the exam and for your actual practice.
