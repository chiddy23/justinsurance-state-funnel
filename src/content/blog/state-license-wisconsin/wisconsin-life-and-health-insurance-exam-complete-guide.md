Wisconsin's Life and Health insurance exams are PSI-administered tests — each separate (100 questions; 2 hours; $75 each). Wisconsin does NOT offer combined Life & Health exams. On both PSI outlines, "Insurance Regulation" is the single largest section at 35 of the 100 items, and it is Wisconsin regulation: OCI Commissioner authority, Wisconsin Statutes Chapters 600-655 and 628 (intermediaries), producer licensing and discipline, and the s. 628.34 marketing practices. Add BadgerCare Plus (Wisconsin's distinctive partial Medicaid expansion — covering adults at or below 100% FPL, NOT the full ACA 138% FPL expansion), Healthcare.gov (federal marketplace — Wisconsin has no state exchange), the Annuity Best Interest training requirement (2021 Wisconsin Act 260, effective October 1, 2022), and LTC training with Wisconsin Medicaid-specific content requirements (2 hours WI Medicaid-specific in initial training; 1 hour WI-specific in refresher). A roughly 2-calendar-day mandatory waiting period applies before candidates can reschedule after a failed attempt. Here's the complete guide.

## Exam Format and Specs

No combined L&H exam — Life and A&H must be taken as separate PSI exams.

Per-question pacing: 100 questions / 120 minutes = 72 seconds per question — comfortable pacing allowing time for review.

Remote testing (PSI Bridge): Requires Google Chrome; check compatibility at syscheck.bridge.psiexams.com before scheduling.

Retakes: Unlimited; approximately a 2-calendar-day mandatory waiting period before rescheduling. Each retake: $75.

First-time candidates: Select "22-01 Life, General and State Specific" (not "State Specific Series" — that's only for previously licensed producers).

Sections 2.0 through 10.0 (65 items) cover the general A&H body of knowledge — health plan types (HMO, PPO, POS, EPO), disability income, medical plans, group health, dental, Medicare Parts A/B/C/D and Medicare Supplement, long-term care, and federal tax treatment.

Critical WI A&H state law facts inside the 35-item Insurance Regulation section:

Healthcare.gov — most tested WI A&H marketplace fact: Wisconsin does NOT have a state-based ACA exchange. Wisconsin residents purchase marketplace coverage through Healthcare.gov. Open Enrollment: November 1 through January 15 (federal calendar). This distinguishes Wisconsin from Minnesota (MNsure), New Jersey (Get Covered NJ), Maryland (Maryland Health Connection), and Colorado (Connect for Health CO).

BadgerCare Plus — Wisconsin's MOST DISTINCTIVE health law item: BadgerCare Plus is Wisconsin's Medicaid program. Wisconsin's Medicaid approach is uniquely distinctive:

Wisconsin used a Section 1115 waiver to extend Medicaid to adults at or below 100% FPL (NOT the ACA's 138% FPL threshold)

Adults from 100-138% FPL are directed to the Healthcare.gov marketplace, where they receive federal premium tax credits

Wisconsin is NOT a full ACA Medicaid expansion state — it is a "partial expansion" state with this specific structure

This approach is specifically Wisconsin and specifically testable — unlike Indiana (HIP 2.0 to 138% FPL), Missouri (MO HealthNet to 138% FPL), and Minnesota (Medical Assistance to 138% FPL), Wisconsin's coverage stops at 100% FPL for Medicaid

LTC training requirements — WI Medicaid-specific content: Wisconsin layers a state-specific block onto the standard LTC training (s. Ins 3.46(26)):

Initial training: no less than 8 hours, of which 2 hours must contain Wisconsin-specific Medicaid and long-term care information

Ongoing training: one 4-hour LTC course each renewal period, of which 1 credit hour must contain Wisconsin-specific Medicaid training

Be precise about which Medicaid this means — it is not BadgerCare Plus. BadgerCare Plus is Wisconsin's full-coverage acute-care Medicaid for residents age 0-64; it is not the benefit that pays for long-term care. Wisconsin's Medicaid long-term care runs through Family Care, Family Care Partnership, PACE, and IRIS, alongside institutional Medicaid. Confusing the two is an easy way to miss a state-section question.

The state-specific block also drives the nonresident rule: OCI accepts another state's LTC training as satisfying Wisconsin's requirement except for the initial 2 hours of Wisconsin-specific Medicaid and long-term care content.

No Wisconsin individual health insurance mandate.

## 5 Frequently Asked Questions

- What is BadgerCare Plus and why is Wisconsin's Medicaid approach specifically testable? BadgerCare Plus is Wisconsin's Medicaid program name. Wisconsin's distinctive approach extends Medicaid to adults at or below 100% FPL (not the ACA's 138% FPL), directing adults from 100-138% FPL to the Healthcare.gov marketplace with federal subsidies. This "partial expansion" structure — approved through a Section 1115 waiver — is specifically Wisconsin. On the exam, understanding that BadgerCare Plus covers adults at ≤100% FPL (not ≤138% FPL) is the key distinguishing fact.
- Why does Wisconsin's LTC training require Wisconsin-specific Medicaid content? Under s. Ins 3.46(26), Wisconsin's 8-hour initial LTC training must include 2 hours of Wisconsin-specific Medicaid and long-term care information, and the 4-hour ongoing course each renewal period must include 1 credit hour of Wisconsin-specific Medicaid training. Be careful about which program that means: it is not BadgerCare Plus. BadgerCare Plus is Wisconsin's full-coverage acute-care Medicaid for residents age 0-64, and it does not pay for long-term care. Wisconsin delivers Medicaid long-term care through Family Care, Family Care Partnership, PACE, and IRIS, alongside institutional Medicaid — and through the Wisconsin Long-Term Care Insurance Partnership, under which benefits paid by a qualifying partnership policy are excluded when DHS counts a person's resources for Medicaid eligibility and when the state calculates estate recovery. That is the material the Wisconsin-specific hours cover. The same block also sets the nonresident rule: OCI treats another state's LTC training as satisfying Wisconsin's requirement except for the initial 2 hours of Wisconsin-specific Medicaid and long-term care content.
- Why does Wisconsin use PSI instead of Pearson VUE for insurance exams? Wisconsin contracts with PSI Services LLC for its insurance licensing exam administration — the same administrator used by Minnesota and New Jersey. The choice is a state contract decision made by OCI. PSI and Pearson VUE both provide similar exam quality and test center networks — the main differences for candidates are the scheduling website (test-takers.psiexams.com/wiins for Wisconsin), the remote testing platform (PSI Bridge with Chrome requirement), and the PSI-specific diagnostic report format.
- What is the Annuity Best Interest training requirement (2021 Wis. Act 260)? Wisconsin's Annuity Best Interest law took effect October 1, 2022 (2021 Wisconsin Act 260 was signed April 15, 2022). It requires life/variable annuity licensees to complete a one-time 4-hour OCI-approved best interest training course before selling, soliciting, or negotiating annuity products. Intermediaries who had completed an approved annuity suitability course before October 1, 2022 had until April 1, 2023 to complete either the 4-hour course or a 1-hour best interest refresher. Resident and non-resident agents may complete this training in any state with substantially similar laws. If you see a question on this, the operative date is the October 1, 2022 effective date — not the April 15, 2022 signing date.
- What is Wisconsin's "first-time vs. previously licensed" exam distinction? Wisconsin's PSI system offers two exam series for each line: "General and State Specific" (covering both national content and Wisconsin laws — required for first-time candidates) and "State Specific Series" (covering only Wisconsin-specific laws — only for candidates previously licensed in the same line). First-time candidates who accidentally register for the State Specific Series will not be testing the full required content — always select "General and State Specific" for initial licensure.
