## Get familiar with licensing steps

Before you fulfill your continuing education obligations, you first need to obtain your [Georgia insurance license](/georgia). A few straightforward tasks will help you get there.

You will begin by taking a [Georgia insurance prelicensing](/georgia/prelicensing) course, which covers the specific lines of insurance you plan to sell. Each course involves a set number of hours, typically taken online or in person, depending on your preference. While the tuition costs can vary, you can usually expect to pay a fee for the course materials plus the exam. Once you have completed your pre-licensing hours, you will register for and pass the state license exam, which tests you on Georgia insurance laws and standard industry practices.

Depending on your background, you may also need to submit a fingerprint or background check. Ensure you budget both time and costs for these tasks. After you pass the exam and verify your background, you will apply for your license with the [Georgia Office of Insurance and Safety Fire Commissioner](/georgia/requirements). Each of these steps can be completed relatively quickly, but it helps to start early so you avoid last-minute stress.

## Understand continuing education rules

With your license in hand, you can now turn your attention to Georgia insurance continuing education requirements, which are in place to keep your industry knowledge current. CE ensures that you stay informed about evolving laws, claim practices, and consumer protection guidelines.

Georgia generally requires you to complete continuing education credits within a specific renewal cycle. These credits must include certain mandatory topics such as ethics, but the rest can be tailored to your specialization. Since no two insurance careers look exactly alike, you have the flexibility to choose courses that align with your areas of focus, whether that is personal lines, life insurance, or something more niche.

You will often find online and classroom-based classes, along with self-study programs if you prefer learning at your own pace. However you decide to complete your CE hours, remember to keep proof of completion and stay organized. You will need to confirm you have met the credit-hour minimum before each renewal deadline to avoid any interruptions in your license status.

It helps to note down your renewal deadline, then count backward to figure out when you should start taking classes. ## Complete the renewal process

Once you have ticked off your continuing education credits, the final step is to renew your license before the deadline. Renewal typically involves logging into your account with the Georgia Office of Insurance and Safety Fire Commissioner, confirming your completed CE hours, and paying a renewal fee. Always verify that your hours have been properly recorded in the state system, and keep a personal file of your certificates or transcripts.

If you forget to renew by the due date, you may face late fees or even a lapse in your license status. That means you would have to stop selling insurance until you meet the requirements and get everything reinstated. Timely renewals keep you in good standing with the state and your clients, so mark it on your calendar as soon as possible.
