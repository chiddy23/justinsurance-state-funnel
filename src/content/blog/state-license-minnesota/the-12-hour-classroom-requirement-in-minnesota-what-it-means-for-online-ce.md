Minnesota's CE requirement is not simply "complete 24 hours of approved courses." It has a structural constraint that significantly affects how producers plan and complete their biennial CE: no more than 12 of the 24 required hours may come from company-sponsored or carrier-affiliated courses. In practice, that means at least 12 hours must come from non-company-sponsored, independently approved providers — a requirement that has nothing to do with whether the course is delivered live or self-paced online. Self-paced online CE from an independent provider satisfies this requirement just as well as any other format. Producers who complete most of their CE through courses sponsored by a single carrier can find themselves short on the non-company-sponsored side of the ledger. This post covers exactly what the 12-hour non-company-sponsored rule means, what counts toward it, how to plan CE to meet it efficiently, and the most common mistakes producers make in managing this component.

## The Source and Structure of the Requirement

Minnesota's non-company-sponsored rule is embedded in the broader CE framework under Minn. Stat. §60K.56 and the Department of Commerce's implementing regulations. It is one of three components of the 24-hour biennial CE obligation — alongside the 3-hour ethics minimum and the 24-hour total — and like the other components, it must be satisfied independently. Meeting the total hour count without satisfying the non-company-sponsored minimum is non-compliance even when every other component is satisfied.

The requirement creates a cap on company-sponsored CE, not a floor on live instruction. If a producer completes more than 12 hours of company-sponsored or carrier-affiliated coursework, the excess above 12 hours does not count toward the 24-hour total — the producer must make up the difference with courses from independent, non-company-sponsored providers.

## What Counts as Non-Company-Sponsored CE

The distinction that matters under Minnesota law is who sponsors and approves the course, not how it is delivered. A course qualifies as non-company-sponsored when it is offered by an independent, Department-approved CE provider that is not an insurance carrier or affiliated with a single carrier's product line.

### Format Is Not the Deciding Factor

Both self-paced online courses and any live or in-person sessions can satisfy the non-company-sponsored requirement, provided they come from an independent, Department-approved provider. There is no format-based floor in Minnesota's CE law — a producer can complete the entire 24-hour requirement, including the 12 non-company-sponsored hours, through self-paced online study from an approved independent provider such as JustInsurance.

### Company-Sponsored and Carrier-Affiliated Courses

Courses developed, sponsored, or delivered by an insurance carrier — often free product training tied to that carrier's offerings — count toward the 24-hour total but are capped at 12 hours combined. Once a producer has logged 12 hours of company-sponsored CE, any additional company-sponsored hours do not count further toward the requirement; the remaining hours must come from independent providers.

## The Intersection With Other CE Components

The non-company-sponsored rule interacts with the other CE components in ways that affect how producers most efficiently plan their CE calendar.

Non-company-sponsored + ethics overlap: A 3-hour ethics course from an independent provider simultaneously satisfies the 3-hour ethics minimum and contributes 3 hours to the 12-hour non-company-sponsored minimum. This overlap makes an independent-provider ethics course one of the highest-efficiency CE investments a Minnesota producer can make.

Carrier-sponsored training: A carrier-sponsored seminar or webinar counts toward the 24-hour total but does not count toward the non-company-sponsored minimum, and hours beyond 12 do not count toward the total at all. Producers who attend carrier product training can use up to 12 of those hours toward their total — but they still need at least 12 hours from non-company-sponsored, independent sources regardless of how much carrier training they complete.

## The Most Efficient CE Planning Approach

Given the structure above, the most efficient CE plan for most Minnesota producers follows this sequence:

Step 1 — Complete at least 12 hours through self-paced online courses from an independent, Department-approved provider, including at least 3 hours of ethics. This single decision satisfies the non-company-sponsored minimum (12 hours) and the ethics minimum (3 hours) simultaneously, entirely on the producer's own schedule.

Step 2 — Complete any remaining hours through either independent-provider courses or up to 12 hours of company-sponsored training, whichever fits the producer's schedule and interests.

The result: 24 CE hours total, with at least 12 non-company-sponsored hours and 3 ethics hours — both components satisfied without ever needing to attend a live session, if the producer prefers self-paced study.

## Common Mistakes and How to Avoid Them

Mistake 1 — Relying too heavily on carrier-sponsored training: A producer completes 18 hours of free carrier product training and only 6 hours of independent-provider CE. Only 12 of the 18 carrier hours count toward the 24-hour total, leaving the producer with 18 counted hours (12 + 6) — 6 hours short of the 24-hour requirement, and still short of the 12-hour non-company-sponsored minimum.

Prevention: Track company-sponsored and non-company-sponsored hours separately throughout the renewal period, and make sure at least 12 hours come from independent providers before relying on any carrier training to fill out the total.

Mistake 2 — Assuming self-paced courses don't count toward the non-company-sponsored minimum: Some producers mistakenly believe that only live or in-person instruction can satisfy the non-company-sponsored requirement. This is incorrect — Minnesota does not require any classroom or live-instruction component. Self-paced online courses from an independent, approved provider fully satisfy the non-company-sponsored minimum.

Prevention: Confirm that your CE provider is an independent, Department-approved provider (not carrier-affiliated) — the delivery format is not the relevant factor.

## Frequently Asked Questions

Does Minnesota require any of my CE hours to be completed live or in a classroom?

No. Minnesota's CE law does not impose a live-instruction or classroom-attendance requirement. All 24 hours, including the 12 non-company-sponsored hours and the 3 ethics hours, can be completed through self-paced online courses from an approved provider.

I attend free product training through the carrier I'm appointed with. Does that count toward my CE?

It can, but only up to 12 hours combined per renewal period. Carrier-sponsored or company-affiliated training counts toward your 24-hour total only up to that 12-hour cap. You still need at least 12 hours from an independent, non-company-sponsored provider to meet the requirement in full.

Can I satisfy my entire Minnesota CE requirement through self-paced online courses?

Yes, provided the courses are from a Department-approved provider and at least 12 of the 24 hours (including your 3 ethics hours) come from an independent provider rather than a single carrier's sponsored training.
