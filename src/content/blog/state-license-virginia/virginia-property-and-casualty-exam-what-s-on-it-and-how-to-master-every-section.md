The Virginia Property and Casualty licensing exam — Series 11-03 — is one integrated exam: 135 scored questions plus 10 unscored pretest questions, drawn from ten weighted content sections, with a 2.5-hour (150-minute) time limit and a $35 exam fee. There is no separate "national" exam and "state law" exam to clear. Prometric computes a single total percentage, and you need 70% overall to pass. Virginia law is not quarantined in its own section — it is written into the content outline itself, starting with the largest section on the blueprint. This post maps the full Series 11-03 outline and tells you exactly where the weight sits.

## How the Series 11-03 Exam Is Scored

This is the part candidates most often get wrong, usually because they have read advice written for a different state's two-part exam.

Virginia scores one number. Per the Virginia Insurance Licensing Information Bulletin: "Your total percentage score is computed by dividing the number of questions you answered correctly by the total number of questions in the examination. The total score is not computed by adding the section percentages and dividing by the total number of sections."

Your score report also shows the percentage you got correct in each major content-outline section — for passing and failing candidates alike. Those section percentages are diagnostic. The Bulletin is explicit that they exist "to guide you, or your employer and/or trainer, about areas requiring additional preparation," and that "even after you pass, you may want to focus on these areas." You cannot fail a section independently, and the section percentages will not average out to your total score, because the sections carry different numbers of questions.

Practically, that means a weak section can be carried by strong ones — but only if you know which sections are big enough to carry it. That is what the blueprint below is for.

Virginia does publish a line-level first-time [pass rate](/pass-rates) for this exam, and it is worth knowing before you decide how much preparation is enough. The SCC Bureau of Insurance posts Prometric's annual demographic report on its Licensing Exam Pass Rates page, broken out by exam title. In the report covering June 1, 2024 through May 31, 2025, 1,552 first-time candidates sat the Virginia Property & Casualty exam and 69.52% of them passed — up from 66.94% of 1,467 first-time candidates the year before. Close to a third of first-time P&C candidates still walk out without a license. That same table also confirms the arithmetic behind the blueprint below: the raw cut score for Series 11-03 is 95, which is 70% of 135 scored questions.

## The Series 11-03 Blueprint: Ten Weighted Sections, 135 Scored Questions

Prometric's Series 11-03 content outline covers the following ten sections. The percentages and item counts are the official weights.

### 1. Insurance Regulation — 15% (20 questions)

The single largest section on the exam, and it is almost entirely Virginia law. Producer licensing: general provisions and definitions (Va. Code § 38.2-1800), the licensing process (§ 38.2-1819), and the types of licensee — agents, consultants, nonresidents, business entities. License maintenance: duration and termination (§§ 38.2-1825, 1826), address and name changes, assumed names, the duty to report felony convictions and other states' actions, and continuing education (§§ 38.2-1866, 1868.1–1871). Appointment procedures (§§ 38.2-1825, 1833–1834.1), including the difference between an agent's contract with an insurer and the agent's appointment, solicitation before appointment, and termination of a license with no active appointment. Disciplinary action: probation, suspension, revocation or refusal to issue or renew (§§ 38.2-1821, 1831, 1832), cease and desist orders (§ 38.2-219) and penalties (§§ 38.2-218, 219, 1823, 1831).

The section also covers State Corporation Commission duties and powers (§ 38.2-200), acting for an unlicensed insurer, record retention (§ 38.2-1809(B)), payment and sharing of commissions (§ 38.2-1812), charging of fees (§ 38.2-310), fiduciary capacity and trust accounts (§ 38.2-1813), the Virginia unfair trade practices — misrepresentation, false advertising, defamation, boycott/coercion/intimidation, unfair discrimination, rebating (§§ 38.2-502 through 509, 512) — plus federal regulation (Fair Credit Reporting Act; 18 USC 1033/1034 fraud and false statements) and the role of the NAIC.

If you under-prepare one section, do not let it be this one.

### 2. General Insurance — 9% (12 questions)

Risk management terminology (risk, exposure, hazard, peril, loss) and the methods of handling risk: avoidance, retention, sharing, reduction, transfer. Elements of an insurable risk, adverse selection, the law of large numbers, reinsurance. Types of insurer: stock, mutual, reciprocal, risk retention groups, surplus lines carriers; purchasing groups; private versus government insurers; admitted versus nonadmitted; domestic, foreign and alien. Agency law: captive versus independent agents, the insurer as principal, and express, implied and apparent authority. Contract law: offer and acceptance, consideration, competent parties, legal purpose, plus adhesion, reasonable expectations, indemnity, utmost good faith, representations and misrepresentations, warranties, concealment, fraud, waiver and estoppel.

### 3. Property and Casualty Insurance Basics — 13% (18 questions)

Insurable interest, underwriting, rates and loss costs, and the hazard triad (physical, moral, morale). Negligence: the elements of a negligent act, defenses against negligence, proximate cause. Damages — compensatory (special versus general) and punitive — plus absolute, strict and vicarious liability. Named perils versus special (open) perils, direct versus consequential loss, blanket versus specific insurance, basic construction types, and the full loss-valuation vocabulary: actual cash value, replacement cost, functional replacement cost, guaranteed replacement cost, market value, agreed value, stated amount, valued policy, salvage value.

Policy structure and common provisions live here too: declarations, definitions, insuring agreement, conditions, exclusions, endorsements; named/first named/additional insureds; policy period and territory; cancellation and nonrenewal; deductibles; other insurance (nonconcurrent, primary and excess, pro rata, contribution by equal shares); limits of liability (per occurrence, per person, aggregate, split, combined single); coinsurance; vacancy; subrogation; duty to defend; and lien holder provisions.

Subsection 3.4 is expressly Virginia law: the Virginia Property and Casualty Insurance Guaranty Association (§§ 38.2-1600–1632), flood and earthquake exclusion notices (§§ 38.2-2125, 2129), cancellation and nonrenewal rules for owner-occupied dwellings (§§ 38.2-2113, 2114), fire (§ 38.2-2105) and commercial liability (§ 38.2-231), optional ordinance-or-law (§ 38.2-2124) and water/sewer backup (§ 38.2-2120) coverage, and binders (§ 38.2-2112).

### 4. Dwelling Policy — 5% (7 questions)

The DP-1 Basic, DP-2 Broad and DP-3 Special forms and their perils. Property coverages A through E: dwelling, other structures, personal property, fair rental value and additional living expense. General exclusions, conditions, and the personal liability supplement. Note the Virginia Special Provisions form (01 45 10 23) and the tested endorsements: automatic increase/inflation guard (DP 04 11), broad theft (DP 05 72), dwelling under construction (DP 11 43).

### 5. Homeowners Policy — 13% (18 questions)

Tied with P&C Basics for the second-largest section. Coverage forms: HO-3 Special, HO-4 Tenants, HO-5 Comprehensive, HO-6 Condominium Unit-Owners. Section I property coverages — A (dwelling), B (other structures), C (personal property), D (loss of use). Section II liability coverages — E (personal liability) and F (medical payments to others), including the base limits and the options to increase them. Exclusions, conditions, and the endorsement set: personal injury (HO 24 82), watercraft liability (HO 24 75), business pursuits.

### 6. Auto Insurance — 16% (21 questions)

The largest single product section, and heavily Virginia-specific. Subsection 6.1 is Virginia law outright: the Virginia Motor Vehicle Financial Responsibility Law and required limits of liability (§ 38.2-2206; Va. Code §§ 46.2-372, 472), the Virginia Automobile Insurance Plan (§§ 46.2-464–471), uninsured and underinsured motorist coverage (§§ 38.2-2202, 2206) including UM/UIM rejection and required limits, and auto cancellation/nonrenewal (§§ 38.2-231, 2208, 2212). The Personal Auto Policy structure is tested alongside it: liability, medical payments, UM/UIM, and physical damage (collision and other than collision), the definition of covered autos, who is an insured, and exclusions.

### 7. Commercial Package Policy (CPP) — 12% (16 questions)

The modular commercial lines system: common policy declarations and conditions plus individual coverage parts. Commercial property — building and personal property form, business income, and the basic, broad and special causes-of-loss forms — along with the endorsement set (peak season limit CP 12 30, value reporting CP 13 10, spoilage CP 04 40, vacancy permit CP 04 50). Commercial general liability: occurrence versus claims-made, Coverage A (bodily injury and property damage), Coverage B (personal and advertising injury), Coverage C (medical payments), products-completed operations, and additional insured endorsements.

### 8. Businessowners Policy (BOP) — 5% (7 questions)

The packaged small-business policy. Section I property (coverage, exclusions, limits, deductibles, loss and general conditions, optional coverages), Section II liability (business liability, medical expenses, exclusions, who is an insured, limits), and Section III common policy conditions. Selected endorsements: earthquake (BP 10 03), protective safeguards (BP 04 30), named perils (BP 10 09), hired and non-owned auto liability (BP 04 04), and the utility services forms (BP 04 56, BP 04 57).

### 9. Workers Compensation Insurance — 7% (9 questions)

Employment covered under the Virginia Workers' Compensation Act (Va. Code §§ 65.2-101, 65.2-300) and the Virginia Workers' Compensation Insurance Plan — the assigned risk mechanism (§ 65.2-820; § 38.2-2000.1). Virginia runs a competitive private market with no exclusive state fund. Coverage is mandatory for employers with three or more employees in most industries, and the Virginia Workers' Compensation Commission — not the Bureau of Insurance — administers claim disputes. Know the benefit categories (medical, disability income, rehabilitation, death), experience modification, and the exclusive remedy doctrine.

### 10. Other Coverages and Options — 5% (7 questions)

Personal and commercial umbrella/excess liability. Specialty liability: professional liability, errors and omissions, fiduciary liability, liquor liability. Surplus lines, including Virginia licensing requirements (§§ 38.2-1857.1–1857.9). Surety bonds: principal, obligee, surety; contract, license and permit, and judicial bonds. Business management liability (D&O and employment practices liability). The National Flood Insurance Program — write-your-own versus government, eligibility, coverage, limits, deductibles. Watercraft coverage (§ 38.2-2232) and difference in conditions. And the residual market: the Virginia Property Insurance Association, the state's FAIR plan (§§ 38.2-2700–2707).

## Where Virginia Law Shows Up on the Exam

Because there is no separate state-law section, candidates sometimes assume Virginia content is a light dusting. It is not. Virginia statutes are cited throughout the outline, concentrated in Insurance Regulation (15%), subsection 3.4, all of Auto subsection 6.1, and Workers Compensation. The facts below are the ones most likely to be tested.

### Virginia Auto Insurance Law

The most heavily tested Virginia-specific material on this exam. Must-know facts:

Minimum liability: 50/100/25 (effective January 1, 2025 — up from 30/60/20)

Insurance mandatory as of July 1, 2024 (the $500 uninsured motor vehicle fee option was eliminated)

UM/UIM stacking effective July 1, 2023 under Va. Code § 38.2-2206 — UIM adds on top of the at-fault driver's liability rather than being offset

FR-44 for DUI convictions: 100/200/50 (double the standard minimums), required for 3 years

SR-22 for certain violations

The Virginia Automobile Insurance Plan as the residual market for drivers who cannot obtain voluntary coverage

Virginia is an at-fault state with pure contributory negligence — a plaintiff is barred from recovery if even 1% at fault

### Virginia Property Insurance Law

Cancellation and nonrenewal notice rules for owner-occupied dwellings (§§ 38.2-2113, 2114), fire policies (§ 38.2-2105) and commercial liability (§ 38.2-231). Required flood (§ 38.2-2125) and earthquake (§ 38.2-2129) exclusion notices. Optional ordinance-or-law and water/sewer backup coverage. Binders (§ 38.2-2112). The Virginia Property and Casualty Insurance Guaranty Association, and the Virginia Property Insurance Association — the FAIR plan that serves as insurer of last resort.

### Workers' Compensation in Virginia

Virginia uses a competitive private workers' comp market with no exclusive state fund, backed by the Virginia Workers' Compensation Insurance Plan for assigned risk. Coverage is mandatory for any employer with 3 or more employees in most industries. Under Va. Code § 65.2-805, an employer that fails to carry required coverage is assessed a civil penalty of not more than $250 per day of noncompliance, subject to a maximum penalty of $50,000, plus the costs of collection — and the Commission can order the employer to cease business operations until it comes into compliance.

### Producer Licensing and Conduct

Virginia licensing and appointment requirements, the SCC's authority, record retention and trust-account duties, commission-sharing and fee rules, the unfair trade practices catalog (§§ 38.2-502 through 509), and grounds for suspension or revocation. This is the backbone of the 20-question Insurance Regulation section.

## Frequently Asked Questions

How many P&C exam questions involve homeowners and auto insurance?

Combined, Homeowners (13%, 18 questions) and Auto (16%, 21 questions) account for 39 of the 135 scored questions — the single largest content concentration on the exam, and Auto carries additional Virginia statutory material inside it. Candidates who master the HO forms, the Personal Auto Policy structure, and Virginia's auto insurance laws have a strong foundation. Do not underestimate commercial lines, however: CPP (16 questions) and BOP (7) together account for 23 questions that many candidates under-prepare, and Insurance Regulation alone is worth 20.

What is pure contributory negligence and why does it matter for the Virginia P&C exam?

Virginia is one of only four states (along with Alabama, Maryland, and North Carolina) that applies the doctrine of pure contributory negligence in civil liability cases. Under this doctrine, if a plaintiff is found even 1% at fault for an accident, they are completely barred from recovering any damages from the defendant. This is dramatically different from the comparative negligence system used in most states, where fault is apportioned and damages are reduced proportionally. For P&C producers in Virginia, this doctrine affects how auto liability claims, premises liability claims, and general liability claims are defended and settled. The outline reaches it through Section 3.0 Property and Casualty Insurance Basics, which expressly tests the elements of a negligent act and the defenses against negligence.

Does the Virginia P&C exam test commercial umbrella and excess liability?

Yes — umbrella and excess liability open Section 10.0 Other Coverages and Options, which is 5% of the exam (7 questions) and also has to cover specialty liability, surplus lines, surety bonds, D&O and EPL, the NFIP and the Virginia FAIR plan. So expect one or two umbrella questions, not a deep dive. The exam tests how umbrella policies layer over primary liability policies, the "drop-down" concept when primary limits are exhausted or a claim falls within the umbrella's self-insured retention, and the difference between a true umbrella (broader coverage than the underlying policy) and an excess policy (which simply extends the underlying limits). Personal umbrellas are tested in relation to homeowners coverage — and note the base limits: the standard ISO homeowners Section II Coverage E personal liability limit is $100,000 per occurrence, with $1,000 per person for Coverage F medical payments. Higher limits such as $300,000 or $500,000 are optional increases, and the umbrella sits above whichever limit the insured actually bought.

How is workers' compensation tested on the Virginia P&C exam, and what is unique about Virginia's system?

Workers' compensation is Section 9.0, worth 7% of the exam — 9 questions — covering both the general mechanics (no-fault system, benefit types, experience modification, exclusive remedy) and Virginia's own rules, which the outline cites directly to Va. Code §§ 65.2-101, 65.2-300 and 65.2-820. The key Virginia distinctions: a competitive private market with no exclusive state fund, the Virginia Workers' Compensation Insurance Plan as the assigned risk mechanism, a coverage mandate for employers with 3 or more employees in most industries, and the Virginia Workers' Compensation Commission (not the Bureau of Insurance) as the body that administers claim disputes. On penalties, know the statute rather than a rule of thumb: § 65.2-805 provides for a civil penalty of not more than $250 per day of noncompliance, capped at $50,000.

What is the best way to tackle the time limit on the Virginia P&C exam?

Series 11-03 gives you 2.5 hours — 150 minutes — for 145 total questions (135 scored plus 10 unscored pretest items). That is roughly 62 seconds per question. Most candidates find this is ample time; the exam is not primarily a speed test. A practical strategy: answer questions in order without stopping to review marked ones. Mark any question you are uncertain about and continue. After completing all questions, return to the marked ones and reconsider. Do not spend more than 90 seconds on any single question — make your best answer, mark it, and move on. Most candidates finish with time to spare. Because the exam is delivered as one integrated set with Virginia and general questions mixed together, you cannot allocate time section by section — just hold a consistent pace throughout. Plan on roughly three hours at the test center once you add the 30-minute check-in Prometric asks for.

The Virginia P&C exam rewards systematic preparation across all ten content sections, with extra weight on Insurance Regulation, Auto and Homeowners — and deep preparation on Virginia's auto insurance law changes. There is one score to clear, 70% overall, and every section you shore up moves that one number.
