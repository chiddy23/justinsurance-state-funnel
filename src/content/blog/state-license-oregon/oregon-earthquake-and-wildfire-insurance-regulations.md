Oregon's natural hazard profile is unlike most states. The Pacific Northwest's seismic risk from the Cascadia Subduction Zone, combined with increasingly intense wildfire seasons, creates unique challenges for property insurance producers and policyholders. Understanding Oregon earthquake and wildfire insurance regulations is essential for producers serving Oregon homeowners and commercial clients.

Here's what Oregon producers need to know about earthquake and wildfire insurance.

## Oregon's Distinctive Natural Hazard Profile

Oregon faces several natural hazards that affect insurance considerations:

Cascadia Subduction Zone earthquake risk. Oregon sits over one of the world's most significant earthquake-generating zones. While major Cascadia events are infrequent, the potential magnitude is severe.

Wildfire risk. Oregon's forested landscape and changing climate have produced increasingly severe wildfire seasons. The 2020 Labor Day fires were among the most devastating in state history.

Volcanic activity. Oregon has multiple active volcanoes (Mount Hood, Mount Bachelor, Crater Lake, etc.), though direct insurance impact is limited.

Coastal storms and flooding. Oregon's coast experiences significant storms and flooding events.

Tsunamis. Oregon's coastal communities face tsunami risk associated with Cascadia events.

Among these, earthquake and wildfire are the most significant for insurance practice.

## Earthquake Insurance in Oregon

The Critical Fact: Standard homeowners insurance does NOT cover earthquakes. Oregon homeowners must purchase separate earthquake coverage if they want protection against earthquake damage.

Despite Oregon's significant earthquake risk, earthquake insurance penetration is relatively low — most Oregon homeowners don't carry it. This represents both an unfortunate gap in coverage for many homeowners and an opportunity for producers to educate clients.

## How Earthquake Insurance Works

Coverage scope. Earthquake insurance covers damage to dwellings, other structures, and personal property caused directly by earthquakes.

What's typically covered:

Damage to the dwelling

Damage to attached structures

Damage to personal property

Additional living expenses during repairs

What's typically NOT covered:

Land damage (settling, sliding)

Damage from secondary effects (fire from damaged gas lines may be covered under fire insurance)

Damage from flooding (separate flood coverage)

Vehicle damage (separate auto coverage)

Specific exclusions varying by policy

Deductibles. Earthquake deductibles are typically percentage-based — often 5%, 10%, 15%, or 20% of dwelling coverage value. On a $400,000 home with a 15% earthquake deductible, the homeowner faces $60,000 out of pocket before insurance pays.

Premium considerations. Earthquake insurance premiums vary significantly based on location, construction type, soil conditions, and other factors. Coastal locations and older homes typically face higher premiums.

## The California Earthquake Authority Connection

Oregon doesn't have a state-level earthquake insurance entity equivalent to the California Earthquake Authority (CEA). Earthquake coverage in Oregon comes from:

Private insurance carriers offering optional earthquake coverage

Surplus lines markets

Specialty earthquake insurers

Coverage availability and pricing varies more significantly in Oregon than in California's CEA-backed market.

## Wildfire Insurance in Oregon

Wildfire is generally covered under standard homeowners insurance as a fire peril. This is different from earthquake — wildfire damage is typically covered without separate policies.

However, wildfire risk has dramatically affected Oregon's homeowners insurance market in recent years:

Coverage availability challenges. In high-wildfire-risk areas, some carriers have limited new business or non-renewed existing policies.

Premium increases. Wildfire-exposed properties have seen substantial premium increases.

Coverage limitations. Some policies have specific provisions affecting wildfire claims.

Mandatory disclosure. Oregon has implemented disclosure requirements regarding wildfire risk in real estate and insurance contexts.

## The Oregon Wildfire Hazard Map

Oregon's Wildfire Hazard Map classifies properties by wildfire risk level. This map affects:

Disclosure requirements. Properties in higher-risk zones may have specific disclosure requirements.

Insurance availability. Carriers consider Wildfire Hazard Map classifications in underwriting decisions.

Mitigation requirements. Properties in higher-risk zones may face mitigation requirements.

Building codes. Oregon has implemented stricter building code requirements for higher-risk wildfire zones.

The map has been controversial, with multiple revisions and ongoing political and regulatory debates affecting its implementation.

## The Oregon FAIR Plan

Oregon does not have a FAIR (Fair Access to Insurance Requirements) Plan equivalent to those operating in California, Florida, or other high-risk states. Oregon homeowners who can't obtain coverage in the standard market typically:

Work with surplus lines insurers

Use specialty high-risk carriers

Pursue mitigation to qualify for standard coverage

Consider relocating from highest-risk areas

This creates challenges for Oregon producers serving clients in high-wildfire-risk areas.

## Wildfire Mitigation and Insurance

Properties with documented wildfire mitigation features may qualify for:

Better insurance availability. Some carriers prioritize properties with mitigation efforts.

Premium discounts. Mitigation can produce premium savings.

Reduced deductibles. Some policies offer reduced deductibles for mitigated properties.

Common mitigation features include:

Defensible space (cleared vegetation around dwelling)

Fire-resistant building materials (metal roofing, fiber cement siding)

Ember-resistant vents

Spark arrestors on chimneys

Multiple evacuation routes

Adequate water supply for firefighting

Producers should help clients understand available mitigation options.

## Coverage Coordination for Oregon Homeowners

For comprehensive natural hazard protection, Oregon homeowners typically need:

Standard homeowners insurance. Covers fire (including wildfire), wind, theft, and most other perils.

Earthquake insurance. Separate policy or endorsement for earthquake damage.

Flood insurance. Separate NFIP or private flood policy for flood damage.

Umbrella coverage. Excess liability coverage for higher-net-worth properties.

Helping clients understand they need multiple coverages is a core service for Oregon producers.

## Producer Responsibilities

Oregon producers selling property insurance have specific responsibilities related to natural hazards:

Complete disclosure. Clients must understand what's covered, what's not, and what separate policies they need.

Coverage adequacy. Helping clients ensure adequate limits given Oregon construction costs and replacement realities.

Mitigation discussions. Educating clients about mitigation options that improve insurability and may reduce premiums.

Documentation. Thorough records of client conversations, recommendations, and coverage decisions.

Regular reviews. Natural hazard considerations change over time. Regular policy reviews keep coverage appropriate.

## Common Oregon Property Insurance Pitfalls

Assuming earthquake is covered. Most Oregon homeowners don't realize earthquake isn't included in standard policies.

Insufficient earthquake limits. Even those with earthquake coverage often have inadequate limits given Oregon construction costs.

Underestimating wildfire impact. Some clients underestimate how dramatically wildfire risk affects insurance.

Missing mitigation opportunities. Clients who could improve insurance terms through mitigation but don't know to pursue it.

Inadequate documentation. Failing to document coverage discussions leaves both client and producer exposed.

## 5 Frequently Asked Questions

- Does standard homeowners insurance cover earthquake damage in Oregon? No. Oregon homeowners must purchase separate earthquake coverage. Most Oregon homeowners don't have it, despite the state's significant earthquake risk.
- Is wildfire damage covered under standard Oregon homeowners insurance? Yes. Wildfire is typically covered as a fire peril under standard homeowners policies. However, coverage availability and pricing in high-risk areas has been significantly affected.
- What's the typical earthquake insurance deductible in Oregon? Earthquake deductibles are typically percentage-based, often 5%-20% of dwelling coverage. The exact percentage varies by carrier and policy.
- Does Oregon have a state-backed insurance pool for earthquake? No. Unlike California's CEA, Oregon doesn't have a state-level earthquake insurance entity. Coverage comes from private carriers and surplus lines.
- What is the Oregon Wildfire Hazard Map? Oregon's official classification of properties by wildfire risk level. The map affects disclosure requirements, insurance availability, and may affect building codes in high-risk areas.
