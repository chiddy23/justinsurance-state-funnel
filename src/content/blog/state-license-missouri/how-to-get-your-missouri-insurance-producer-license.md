Missouri's insurance licensing process has several features that make it one of the most accessible in the comparison states. The Missouri Department of Commerce and Insurance (DCI) — also called the Department of Insurance, Financial Institutions & Professional Registration (DIFP) — does not require [prelicensing](/missouri/prelicensing) education, making Missouri join Maryland (eliminated PLE in October 2024) and Virginia among states where the Pearson VUE exam is the first mandatory step. There is no fingerprinting requirement. The exam fee of $29-$35 is the lowest of all comparison states. And the 16-hour CE requirement (vs. 24 hours in most comparison states) means ongoing license maintenance is less burdensome. Missouri's location at the geographic center of the United States — with Edward Jones (world's largest independent broker-dealer by advisor count) headquartered in St. Louis, Centene Corporation (Fortune 500 managed care), Boeing Defense, Cerner/Oracle Health in Kansas City, and Anheuser-Busch InBev in St. Louis — creates a distinctive professional insurance advisory market. Here's the complete guide.

## Who Regulates Missouri Insurance

Missouri Department of Commerce and Insurance (DCI):

Mailing address: PO Box 4001, Jefferson City, MO 65102

Website: insurance.mo.gov

Insurance Code: Missouri Revised Statutes (RSMo), Chapters 375/379 (insurance)

DCI is also known as the Department of Insurance, Financial Institutions & Professional Registration (DIFP)

DCI's distinctive multi-sector mandate: Like Minnesota's DOC, Missouri's DCI regulates not just insurance but also financial institutions, professional registration (physicians, architects, CPAs, bail bond agents, tattoo artists, and many others), and bank/credit union chartering. This multi-sector mandate makes Missouri's regulator broader than a standalone insurance department.

## Step 1: Prepare for the Pearson VUE Exam (No PLE Required)

Missouri does not require prelicensing education. Candidates can schedule the Pearson VUE exam immediately without completing any DCI-approved prelicensing course.

## Step 2: Schedule and Pass the Pearson VUE Exam

Exam administrator: Pearson VUE

Website: pearsonvue.com/us/en/mo/insurance.html

Phone: (866) 247-4740

Effective May 7, 2025: Remote online exams (OnVUE) are NO LONGER available for Missouri — all exams must be taken in-person at Pearson VUE test centers

Schedule at least 24 hours in advance (electronic check payments require 5 days)

Exam fee: $29-$35 (lowest of all comparison states) Passing score: 70% on BOTH sections separately — national AND state sections each require 70%; scores are NOT averaged Score validity: 1 year Results: Immediate pass/fail; diagnostic report available in Pearson VUE account

Retakes: Unlimited; 24-hour wait; can retake ONLY the failed section if one section was passed.

Cancel/reschedule: At least 48 hours in advance or forfeit fee.

Two-section format — critical: Missouri's exam is split into a national section and a Missouri state section. Both must be passed separately at 70%+. This is different from most states (like Maryland and Indiana) where national and state content are interleaved as one score.

Missouri Exam Specifications:

Exam day: Arrive 30 minutes early; two government-issued IDs (one with photo); no personal items in testing room.

## Step 3: Submit License Application

No fingerprinting required in Missouri — one of the most applicant-friendly features.

After passing both sections of the Pearson VUE exam:

Wait 24-48 hours after passing (to allow results to load in DCI system), then apply through NIPR:

Application fee: $100

NIPR transaction fee: $5.60

Paper alternative: Download Uniform Application form → mail with $100 check to DCI PO Box 4001, Jefferson City, MO 65102.

Processing: 5-10 business days.

Fee waiver: Certain low-income individuals and veterans can apply for a License Fee Waiver with DCI.

Temporary license: Missouri does not issue temporary insurance licenses.

## Step 4: Maintain Your License

Renewal: 2 years; last day of birth month. CE: 16 hours every 2 years (3 Ethics) — the lowest CE requirement of all comparison states (most require 24 hours).

## 5 Frequently Asked Questions

- What does Missouri's two-section exam format mean for candidates? Missouri's Pearson VUE exam has two distinctly scored sections: a national/general section and a Missouri state section. Each must score 70% or higher. The two scores are NOT averaged — if you score 80% on national and 65% on state, you fail the state section and must retake it (after a 24-hour wait). This format is unique among comparison states and rewards candidates who prepare specifically for Missouri state law, not just national insurance content.
- Why are Missouri's exam fees so much lower than other states? Missouri's Pearson VUE exam fees ($29-$35) are the lowest of all comparison states — MD charges $60; IN charges $69; MN charges $45. This reflects Missouri's deliberately accessible licensing structure. The $29 individual line exam fee and $35 combined exam fee create minimal financial barrier to entry.
- What happened to remote exams in Missouri? Effective May 7, 2025, Pearson VUE discontinued remote online exam delivery for Missouri. All Missouri insurance licensing exams must now be taken in-person at a Pearson VUE test center. Candidates who were hoping to test from home must schedule an in-person appointment. This is a significant recent change from the prior OnVUE availability.
- Why does Missouri have a $100 application fee when Indiana charges $40? Missouri's $100 application fee — regardless of lines of authority — is one of the highest per-application fees among comparison states. The fee is the same whether applying for one line or multiple lines on the same application. Indiana charges $40 per line; Minnesota charges $50 per line + $20 technology; Maryland charges $54. Missouri's flat $100 means multiple-line candidates pay the same as single-line candidates — a relative advantage for full-market agents.
- Why doesn't Missouri have a temporary insurance license? Missouri eliminated temporary insurance licenses. Candidates who need to begin selling before the full license is issued have no temporary license option. At 5-10 business day processing, this means the fastest path from passing the exam to licensed is approximately 2 weeks — which is fast but without the bridge that states like Indiana (180 days), Minnesota (180 days), and Maryland (15-month Life) provide.
