## Learn maryland insurance basics

Before diving into any coursework, it helps to know what these education requirements include. Maryland eliminated its mandatory pre-licensing education requirement effective October 1, 2024, so candidates for life, health, property, and casualty lines can now go straight to the Prometric state exam. Pre-licensing study is still available and recommended as optional exam prep, and requirements can vary based on future changes from the Maryland Insurance Administration, so it's worth confirming the current rules before you begin.

Because insurance covers so many areas, your training will touch on topics like state regulations, general policy provisions, underwriting principles, and ethical standards. You’ll quickly realize that pre-licensing courses aren’t there just to meet a bureaucratic obligation. They give you a foundation of knowledge that can set you apart in the field. Understanding everything from how premiums are calculated to the scenarios in which claims are approved prepares you to serve clients responsibly.

## Complete pre-licensing courses

While Maryland no longer requires it, many candidates still choose to complete an optional [Maryland prelicensing course](/maryland/prelicensing) before sitting for the state exam. You’ll find both in-person and online classes, so you can pick a format that aligns with your schedule. Many providers even offer practice exams, flash cards, or extra study sessions to reinforce challenging topics. Take the time to find a course that fits your learning style, because a strong grasp of insurance concepts can make the official licensing exam less intimidating.

Because Maryland no longer requires a completion certificate to sit for the exam, you can register directly with Prometric once you feel ready. Before exam day, review everything from general insurance terms to more nuanced topics like annuities or workers’ compensation coverage. A thorough review can boost your confidence and sense of readiness.

## Fulfill continuing education

Once you earn your Maryland insurance license, your learning journey isn’t over. [Maryland requires continuing education](/maryland/continuing-education) (CE) to keep you up to speed on policy changes, evolving products, and best practices for serving policyholders. These CE requirements generally apply every renewal period (commonly two years) and can include mandatory ethics courses to ensure you hold yourself to high professional standards.

You’ll find that completing CE can feel more manageable when you treat it as an opportunity for career growth. Taking targeted courses—like advanced life insurance planning or property damage claims—enhances the expertise you share with your clients. Plus, you’ll stay in good standing with the Maryland Insurance Administration. Keep track of your credits and deadlines to avoid late fees or license suspensions. It’s all part of ensuring you maintain the knowledge you worked so hard to gain in your pre-licensing process.

## Explore common FAQs

Below are some frequently asked questions about Maryland insurance education requirements and the licensing process. If you still have uncertainties, consider reaching out for personalized guidance so you can feel completely prepared.

1.  How many pre-licensing hours are required for each line of authority?  
    None — Maryland eliminated its mandatory pre-licensing education requirement effective October 1, 2024. Optional exam-prep courses are commonly around 20 hours for life insurance, 20 hours for health, and roughly 40 hours if you study life and health together, but these are course lengths rather than state requirements, and availability/length can vary by line and provider. Requirements do change from time to time, so be sure to check the latest details with the Maryland Insurance Administration.
2.  Do I need to take separate courses if I want to hold multiple licenses?  
    Since pre-licensing is optional in Maryland, you aren’t required to take any course. If you do choose optional exam prep for multiple license types, you’ll generally need separate study materials for each, unless a combined course is available (such as life and health). Always verify with your chosen provider whether they offer bundled classes that can save you time and money.
3.  Can I take my pre-licensing course online, or do I have to attend in-person classes?  
    Many course providers let you study online, which can be helpful if you work full-time or have a busy schedule — Maryland doesn't approve or "recognize" prelicensing or exam-prep courses, so choose one built to the current Prometric Maryland content outline rather than looking for a state seal. In-person options still exist for those who learn best in a classroom environment. Pick whichever format helps you absorb the material effectively.
4.  How do I schedule my [Maryland insurance licensing exam](/maryland/requirements)?  
    You can register for the exam directly through Prometric’s website or phone system whenever you feel ready — Maryland doesn’t require a pre-licensing certificate of completion to schedule it. You’ll just need to pay the exam fee. Exam sessions are offered regularly, so you can usually choose a date and location that works for you.
5.  What if I fail my exam on the first try?  
    Failing an exam isn’t the end of the road. You can retake it, though you may need to wait a set number of days and pay another exam fee. As soon as you know which topics tripped you up, go back through your course materials and focus on those areas. With additional study and practice exams, your next attempt will likely be more successful.
