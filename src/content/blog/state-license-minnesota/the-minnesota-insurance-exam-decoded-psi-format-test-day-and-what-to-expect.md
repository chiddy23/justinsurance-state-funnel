The Minnesota state insurance licensing exam is the final academic hurdle between completing your [prelicensing](/minnesota/prelicensing) education and holding an active producer license. Most candidates who fail do so not because they lack insurance knowledge but because they underestimated the state law section, did not understand the exam's structure, or arrived at the test center unprepared for the procedural requirements. This post decodes the Minnesota PSI exam completely — how it is structured, what question types appear, how the scoring works, what to bring, what to expect on test day whether you test in person or remotely, and what happens immediately after you finish.

## Who Administers the Minnesota Insurance Exam

Minnesota contracts with PSI Services LLC to administer all insurance producer licensing exams. PSI is a national testing company that administers licensing exams across multiple professions and states. For Minnesota insurance licensing purposes, PSI manages exam registration, test center operations, remote testing, score reporting, and result transmission to the Minnesota Department of Commerce.

PSI's Minnesota insurance exam portal: test-takers.psiexams.com/mnins PSI scheduling phone number: (866) 395-1006

The Department of Commerce does not administer exams directly. All exam-related logistics — scheduling, rescheduling, score reports, technical support — go through PSI, not the Department.

## The Two-Part Structure: General and State-Specific

Every Minnesota insurance licensing exam is divided into two content sections that appear within a single exam session:

The General Section tests foundational insurance knowledge that is applicable across all states — policy types, insurance terms and concepts, policy provisions and contract law, the role of producers, basic underwriting principles, and federal regulations that affect insurance. This section is grounded in the universal principles of insurance that apply regardless of where a producer is licensed.

The State-Specific Section tests Minnesota insurance law, statutes, regulations, and practices that are unique to Minnesota. This is where many candidates underperform — they study the general insurance principles thoroughly but do not dedicate sufficient attention to Minnesota-specific law. The state law section covers Minnesota's producer licensing statutes (Chapter 60K), Minnesota's insurance code provisions (Chapters 60A–79A), Minnesota-specific auto insurance rules (the no-fault system, 30/60/10 minimums, mandatory PIP and UM/UIM), Minnesota workers' compensation requirements, Minnesota unfair trade practices law, and the regulatory structure of the Minnesota Department of Commerce.

Both sections appear within the same exam sitting — you do not take them separately. Your score reflects your performance across the full exam.

## Exam Specifications by Line of Authority

The number of questions, time allowed, and content distribution differ by line of authority. All questions are multiple-choice.

Pretest questions: PSI exams may include a small number of unscored pretest questions that are being evaluated for future use. These questions are not identified during the exam — they look identical to scored questions. Do not try to identify pretest questions; simply answer every question as if it counts.

Time per question: On a 2-hour, 85-question exam, you have approximately 85 seconds per question on average. On a 3-hour combined exam, approximately 75 seconds per question. These are averages — some questions take 20 seconds and others take two minutes. Manage your time by moving through questions at a steady pace and flagging difficult ones to return to rather than stalling.

## Question Format and Style

All Minnesota PSI exam questions are four-option multiple choice. One answer is definitively correct; three are incorrect or inferior. The questions are written in a direct, factual style — they test whether you know the rule, the definition, the statute, or the procedure, not whether you can reason around ambiguity.

Question stems are precise. PSI questions are written to test a specific piece of knowledge. The question will ask about a specific concept, a specific statute, or a specific scenario — not a general discussion topic. If a question asks "Under Minnesota law, what is the minimum PIP coverage required in an auto insurance policy?" the correct answer is $40,000. There is no room for interpretation.

Common question types:

Definition questions: "Which of the following best describes [term]?" Tests whether you know the precise definition of an insurance concept or statutory term.

Application questions: "A Minnesota producer does X. Which of the following best describes the regulatory consequence?" Tests whether you can apply a rule or statute to a fact pattern.

Procedure questions: "What must a producer do before [specific action]?" Tests whether you know the required sequence or procedural steps under Minnesota law.

Calculation questions (rare): Some exams include simple numerical calculations — premium calculations, benefit calculations, or coverage limit applications. These are straightforward when you know the underlying rule.

Answer choice patterns: PSI exam answer choices are typically constructed so that two of the four options are clearly incorrect, one is plausible but wrong, and one is correct. After eliminating the clearly incorrect options, focus your analysis on distinguishing between the plausible-but-wrong answer and the correct answer. The distinguishing factor is almost always a specific word, a numerical value, or a statutory requirement that makes one option precisely correct and the other subtly wrong.

## The Passing Standard

The minimum passing score for the Minnesota insurance licensing exam is 70% across all lines and exam types. PSI calculates scores on a scaled basis — the raw score is converted to a scaled score ranging from 0 to 100. A scaled score of 70 or higher is a pass.

Score reports are immediate. At the conclusion of your exam, your pass or fail result appears on the computer screen before you leave the testing area. You do not wait days or weeks for results — you know when you walk out whether you passed.

If you pass: Your score report includes information about next steps for applying for your Minnesota license through NIPR or Sircon. PSI transmits your results to the Department of Commerce electronically, typically within one business day.

If you fail: Your score report includes your numerical score and diagnostic information organized by content area — showing which topic areas you answered correctly versus incorrectly. This diagnostic breakdown is the most valuable tool for targeted retake preparation. Study specifically the areas where your diagnostic shows weakness; do not re-study everything equally.

Retakes: There is no limit on the number of times you can retake the Minnesota insurance exam. A 24-hour waiting period is required between attempts. The $45 exam fee must be paid for each attempt. Your exam results are valid for three years from the date you pass — giving you substantial time to complete fingerprinting and submit your license application after passing.

## Scheduling Your Exam

Registration: Schedule at test-takers.psiexams.com/mnins or call PSI at (866) 395-1006. You must have your Certificate of Completion from your prelicensing course in hand before scheduling — do not schedule before your certificate is issued.

Advance notice: Exams must be scheduled at least two days in advance for remote testing. In-person exams can often be scheduled with shorter lead time depending on test center availability.

Cancellation and rescheduling: You may cancel or reschedule up to 48 hours before your appointment without forfeiting the exam fee. Cancellations with less than 48 hours' notice forfeit the full $45 fee regardless of the reason.

Payment: The $45 exam fee must be paid at the time of reservation by credit card, debit card, or voucher. Payment cannot be made at the test center on the day of the exam.

## In-Person Testing: What to Expect

Test center locations: PSI operates insurance exam centers in Minnesota at Rochester, St. Paul (Brainseed Testing Center, Hamline Park Plaza, 570 Asbury St. Suite 206), Willmar, Woodbury, and Brainerd, among others. The full current list of test center locations and hours is available through PSI's scheduling portal.

Arrival: Arrive at the test center 30 minutes before your scheduled exam time. This allows time for check-in procedures — identity verification, biometric processing, locker assignment, and seating. Arriving late — even by a few minutes — may result in being denied entry and forfeiting your exam fee. The test center does not accommodate late arrivals.

What to bring:

Valid government-issued photo ID (driver's license, passport, military ID, or employee ID)

Your Certificate of Completion from your prelicensing course

What you cannot bring into the testing area: All personal items are prohibited in the testing room. This includes bags, wallets, cell phones, calculators, electronic devices of any kind, wrist watches, food, chewing gum, and tobacco products. A beverage is typically permitted. Personal items must remain in your vehicle or be secured in the provided storage space at the test center. Do not bring anything you cannot leave outside the testing room.

At your workstation: You will be seated at a computer workstation with a monitor, keyboard, and mouse. Scratch paper or an erasable notepad may be provided for calculations or notes — confirm with the test center. The exam interface is straightforward and does not require any technical skill beyond basic computer operation. You can flag questions to review, move forward and backward through questions, and change answers before final submission.

Breaks: The exam clock continues to run during any breaks. There are no scheduled breaks built into the exam. If you need a restroom break during the exam, you may take one — but the clock does not pause. For a 2-hour exam this is rarely an issue; plan accordingly.

## Remote Testing via PSI Bridge: What to Expect

Minnesota insurance exams can be taken remotely through PSI Bridge — PSI's online proctored testing platform. Remote testing allows candidates to take the exam from home or any private space with a compatible setup.

Technical requirements:

Computer or laptop (not a tablet or phone)

Compatible with PSI Bridge — test compatibility at syscheck.bridge.psiexams.com before scheduling

Google Chrome browser (required — no other browser works)

Functioning webcam (built-in or external)

Functioning microphone

Stable internet connection

Download PSI Bridge: Download the PSI Bridge software before your exam day. The download can take up to 30 minutes — do this well in advance, not the morning of your exam.

Login timing: Log in to the testing platform 30 minutes before your exam reservation time. This allows for identity verification, room scan, and system check before the exam begins.

Room requirements: You must be in a private room with the door closed. No other person may be in the room during the exam. The surface your computer is on must be clear of all objects — papers, books, notebooks, additional monitors, phones. You cannot have headphones, a phone, or a wristwatch visible during the exam. Food and drink are prohibited except for a clear beverage in a clear container (confirm with PSI's current guidelines at the time of scheduling).

Identity verification: The proctor verifies your identity through webcam before the exam begins. Have your government-issued photo ID ready and visible to the camera.

Monitoring: A PSI proctor monitors your exam session in real time via webcam. The proctor can communicate with you through a live chat function for non-content questions. Any suspicious behavior — looking away from the screen, leaving the room, talking — may result in the exam being terminated.

Spanish language option: PSI offers the Minnesota insurance exam in Spanish. Select the Spanish option when scheduling if you prefer to test in Spanish.

## What the State Law Section Actually Covers

The state-specific section of the Minnesota exam is where candidates most often underperform, and it warrants specific preparation attention beyond the general principles content.

Key Minnesota-specific topics tested:

Producer licensing (Chapter 60K): Prelicensing requirements (20 hours per line), the certification exam proctor requirement, appointment and termination procedures (Minn. Stat. §60K.49), license renewal (birth month, biennial), CE requirements (24 hours including 3 ethics), reinstatement rules, and grounds for license denial, suspension, or revocation.

Minnesota auto insurance — no-fault system: Minnesota is a no-fault state under Minn. Stat. §65B.49. Required minimum coverages: 30/60/10 liability, $40,000 PIP ($20,000 medical + $20,000 non-medical), and mandatory UM/UIM at 25/50. PIP pays regardless of fault. Tort threshold: medical expenses exceeding $4,000 (excluding diagnostic tests), permanent injury, disfigurement, or death.

Minnesota workers' compensation: Threshold of one employee triggers mandatory coverage. Competitive market (not monopolistic). Penalty for non-coverage: up to $1,000 per employee per week plus 65% surcharge on claims. Minnesota Department of Labor and Industry administers claims; Department of Commerce regulates carriers.

Minnesota unfair trade practices (Minn. Stat. §72A.20): Misrepresentation, false advertising, defamation, boycott/coercion/intimidation, unfair discrimination, rebating, and unfair claims settlement practices.

Minnesota Department of Commerce structure: The Commissioner of Commerce holds regulatory authority over insurance in Minnesota. The Department is part of the Department of Regulatory Agencies. Contact: 85 7th Place East, Suite 280, St. Paul, MN 55101; (651) 539-1599.

Minnesota health insurance: MNsure as the state-based exchange; Medical Assistance (Medicaid); MinnesotaCare (Basic Health Program for incomes up to 200% FPL, unique to Minnesota); guarantee funds (Minnesota Life and Health Guaranty Association under Minn. Stat. §61B; Minnesota Insurance Guaranty Association for P&C under Minn. Stat. §60C).

## The Day Before and Day Of: A Preparation Checklist

Day before:

Confirm your exam appointment time and location (or remote setup)

Test your PSI Bridge setup if testing remotely

Locate your Certificate of Completion and government-issued ID

Review the state law section topics one final time — particularly Minnesota-specific auto insurance minimums, CE requirements, licensing fees, and unfair trade practices definitions

Get a full night of sleep — cognitive performance on a knowledge exam is directly affected by fatigue

Morning of the exam:

For in-person: eat a normal meal, allow travel time to arrive 30 minutes early, bring only your ID and certificate

For remote: log in 30 minutes early, ensure your room is clear and private, have your ID visible and ready for camera verification

Do not attempt last-minute cramming of new material — review familiar concepts only

During the exam:

Read every question completely before looking at the answer choices

Eliminate clearly wrong answers before comparing the remaining options

Flag questions you are uncertain about and return to them after completing the rest

Do not leave any questions blank — an unanswered question is automatically wrong; a guess has a 25% chance of being correct

Trust your preparation on factual questions; do not second-guess answers you know confidently

## Frequently Asked Questions

How long does it take to receive my Minnesota license after passing the exam?

After passing the PSI exam, PSI transmits your results to the Minnesota Department of Commerce electronically, typically within one business day. Once your results are on file, you can submit your license application through NIPR or Sircon. Processing takes approximately 10 business days from submission. If you complete fingerprinting at the PSI test center on the day of your exam, that step is done simultaneously. A producer who passes the exam, gets fingerprinted the same day, and submits their NIPR application promptly can expect to receive their active license within two to three weeks of passing the exam.

The exam has both general and state sections. Should I study them equally?

No. Most candidates have better general insurance knowledge coming into the exam because the general section covers concepts they have studied in the prelicensing course and are more intuitive. The state-specific section is where preparation time pays the highest marginal return. Candidates who pass on their first attempt consistently report that focused study of Minnesota-specific statutes — particularly the auto insurance no-fault rules, the CE and renewal requirements, the unfair trade practices definitions, and the producer licensing statute numbers — made the difference. If your study time is limited before the exam, allocate more of it to Minnesota law than to general principles.

What happens if I pass only one section of the combined exam but not the other?

Minnesota does not offer partial credit for combined exams — passing one section does not give you partial credit or reduce what you must cover on a retake. The combined Life, Accident and Health exam (145 questions) and the combined Property and Casualty exam (130 questions) are scored as single exams. If you fail the overall exam, you retake the full exam — both sections — on your next attempt. There is no mechanism to sit for only the general section or only the state section. Your diagnostic score report after a failed attempt shows your performance by topic area, which allows you to identify where to focus your retake preparation.

Can I use scratch paper or notes during the exam?

At in-person PSI test centers, scratch paper or an erasable notepad may be provided for calculations — confirm the specific test center's policy when you check in. You may not bring any papers, notes, or study materials into the testing area. At remote exams via PSI Bridge, you may use blank scratch paper that you show to the proctor's camera before the exam begins. You cannot use pre-written notes, printed materials, or any other reference during either in-person or remote testing. The exam is closed-book by design — all answers must come from your prepared knowledge.

The Minnesota PSI insurance exam rewards candidates who prepare systematically, understand the exam's structure, and give specific attention to the state law section where most points are left on the table by underprepared candidates. Walk into the test center — or log into PSI Bridge — knowing the format, knowing Minnesota's specific rules, and knowing what to do when you encounter a question you are unsure about. The 70% passing standard is achievable for any candidate who treats prelicensing study as genuine exam preparation rather than a box to check.
