If you’re ready to pursue an exciting new career path, it’s important to fully understand the [Tennessee insurance license requirements](/tennessee/requirements) before you dive in. Whether you plan to tackle life and health or property and casualty lines, your next steps will involve some formal education, a licensing exam, and a bit of paperwork. A streamlined approach can make the entire process simpler—especially if you know what to expect. Below, you’ll find a helpful roadmap to get you from curious candidate to newly licensed insurance professional.

## Confirm your eligibility

The state of Tennessee requires you to be at least 18 years old to apply for an insurance license. You should also have a clear background, which generally means no serious legal or financial infractions on record. The Department of Commerce and Insurance may conduct a background check to verify that you meet all standards for licensure.

If you have concerns about your eligibility, you can consult the state’s guidelines or speak with a professional from JustInsurance. They’re a useful resource for clarifying how your recent history—like a past issue with credit or a minor offense—might impact your chances. Most people pass this step easily, so long as you’re forthcoming and provide any requested documentation.

## Complete pre-licensing education

Before you can take the licensing exam, you need to complete a [Tennessee prelicensing course](/tennessee/prelicensing) approved by the state. The number of hours required can differ depending on the line of authority—life, health, property, or casualty—but you can generally expect to complete at least 20–40 hours for each line you want to sell. This coursework is designed to ensure you have all the knowledge necessary to advise clients responsibly.

Pre-licensing courses are offered in various formats. Online programs often let you learn at your own pace, while in-person classes provide a structured schedule. It all comes down to your personal preference, but make sure you choose a Tennessee-approved provider so your completion certificate is valid when it’s time to schedule your exam. Many providers, including JustInsurance, cater specifically to Tennessee regulations and can pinpoint the key topics you must master.

## Pass the licensing exam

After you’ve completed your coursework, you’re ready to register for the state-approved exam. Each line of insurance—like life and health or property and casualty—has its own separate test. If you plan to sell multiple lines, you should expect to take more than one exam. The test itself is typically given at authorized testing centers and generally features multiple-choice questions about policy types, ethics, and state-specific regulations.

Exam success depends on how confidently you apply the material you’ve learned. Because the tests can be time-limited, practicing with mock exams is a good way to build confidence and identify any gaps in your knowledge. Below are a few additional tips to boost your chances of passing on the first try:

-   Start studying early and focus on any topics you find challenging
-   Use your pre-licensing textbook or online course materials as your primary guide
-   Take timed practice exams to get used to the format
-   Build a solid understanding of Tennessee’s unique insurance rules

When exam day arrives, make sure you bring any required identification documents and arrive early. If you do not pass on your first attempt, don’t let it discourage you. You can usually reschedule after a waiting period, so use that extra time to study the areas that gave you trouble.

## Submit your application

Once you’ve passed your exam, the next step is to submit your license application. You’ll normally apply online through the National Insurance Producer Registry (NIPR) or directly with the Tennessee Department of Commerce and Insurance. During this process, you’ll complete an application form and pay the required licensing fee.

Be prepared to provide details about your background, including any prior licenses or certifications. You may also need to grant permission for a fingerprinting process if you haven’t already done so. Once the department processes your application, you will receive confirmation that your license is active, and you’re officially authorized to offer insurance services in Tennessee.

## Maintain your license

Receiving your license is a major milestone, but your work doesn’t end there. The state requires you to meet [Tennessee continuing education](/tennessee/continuing-education) (CE) requirements to keep your license in good standing. Although the specific rules may vary, expectancy is around 24 credit hours of approved CE courses every two years, usually including a portion dedicated to ethics.

To avoid any last-minute scrambles, consider setting reminders as soon as you’re licensed. Map out which courses you’ll take and ensure they’re all approved for Tennessee’s CE requirements. Consistent compliance not only helps you renew on time but also keeps your knowledge current so you can offer sound advice to your clients.

## FAQs about [Tennessee insurance license](/tennessee) requirements

1.  Which lines of insurance can I get licensed for in Tennessee?  
    Tennessee offers licenses for several lines of authority. Common types include life, health, property, and casualty insurance. You can opt for one line at a time or pursue multiple lines if you’d like to expand your skill set.
2.  How long does the licensing process usually take?  
    This can vary, but most people complete the pre-licensing course in a few weeks, then schedule and take the exam shortly after. After passing, you’ll typically wait a few days to a couple of weeks for the Tennessee Department of Commerce and Insurance to process your application.
3.  Are fingerprinting and background checks required?  
    Yes. Tennessee generally requires fingerprinting and a background check to confirm you meet their standards. Make sure to factor in scheduling and fees for fingerprinting when budgeting your time and finances.
4.  What if I fail the insurance licensing exam?  
    You can retake the exam, though a waiting period or additional fees may apply. Many applicants pass on their first or second attempt, so focus on weak spots in your understanding before going back for a retest.
5.  Can an insurance license from another state be transferred to Tennessee?  
    If you already hold a valid license in a state with a reciprocal agreement, you may qualify for a license transfer or exemption from certain requirements. You’ll likely still have to apply formally and submit any necessary documents. Check Tennessee’s official guidelines to confirm your eligibility.
