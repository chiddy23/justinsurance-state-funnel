Natural curiosity about the possibilities of a new career often brings you to the question of how to get your [Minnesota insurance license](/minnesota). Whether you are switching careers or looking to expand your professional options, focusing on a streamlined path can save you both time and effort. Below, you will find a friendly, step-by-step guide designed to help you achieve your license as quickly and efficiently as possible. By following these steps, you will be poised to earn your credentials and begin offering life and health or property and casualty insurance services to the residents of Minnesota.

## Gather your prerequisites

Before beginning any formal insurance training or exam preparation, make sure you meet the basic requirements set by the Minnesota Department of Commerce. You must be at least 18 years old and have a reliable form of identification. It is also important to confirm which specific license type you need. Common options include life and health or property and casualty, though some applicants prefer to pursue both for broader career opportunities.

Spend a little time researching these license categories so you feel confident about your selection. Consider your future career goals, talk to potential employers, and explore different insurance lines to ensure that your choice aligns with your professional path.

## Complete your pre-licensing education

Before scheduling your exam, Minnesota requires you to complete a set number of pre-licensing training hours. While the exact requirements can vary depending on the type of insurance license, you should expect at least 20 hours of education for each major line of authority. For example, 20 hours might be required for health insurance and 20 hours for life insurance.

You can fast-track this process by choosing a structured, reputable course provider. Many prefer online programs since you can tackle lessons at your own pace and fit them into a busy schedule. As you progress through your coursework, pay attention to state regulations, ethical requirements, and basic insurance concepts. Your goal is to develop a solid foundation of industry knowledge so you feel prepared for the official exam.

## Pass your licensing exam

When you have met your pre-licensing education requirements, it is time to sit for the [Minnesota insurance licensing exam](/minnesota/requirements). Here is how to make the testing process as efficient as possible:

1.  Schedule promptly. Register through the authorized testing provider so you can secure your preferred test date and location. It is generally best to book your exam soon after you finish pre-licensing training so the information stays fresh in your mind.
2.  Review the exam details. Familiarize yourself with the test format. For most lines of authority, you can expect a combination of multiple-choice questions covering general insurance concepts and state-specific regulations.
3.  Practice, practice, practice. Work through practice exams, sample questions, and study guides to pinpoint any weak spots. Consistency in your study habits can dramatically improve your speed and accuracy on test day.
4.  Prepare on exam day. Arrive early, bring valid identification, and approach each question methodically. If you feel stuck on a concept, mark it and move on so you can return to it with a fresh perspective.

After completing the exam, you will receive your score. If you pass, you are one step away from officially earning your Minnesota insurance license.

## Apply for your insurance license

Passing your exam marks an important milestone. Next, you need to apply for your Minnesota insurance license. Here is what you need to do:

• Submit your application. Complete the official application through the Minnesota Department of Commerce. You may be asked to provide proof of your pre-licensing education and exam results during this process.  
• Pay the required fee. Bear in mind that licensing fees are subject to change, so check the latest guidelines to be sure of the current amount. You will often pay online when sending your application.  
• Finish the background check. Certain fingerprinting and background check procedures may apply. Follow the steps carefully and promptly to avoid delays.

Once the state approves your application, you will receive your official insurance producer license. Keep a copy of this documentation for your records, and be sure to notify any potential employers that you are ready for work.

## Maintain your license

After the initial excitement of earning your Minnesota insurance license, do not forget about the ongoing continuing education (CE) requirements. Minnesota wants producers to stay current on industry knowledge and best practices, so you must complete a set number of CE hours (often 24 hours every two years) to renew your license. These courses typically cover relevant state regulations and advanced insurance topics that help you grow professionally.

Staying on top of your CE credits also enhances your reputation as a reliable, well-prepared insurance professional. It keeps you informed about emerging trends, helps you serve clients better, and ensures that you maintain compliance with state regulations.

## Frequently asked questions

1.  How long does it take to finish pre-licensing courses?  
    This timeline can vary depending on your chosen format. Some people complete a 20-hour course in a few days with intensive study, while others spread it out over multiple weeks. Plan according to your schedule and learning style.
2.  Do I need separate licenses for life insurance and health insurance?  
    Yes. Each line of insurance has its own license, and you must meet the pre-licensing and exam requirements for each. If you want to sell both life and health, you will need to obtain both licenses.
3.  How many times can I retake the exam?  
    Minnesota does not have a strict limit on retakes, but each exam attempt requires a new registration and fee. If you need to retake the exam, spend extra time reviewing your weak areas before trying again.
4.  What if I need to move or change my name after I am licensed?  
    You must notify the Minnesota Department of Commerce when significant changes occur, such as an address or name change. It is best to handle these updates as soon as possible to keep your records accurate.
5.  Can I switch from a resident license to a non-resident license if I move out of Minnesota?  
    Yes. You can generally request a non-resident license in your old state of residence if you move. Be sure to check the local guidelines and notify the Minnesota Department of Commerce in a timely manner.
