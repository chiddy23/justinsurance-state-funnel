Every licensed insurance agent and adjuster in Texas has to complete ethics [continuing education](/texas/continuing-education) — it's not optional, and it's not something you can swap out for another topic. TDI treats ethics as a core part of staying licensed. Understanding why it's required and what's actually covered makes the 3-hour requirement feel a lot less like a box to check and more like a tool that protects your career.

Here's what Texas ethics CE actually covers.

## The Basic Requirement

Texas requires [3 hours of ethics continuing education in every 2-year renewal cycle](https://www.tdi.texas.gov/agent/agcehome.html). These 3 hours are [part of your total 24-hour CE requirement](https://www.tdi.texas.gov/agent/agcehome.html), not in addition to it — but they're a mandatory subset that can't be substituted with other coursework.

The requirement [applies to both agents and adjusters](https://www.tdi.texas.gov/agent/agcehome.html). Whether you sell policies or handle claims, ethics CE is required to renew your license.

## Why Ethics CE Is Required

Insurance is built on trust. Agents and adjusters hold significant responsibility — they guide financial decisions, handle sensitive personal information, and process claims that can change a client's life. When that trust breaks down, it hurts clients, companies, and the industry as a whole.

Texas requires ethics CE to make sure licensed professionals regularly revisit the standards that define responsible conduct. It's also a safeguard against problem areas that come up repeatedly in regulatory actions — things like misrepresentation, churning, and fiduciary breaches.

## Core Topics Covered in Texas Ethics CE

Approved ethics courses typically cover a standard set of topics, though individual courses may emphasize different areas. Expect to see:

Fiduciary duty to clients. Agents act in a position of trust. This means putting the client's interests ahead of your own commission and recommending products that genuinely fit their needs.

Fair dealing and honest representation. Misrepresenting policy terms, exaggerating benefits, or downplaying exclusions are all forms of ethical failure. Courses walk through real examples of what fair dealing looks like in practice.

Confidentiality and handling of personal information. Clients share sensitive financial and medical data with agents. Ethics CE reinforces the responsibility to protect that information.

Conflicts of interest. When an agent stands to benefit personally from a recommendation, that's a conflict. Ethics CE helps agents identify and disclose these situations rather than ignoring them.

Unfair trade practices. Texas law prohibits specific practices — twisting, churning, rebating, misrepresentation. Ethics CE covers each of these and explains what they look like in real agent conduct.

Replacement ethics. Replacing an existing policy with a new one is allowed — but only when it benefits the client. Doing it to generate commission is unethical and illegal.

Complaint handling and regulatory responsibility. How to respond when a client has a complaint, and your obligations when you're aware of unethical conduct by others.

## Why This Matters for Your Career

Ethics violations aren't an abstract concern. The conduct these 3 hours walk through — misrepresentation, twisting, churning, misappropriation of premium — is conduct Texas law prohibits outright, and TDI's authority in those cases runs against your license, not just against a single sale. That's the practical reason the ethics hours are mandatory rather than optional: the course covers the exact lines that carry licensing consequences if you cross them.

Treating ethics CE as real training rather than a checkbox is inexpensive protection for a license you spent real money and study time to earn.

## 5 Frequently Asked Questions

- Can I take more than 3 hours of ethics and count the extra toward my general CE? Yes. If you complete 6 hours of ethics, 3 count toward the ethics requirement and the other 3 count toward your 21 general hours.
- Does the same ethics course count every renewal cycle? Technically yes, as long as it's still TDI-approved. However, taking the same course repeatedly isn't recommended — you get more value from courses that cover different angles each cycle.
- Are online ethics courses accepted? Yes. TDI accepts approved online ethics courses, and most agents complete their ethics CE online.
- What happens if I complete all 24 hours but skip the ethics portion? Your CE won't be considered complete. The 3-hour ethics requirement is mandatory — missing it means your renewal won't be accepted even if your total hours meet 24.
- Is there a Texas-specific ethics requirement or is it generic? Approved Texas ethics CE covers ethical conduct as it applies under Texas law and TDI regulations. Generic national ethics content doesn't fully meet the Texas standard.
