Passing the state exam gets you licensed. Staying compliant as a working Alabama agent is a separate challenge — and the Alabama Insurance Code (Title 27 of the Code of Alabama, 1975) is the master rulebook you need to navigate. It governs how you sell, market, and service insurance in Alabama, and understanding its key sections protects your license, your clients, and your career.

Here are the most important areas of the Alabama Insurance Code every producer needs to understand.

## What the Alabama Insurance Code Is

The Alabama Insurance Code is the statutory framework that regulates insurance in Alabama. It's found in Title 27 of the Code of Alabama, 1975, with producer-specific provisions primarily in Chapter 27-7 (Insurance Producers) and [continuing education](/alabama/continuing-education) provisions under Chapter 27-8A.

The Code is administered by the Alabama Department of Insurance (ALDOI), led by the Commissioner of Insurance.

The Code covers everything from licensing and producer duties to policy forms, claims handling, consumer protections, and enforcement. Every licensed producer, broker, adjuster, and insurance company operating in Alabama is bound by it.

## Key Sections Every Producer Should Know

Chapter 27-7 — Insurance Producers. Covers who can be licensed, qualifications required, standards of conduct, and ALDOI's authority to discipline licensees. This is the foundation of your legal authority as an Alabama producer.

Chapter 27-8A — Continuing Education. The statutory basis for Alabama's CE requirements (24 hours biennially, 3 ethics) implemented through ALDOI Regulation 110.

Chapter 27-9A — Adjusters. Separate framework for insurance adjusters, with specific licensing and CE requirements.

Unfair Methods of Competition and Unfair Practices. Alabama prohibits specific conduct considered unfair or deceptive — misrepresentation, twisting, churning, rebating, defamation, and more. Violations here are the most common source of producer disciplinary action.

Replacement of Life Insurance and Annuities. Specific rules govern the replacement of existing life insurance and annuity contracts. Disclosure requirements, required forms, and notification obligations to the existing insurer are covered.

Unfair Claims Settlement Practices. Rules governing how claims must be handled by insurers and adjusters. Producers need to understand these to advise clients when claims don't go smoothly.

Free-Look Periods. Alabama requires free-look periods on life insurance and annuity contracts, with longer periods for replacement policies.

Consumer Protection Laws. Provisions that protect policyholders during the sales process, policy delivery, and ongoing service.

Long-Term Care Insurance Standards. Specific rules for LTC sales, including the initial 8-hour training requirement and 4-hour ongoing training every 24 months.

Annuity Best Interest Standard. Alabama requires producers to complete a one-time 4-hour Annuity Best Interest training before selling annuities, reflecting enhanced suitability standards.

Storm and Windstorm Insurance. Given Alabama's Gulf Coast exposure, specific provisions address windstorm and flood insurance in coastal areas.

Privacy and Information Protection. How you collect, store, and share client information is regulated under both Alabama law and federal frameworks like HIPAA and GLBA.

## The Role of the Alabama Commissioner of Insurance

Alabama's insurance regulator is the Commissioner of Insurance, appointed by the Governor. Unlike states with elected commissioners, Alabama uses an appointment model. The Commissioner leads the ALDOI and has substantial authority:

Licensing insurance producers and companies

Regulating insurance companies operating in Alabama

Enforcing the Insurance Code

Investigating consumer complaints

Imposing administrative penalties

Issuing cease and desist orders

Adopting regulations to implement the Code

## Recent Significant Changes

Alabama's insurance regulatory environment has seen meaningful recent changes, including:

Alabama Act No. 2023-104. Effective January 1, 2024, this act removed Alabama's [prelicensing](/alabama/prelicensing) education requirement for insurance producers. The ALDOI amended Insurance Regulation No. 147 to implement this change.

Surplus Line Broker Bond Requirement. Effective January 1, 2025, Alabama requires nonresident surplus line brokers to maintain a $50,000 bond on file (Form AL-SLB-13). Resident surplus line brokers were already subject to this requirement.

Title Insurance Agent Fee Updates. Effective October 1, 2024, Alabama updated Title Insurance Agent license class fees for resident renewals.

Limited Lines Credit Insurance Updates. Effective July 1, 2024, Alabama updated verbiage and requirements for resident and nonresident Limited Lines: Credit Insurance licenses, including for business entities.

These changes reflect Alabama's ongoing modernization of its regulatory framework. Subscribe to ALDOI bulletins to stay current.

## Why Knowing the Code Matters

New producers sometimes treat the Insurance Code as academic — material to study for the exam and forget afterward. That's a serious mistake.

Every disciplinary action the ALDOI takes against an Alabama producer is rooted in a specific section of the Code. Understanding the rules isn't just about passing the exam — it's about recognizing what you can and can't do, what you must disclose, and what conduct puts your license at risk.

The ALDOI also publishes bulletins, regulations, and guidance through aldoi.gov that clarify how specific sections are applied. Following these updates is part of being a compliant working producer.

## Alabama's Regulatory Environment

Compared to some other states, Alabama's regulatory environment is generally viewed as:

Business-friendly while maintaining consumer protection

Modernized with the recent removal of prelicense education and other streamlining changes

Well-defined with clear rules in major areas

Balanced between facilitating market access and protecting consumers

Responsive to coastal insurance challenges given Gulf Coast hurricane exposure

Working in Alabama means real attention to compliance — but with clearer rules than in some more heavily-regulated states.

## Distinguishing Code from ALDOI Regulations

Alabama insurance law has two layers:

The Insurance Code (Title 27) — laws passed by the Alabama Legislature

ALDOI Regulations — administrative rules adopted by the ALDOI to implement statutes (notably Regulation 110 for CE and Regulation 147 for licensing)

Both are legally binding. "Alabama insurance law" typically refers to both together.

## How to Stay Compliant

Know the rules for your products. If you sell life insurance, know replacement rules. If you sell annuities, know Best Interest. If you sell LTC, know the specific LTC requirements. If you sell flood or coastal property, know the specific coastal rules.

Document everything. Notes on client conversations, reasons for recommendations, forms signed and retained. Documentation is your primary defense if questions arise.

Follow ALDOI bulletins. Subscribe to ALDOI communications and review them periodically through aldoi.gov.

Complete your CE seriously. Continuing education is where compliance knowledge stays current. Don't treat it as busywork.

When uncertain, ask. The ALDOI and reputable industry attorneys can provide guidance on specific situations. Getting clarification before acting beats explaining after the fact.

## 5 Frequently Asked Questions

- Do I need to read the entire Alabama Insurance Code? No. Focus on the sections that apply to your practice — unfair practices, replacement rules, suitability, free-look periods, lapse and grace periods, and the specific product areas you sell.
- Where can I access the Alabama Insurance Code? Title 27 of the Code of Alabama, 1975 is available through the Alabama Legislature's website. ALDOI regulations and guidance are available at aldoi.gov.
- How often does Alabama insurance law change? Legislative changes happen periodically. The ALDOI also issues regulations and bulletins regularly. Significant changes are typically publicized through ALDOI notices.
- What happens if I unintentionally violate a Code provision? Intent matters in the ALDOI's evaluation, but it's not a complete defense. Penalties range from warnings to fines, suspension, or revocation depending on severity, history, and circumstances.
- Is the Code the same as the insurance laws tested on the exam? Yes — the state law portion of the Alabama exam draws directly from the Insurance Code and ALDOI regulations.
