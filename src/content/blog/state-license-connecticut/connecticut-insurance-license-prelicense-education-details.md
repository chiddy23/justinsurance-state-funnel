Connecticut requires prelicense education for most insurance producer licenses, and understanding the specific requirements helps you choose the right course and complete it efficiently. Whether you're pursuing Life, Health, Property, Casualty, or combined licenses, Connecticut's prelicense rules have specific details every candidate should know.

Here's everything you need to know about Connecticut insurance prelicense education.

## Why Connecticut Requires Prelicense Education

Connecticut requires prelicense education to ensure new producers have foundational knowledge before sitting for the state exam. Unlike states that allow exam-only licensing, Connecticut wants candidates to demonstrate they've been exposed to required content.

The requirement is established under Connecticut General Statutes §38a-702 and implementing regulations.

## Hour Requirements by License Type

Connecticut requires specific hours of prelicense education for each line of authority:

Per individual line of authority:

Life Insurance: 20 hours

Accident & Health Insurance: 20 hours

Property Insurance: 20 hours

Casualty Insurance: 20 hours

Personal Lines: 20 hours

For combined licenses:

Life and Health combined: 40 hours total

Property and Casualty combined: 40 hours total

Most candidates pursue the combined Life and Health or combined Property and Casualty path, requiring 40 hours of prelicense education.

## What Counts as Approved Prelicense Education

Only prelicense courses approved by the Connecticut Insurance Department count toward your prelicense requirement. Approved courses must:

Be from a Connecticut-approved provider

Cover required content per Connecticut standards

Include forced progression (you must complete each section before moving to next)

Include chapter quizzes (70%+ required to advance)

Include a final certification exam

Be monitored by a proctor for the final exam

If a provider isn't Connecticut-approved, completing their course doesn't satisfy Connecticut's prelicense requirement, even if the content is excellent.

## Course Format and Structure

Connecticut prelicense courses follow specific structural requirements:

Forced Progression. Connecticut is a "forced progression" or "locked" state. This means:

You must review each lesson before moving to the next

You must pass chapter quizzes (70%+ minimum) before advancing

You can't skip ahead even if you know the material

The course tracks your time and progression

Time Tracking. The course tracks time spent on materials. The required total hours must be invested before completion.

Chapter Quizzes. Each chapter typically includes a quiz. You must pass with 70%+ before advancing.

Final Certification Exam. A final exam covers all course content. You must pass with 70%+ to receive your Certificate of Completion.

## Proctoring Requirements

Connecticut requires the final certification exam to be monitored by a proctor.

Proctor Definition. A "disinterested third party with no conflict of interest who verifies the student's identity and processes an affidavit testifying that the student has received no outside assistance" during the exam.

Acceptable proctors:

Public librarians

State or community college employees

Teachers (not your own teacher)

Various other professionals serving as disinterested third parties

Certain online proctoring services (verify approval)

Unacceptable proctors:

Family members

Relatives or dependents

Employers or supervisors

Employees or subordinates

Business partners

Anyone with economic or other interest in your success

Proctor's Role. Verify your identity, ensure no outside assistance, and sign an affidavit confirming the exam was completed properly.

## Course Completion and Certificate

When you successfully complete prelicense:

You receive a Certificate of Completion. This certificate is your evidence of prelicense compliance.

Certificate validity: Valid for one year from date of completion. You must take the state exam within this window.

Bring certificate to exam. You'll present this at the Pearson VUE testing center when taking your state exam.

Save the certificate. Keep digital and printed copies as backup.

Reporting: The provider electronically reports your completion to Connecticut, but maintaining your certificate is essential.

## Designation-Based Exemptions

Connecticut allows certain industry designation holders to bypass prelicense education entirely. To qualify, you must hold one of the recognized designations and provide a current Letter of Designation.

For Life license exemption:

Certified Employee Benefit Specialist (CEBS)

Chartered Financial Consultant (ChFC®)

Certified Insurance Counselor (CIC)

Certified Financial Planner (CFP®)

Chartered Life Underwriter (CLU®)

Fellow, Life Management Institute (FLMI)

Life Underwriter Training Council Fellow (LUTCF)

For Accident & Health license exemption:

Certified Employee Benefit Specialist (CEBS)

Chartered Life Underwriter (CLU®)

Health Insurance Associate (HIA)

Registered Employee Benefits Consultant (REBC)

Registered Health Underwriter (RHU)

For Property, Casualty, and Personal Lines exemption:

Accredited Adviser in Insurance (AAI)

Associate in Risk Management (ARM)

Certified Insurance Counselor (CIC)

Chartered Property Casualty Underwriter (CPCU®)

How to apply for exemption:

Email a request and current Letter of Designation to: cid.licensing@ct.gov (Attn: Producer Examination Exemption)

The CID reviews exemption requests and confirms your status. Note: Even with prelicense exemption, you may still need to take the state exam — the exemption applies specifically to prelicense education, not necessarily the exam (verify with CID).

## Choosing a Prelicense Provider

When selecting a Connecticut prelicense provider:

Verify CID approval. Ensure the provider and specific course are CID-approved. Approval lists are typically available through the CID.

Check course content quality. Reading reviews, comparing course outlines, and reviewing sample materials helps choose effective providers.

Consider learning format. Connecticut prelicense is typically self-paced online, though some classroom options may exist.

Evaluate practice exam availability. Quality prelicense courses include practice exams that significantly improve real exam pass rates.

Verify proctoring options. Make sure the provider's proctoring options work for your situation.

Confirm reporting practices. Provider should report completions promptly to Connecticut.

Consider customer support. Access to help when questions arise matters.

## Time to Complete Prelicense

Most candidates complete Connecticut prelicense in 1-2 weeks:

Highly motivated candidates studying intensively: 5-7 days

Working professionals studying part-time: 1-2 weeks

Candidates studying very part-time: 3-4 weeks

The required total hours must be invested regardless of pace. Don't try to shortcut the time tracking — it doesn't work and creates compliance issues.

## Common Prelicense Mistakes

Choosing unapproved providers. Providers must be CID-approved. Verify before enrolling.

Skipping ahead in materials. Forced progression prevents this, but some candidates try and create problems.

Inadequate proctoring. Using inappropriate proctors invalidates the exam.

Letting Certificate expire. Your one-year window passes faster than expected. Schedule your state exam early.

Insufficient practice. Treating prelicense as completion without engaging with practice questions limits exam preparation.

Failing to bring Certificate to exam. Your Certificate of Completion is required at Pearson VUE testing center.

## Connecting Prelicense to Exam Success

Quality prelicense education sets you up for state exam success:

Foundation knowledge. The required hours give you exposure to all major content areas tested on the state exam.

Practice opportunities. Quality prelicense includes practice questions building exam-style thinking.

Final exam similarity. The prelicense final exam mirrors state exam format, giving you practice with the testing approach.

Connecticut-specific content. Quality prelicense covers Connecticut-specific laws and regulations tested on the state exam.

Don't treat prelicense as just paperwork — treat it as exam preparation.

## What Comes After Prelicense

Once you have your Certificate of Completion:

Schedule your state exam. Within one year of prelicense completion.

Pass the state exam. With 70%+ score.

Apply for license. Through NIPR with $140 application fee.

Complete background check. State and federal criminal history check (no fingerprinting required).

Receive license. Typically 7-10 business days for clean applications.

## 5 Frequently Asked Questions

- How long is Connecticut prelicense education? 20 hours per individual line, or 40 hours for combined Life and Health or Property and Casualty licenses.
- How long is my Certificate of Completion valid? One year from date of completion. You must take the state exam within this window.
- Can I skip prelicense if I have an industry designation? Yes. Connecticut allows certain designations (CEBS, ChFC®, CLU®, CFP®, CPCU®, etc.) to exempt holders from prelicense. Email cid.licensing@ct.gov with your Letter of Designation.
- Who can serve as a proctor for the final certification exam? A disinterested third party with no conflict of interest. Public librarians, college employees, teachers (not your own), and similar professionals. Family members, employers, and people with economic interest in your success cannot serve.
- What happens if my Certificate expires before I take the state exam? You'll need to retake prelicense education. The one-year validity is firm.
